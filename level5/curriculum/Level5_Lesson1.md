# Level 5 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 1 (Week 1): How Long Have You Been Working Here?

**Status:** Published. Listening audio, lesson pages, and teacher slides are ready.

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures + `images/CREDITS_L5_L1.md`). Listening audio: `L5_L1_listening.mp3` (1:37).

> **Topic:** A practice job interview at a community center. Learners say how long they have been doing something, give one accomplishment with an example, and ask a follow-up question. Week 1 also has a short welcome, the first can-do check, and a baseline snapshot for about five learners.

## How to Use This Plan

> **Teach in English.** You do not need to speak the learners' languages. Show the picture, draw the line, and point to the grammar box. There is no contrast-tip box. The common mix-up is a row in the mistakes table.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.*

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Is the job still true, or is the year finished?"), **then go**.

> **When a learner is stuck:** (1) show it (picture, gesture, or the timeline); (2) give one more English example; (3) point to the grammar box; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English.

> **About four sentences:** the Talk Time answer and the exit ticket are about four connected sentences: how long, what you have been doing, one example, and why it matters.

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk · 6 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Week 1:** there is no homework to check. Say: "Welcome. Tonight is practice, not a real interview. You may invent the hotel and the dates."
- **Before class:** write on the board: *How long have you…?* and *I've been…*
- **Opening Talk:** learners open the Opening Talk page. A asks a card question. B answers in a short summary (the answer, one detail). A asks one follow-up. Then switch.
- **Model with one learner:** "How long have you lived in this city?" Help the learner answer in two or three sentences. Then ask, "How long? Can you give an example?"
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. ★ = most important.
- **Show the hard words:** a *résumé* is a paper about your work. An *accomplishment* is one thing you did well. An *example* makes it easy to see. A *follow-up* comes after the interview (a note or a call). A *qualification* is a fact that shows you can do the job. A *name tag* shows your name. It is not a skill.
- **Check the meaning:** "Is a résumé a paper about your work?" (yes, a work paper) • "Is a handshake a follow-up?" (no) • "Does a name tag show a skill?" (no, it shows a name)
- **Partner game (about 2 min):** A points to a picture and asks one question. B answers. Switch. 15 learners: finish with 3 teams if you have a minute. 7–8: 2 teams. 5 or fewer: one team against the clock.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

### Listening script

Audio is recorded (about 1:37). Read it with volunteers at a natural, friendly speed. The spoken lines use *going to* and *want to*. *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms.

> **Narrator:** Lesson one. The community center is holding a practice interview night. Nora wants a hotel front-desk job.
> **Samir:** Hi, Nora. I'm Samir, your coach. Are you ready to practice?
> **Nora:** Yes. I want to practice for a front-desk job at the Harbor Hotel.
> **Samir:** Good. Ms. Hale is going to ask the questions. I am going to take notes.
> **Ms. Hale:** Welcome, Nora. How long have you been working with customers?
> **Nora:** I've been working with customers for three years. I work at a store now.
> **Ms. Hale:** Tell me one accomplishment.
> **Nora:** I trained two new cashiers last spring.
> **Ms. Hale:** Can you give an example?
> **Nora:** Yes. One new cashier was nervous. I stayed with her for a week.
> **Ms. Hale:** Have you finished any hotel training?
> **Nora:** Not yet. I want to start the front-desk training next month.
> **Samir:** That example was clear. You said, "I've been working."
> **Ms. Hale:** What did you do in 2022?
> **Nora:** I worked at a bakery in 2022. That job is finished.
> **Ms. Hale:** Do you have a follow-up question?
> **Nora:** Yes. What do you send after the interview?
> **Ms. Hale:** A short note. A handshake and a thank-you are a good start.
> **Samir:** Next, you are going to put one accomplishment in your portfolio.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Check the meaning:* 1 yes, a work paper. 2 no. 3 no, it shows a name.
- *Listening Task A:* **b. a hotel front-desk job.**
- *Listening Task B:* 1 three years. 2 trained two new cashiers. 3 stayed for a week. 4 a bakery.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 been working. 2 worked. 3 been working. 4 worked.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 working. 2 since. 3 for. 4 worked. 5 finished. 6 taking.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 've been working. 2 worked. 3 have you been working. 4 been working. 5 for. 6 since.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary. A good answer has how long, one accomplishment, one example, and one reason.
- *Homework 1:* 1 She's been taking this class since September. 2 I worked at a bakery in 2022. 3 They've been working here since 2024. (They've worked here since 2024 is also correct.) 4 He helped customers at a store. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 for. 2 since. 3 for. 4 since.
- *Read & Write:* 1 Front desk at Harbor Hotel. 2 For one year or more. 3 Stayed for a week. 4 The simple past. 5 A short follow-up note.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "I've been studying English for two years. I've been taking this class since September. One example is my interview practice tonight. It matters because I want to give a clear answer."

### Teacher notes

- **This is practice.** Do not ask why someone left a job, what they earn, or how they came to this country. Learners may invent the hotel and the dates. Model a 30-second answer first.
- **Both forms are accepted** when the work is still true: *I've been working here since 2024* and *I've worked here since 2024*. Do not mark the Level 4 form wrong.
- **A finished year** (*in 2022*) needs the simple past. *I've been working here in 2022* is the mix-up to fix.
- **Baseline:** during Talk Time, listen to about five learners. Use the prompts in Step 5. Write a few words for your folder. It is not a grade you announce.
- **Reductions:** the script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms. They can always say the full forms.
- **Weak *been*:** in *I've been working*, *been* sounds like "bin." Stress the job word (*working*, *customers*), not *been*.

## Lesson 1 (Week 1) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads the question. B says the answer.**

### Grammar Box 1: *been* + *-ing* (the activity, still going on)

| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| How long **have** you **been working** here? | I**'ve been working** here **for** two years. |
| How long **have** you **been working** at the hotel? | I**'ve been working** there **for** two years. |
| How long **has** she **been taking** this class? | She**'s been taking** this class **since** September. |

### Grammar Box 2: *have* + past participle is also correct for *How long*

| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| How long **have** you **worked** here? | I**'ve worked** here **since** 2024. |
| How long **have** you **worked** there? | I**'ve worked** there **for** two years. |
| **Have** you **finished** the training? | Yes, I **have**. |

### Grammar Box 3: a finished time needs the simple past

| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| What **did** you **do** in your last job? | I **helped** customers at a store. |
| Where did you work **in 2022**? | I **worked** at a bakery **in 2022**. |

### Grammar Box 4: Which form?

| FORM | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|
| *been* + *-ing* | the activity, still going on: I've been working here for two years. |
| *have* + past participle | also correct for *How long*: I've worked here since 2024. |
| simple past | a finished time (*in 2022*): I worked at a bakery in 2022. |

**Rule of thumb:** *been* + *-ing* stresses the activity, still going on. *have* + past participle is also correct for *How long*, and it is the form from Level 4. Both answers are accepted. A finished time (*in 2022*) still needs the simple past.

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *I am working here since 2024*?**

A: No. Say *I've been working here since 2024.* *I've worked here since 2024* is also correct.

**Q: Can I say *I've been working here in 2022*?**

A: No. That year is finished. Say *I worked here in 2022.*

**Q: Is *I've worked there for two years* wrong?**

A: No. For *How long*, both *been working* and *worked* are accepted when you still have the job.
