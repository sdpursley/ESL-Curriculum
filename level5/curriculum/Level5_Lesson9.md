## How to Use This Plan

> **Teach in English.** Show, point, and gesture. The common mix-up is a row in the mistakes table.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.*

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it**, **check it** (ask: "Almost sure, less sure, or not possible?"), **then go**.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Groups:* 3 learners. With an odd number, one group may have 4. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck:** (1) point to the author, the date, or the button on the class page; (2) give one more English example; (3) point to the grammar box; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English.

> **Four sentences on the exit ticket:** use *must*, *might*, or *can't*, and give one reason.

## Lesson 9 (Week 10): It Must Be an Ad

### At a glance

By the end of class, you can do these four things.

**Today you will:** Say how sure you are with *must*, *might*, and *can't*. Name two clues. Tell a classmate, with a reason.

**Now you can:** Say how sure you are with *must*, *might*, and *can't*. Name two clues. Tell a classmate, with a reason.

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- I can say how sure I am with *must*, *might*, and *can't*.
- I can name two clues (author, date, ad, a mismatch).
- I can say what I would do before I believed the page.
- I can tell a classmate, with a reason.
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 6 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *Do you know if the office is open?* and the wrong order *if is the office open?*
- **Opening Talk:** A asks *Do you know if…?* B keeps statement order and reports one answer with *said* or *told*.
- **Model:** "Do you know if the office is open?" "He said the office was open. He told me to come at 6:30."
- **Homework Check:** pairs read one indirect question from the Lesson 8 email. Write 2 corrections (often *Do you know if is…* and *told to me*). No names.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. ★ = the most important words.
- **Show the hard words:** *author* (the person or office that wrote it) • *date stamp* (how old the page is) • *advertisement* (a page that wants you to buy) • *rumor* (a story with no good source) • *reliable* (you can name the author and the date, and it matches a second source) • *share button* (the button that sends the page to someone else).
- **Check the Meaning:** 1. A page with Buy now is what? (an ad) • 2. Who writes a page? (the author) • 3. What shows how old a page is? (the date stamp).
- **Partner game: Must, might, or can't?** A points to a picture. B says one sentence with *must be*, *might be*, or *can't be*. Switch. **15:** then 3 teams. **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:12–6:32 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 20 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, the whole group checks together.
- **Before you listen:** "Noah shows Aisha a post. The post says their grocery store is closing. Listen: is the store closing?"
- **Voices:** Narrator, Noah, Aisha.
- **Play 1 → Task A.** Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together.**
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Then show the answers.
- **"Hear it, say it":** *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms. You can say *going to* and *want to*. Say each line two times. Stress the modal: *It MUST be a rumor. It CAN'T be a fact.* *Might* stays softer.
- **The audio is not recorded yet (about 2:20).** When it is ready, it plays the conversation once. Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. Until then, read the script with a volunteer at a normal, friendly speed, two times.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A + pair check · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. The left side is the clue or the question. The right side is the answer.
- **Show it:** point to a "Buy now" line and say, "It must be an ad." Point to "2019" and say, "It might be old." Point to a different logo and say, "It can't be the city's page."
- **The boxes:** Box 1 *must be*. Box 2 *might be*. Box 3 *can't be*. Box 4 how sure.
- **Rule of thumb:** *must / might / can't* + *be* or another base verb. *Can't* here means "that is not possible," not "is not able."
- **Questions and answers.** Then Practice A and B and the mistakes table.
- **Pair check:** A says, "It has a Buy now button." B says, "It must be an ad." Then switch. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *almost sure → must be. Less sure → might be. Not possible → can't be.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:18 · Talk Time: Three Pages + Before You Share · 18 min
- **Say at the start:** "We use only the class pages." If a learner brings up a real post, say: "Let's stay with our class pages."
- **Round 1, Three Pages:** groups label each page *must be*, *might be*, or *can't be* and give a reason. **15:** 5 groups of 3. **7–8:** 2 groups. **5 or fewer:** one group, all three pages.
- **Round 2, Before You Share:** each person says what they would check before they forwarded the page. The group agrees on one check.
- **No elections, no health cures, no crime stories.**
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:18–7:25 · Read & Write · 7 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read both pages aloud.
- **Read:** two short pages about the same library event. One is the library's page. One is a repost with a wrong time.
- **Check:** questions 1–5.
- **Write:** a 6–8 sentence message to a friend: which page you trust, two clues, and what the person should do. Start in class. Finish at home.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** *How can you tell that a page might be wrong?* Four sentences with *must*, *might*, or *can't*, and one reason.
- **Collect the pages.** Sort them into yes / almost / not yet. No names next week.
- **Homework:** about 20–25 minutes. Do item 1 together: *It must be an ad.*
:::

### Listening script

*About 2:20. The conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen. Not recorded yet.*

> **Audio:** No speaker yet. Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson nine. Noah shows Aisha a post. The post says their grocery store is closing. They want to check the source.
> **Noah:** Aisha, please look at this post. It says our grocery store is closing on Friday.
> **Aisha:** Do you know who wrote it? I don't see an author.
> **Noah:** There is no author. And I don't see a date stamp.
> **Aisha:** Then it might be old. It might be a rumor. I'm going to look for the store's own page.
> **Noah:** Here it is. The logo matches our store.
> **Aisha:** What does that page say?
> **Noah:** It says the store is open. The hours are the same.
> **Aisha:** Then the closing post can't be true. It must be a rumor.
> **Noah:** The post has a share button. I was going to share it.
> **Aisha:** Don't share it yet. We want to check a second source first.
> **Noah:** The store page names the author. The author is the store. The date is this week.
> **Aisha:** That page must be the one we trust. The first post has no author and no date.
> **Noah:** So the store isn't closing. I'm not going to share the post.
> **Aisha:** Good. It can't be a fact when the store's own page says the opposite.
> **Noah:** There is a comment under the post, but the comment has no source.
> **Aisha:** A comment is not a fact. It might be someone's guess.
> **Noah:** I see. A reliable page names the author and the date.
> **Aisha:** Yes. And it matches a second source. This one does.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Check the Meaning:* 1 an ad, 2 the author, 3 the date stamp.
- *Listening Task A:* **b** (No. The store's page says it is open.)
- *Listening Task B:* 1 no, 2 no, 3 matches, 4 open, 5 he does not share it, 6 it is not a fact.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 might, 2 might, 3 can't, 4 must, 5 must, 6 can't.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 must, 2 might, 3 can't, 4 might, 5 can't, 6 must.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 must be, 2 can't be, 3 might be, 4 must be, 5 be, 6 not possible.
- *Talk Time:* Page 1 must be a real notice. Page 2 must be an ad. Page 3 might be old, and it can't be the library's page. Reasons will vary if they name a real clue on the card.
- *Homework 1:* 1 must, 2 might, 3 can't. *Homework 4:* Do you know if the library is open tonight? *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 The library's page. 2 Thursday at 6:30. 3 Thursday at 8:00. 4 The library's page. 5 The author and the date. *Writing (C):* answers will vary. A good message names the library's page, two clues, and one next step.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "A page with no author might be wrong. A page with no date might be old. It can't be a fact if a second source does not match. I must check the author and the date before I share it."

### Teacher notes

- **Use only the invented pages in the lesson.** If a learner brings up a real post, say: "Let's stay with our class pages."
- **No elections, no health cures, no crime stories.**
- **Reliable** means you can name the author and the date, and the page matches a second source. It does not mean "I like it."
- **Stress** *MUST* and *CAN'T*. *Might* stays softer.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms.
- **Noah, Aisha, the store, and the library pages are invented.**

## Lesson 9 (Week 10) · Grammar

**Read across. The left side is the clue. The right side is what you say.**

### Grammar Box 1: must be

<!-- subst -->
| CLUE | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| The page has a Buy now button. What is it? | **It must be an ad.** |
| The store's page says the store is open. What is the closing post? | **It must be a rumor.** |
| The logo, the author, and the date all match. What is the page? | **It must be the store's page.** |

*Must* means you are almost sure.

### Grammar Box 2: might be

<!-- subst -->
| CLUE | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| The date says 2019. What do you think? | **It might be old.** |
| There is no author. What do you think? | **It might be a rumor.** |
| The comment has no source. What do you think? | **It might be a guess.** |

*Might* is a guess you are less sure about.

### Grammar Box 3: can't be

<!-- subst -->
| CLUE | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| The logo is different. Is it the city's page? | **It can't be the city's page.** |
| The store's page says open. Is the store closing? | **It can't be closing.** |
| The first post has no author and no date. Is it a fact? | **It can't be a fact.** |

### Grammar Box 4: how sure?

<!-- subst -->
| HOW SURE? | SAY |
|---|---|
| You are almost sure. | **must be** |
| You are less sure. | **might be** |
| It is not possible. | **can't be** |

**Rule of thumb:** *must / might / can't* + *be* or another base verb. *Can't* here means "that is not possible," not "is not able."

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *It must an ad?***

A: No. ***It must be an ad.***

**Q: The logo is different. Can I say *It can be not the city page?***

A: No. ***It can't be the city page.***

**Q: Does *can't* here mean "is not able"?**

A: No. It means **that is not possible.**

## Lesson 9 (Week 10) · Read & Write

### A. Read the two pages. They are about the same library event. One time is wrong. *Read fast first. Then read again and answer the questions.*

:::box Page 1 · The city library
**Author:** the city library. **Date stamp:** this month. **Logo:** the library logo.

The evening reading hour is on Thursday at 6:30. The room is the meeting room on the first floor. Please come ten minutes early so you can find a seat. The page on the library website matches the paper newsletter at the front desk. Both say Thursday at 6:30. Both name the city library as the author.

You can trust this page. It names the author and the date, and it matches a second source. The logo is the same logo on the door. There is no Buy now button. This page is not an advertisement. If a friend asks, "Do you know if the reading hour is at 6:30?" you can say yes.

The hour ends at 7:30. The evening bus still stops outside at 6:15, so 6:30 is an easy time to arrive. We are not going to move the time. If the time changes, the library will change this page and the newsletter on the same day.
:::

:::box Page 2 · A repost
**Author:** none. **Date stamp:** none. **Logo:** a star, not the library logo. **Share button:** yes.

This repost says the reading hour is on Thursday at 8:00. It does not say who wrote it. It does not give a date. The logo does not match the library. A comment under the post says, "I heard it moved." The comment has no name and no source. That comment might be a guess. It can't be a fact by itself.

The time does not match. The library says 6:30. This page says 8:00. That mismatch is the clue. The repost might be old, or it might be a rumor. It can't be the library's page, because the logo is different and there is no author. Do not press the share button yet. Check the library's page first. If you already shared the repost, send a correction: the reading hour is Thursday at 6:30.

A reliable page names the author and the date, and it matches a second source. This repost does not. Liking a page is not the same as trusting it. Before you believe a time, look for the author, the date stamp, and one more source.
:::
