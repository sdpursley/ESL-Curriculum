## How to Use This Plan

> **Teach in English.** Show, point, and gesture. The common mix-up is a row in the mistakes table. There is no separate tip to translate.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.*

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Positive statement or negative? What tag?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English.

> **Four sentences on the exit ticket:** two of them are tags, and one is a correction or one *Yes, it is.*

## Lesson 7 (Week 7): You Sent It, Didn't You?

### At a glance

By the end of class, you can do these four things.

**Today you will:** Add a tag to a statement I think is true. Answer a tag with a short yes or no. Write a confirmation email.

**Now you can:** Add a tag to a statement I think is true. Answer a tag with a short yes or no. Write a confirmation email.

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- I can add a tag to a statement I think is true.
- I can answer a tag with a short yes or no.
- I can correct a wrong detail politely.
- I can write a confirmation email.

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 6 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *She said… / She told me… / She asked me to… / She asked if…*
- **Opening Talk:** A asks last week's question. B answers with a short summary. The quote can be from Lesson 6, not from a classmate's private life.
- **Model with one learner:** "The nurse said the checkup was on Thursday. She told me the time had changed. She asked me to call before 3:00. She asked if Thursday was OK."
- **Homework Check:** pairs read one sentence from the Lesson 6 message. Write 2 corrections on the board (often *said me* and *told that*). No names.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. ★ = the most important words.
- **Show the hard words:** *email address* (not the message, the address) • *signature* (your name in writing) vs. *initial* (the first letters only) • *checkbox* (a small box you mark) • *double-check* (look a second time) • *capital letter* (L, not l).
- **Check the Meaning:** 1. Is this a phone number or an email address? (an email address) • 2. What small box do you mark on a form? (a checkbox) • 3. Is the L in Leah small or capital? (capital).
- **Partner game: Check the form!** A points to a picture. B names it and uses it in one sentence. Switch. **15 learners:** then 3 teams. **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:12–6:32 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 20 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen:** "Leah is at a community center desk. She wants to check her evening-class form with Mr. Ortiz. Listen: is every detail correct at the start?"
- **Voices:** Narrator, Leah, Mr. Ortiz.
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers.
- **"Hear it, say it":** *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms. You can say *going to* and *want to*. Say each line two times. The tag falls when you are only checking. A rising tag can mean a real question. This lesson practices the falling check.
- **The audio is recorded (about 1:30).** When it is ready, it plays the conversation once. Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. Until then, read the script with a volunteer at a normal, friendly speed, two times.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A + pair check · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. The left side is the question. The right side is the answer.
- **Show it:** say a sure detail with a falling tag: "You sent the form, didn't you?" Nod on *didn't you.* Then shake your head and say, "Actually, the room is C."
- **The boxes:** Box 1 positive statement, negative tag. Box 2 negative statement, positive tag. Box 3 no helping verb, so *do / does / did*. Box 4 *Actually* plus the right detail.
- **Rule of thumb:** The tag uses the same helping verb as the statement, and it flips the yes/no. If there is no helping verb, use *do / does / did*.
- **Questions and answers:** read each Q. The class reads the A.
- **Practice A and B:** learners write, then pairs check. Read the Common mistakes table: "Don't say… Say…"
- **Pair check:** A says, "The room is A." B adds the tag: "The room is A, isn't it?" A answers, "Actually, it's room C." Then switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once if time. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *Positive statement → negative tag. Negative statement → positive tag. Yes stays yes: Yes, I did.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:18 · Talk Time: Detail Check + Read-Back · 18 min
- **Model it first:** hold up the Tuesday copy and the Wednesday copy. Ask, "It's Tuesday, isn't it?" Answer, "Actually, it's Wednesday."
- **Round 1, Detail Check (pairs):** A has a form with four details. B has a slightly different copy. They confirm with tags and correct one item. **15:** pairs, swap forms once. **7–8:** pairs. **5 or fewer:** one form, and you read a wrong detail.
- **Round 2, Read-Back:** B says a two-sentence confirmation. A listens and answers the tag. Then switch.
- **The forms use a class ID only.** Do not collect real Social Security numbers, immigration papers, bank numbers, or medical record numbers. Praise the correction, not the speed.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:18–7:25 · Read & Write · 7 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the email aloud together.
- **Read:** the registration form and Mr. Ortiz's email. One detail does not match. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the mismatch."
- **Check:** go over questions 1–5.
- **Write:** a confirmation email of 6–8 sentences that names the mismatch and the correction. This is writing check 3. Learners start in class and finish at home. After class, write one strength and one fix. No grade on the person, only on the email.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** *Confirm two details about this class.* Four sentences, two of them tags, and one correction or one *Yes, it is.* Example: *The class is on Tuesday, isn't it? Yes, it is. The room is A, isn't it? Actually, it's room C.*
- **Collect the pages.** Sort them into yes / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections. No names.
- **Homework:** about 20–25 minutes. Do item 1 together: *You sent the form, ______?* → *You sent the form, didn't you?*
:::

### Listening script

*1:30. The conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen.*

> **Audio:** Play `L5_L7_listening.mp3` (1:30) from a phone and a small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson seven. Leah is at the community center desk. She wants to check her evening-class form. Mr. Ortiz has the copy.
> **Mr. Ortiz:** Good evening. You sent the form, didn't you?
> **Leah:** Yes, I did. I want to double-check every line.
> **Mr. Ortiz:** Your class ID is C42, isn't it?
> **Leah:** Yes, it is.
> **Mr. Ortiz:** And the email address is right, isn't it?
> **Leah:** Yes, it is. The name starts with a capital letter. Please check the spelling.
> **Mr. Ortiz:** I will. You wrote a phone number on line three, didn't you?
> **Leah:** Yes, I did. I double-checked it at home.
> **Mr. Ortiz:** The class is on Tuesday at six, isn't it?
> **Leah:** Yes, it is. I'm going to come straight from work.
> **Mr. Ortiz:** And the room is A, isn't it?
> **Leah:** Actually, it isn't. The schedule says room C. Can you make that correction?
> **Mr. Ortiz:** You're right. I'll change the room to C and initial the correction.
> **Leah:** Thank you. My signature is on the form, isn't it?
> **Mr. Ortiz:** Yes, it is. I'll check the box and give you this copy.
> **Leah:** Great. I'm going to keep the copy.
> **Mr. Ortiz:** Your friend isn't in this class, is she?
> **Leah:** No, she isn't. This copy is only for me.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **b** (No. The room is wrong.)
- *Listening Task B:* 1 Correct, 2 Correct, 3 Correct, 4 Correct, 5 Wrong. C42 is the class ID. Tuesday at six is 6:00. The room on his copy is A; the schedule says room C.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 didn't you, 2 isn't it, 3 isn't it, 4 didn't you, 5 isn't it, 6 is she.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 didn't you, 2 isn't it, 3 is she, 4 don't you, 5 doesn't it, 6 didn't she.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 Yes, I did. 2 isn't it, 3 is she, 4 didn't you, 5 isn't it, 6 No, I didn't.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary. One good correction: *It's Tuesday, isn't it? Actually, it's Wednesday.*
- *Homework 1:* 1 You sent the form, didn't you? 2 The class is on Tuesday, isn't it? 3 She isn't on the list, is she? 4 You need a copy, don't you? *Homework 2–4:* answers will vary. A good item 4: *She told me to check the room.*
- *Read & Write B:* 1 C-42. 2 Tuesday at 6:00. 3 Room C. 4 Room A. 5 The room. *Writing (C):* answers will vary. A good email names room A and room C and asks for the correction.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "The class is on Tuesday, isn't it? Yes, it is. The room is A, isn't it? Actually, it's room C."

### Teacher notes

- **The forms use a class ID, an email, and an invented phone number.** Do not collect real Social Security numbers, immigration papers, bank numbers, or medical record numbers.
- **Praise the correction, not the speed.** *Actually* plus the right detail is the goal.
- **Falling tag:** you are checking, not starting an argument. Mention that a rising tag can mean a real question. Practice only the falling check tonight.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms.
- **Writing check 3:** on the confirmation email, write one strength and one fix. Learners keep the email in a folder.
- **All names, the class, the phone number, and the email are invented.**

## Lesson 7 (Week 7) · Grammar

**Read across. The left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**

### Grammar Box 1: a negative tag

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| You sent the form, **didn't you?** | **Yes, I did.** |
| The class is on Tuesday, **isn't it?** | **Yes, it is.** |
| She signed the copy, **didn't she?** | **Yes, she did.** |

A positive statement takes a negative tag. The falling tone means you are checking, not starting an argument.

### Grammar Box 2: a positive tag

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| She **isn't** on the list, **is she?** | **No, she isn't.** |
| The room **isn't** A, **is it?** | **No, it isn't.** |
| You **didn't** initial it, **did you?** | **Actually, I did.** |

A negative statement takes a positive tag.

### Grammar Box 3: do, does, or did

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| You need a copy, **don't you?** | **Yes, I do.** |
| The schedule starts at six, **doesn't it?** | **Yes, it does.** |
| Leah checked the spelling, **didn't she?** | **Yes, she did.** |

**Rule of thumb:** The tag uses the same helping verb as the statement, and it flips the yes/no. If there is no helping verb, use *do / does / did*.

### Grammar Box 4: correct a detail

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| The room is A, **isn't it?** | **Actually, it's room C.** |
| The class is on Wednesday, **isn't it?** | **Actually, it's on Tuesday.** |
| Your email ends in mail.example, **doesn't it?** | **Yes, it does.** |

### Questions and answers

**Q: The person says yes. Can I answer *Yes, I didn't*?**

A: No. If the answer is yes, the short answer stays positive: ***Yes, I did.***

**Q: I am sure, and I only want to check. Do I say *You sent it, did you?***

A: No. The check on a positive sentence takes *didn't*: ***You sent it, didn't you?***

**Q: The statement is negative. What tag do I use?**

A: A positive tag. ***She isn't on the list, is she?***
