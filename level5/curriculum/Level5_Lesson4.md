## How to Use This Plan

> **Teach in English.** You do not need to speak the learner's language. Show, point, and gesture. There is no contrast box on the student page. The common mix-up is in the mistakes table, in English.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.*

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Does *unless* mean *if* or *if not*?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck:** (1) show the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English.

> **About four sentences:** in Level 5, the Talk Time product and the exit ticket are about four connected sentences. Opening Talk is shorter: two or three sentences, then one follow-up.

## Lesson 4 (Week 4): Unless You Call First

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk · 6 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Say this once:** "This lesson is not legal advice. For a real question about your job or your building, ask that office, or use the local help line on the Keep practicing sheet."
- **Before class:** write on the board: *unless = if not* and *You can't go in unless you call first.*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **two or three sentences**; A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you ask, "What do you wish you had asked last week?" The learner answers. Then you ask, "Why?" Help them stop after two or three sentences.
- **Card 4 is the homework check:** one *I wish I had* sentence from last week. Do not ask about family loss, money trouble, or leaving a country.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. ★ = the most important words.
- **Show the hard words:** *appointment* (a set time) vs. *permission* (someone says you may); *confirm* (the time is yes) vs. *cancel* (the time is off); *requirement* (you have to) vs. *exception* (one time, not the usual rule). *Office hours* on the card are nine to five.
- **Check the meaning (1 min):** "Does *unless* mean *if* or *if not*?" (if not) • "What must a visitor wear?" (a visitor badge) • "Where do you ask about a rule?" (the front desk).
- **Partner game (2 min):** A points to a picture and asks a question. B answers. Switch. *Example:* "**visitor**: What does a visitor wear?" "A visitor badge."
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:12–6:32 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 20 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Andre did not call first. Helen explains the rule, and Mei says he can wait. Listen: can he go in now?"
- **Voices:** Andre, Helen, and Mei. A narrator opens and closes the audio.
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together. Then read Tasks B and C together so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms. You can say *want to* and *going to*. Then say *unless* with stress on the second syllable, and say *as long as* as one chunk. The class repeats each line two times.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (1:48).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the sentence. The right side is the meaning.**
- **Show it (2 min):** on the board, write *if you do not call* and draw an arrow to *unless you call*. Say both. Then say *as long as* as "only if this stays true," and *even though* as "the result happens anyway."
- **The boxes:** Box 1 *unless*. Box 2 *as long as*. Box 3 *even though*. Box 4 *have to*, *must*, and *if* + *will*.
- **Rule of thumb (read it aloud):** *unless* means *if… not*. *As long as* means "only if this stays true." *Even though* means the result happens anyway.
- **Questions and answers:** Q: Can I say *Unless you will call*? A: No. Use the present: *Unless you call.* Q: What does *as long as* mean? A: Only if this stays true. Q: What does *even though* mean? A: The result happens anyway.
- **Practice A and B:** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 2 min):** A reads a rule strip. B says *unless*, *as long as*, or *even though*. Switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *unless* = if not · *as long as* = only if this stays true · *even though* = the result happens anyway.
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:18 · Talk Time: Notice Sort + Ask the Desk · 18 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** hold up "You can't go in unless you call first." Ask: "Which word?" (*unless*). Then ask one learner, "What happens unless I call?" Answer with the rule.
- **Round 1 (8 min):** **Notice Sort (pairs).** Pairs get six rule strips and match each one to *unless*, *as long as*, or *even though*. They say the rule in their own words.
- **Round 2 (8 min):** **Ask the Desk (pairs).** A is the front desk. B asks one question about a rule (*What happens unless I call?*). A answers with *unless*, *as long as*, or *even though*. Switch.
- **15 learners:** pairs, then a new partner. **7–8:** pairs. **5 or fewer:** the whole group sorts the strips, then pairs ask.
- **Do not role-play evictions, firings, or punishments.** The notices are invented.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:18–7:25 · Read & Write: A visitor policy + a note · 7 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the notice aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** learners read the visitor policy and the six-line building notice. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (2 min, finish at home):** a 6-sentence note to a coworker explaining two rules, with *unless* and *as long as*. Invented details are fine.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** *Explain one rule from tonight.* Four sentences: the rule, *unless* or *as long as*, an example, and who to ask if you are not sure. With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** about 15–20 minutes. Item 1 is three sentences, one with each new word. Item 2 finishes the note. Item 3 is one polite question about a **class** rule, not a real job problem. Item 4 reviews *have to* and *must*.
:::

### Listening script: `L5_L4_listening.mp3` (1:48; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play `L5_L4_listening.mp3` from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson four. Andre is at the front desk. He wants to go to a meeting, but he did not call first.
> **Helen:** Good afternoon. Do you have an appointment?
> **Andre:** I want to go to the four o'clock meeting. I did not call this morning.
> **Helen:** I'm sorry. You can't go in unless you call first. That rule is on the notice.
> **Andre:** Even though I am on time?
> **Helen:** Even though you are on time, you still need to confirm.
> **Andre:** What do I have to do?
> **Helen:** You have to sign the book, and you must wear a visitor badge.
> **Andre:** Is there an exception if I already have permission?
> **Helen:** Permission is not the same as an appointment. Unless you confirm, the desk cannot make an exception.
> **Helen:** The requirement is on the door. Please read the policy before you sit down.
> **Mei:** Hi, Andre. You can wait as long as the four o'clock group finishes on time.
> **Andre:** What happens unless they finish?
> **Mei:** Then the room is closed. If you call first next time, they will hold the room.
> **Helen:** You can bring a guest as long as you tell the front desk.
> **Andre:** I am going to call tomorrow. Can I cancel today's meeting?
> **Helen:** Yes. I can cancel it, or you can wait. Office hours end at five.
> **Andre:** What happens unless I call tomorrow too?
> **Mei:** Call during office hours, from nine to five. Then they can confirm the room.
> **Andre:** I'll wait. Thank you for explaining the policy.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Teacher notes

- **This lesson is not legal advice.** For a real question about a job or a building, the learner asks that office, or uses the local help line on the Keep practicing sheet. Say that once. Do not role-play evictions, firings, or punishments.
- **Invented only.** The policy, the hours, and the meeting are invented. Learners may invent the office in Talk Time.
- **The three words:** *unless* = if not. *As long as* = only if this stays true. *Even though* = the result happens anyway. After *unless*, *as long as*, and *if*, the verb for a future meaning stays present: *unless you call*, not *unless you will call*.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms; they can always say the full forms.
- **Pronunciation:** *unless* is stressed on the second syllable. *As long as* is one chunk.

## Lesson 4 (Week 4) · Grammar

**Read across. The left side is the sentence. The right side is the meaning.**

### Grammar Box 1: *unless* means *if not*

| SENTENCE | MEANING |
|---|---|
| You can't go in **unless** you sign the book. | if you do **not** sign |
| You can't go in **unless** you call first. | if you do **not** call |
| **Unless** you confirm, they will not hold the room. | present verb after *unless* |

### Grammar Box 2: *as long as*

| SENTENCE | MEANING |
|---|---|
| You can bring a guest **as long as** you tell the desk. | only if you tell them |
| You can wait **as long as** the group finishes on time. | only if that stays true |
| **As long as** you call first, they will hold the room. | present verb, same as *if* |

### Grammar Box 3: *even though*

| SENTENCE | MEANING |
|---|---|
| **Even though** the office is busy, you still need an appointment. | the result happens anyway |
| **Even though** you are on time, you still need to confirm. | on time does not change the rule |
| **Even though** you have permission, you still sign the book. | permission is not enough |

### Grammar Box 4: *have to*, *must*, and *if*

| SENTENCE | MEANING |
|---|---|
| You **have to** sign the book. | a requirement |
| You **must** wear the visitor badge. | a requirement |
| **If** you call first, they **will** hold the room. | *if* + present, then *will* |

**Rule of thumb:** *unless* means *if… not*. *As long as* means "only if this stays true." *Even though* means the result happens anyway.

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *Unless you will call*?**

A: No. Use the present: *Unless you call.*

**Q: What does *as long as* mean?**

A: Only if this stays true.

**Q: What does *even though* mean?**

A: The result happens anyway.

## Lesson 4 (Week 4) · Read & Write

### A. Read the policy and the notice. *Read fast first. Then read again and answer the questions.*

:::box VISITOR POLICY · a class example
Please read this policy before you go to the front desk. The office and the rules on this page are invented for class.

A visitor needs an appointment. You can't go in unless you call first. Call the front desk during office hours. Office hours are nine to five, Monday to Friday. If you call first, the front desk will hold the room until your time. Unless you confirm, they will not hold the room.

When you arrive, you have to sign the book. Write your name and the time. You must wear a visitor badge. Wear the badge where people can see it. Give it back at the front desk when you leave. A badge is not a key. It only shows that you signed in.

You can bring one guest as long as you tell the front desk before you come. Tell them the guest's name. The guest has to sign the book. The guest must wear a badge too. You cannot bring a guest unless the desk says yes. Two guests are too many for one visit.

Even though the office is busy, you still need an appointment. Even though you are on time, you still need to confirm. A busy lobby does not change the rule. A call the day before is best. A call the same morning is OK as long as someone answers the phone.

There is one exception. A worker who already has permission from the front desk may walk a visitor to the desk. The visitor still has to sign the book and wear a badge. Permission is not the same as an appointment. Unless you confirm, the desk cannot make an exception for a meeting room. The exception is for the walk to the desk, not for the room.

If you need to cancel, call the front desk. Say your name and the time. Do not ask a friend to cancel for you unless you cannot call. If you do not understand a requirement, ask one polite question. You can say, "What happens unless I call?" The person at the desk will point to the notice and explain the rule.

Keep the policy near the door. Read the notice again if you are not sure. The front desk can repeat a rule. They cannot change the policy during your visit. Write the two rules you need in a note to a coworker, and ask the desk if a word is not clear.
:::

:::box BUILDING NOTICE · six lines
1. Office hours are nine to five.
2. You can't go in unless you sign the book.
3. You must wear a visitor badge.
4. You can wait in the lobby as long as a meeting is still in the room.
5. Even though the lobby is open, the offices close at five.
6. Ask the front desk if you do not understand a rule.
:::
