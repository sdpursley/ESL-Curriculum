## Lesson 11 (Week 12): I'd Like to File a Claim

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- start a sentence with a gerund (*Waiting on hold is tiring*)

**Today you will:** Start with a gerund. • Soften a request. • Write a short letter.

- I can start a sentence with a gerund (*Waiting on hold is tiring*).

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 6 min
- **Opening Talk:** A asks a card question. B answers in about four sentences. A asks one follow-up. Then switch.
- **Homework Check:** pairs read one *so… that* or *such… that* sentence from Lesson 10. Write 2 corrections on the board. No names.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- Point, say, repeat. ★ = most important.
- **Show:** a *claim form* is the paper. A *policy number* and a *reference number* are two different numbers. An *attachment* is a file you send with a letter. *On hold* means you are waiting on the phone. A *resolution* is the answer at the end. The *cracked screen* is the phone. The *water stain* is already dry.
- **Check the meaning:** "Is *Waiting* one activity or many?" (One.) • "Does the verb after *Waiting* take *is* or *are*?" (*is*) • "Was the photo attached, or did Hector forget it?" (It was attached.)
- **Partner game:** A points. B makes a sentence. 15: 3 teams. 7–8: 2 teams. 5 or fewer: one team against the clock.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:12–6:32 · Listening + Hear it, say it · 20 min
- **Before you listen:** Hector calls about a cracked-screen claim. Ms. Lane says the form was received and asks him to send the estimate.
- **Voices:** Narrator, Hector, and Ms. Lane.
- **Play 1 → Task A.** **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.**
- **Hear it, say it:** *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms. You can say *going to* and *want to*. Say *I was wondering if* as one soft chunk. Keep the *-ing* in *filing*, *waiting*, and *sending*.
- This letter is practice. It is not insurance advice.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A + pair check · 13 min
- **Box 1:** the *-ing* word is the subject. *Waiting on hold is tiring. Filing a claim takes time. Sending a photo helps.*
- **Box 2:** *I was wondering if you could check the claim.* Statement order after *if*. *Would you mind looking up the reference number?*
- **Box 3:** *The photo was attached. The form was received.*
- **Box 4:** *You have to include the policy number.* You don't have to stay on hold.
- **Rule of thumb:** A gerund subject is one activity, treated as it. The verb after it is singular: *Waiting is*, not *Waiting are*.
- **Pair check:** A reads a gerund subject. B finishes with a singular verb. Then switch.
- **Break:** On the board: *Waiting is…* / *I was wondering if you could…* / *was attached*.
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:18 · Talk Time · 18 min
- **Round 1, The Call:** A is the customer with a claim card (cracked screen, or a dry water stain). B is customer service with a status card (received, missing the photo, or in review). A must use *I was wondering if* and one gerund subject. Then switch.
- **Round 2, Letter Outline:** pairs list the two paragraphs (what happened; what you are asking) before anyone writes full sentences.
- **15:** pairs, then switch with a new card. **7–8:** pairs. **5 or fewer:** one call the group coaches, then individual outlines.
- Do not ask if learners have filed a claim. Invented facts only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:18–7:25 · Read & Write · 7 min
- **Read** the claim-status letter. Pairs answer the five questions.
- **Write** a two-paragraph reply, about 8–12 sentences. Paragraph 1: what happened and what was sent. Paragraph 2: what you are asking, with *I was wondering if* and a thank-you. Start in class. Finish at home. Bring it next week.
- This letter is practice. It is not insurance advice.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** *Start a claim letter.* Four sentences: a gerund subject, one passive, the request, and a polite close.
- **Homework:** do item 1 together. *Waiting on hold ____ tiring.* → *is*. About 20–25 minutes. Bring the finished letter next week.
:::

### Listening script

About 2:30. The phone call is the whole story. Spoken lines use *going to* and *want to*.

> **Narrator:** Lesson eleven. Hector calls customer service about a cracked phone screen. Ms. Lane answers. The phone call is the whole story.
> **Ms. Lane:** Thank you for calling customer service. This is Ms. Lane. How can I help you?
> **Hector:** Hello. I want to file a claim for a cracked screen. Waiting on hold was tiring, but I'm glad you answered.
> **Ms. Lane:** I can help. What is your policy number?
> **Hector:** It is four two eight. I was wondering if you could look up my claim.
> **Ms. Lane:** The form was received. The claim is about a cracked screen. Is that right?
> **Hector:** Yes. Filing a claim takes time, so I sent a photo last week.
> **Ms. Lane:** The photo was attached. Sending a photo helps, but we still need the estimate.
> **Hector:** I was wondering if you could tell me the reference number. I want to send the estimate today.
> **Ms. Lane:** The reference number is seven one. Please send the estimate as an attachment.
> **Hector:** Would you mind saying that number again? I want to write it on the claim form.
> **Ms. Lane:** Of course. Seven one. You have to include the policy number on the estimate.
> **Hector:** Sending the estimate is my next step. I don't want to stay on hold again.
> **Ms. Lane:** You don't have to wait on hold. You can send the attachment with the reference number.
> **Hector:** This is not a water stain. A water stain can be a claim too, but that stain is already dry. Nobody was hurt.
> **Ms. Lane:** This file is only the cracked screen. The home is safe. When the estimate arrives, we can look for a resolution.
> **Hector:** I'm going to send it today. Thank you for your help.
> **Ms. Lane:** Thank you, Hector. A clear photo and a clear estimate are enough for this practice file.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **b** (A cracked phone screen.)
- *Listening Task B:* 1 was received, 2 was attached, 3 the estimate, 4 seven one, 5 four two eight, 6 send it today.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 Waiting, 2 was received, 3 was attached, 4 Filing, 5 Sending, 6 Sending.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 is, 2 attached, 3 takes, 4 could, 5 received, 6 helps.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 is, 2 you could, 3 attached, 4 Filing, 5 have to, 6 helps.
- *Homework 1:* is.
- *Read & Write:* 1 A cracked phone screen. 2 The photo. 3 The estimate. 4 Seven one. 5 Four two eight.
- *Exit ticket:* four sentences: a gerund subject, one passive, the request, and a polite close.

### Teacher notes

- This letter is practice. It is not insurance advice. For a real claim, use the phone number on your own policy.
- The damage is a phone screen or a dry stain. Nobody is hurt, and the home is safe. Do not invent a fire, a flood, or an injury.
- Do not ask if learners have filed a claim.
- *I was wondering if could you* is the wrong order. Say *if you could*.
- *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms.

## Lesson 11 (Week 12) · Grammar

### Grammar Box 1: a gerund is the subject

| THE ACTIVITY | THE VERB IS SINGULAR |
|---|---|
| Waiting on hold | is tiring. |
| Filing a claim | takes time. |
| Sending a photo | helps. |
| Sending the estimate | is my next step. |

### Grammar Box 2: a soft request

| YOU CAN SAY | STATEMENT ORDER |
|---|---|
| I was wondering if | you could check the claim. |
| I was wondering if | you could look up the reference number. |
| Would you mind | looking up the reference number? |

### Grammar Box 3: what was sent

| THE THING | PASSIVE |
|---|---|
| The photo | was attached. |
| The form | was received. |
| The estimate | was not attached yet. |

### Grammar Box 4: *have to*

| YOU HAVE TO | YOU DON'T HAVE TO |
|---|---|
| include the policy number | stay on hold |
| send the estimate | call again if the letter is complete |
| write the reference number | add a different problem |

**Rule of thumb:** A gerund subject is one activity, treated as it. The verb after it is singular: *Waiting is*, not *Waiting are*.

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *Waiting on hold are tiring*?**

A: No. *Waiting is* tiring.

**Q: Can I say *I was wondering if could you check*?**

A: No. Say *if you could check*. That is statement order.

**Q: *Was attach* or *was attached*?**

A: *Was attached.*

### Practice A: Write the missing word.

1. Waiting on hold ____________ tiring.
2. The photo was ____________.
3. Filing a claim ____________ time.
4. I was wondering if you ____________ check it.
5. The form was ____________.
6. Sending a photo ____________.

### Practice B: Circle the correct words.

1. Waiting on hold (**is** / **are**) tiring.
2. I was wondering if (**could you** / **you could**) check.
3. The photo was (**attach** / **attached**).
4. (**Filing** / **File**) a claim takes time.
5. You (**have to** / **has to**) include the policy number.
6. Sending a photo (**help** / **helps**).

### Common mistakes

| Don't say | Say |
|---|---|
| Waiting on hold are tiring. | Waiting on hold **is** tiring. |
| I was wondering if could you check. | I was wondering if **you could** check. |
| The photo was attach. | The photo **was attached**. |
| Waiting are hard. | **Waiting is** hard. |

### Pair check

A says: "Waiting on hold." B says: "Waiting on hold is tiring." Then switch. Use *filing* and *sending* too.

## Lesson 11 (Week 12) · Read & Write

This letter is practice. It is not insurance advice.

### A. Read the letter from Northline Cover.

Dear Hector,

We received your claim form. This letter is about a cracked phone screen. Nobody was hurt. The form was received on Monday. The photo was attached. Thank you for sending it. Please keep a copy of the form at home. You do not need to send the same photo again.

Your policy number is four two eight. Your reference number is seven one. Please keep both numbers on every page you send. Waiting on hold is tiring, so you do not have to call if the estimate is complete. You can write to customer service instead. A short letter is enough.

We still need the estimate. Sending a photo helps. The estimate shows the cost to fix the screen. Please send the estimate as an attachment. Put the policy number on the first line. You have to include it. If the number is missing, we cannot match the file to your claim. Write the reference number on the same line so the two numbers stay together.

The claim is in review. A resolution is not ready yet. Filing a claim takes time. A file like this often takes about two weeks after the estimate arrives. That time is only for this practice letter. It is not a promise. Please do not call every day while you wait.

If you want a person to look, write: I was wondering if you could check the claim. You can also write: Would you mind looking up the reference number? Please use statement order after *if*: *if you could*, not *if could you*. A polite sentence is easier to answer than a complaint with no facts.

A dry water stain is a different kind of claim. This file is only the cracked screen. Do not add a stain to this letter. The home in this practice is safe. The water-stain card in class is already dry. Leave it out of this file. Do not describe a bigger problem than the screen.

Customer service is open from nine to five on weekdays. If you call, have the reference number ready. You can also reply by letter. Paragraph 1 should say what happened and what was sent. Paragraph 2 should say what you are asking, with *I was wondering if*, and a thank-you close. Sign your name under the thank-you.

Sincerely,
Ms. Lane
Customer service
Northline Cover
