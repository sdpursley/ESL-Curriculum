## How to Use This Plan

> **Teach in English.** Show, point, and gesture. The common mix-up is a row in the mistakes table.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.*

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it**, **check it** (ask: "Is the word order a statement?"), **then go**.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck:** (1) show the picture or the board; (2) give one more English example; (3) point to the grammar box; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English.

> **Four sentences on the exit ticket:** two with *Do you know if / whether*, one reported answer, and a reason.

## Lesson 8 (Week 9): Do You Know If It's Tonight?

### At a glance

By the end of class, you can do these four things.

**Today you will:** Ask *Do you know if / whether…?* Keep statement order after *if*. Report the answer with *said* or *told*.

**Now you can:** Ask *Do you know if / whether…?* Keep statement order after *if*. Report the answer with *said* or *told*.

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- I can ask *Do you know if / whether…?*
- I can keep statement order after *if*.
- I can ask *Do you know when / where…?* the Level 4 way.
- I can report the answer with *said* or *told*.
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 6 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *…, didn't you? / …, isn't it? / Actually, …*
- **Opening Talk:** A asks a Lesson 7 tag. B answers with a short yes, a short no, or *Actually.*
- **Model:** "The class is on Tuesday, isn't it?" "Yes, it is." Then: "The room is A, isn't it?" "Actually, it's room C."
- **Homework Check:** pairs read one sentence from the Lesson 7 confirmation email. Write 2 corrections on the board (often *Yes, I didn't* and a positive sentence with *did you*). No names.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. ★ = the most important words.
- **Show the hard words:** *school office* (the desk, not a classroom) • *conference* (the meeting) • *permission slip* (the paper a parent brings) • *dismissal* (the end of the school day) vs. *pickup* (someone comes to get a child early) • *sign-up* (the sheet you put your name on) • *office window* (the window at the desk).
- **Check the Meaning:** 1. What do you carry books in? (a backpack) • 2. What rings in the hallway? (a bell) • 3. What paper does a parent bring for early pickup? (a permission slip).
- **Partner game: Point and ask!** A points. B asks *Do you know where the ______ is?* Switch. **15:** then 3 teams. **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:12–6:32 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 20 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, the whole group checks together.
- **Before you listen:** "Maya calls the office at Northside School. Mr. Reed answers. Listen: why is she calling?"
- **Voices:** Narrator, Maya, Mr. Reed.
- **Play 1 → Task A.** Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together.**
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Then show the answers.
- **"Hear it, say it":** *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms. You can say *going to* and *want to*. Say each line two times. *Do you know if* is one friendly chunk. The voice rises a little because it is a real question. Statement order stays even when the voice rises.
- **The audio is not recorded yet (about 2:20).** When it is ready, it plays the conversation once. Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. Until then, read the script with a volunteer at a normal, friendly speed, two times.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A + pair check · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. The left side is the question. The right side is the answer.
- **Show it:** write the wrong order and cross it out: "Do you know if is the conference tonight?" Then write, "Do you know if the conference is tonight?"
- **The boxes:** Box 1 *if / whether*. Box 2 *when / where*. Box 3 report with *said* or *told*. Box 4 statement order.
- **Rule of thumb:** After *Do you know if / whether / when / where*, use statement order. Not *Do you know if is the conference tonight?*
- **Questions and answers:** read each Q. The class reads the A.
- **Practice A and B.** Then read the mistakes table.
- **Pair check:** A asks, "Do you know if the conference is tonight?" B answers, "Yes. It starts at 6:30." Then switch. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *Do you know if + statement. He said… / He told me to…*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:18 · Talk Time: Office Call + Pass It On · 18 min
- **Say at the start:** "Some cards are a parent. Some are a volunteer. Pick either one." Do not ask who has children.
- **Model one question:** "Do you know if the conference is tonight?" Answer from the information card.
- **Round 1, Office Call (back to back):** A is the caller with a goal card. B is the office, with an information card. A must use *Do you know if* at least twice. **15:** pairs, then a new partner for round 2. **7–8:** pairs. **5 or fewer:** one call the whole group can hear, then each person asks one more question.
- **Round 2, Pass It On:** A tells a new partner what the office said. Use *said* or *told*. The partner reads it back.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:18–7:25 · Read & Write · 7 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the newsletter aloud together.
- **Read:** the Northside School newsletter page about conference week. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the time, the place, and what to bring."
- **Check:** questions 1–5.
- **Write:** a 6–8 sentence email to the office that asks two indirect questions and confirms one detail. Start in class. Finish at home.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** *Write the question you would ask the office, and the answer you hope for.* Four sentences: two with *Do you know if / whether*, one reported answer, and a reason.
- **Collect the pages.** Sort them into yes / almost / not yet. No names next week.
- **Homework:** about 20–25 minutes. Do item 1 together: *Do you know if the office is open?*
:::

### Listening script

*About 2:20. The conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen. Not recorded yet.*

> **Audio:** No speaker yet. Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson eight. Maya is calling the office at Northside School. She volunteers for the after-school reading hour. Mr. Reed answers the phone.
> **Mr. Reed:** Northside School office. This is Mr. Reed.
> **Maya:** Hello. This is Maya. I want to ask about the conference.
> **Mr. Reed:** Of course. How can I help you?
> **Maya:** Do you know if the conference is tonight?
> **Mr. Reed:** Yes. It starts at six thirty, in the library.
> **Maya:** Thank you. Do you know whether I should bring the signup sheet?
> **Mr. Reed:** Yes. Please bring the signup sheet. I'm going to set it by the office window.
> **Maya:** Do you know where the office window is?
> **Mr. Reed:** It's down the hallway, past the bell.
> **Maya:** Can you tell me if I need a permission slip? I'm a volunteer, not a parent.
> **Mr. Reed:** A volunteer does not need a slip. A parent brings a permission slip for early pickup.
> **Maya:** Do you know when I should arrive?
> **Mr. Reed:** Please arrive ten minutes early.
> **Maya:** I will. I'm going to put the sheet in my backpack.
> **Maya:** Do you know where I should leave my backpack?
> **Mr. Reed:** Leave it by the office window.
> **Mr. Reed:** The newsletter also says the conference is tonight.
> **Maya:** Do you know if dismissal is at the usual time?
> **Mr. Reed:** Yes. Dismissal is at three. The conference is in the evening.
> **Maya:** Thank you. I don't want to miss the start.
> **Mr. Reed:** You're welcome. We'll see you ten minutes early.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Check the Meaning:* 1 a backpack, 2 a bell, 3 a permission slip.
- *Listening Task A:* **b** (to ask about the conference).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 tonight, 2 6:30, 3 the signup sheet, 4 ten minutes early, 5 down the hallway, 6 no. Six thirty in the script is 6:30.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 if, 2 whether, 3 where, 4 if, 5 if, 6 where.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 where, 2 when, 3 whether, 4 if, 5 where, 6 when.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 the conference is, 2 the office is, 3 it starts, 4 said, 5 me, 6 the office is.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary. A good report: *He said the conference was tonight. He told me to bring the signup sheet.*
- *Homework 1:* 1 Do you know if the office is open? 2 Do you know where the conference is? 3 Do you know when it starts? *Homework 4:* The class is on Tuesday, isn't it? *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 Tonight at 6:30. 2 In the library. 3 The signup sheet. 4 A permission slip. 5 Ten minutes early. *Writing (C):* answers will vary. A good email has two *Do you know if / whether* questions and confirms one detail.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "Do you know if the conference is tonight? Do you know whether I should bring the sheet? He said it starts at 6:30. I want to arrive on time."

### Teacher notes

- **Say at the start:** "Some cards are a parent. Some are a volunteer. Pick either one." Do not ask who has children.
- **The school is invented.** No grades, no discipline stories, and no immigration paperwork.
- **Statement order** stays even when the voice rises. *Do you know if* is one friendly chunk.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms.
- **All names and the school are invented.**

## Lesson 8 (Week 9) · Grammar

**Read across. The left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**

### Grammar Box 1: if or whether

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| Do you know **if** the conference is tonight? | **Yes. It starts at 6:30.** / **I'm not sure.** |
| Do you know **whether** I need a permission slip? | **Yes. Please bring the slip.** |
| Can you tell me **if** the office is still open? | **Yes. It closes at seven.** |

### Grammar Box 2: when or where

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| Do you know **where** the office is? | **It's down the hallway, by the office window.** |
| Do you know **when** it starts? | **It starts at 6:30.** |
| Do you know **where** I sign up? | **At the office window.** |

### Grammar Box 3: said or told

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| What did he say about the night? | **He said the conference was tonight.** |
| What did he tell you to bring? | **He told me to bring the slip.** |
| What did he say about the time? | **He said it starts at 6:30.** |

### Grammar Box 4: statement order

<!-- subst -->
| NOT THIS | SAY THIS |
|---|---|
| Do you know if is the conference tonight? | **Do you know if the conference is tonight?** |
| Do you know where is the office? | **Do you know where the office is?** |
| Do you know when does it start? | **Do you know when it starts?** |

**Rule of thumb:** After *Do you know if / whether / when / where*, use statement order. Not *Do you know if is the conference tonight?*

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *Do you know if is the office open?***

A: No. Statement order: ***Do you know if the office is open?***

**Q: Can I say *Do you know where is the conference?***

A: No. ***Do you know where the conference is?***

**Q: How do I report the answer?**

A: ***He said the conference was tonight. He told me to bring the slip.***

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| Do you know if is the office open? | Do you know **if the office is** open? |
| Do you know where is the conference? | Do you know **where the conference is?** |
| He told to me to bring the slip. | He **told me** to bring the slip. |
| He said me the conference was tonight. | He **said** the conference was tonight. |
