## Lesson 13 (Week 14): A Short Talk

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- open a talk with one clear sentence
- give two examples
- close with a next step
- speak for about two minutes from notes
:::

**Today you will:** Open with one clear sentence. • Give two examples. • Close with a next step.

**By the end of class, you can do these four things.** Every goal is a good goal.

- I can open a talk with one clear sentence.
- I can give two examples.
- I can close with a next step.
- I can speak for about two minutes from notes.

:::box Materials
- Student pages for Lesson 13
- Notecards and a stopwatch or phone timer
- Listening about 2:20, or read the script with a volunteer
- **Prep time:** 15 minutes. Model a one-minute version of your own first: introduction, one example, conclusion.
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 6 min
- A asks a card question. B answers in about four sentences. Then switch.
- Homework check: one *which* or *where* sentence from Lesson 12. Write 2 corrections. No names.
- Topics tonight are class, job, or a person who helped. Do not ask anyone to tell a harder story.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- Point, say, repeat.
- **Show:** an *outline* is the four parts. A *notecard* holds a few words, not the whole talk. A *pause* is a short stop between parts. *Eye contact* means looking up from the card. *Applause* is for finishing. The *community center* is the room for the practice night. A *stopwatch* keeps the time.
- **Check the meaning:** "Does the introduction name the point?" (Yes.) • "Does each example need a detail?" (Yes.) • "Is a conclusion a new story?" (No.)
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:12–6:32 · Listening + Hear it, say it · 20 min
- **Before you listen:** Imani, a former learner, gives a short talk at a practice night: what she has been learning, one hard moment, one person who helped, and her next step. Mr. Gordon asks one question.
- **Voices:** Narrator, Imani, and Mr. Gordon.
- **Play 1 → Task A. Play 2 → Tasks B and C.**
- **Hear it, say it:** *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms. Pause between the four parts. Slow down on the introduction and the conclusion.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A + pair check · 13 min
- This is a talk frame, not a new tense. Introduction → Example 1 → Example 2 → Conclusion.
- **Useful review sentences, one each:** *I've been learning… / By the time I finished…, I had… / I wish I had…, so next time I will… / I'm going to…*
- **Rule of thumb:** The introduction names the point. Each example has a detail. The conclusion repeats the point in new words and adds a next step.
- **Pair check:** A says "introduction," "example," or "conclusion." B says what that part must do.
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:18 · Talk Time · 18 min
- **Round 1, Outline Aloud:** partners read their four-part outline. The listener asks one question about an example.
- **Round 2, Timed Rehearsal:** groups of 3. A learner is the timekeeper with a stopwatch (about 2 minutes, up to 3). Listeners write one strength and one tip. Everyone rehearses.
- Topics: my class, my job, or a person who helped me practice. You choose how personal to be.
- Reading from cards is fine. Applause is for finishing, not for the most dramatic story.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:18–7:25 · Read & Write · 7 min
- Read Imani's talk. The four parts are marked. Pairs answer the five questions.
- Write your own outline, then the talk in 12–16 sentences. Start the outline in class. Finish at home.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** *Write your introduction and your conclusion only.* Four sentences. You will say the examples in the rehearsal.
- **Homework:** do item 1 together. Label the three parts. About 20–25 minutes. Bring the outline and the talk next time you speak.
:::

### Listening script

About 2:20. Spoken lines use *going to* and *want to*.

> **Narrator:** Lesson thirteen. Imani, a former learner, gives a short talk at a practice night at the community center. Then Mr. Gordon asks one question.
> **Imani:** Good evening. My point is simple. I've been learning to speak up at work, and a mentor helped me.
> **Imani:** That is my introduction. I want to give two examples.
> **Imani:** Example one. By the time I finished my first month, I had learned to pause.
> **Imani:** The hard moment was a team meeting. I wish I had looked up sooner, so next time I will make eye contact.
> **Imani:** Example two. My mentor at the community center listened and asked one question.
> **Imani:** That question helped me find my point. The achievement was small, and it was mine.
> **Imani:** I'm going to practice with a stopwatch for two minutes. The outline is on my note card.
> **Imani:** This is my conclusion. I can speak from notes. My next step is one short talk a week at work.
> **Mr. Gordon:** Thank you. Which example do you want to keep?
> **Imani:** I want to keep the mentor example. I'm going to say it again, more slowly.
> **Mr. Gordon:** Good. A pause between the parts helps. Applause is for finishing, not for the longest story.
> **Imani:** I am not going to memorize the talk. Eye contact means I look up from the card.
> **Mr. Gordon:** What is your next step, in one sentence?
> **Imani:** I'm going to practice one talk a week. I want to keep the introduction short.
> **Mr. Gordon:** The conclusion should repeat the point, not start a new story.
> **Imani:** An achievement can be small. My class, my job, or a person who helped me is enough.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **b** (speaking up at work.)
- *Listening Task B:* 1 introduction, 2 example, 3 example, 4 conclusion, 5 a stopwatch, 6 finishing.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 Introduction, 2 Example 1, 3 Example 2, 4 Conclusion.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 Introduction, 2 Example, 3 Example, 4 Conclusion, 5 Conclusion, 6 Example.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 names the point, 2 has a detail, 3 repeats the point, 4 I'm going to, 5 had learned, 6 learning.
- *Homework 1:* 1 Introduction, 2 Example, 3 Conclusion.
- *Read & Write:* 1 She has been learning to speak up at work. 2 She did not look up. 3 Her mentor. 4 One short talk a week. 5 It repeats the point and adds a next step.
- *Exit ticket:* introduction and conclusion only, four sentences.

### Teacher notes

- "My class," "my job," or "a person who helped me practice" is always enough. Never ask about a journey to this country, family separation, or a hard personal event.
- Reading from cards is fine. Model a one-minute version of your own first. Applause is for finishing, not for the most dramatic story.
- The frame is not a new tense. The review sentences are ones learners already know.
- A talk that starts with the example never says the point. A conclusion that is a new story does not close the talk.
- *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms.

## Lesson 13 (Week 14) · Grammar

This is a talk frame, not a new tense.

### Grammar Box 1: introduction

| THE INTRODUCTION | IT NAMES THE POINT |
|---|---|
| I've been learning to speak up at work. | That is my point. |
| A mentor helped me. | That is part of the point. |
| I want to give two examples. | Then the examples start. |

### Grammar Box 2: two examples

| THE EXAMPLE | A DETAIL |
|---|---|
| Example 1 | By the time I finished my first month, I had learned to pause. |
| The hard moment | I wish I had looked up sooner, so next time I will make eye contact. |
| Example 2 | My mentor asked one question. |

### Grammar Box 3: conclusion

| THE CONCLUSION | NOT A NEW STORY |
|---|---|
| Repeat the point in new words. | I can speak from notes. |
| Add a next step. | I'm going to practice one talk a week. |
| Stop. | Applause is for finishing. |

### Grammar Box 4: review sentences, not a new tense

| REVIEW | ONE SENTENCE |
|---|---|
| I've been learning… | I've been learning to speak up at work. |
| By the time I finished…, I had… | By the time I finished my first month, I had learned to pause. |
| I wish I had…, so next time I will… | I wish I had looked up sooner, so next time I will make eye contact. |
| I'm going to… | I'm going to practice with a stopwatch. |

**Rule of thumb:** The introduction names the point. Each example has a detail. The conclusion repeats the point in new words and adds a next step.

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can the talk start with the example?**

A: No. The introduction names the point first.

**Q: Can the conclusion be a new story?**

A: No. It repeats the point and adds a next step.

**Q: Can I say *I gonna practice*?**

A: No. Say *I'm going to practice*.

### Practice A: Write *Introduction*, *Example*, or *Conclusion*.

1. My point is simple. I've been learning to speak up. ____________
2. By the time I finished my first month, I had learned to pause. ____________
3. My mentor asked one question. ____________
4. I can speak from notes, and my next step is one talk a week. ____________
5. I'm going to practice with a stopwatch. ____________
6. I wish I had looked up sooner, so next time I will make eye contact. ____________

### Practice B: Circle the correct words.

1. The introduction (**names the point** / **tells a new story**).
2. Each example (**has a detail** / **has no detail**).
3. The conclusion (**is a new story** / **repeats the point**).
4. Next step: (**I'm going to** / **I gonna**) practice.
5. By the time I finished, I (**had learned** / **have learn**) to pause.
6. I've been (**learning** / **learn**) to speak up.

### Common mistakes

| Don't say | Say |
|---|---|
| Start with the example and never say the point. | Start with the **introduction**. It names the point. |
| A conclusion that is a new story. | The conclusion **repeats the point** and adds a next step. |
| I gonna practice. | **I'm going to** practice. |

### Pair check

A says "introduction," "example," or "conclusion." B says what that part must do. Then switch.

## Lesson 13 (Week 14) · Read & Write

### A. Read the talk. The four parts are marked.

**Imani's practice talk · Community center**

**Introduction.** My point is simple. I've been learning to speak up at work, and a mentor helped me. I want to tell you two examples, and then a next step. You can follow the outline on my notecard. I am not going to memorize the words.

**Example 1.** By the time I finished my first month, I had learned to pause and look up from my notecard. The hard moment was a team meeting. I read every word, and I never looked up. I wish I had looked up sooner, so next time I will make eye contact. A pause is not a mistake. It gives people time to hear the point. Eye contact means looking up from the card, not memorizing the talk.

**Example 2.** My mentor at the community center listened and asked one question: What is the one thing you want us to remember? That question helped me. The achievement was small. I answered in two sentences, and then I stopped. I'm going to keep that question on my notecard. A person who helped me practice is enough for this example. I do not need a longer story.

**Conclusion.** I can speak from notes. That is the same point, in new words. My next step is one short talk a week at work. I'm going to practice with a stopwatch for two minutes. Applause is for finishing, not for a dramatic story. Thank you.

Mr. Gordon asked, "Which example do you want to keep?" Imani said, "I want to keep the mentor example." She is going to say it again, more slowly.

The introduction names the point. Each example has a detail. The conclusion repeats the point in new words and adds a next step. A talk that starts with the example never says the point. A conclusion that is a new story does not close the talk. Topics that are always enough: my class, my job, or a person who helped me practice.
