## How to Use This Plan

> **Teach in English.** You do not need to speak the learners' languages. Draw two X's on a line. There is no contrast-tip box. The common mix-up is a row in the mistakes table.

> **Instructions:** *Listen. Circle. Number. Write. Work with a partner. Give a reason.*

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it**, **check it** ("Which time is earlier?"), **then go**.

> **When a learner is stuck:** show the two X's, give one more English example, point to the grammar box, let a classmate help for a few seconds, then stay in English.

> **About four sentences:** the exit ticket is four sentences: an earlier event, a later event, one more event, and why the order mattered.

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:06 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 6 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Opening:** one question from last week. A asks. B answers in a short summary. A asks one follow-up. Then switch.
- **Model:** "How long have you been studying English?" If the learner says *I am studying since September*, point to the Lesson 1 fix: *I've been studying since September* or *I've studied since September*.
- **Homework check (last 2 min):** pairs read one sentence from Lesson 1 Homework item 1. You write two corrections on the board. No names.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:06–6:12 · New Words · 6 min
- **Do:** point, say, repeat. ★ = most important.
- **Show the hard words:** the *oven* bakes. It is not a person. The *inspector* visits before opening day. A *permit* is the paper that says the work is allowed. A *paint roller* spreads paint on a wall. The *awning* is a cover over the door, not a cake. The *store sign* and the *poster* are two different things. In our story, the sign went up first.
- **Check the meaning:** "Is the oven a person?" (no, it bakes) • "Who comes before opening day?" (the inspector) • "Is an awning a cover or a cake?" (a cover)
- **Partner game:** A points to two pictures and asks, "Which one comes first in a bakery opening?" B answers with *before*. Switch.
- **If behind:** the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:12–6:32 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 20 min
- **Before you listen:** "Priya and Ben work at a new bakery. Ms. Cole, the inspector, visits before opening day. Listen: is the bakery open yet?"
- **Voices:** Priya, Ben, and Ms. Cole. A narrator opens and closes. Audio is not recorded yet (about 2:15). Read the script two times.
- **Play 1 → Task A.** Answer: **b. No. It opens tomorrow.**
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** B is the order of five events. C is *had* or the simple past.
- **"Hear it, say it":** *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms. Say the full *had* so the two times stay clear. *We'd already* is easy to miss. In this lesson, say *had*.
- **If behind:** Task C on the board.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 13 min
- **Draw two X's.** Label the left one "earlier: had painted" and the right one "later: the inspector arrived."
- **Rule of thumb:** the earlier past uses *had* + past participle. The later past uses the simple past. *By the time* marks the later time.
- **Read the boxes across.** Box 1 is *by the time* and *when*. Box 2 is earlier and later. Box 3 is the past continuous at the later moment. Box 4 is the rule in three lines.
- **Questions and answers:** *Had you finished the sign before the poster went up?* → *Yes, we had. / No, we hadn't.*
- **Practice, mistakes, then the pair check:** A says a later time (*the inspector arrived*). B says what had already happened. Switch.

### Listening script

Audio is not recorded yet (about 2:15). Read it with volunteers at a natural, friendly speed. The spoken lines use *going to* and *want to*. *gonna* and *wanna* are short forms. This listening uses the full forms.

> **Narrator:** Lesson two. Priya and Ben work at a new bakery. Opening day is tomorrow. Ms. Cole, the inspector, is here today.
> **Ms. Cole:** Good morning. I'm Ms. Cole. Is the bakery open yet?
> **Priya:** Not yet. We want to open tomorrow. We are going to cut the ribbon in the morning.
> **Ms. Cole:** What had you already finished before I arrived?
> **Ben:** By the time you arrived, we had already painted the walls.
> **Priya:** When the oven was delivered, Ben had already cleaned the kitchen.
> **Ms. Cole:** Had you finished the store sign before the poster went up?
> **Ben:** Yes, we had. The sign was first. Then the poster went up.
> **Priya:** We had already picked up the permit before we painted.
> **Ben:** First I cleaned the kitchen. Second, the oven arrived. Third, we painted. Fourth, the poster went up. You arrived last.
> **Ms. Cole:** Was the painter still working when I knocked?
> **Priya:** Yes. The painter was still working. The walls are dry now.
> **Ms. Cole:** The display case and the awning look ready. You can open tomorrow.
> **Ben:** The counter was ready yesterday. Thank you.
> **Ms. Cole:** Bring the permit to the door in the morning.
> **Ben:** We will. It is already in the folder.
> **Priya:** We want to tell the staff tonight. We are going to hang the ribbon in the morning.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Check the meaning:* 1 no, it bakes. 2 the inspector. 3 a cover.
- *Listening Task A:* **b. No. It opens tomorrow.**
- *Listening Task B:* 1 cleaned the kitchen. 2 the oven arrived. 3 we painted. 4 the poster went up. 5 the inspector.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 had. 2 arrived. 3 had. 4 went. 5 had. 6 was.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 had. 2 had. 3 arrived. 4 went. 5 had. 6 was.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 arrived. 2 had already painted. 3 opened. 4 was delivered. 5 Yes, we had. 6 was working.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary. Four events, in order, with *had already* or *by the time*.
- *Homework 1:* 1 By the time the inspector arrived, we had already painted the walls. 2 When the oven was delivered, Ben had already cleaned the kitchen. 3 Yes, we had. 4 The painter was still working when the inspector knocked. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 I've been working. 2 worked. 3 I've worked. 4 She's been taking.
- *Read & Write:* 1 No. It opens tomorrow. 2 He had cleaned the kitchen. 3 They had painted the walls. 4 The store sign was first. 5 The permit and dry paint.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "By the time the inspector arrived, we had already painted the walls. When the oven was delivered, Ben had already cleaned the kitchen. The poster went up after the sign. The order mattered because the inspector needed the permit and dry paint."

### Teacher notes

- **Draw two X's** and leave them up all night. Left: "earlier: had painted." Right: "later: the inspector arrived."
- **Say the full *had*.** *We'd already* is easy to miss, and *have* is a different time. This lesson keeps *had* strong.
- **The *by the time* half is the later event.** *By the time we had opened, the inspector arrived* swaps the times. The fix is *By the time we opened, the inspector had arrived.*
- **Projects can be invented.** Do not ask learners to describe a move between countries. A class, a team at work, or a move inside the city is enough.
- **Writing check 1:** the 6–8 sentence note. Write one strength and one fix. Learners keep it.
- **Reductions:** the script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms.

## Lesson 2 (Week 2) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads the question. B says the answer.**

### Grammar Box 1: *by the time* and *when*

| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| What had you done **by the time** the inspector arrived? | We **had already painted** the walls. |
| What had Ben done **when** the oven was delivered? | Ben **had already cleaned** the kitchen. |
| **Had** you **finished** the sign before the poster went up? | **Yes, we had.** / **No, we hadn't.** |

### Grammar Box 2: earlier and later

| EARLIER: *had* + past participle | LATER: simple past |
|---|---|
| we had already painted the walls | the inspector arrived |
| Ben had already cleaned the kitchen | the oven was delivered |
| we had finished the sign | the poster went up |

### Grammar Box 3: the later moment's background

| QUESTION | ANSWER |
|---|---|
| What was the painter doing when the inspector knocked? | The painter **was still working**. |

### Grammar Box 4: Which time?

| FORM | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|
| earlier past | *had* + past participle: we had already painted |
| later past | simple past: the inspector arrived |
| *by the time* | marks the later time: By the time the inspector arrived, … |

**Rule of thumb:** the earlier past uses *had* + past participle. The later past uses the simple past. *By the time* marks the later time.

### Questions and answers

**Q: Where does *already* go?**

A: Before *had*'s participle: *we had already painted*. Not *we already painted* for the earlier time.

**Q: Which half of *by the time* is later?**

A: The *by the time* half. *By the time the inspector arrived* is later. *we had painted* is earlier.

**Q: *Yes, we did* or *Yes, we had*?**

A: After *Had you…?*, say *Yes, we had.*

### Practice A: Write *had* or the simple past.

1. By the time the inspector arrived, we ____________ already painted the walls.
2. When the oven was delivered, Ben ____________ already cleaned the kitchen.
3. The inspector ____________ after the painting.
4. The poster ____________ up after the sign.
5. Had you finished the sign? Yes, we ____________.
6. The painter ____________ still working when the inspector knocked.

### Practice B: Circle the correct words.

1. By the time the inspector (**arrived** / **had arrived**), we had already painted.
2. We (**already painted** / **had already painted**) the walls.
3. By the time we (**had opened** / **opened**), the inspector had arrived.
4. When the oven (**was delivered** / **had delivered**), Ben had cleaned the kitchen.
5. Had you finished the sign? (**Yes, we had.** / **Yes, we did.**)
6. The painter (**was working** / **had worked**) when the inspector knocked.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| When the inspector arrived, we already painted. | When the inspector arrived, we had already painted. |
| By the time we had opened, the inspector arrived. | By the time we opened, the inspector had arrived. |

### A. Read Priya's note to the staff.

:::box Project update · Opening day is tomorrow
Opening day is tomorrow. The bakery is not open yet. This note is the order of the work, so the next shift can see what was already done.

By the time Ms. Cole arrived, we had already painted the walls. When the oven was delivered, Ben had already cleaned the kitchen. We had already picked up the permit before we painted. Had we finished the store sign before the poster went up? Yes, we had. The sign went up first. The poster went up after the sign, and it was already up when the inspector arrived.

The painter was still working when Ms. Cole knocked. He stopped. The walls are dry now. She looked at the permit, the oven, and the clean kitchen. She said we can open tomorrow. She needed the permit and dry paint before she could say yes.

Here is the same order in one list. First, Ben cleaned the kitchen. Second, the oven arrived. Third, we painted the walls. Fourth, the poster went up. Last, the inspector arrived. The ribbon is still on the counter. We are going to hang it in the morning. We want every guest to see it on the door. The display case was ready before her visit, and the awning was already up. The paint roller is in the back room. Please do not leave it on the counter.
