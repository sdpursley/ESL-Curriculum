## How to Use This Packet

- **The test for learners is a separate file:** `Level5_Final_Student_Test.pdf` (no answers, no cover page). Print 1 per learner + 3 extra.
- **This packet** is for you: directions, the listening script, the answer key, the scoring guide, and the speaking papers.
- **Audio:** the recording `L5_Final_listening.mp3` is not ready yet (the script is about 2:38 long). Until it is ready, read the listening script aloud two times, at a natural speed. Two readers are better: one reads the women's lines and one reads the men's lines.
- **All instructions are in English.** Read each instruction aloud once and show an example on the board if learners look unsure.

## Week 15: Final: Step by Step

### What the test has

| Part | What learners do | Points | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Listening | Listen to three parts (a website conversation; a claim call; a short meeting). Circle an answer, mark true or false, or write one word (10 items). | 10 | 18 min (6:10–6:28) |
| B. Words | Complete sentences with words from a box (8 items). | 8 | 17 min (6:28–6:45) for B + C |
| C. Grammar | Part 1: circle the correct words (12 items). Part 2: find and fix one mistake in each sentence (4 items). | 16 | (with B) |
| D. Reading | Read a text of about 260 words. Answer 8 questions. | 8 | 13 min (6:55–7:08) |
| E. Writing | Choose one of two tasks. Write 8–12 sentences (a letter of one or two paragraphs). | 8 | 19 min (7:08–7:27) |
| **Written total** | | **50** | **67 min** |
| Speaking interview | Pairs with the teacher, during the written parts. Scored with the rubric, not added to the written total. | 3–9 | 4 min per pair |

*Covers Lessons 1–13. Most items are from Lessons 8–13 (school and office questions, checking a source, compromise, a claim, a meeting, a short talk); some items review Lessons 1–7. The speaking interview uses the same first questions as the midterm, so you can hear the growth.*

### Before class (20 minutes)

1. Print the student test (1 per learner + 3). From this packet, print the Speaking Task Cards (1 set per pair), the Score Sheet, and the Self-Check (1 per learner).
2. The recording is not ready yet: ask a second reader to help, and read the listening script through once before class.
3. Set 2 chairs and 1 chair for you in a quiet corner (for the speaking interviews).
4. Have pencils, a clock or timer, and the class list.

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Welcome and one review station · 10 min
- **Say:** "Tonight is the test. First listening, then words and grammar, then a short break, then reading and writing. I will call you in pairs for a short speaking interview."
- One review station for the whole class (the midterm: a one-minute interview in pairs; the final: a must / might / can't source check). Call the first pair for the interview.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:28 · A. Listening · 18 min
- Hand out the test. Learners write their name. Read the Part A instruction and the scene line aloud.
- Give 2 minutes to read the questions. Play the audio (or read the script) **once**. Wait 1 minute. Play it **again**. Give 2 more minutes.
- No interviews during the listening.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:28–6:45 · B. Words and C. Grammar · 17 min
- Read the Part B and Part C instructions aloud. Show Part C2 on the board: *She said me the time.* → *She told me the time.*
- Learners work alone and quietly. Call pairs for the speaking interview (4 minutes per pair).
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:45–6:55 · Break · 10 min
- Collect nothing yet: learners turn the test over. Continue interviews if needed.
:::

:::step Step 5 · 6:55–7:08 · D. Reading · 13 min
- Read the instruction. Learners read the text and answer. Continue interviews.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:08–7:27 · E. Writing · 19 min
- Read both tasks aloud. Learners check the box for the task they chose and write the number of sentences on the test.
- Say: "Put your ideas in order. Read the questions under your task." At 7:25, say: "Two more minutes. Read your writing one time."
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:27–7:30 · Self-check and goodbye · 3 min
- Collect the tests. Hand out the Can-Do Self-Check. Learners check the boxes and write one goal. Collect it.
- Finish any last interviews after class or at the start of the next class.
:::

:::box Speaking interview (4 minutes per pair)
- **Part 1 (about 2 minutes).** Ask each learner 2 of the questions on the Speaking Interview Card. After each answer, ask one follow-up question (Why? When? Who with? What happened next?).
- **Part 2 (about 2 minutes).** Give the pair one Task Card. They read it for 20 seconds and then talk to each other. You listen and only help if they stop.
- **Score** each learner on the 3 rubric areas (1–3 each, 3–9 in total). Write the score at once, before the next pair.
:::

### Speaking rubric (1–3 in each area)

| Area | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Communication | Short answers only; needs a lot of help. | Answers in 1–3 sentences, or gives no reason, or does not respond to the partner. | Answers in about **four** connected sentences with a reason **and** responds to the partner (a follow-up question, a check, or polite agreement or disagreement). |
| Target grammar | Few lesson forms; many mistakes stop the meaning. | Uses some lesson forms (been + -ing, had + past participle, I wish I had, said / told / asked, must / might / can't, which / where / who); mistakes do not stop the meaning. | Uses lesson forms correctly most of the time. |
| Pronunciation and fluency | Hard to understand; long stops. | Understandable with some effort; some stops. | Easy to understand; few stops. |

*From Level 5 Scope §7 (the Level 4 rubric; a 3 in Communication means about four connected sentences, a reason, and a response to the partner). Score 3–9 in total.*

## Listening Script

### Listening script: `L5_Final_listening.mp3` (about 2:38, estimated from the script; not recorded yet; play it two times)

Speakers: Aisha, Ms. Lane, Noah, Tom, and a narrator.

> **Narrator:** Final exam listening. Part one. Aisha and her friend Noah are looking at a website. Listen.
> **Noah:** Aisha, look at this website. It says the city is giving free laptops to adult students.
> **Aisha:** Really? Do you know if it's from the city?
> **Noah:** I'm not sure. There's no author, and I can't find a date stamp.
> **Aisha:** And look at the logo. It's different from the city logo. It can't be the city page.
> **Noah:** You're right. It asks for your bank card number, too. It must be an ad, or maybe a scam.
> **Aisha:** Let's check the source. I'll call the library and ask whether the program is real.
> **Narrator:** Part two. The next day, Aisha calls customer service about a table that she ordered.
> **Ms. Lane:** Thank you for waiting. My name is Ellen Lane. How can I help you?
> **Aisha:** Hi. I'd like to file a claim. My new kitchen table arrived on Monday, and one leg was broken.
> **Ms. Lane:** I'm sorry to hear that. Do you have your receipt?
> **Aisha:** Yes, I do. I was wondering if you could send a new leg instead of a new table.
> **Ms. Lane:** We can do that. Were photos attached to your email?
> **Aisha:** Yes, two photos were attached. I sent them yesterday.
> **Ms. Lane:** Good. I'll send you a reference number by email. The new leg should arrive in five days.
> **Aisha:** Thank you. Waiting on hold was a little long, but you've been very helpful.
> **Narrator:** Part three. That evening, Aisha and Noah go to a meeting in their building. Tom is the chairperson.
> **Tom:** Good evening, everyone. Let's start. The first item on the agenda is the laundry room, which is closed on weekends.
> **Aisha:** I have a proposal. The laundry room is so busy on weekdays that many people can't use it. Could we open it on Saturdays?
> **Noah:** I agree. Keeping it closed all weekend isn't fair to people who work during the week.
> **Tom:** Good point. But the building manager is worried about noise on Sundays.
> **Aisha:** Then let's meet in the middle. We could open it on Saturdays and keep it closed on Sundays.
> **Tom:** That's a fair compromise. Let's vote at the next meeting. Noah, can you take the minutes?
> **Noah:** Sure. I'll send them by Friday.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

## Scoring Guide

### Written test: score bands (proposed)

| Written score | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 39–50 | **Meets** | The learner can use most of the Level 5 language on their own. |
| 26–38 | **Almost** | The learner understands a lot but still needs practice. Look at which part was low. |
| 0–25 | **Not yet** | Talk with the learner. Plan extra practice. |

*These bands are **proposed** (about 78% and 52%, the same as the Level 1 and Level 2 exams). The program confirms them after the first classes use the test.*

### Writing rubric (Part E, 8 points)

| Area | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task | Off topic, or much shorter than the task asks. | On topic, but shorter than the task asks, or a part of the task is missing. | Does the whole task in the number of sentences asked, with reasons or details. |
| Order | A reader cannot follow the order of the ideas. | Some order, but a reader has to guess what happened first or what the main point is. | Clear order: first, then, by the time…; or a greeting, the reason for writing, details, and a closing. |
| Grammar | Many mistakes; the meaning is hard to follow. | Some mistakes in the lesson forms. | Uses the lesson forms for the task with few mistakes. |
| Clear for a reader | A reader cannot follow it. | A reader understands most of it after reading it two times. | A reader understands it on the first reading. |

*Spelling mistakes do not lose points if the word is clear. Score what the learner wrote, not what is missing from the model answer.*

### Speaking (3–9, separate from the written score)

- 7–9: strong for this level. 5–6: on track. 3–4: needs more speaking practice. *(Proposed.)*

### Completion note (proposed, for the teacher's folder)

- came to at least 9 of the 13 lessons;
- took the final exam (written and speaking);
- scored 2 or more in each of the 3 speaking rubric areas;
- wrote a letter or paragraph of at least 8 sentences (Part E) that a reader understands on the first reading (Clear for a reader = 2).

*Proposed, from Level 5 Scope §7. The note is for the teacher's folder. It is not a rule that keeps anyone out of graduation: every learner gives the Lesson 13 talk and receives a certificate. There is no Level 6; the teacher can invite a learner to repeat Level 5.*

## Papers to Copy

### Speaking Interview Card (Teacher)

**Part 1. Ask each learner 2 questions (the same questions as the first weeks, so you can hear the growth). Then ask one follow-up question.**

1. How long have you been living in this city?
2. Tell me about a job, a class, or a project. What had you already done before you started?
3. What is something you wish you had known when you started this class?
4. What is a rule at your work, school, or building? Use unless or as long as.
5. Tell me about a message someone gave you. What did the person say or ask?
6. You like this class, don't you? Why?

**Follow-up questions:** Why? When was that? Who were you with? What happened next? How did you feel?

**Part 2. Give the pair one Task Card.** Listen for:

- **Card A (Judge a web page):** It must be…, It might be…, It can't be…, because…; the source, the author, the date stamp; each learner responds to the partner.
- **Card B (Agree on a compromise):** Can we meet in the middle?, so… that, I was wondering if…, a reason; about four sentences each, and each learner responds to the partner.

### Speaking Task Cards

*Cut out the cards. Give one card to each pair.*

:::box Card A: Judge a web page
Read the page on the card: "FREE phones for all students! Send your bank card number today!" It has no author, no date stamp, and a strange logo.
Learners A and B decide together: Is it a fact, an ad, or a rumor? Each learner gives a reason with **must be, might be, or can't be** and responds to the partner.
:::

:::box Card B: Agree on a compromise
Two coworkers both want next Friday off. The store needs one of them on Friday.
Each learner says what he or she wants and why. Then find a solution in the middle (a swap, a shorter shift, or another day).
:::
