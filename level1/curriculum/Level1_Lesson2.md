## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. The student pages have a Spanish line and a "My language" line for every task. Learners read those on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Listen or write?"), **then go**. Say less, show more. Use the same short words every time: *Listen. Repeat. Point. Circle. Write. Partner. Group.*

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the instruction and the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 2 (Week 2): Where Are You From?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- say where they are from (*I'm from Mexico. I'm from Guadalajara.*)

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Hello + Review · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. Late arrivals join any pair.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **What's your name? How do you spell it? How are you? Are you a student?**
- **Model with one learner:** "Hello! What's your name?" → "Rosa." "How do you spell it?" → "R-O-S-A." "How are you?" → "I'm fine, thanks. And you?"
- **Do:** pairs use the **Hello + Review** page. A asks the 4 questions; B answers. Then switch. Then find a new partner and do it again.
- **Homework check (last 3 min):** partners show each other their Lesson 1 homework (the name, the friend's name, the 4 words). Walk around and look. Collect any Lesson 1 Progress Checks that were not done.
- **New learners:** pair them with a strong partner. They can do the Lesson 1 Progress Check at home.
- **If behind:** one partner only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:20 · New Words · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** the New Words page (or the world map). Point to a country on the map, then to the flag on the page: "**Mexico.** This is a **country**."
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat twice. Stress the strong part of each word: **MEX**-i-co, gua-te-**MA**-la, hon-**DU**-ras, el **SAL**-va-dor, co-**LOM**-bi-a, the u-**NI**-ted **STATES**.
- **country / city:** "Mexico is a **country**. Guadalajara is a **city**." Say two more: "Honduras: country. Tegucigalpa: city."
- **he / she / they:** point to a man in the room: "**he**." A woman: "**she**." A group: "**they**." Learners point as you say the word (fast, like a game).
- **How old?:** point to the cake. "How old? … 12. 20. 35." Hold up fingers.
- **Pairs (4 min):** A points to a picture, B says the word. Then switch. Then: A points to a classmate, B says *he* or *she*.
- **Learners from other countries:** write their country on the board and say it together. Every country is welcome; the page has the 6 most common in this program.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:20–6:32 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "This is Rosa. This is Marco. They are new students. It's the break." Point to the flags in Task A: "Where are they from? Listen."
- **Listen 1 → Task A** (match the person and the flag; write the letter).
- **Listen 2 → Task B** (Yes or No, 4 pictures). **Listen 3 → Task C** (write the age).
- **Pairs compare** after each task: "Show your partner." Then show the answers on the board.
- **"Say it" (2 min):** read 2 lines: "**Where are you from?**" and "**How old is he?**" The class repeats each line twice. Then pairs ask and answer each other.
- **The audio plays the conversation once.** Press play again for the next listen.
- **If behind:** play the audio 2 times, and do Task C together on the board.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.** Point left, then right.
- **Box 1:** you read a question; the class reads an answer. Point to a learner: "Where is **he** from?" → "He's from…".
- **Box 2:** ask about learners in the room: "Is Rosa from Mexico?" → "No, she isn't. She's from Guatemala." Stress the short answers: **Yes, she is. No, she isn't.**
- **Box 3 (2 min):** read the long form; the class reads the short form. Write on the board: **I → am. he, she, it → is. you, we, they → are.**
- **Box 4:** "How old are you?" Answer for yourself (a real or a practice age). Write: **I'm 35.** Then write *I have 35 years* and draw a big ✗ on it.
- **Check questions:** "She ___ from Colombia." (*is*) • "Where ___ they from?" (*are*) • "Is he from Mexico? No, he ___." (*isn't*)
- **Pair drill (last 3–4 min):** A asks from the left side about B or about a classmate; B answers from the right side. Then switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. It becomes homework, and Talk Time practices the same questions.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Put up the world map. Write on the board: **Where are you from? What city? This is ___. He's / She's from ___.**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:10 · Talk Time: Class Map · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, then groups of 3–4. 7–8 learners: pairs, then one group (the whole class).
- **Model with one learner:** "Where are you from?" → "Honduras." "What city?" → "San Pedro Sula." Write it in the table on the board. Then turn to the class: "**This is** Marco. **He's from** Honduras. He's from San Pedro Sula."
- **Part 1 (3 min):** pairs interview each other and write the partner's name, country, and city on the Talk Time page.
- **Part 2 (4 min):** each pair joins another pair. Each learner introduces the partner: "This is ___. He's / She's from ___." The group asks one question: "What city?" or "Is she from ___?"
- **Part 3 (2 min):** each learner puts a sticky note with their name on the map (or the teacher writes the names on the board under each country).
- **Any country is OK.** Learners can choose a city or country they like. **Never ask** how or why someone came here.
- **Baseline snapshot:** while groups talk, call about 5 learners you didn't check in Week 1, one at a time, for 2 minutes. Use the checklist in the answer key.
- **If behind:** skip Part 3.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:10–7:15 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner **says** one sentence to you at the door ("I'm from ___." or "This is ___. She's from ___.") **or writes** one sentence on a slip. Copying from the grammar box is fine.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together. Point to each instruction so learners know what to do at home.
:::

:::step Letters & Numbers · 7:15–7:25 · Numbers 0–20 · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Use the Letters & Numbers page and the numbers on the board.
- **See it (2 min):** point to each number, 0 to 20, and say it once. Hold up fingers for 0–10.
- **Say it (3 min):** learners repeat 0–10, then 11–20. Then go to the **tricky numbers** box. Say each one 3 times. Spend the most time on **3** (*th*: tongue between the teeth), **13 / 14…19** (stress the end: thir-**TEEN**), **12 / 20** (*twelve, twenty*), and **7** (*v*, not *b*).
- **Quick check (1 min):** say a number; learners hold up fingers or point on the page.
- **Write it (3 min):** Task B: say the 5 numbers in the answer key, 2 times each. Learners circle. Task C: read the 3 ages (answer key). Learners write numbers, not words. Task D: learners write the sentence again with capital letters (*Maria*, *Colombia*).
- **Partners:** Task E: A says a number from 0 to 20; B writes it. A checks. Switch.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they say the numbers with everyone, hold up fingers, and point instead of circle. A helper, if you have one, sits with them.
- **If behind:** do Tasks A and B only.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 5 numbers (answer key), slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle.
- **Parts B and C:** learners match and write on their own. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "A little." 😐 "Not yet." Learners check one.
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 3). Part A shows who needs more help with numbers.
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 3 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Listening script: `L1_L2_listening.mp3` (about 1:17; the conversation plays once, so play the audio again if needed)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer. Read slowly, with a short stop between speakers.

> **Narrator:** Lesson two. Listening. It's the break at English class. Rosa and Marco are new students. Listen.
> **Rosa:** Hi! I'm Rosa. What's your name?
> **Marco:** Hi, Rosa. I'm Marco. Nice to meet you.
> **Rosa:** Nice to meet you, too. Where are you from, Marco?
> **Marco:** I'm from Mexico.
> **Rosa:** Oh, nice! What city?
> **Marco:** Guadalajara. And you? Are you from Mexico, too?
> **Rosa:** No, I'm not. I'm from El Salvador.
> **Marco:** Oh, OK. What city?
> **Rosa:** San Salvador. My son is here, too. He's in the kids' class.
> **Marco:** Oh, how old is he?
> **Rosa:** He's twelve years old.
> **Marco:** Twelve? That's great. My friends are here, too. They're from Honduras.
> **Rosa:** Is the teacher from Mexico?
> **Marco:** No, she isn't. She's from the United States.
> **Rosa:** OK. Time for class! See you later, Marco.
> **Marco:** See you later!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* 1 Rosa **b** (El Salvador), 2 Marco **a** (Mexico), 3 the friends **d** (Honduras), 4 the teacher **c** (the United States).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 Yes, 2 No (Rosa is from El Salvador), 3 Yes, 4 No (the teacher is from the United States).
- *Listening Task C:* **12** (twelve).
- *Grammar practice (Write am, is, or are):* 1 am, 2 is, 3 are, 4 Is / is, 5 are, 6 are.
- *Homework 2 (am, is, are):* 1 am, 2 is, 3 are, 4 is, 5 are.
- *Homework 3 (the country):* 1 Mexico, 2 Colombia, 3 Honduras, 4 the United States.
- *Letters & Numbers Task B (the teacher says):* 1 **12**, 2 **3**, 3 **15**, 4 **7**, 5 **20**. Say each number 2 times.
- *Letters & Numbers Task C (the teacher says):* 1 "He's **9**." 2 "She's **14**." 3 "They're **18** and **20**." Say each one 2 times.
- *Letters & Numbers Task D:* Maria is from Colombia.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says):* 1 **13**, 2 **6**, 3 **20**, 4 **11**, 5 **17**. Say each number 2 times.
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 c, 2 d, 3 a, 4 b.
- *Progress Check Part C:* 1 am, 2 is, 3 are.
- *Progress Check scoring:* Part A /5 + Part B /4 + Part C /3 = /12. This is not a test grade. It shows who needs help with numbers (Part A) and with *am / is / are* (Parts B and C).

### Teacher notes

- **Countries and privacy:** learners can talk about any country or city: where they were born, where they lived, or a place they like. **Never ask** about immigration, papers, or how or why someone came to the U.S. If a learner doesn't want to answer, "I'm from (this city)" is a fine answer.
- **Age is optional:** some adults don't like to say their age. In Box 4 and in the listening, practice with children's ages and invented ages. Say: "You can say a practice age."
- **The big mistake this week:** Spanish can leave out the person (*Es de México*). English can't. Listen for *Is from Mexico* and give back *She's from Mexico.* The second big mistake is *I have 35 years* → *I'm 35.* Learners can read the Spanish note on their Grammar page; you don't need to explain it.
- ***the* United States:** say *the United States* (or *the U.S.*). Other countries on the page have no *the*: *Mexico, Honduras*.
- **Short answers:** *Yes, she is.* (never *Yes, she's.*) • *No, she isn't.* or *No, she's not.* Both are correct; the page teaches *isn't*.
- **Mixed levels:** strong learners add the city (*I'm from Mexico. I'm from Puebla.*) and ask a second question in Talk Time. New learners use the grammar box as a script.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they take part fully in the speaking. In writing tasks, they point and circle, and copy the country from the New Words page. Don't make them read aloud.
- **Looking ahead:** Lesson 3 adds *his / her* (*Her name is Rosa. His phone number is…*) and numbers to 100. Lesson 4 adds *Do you have kids?*

### Questions and answers

**Q: *am*, *is*, or *are*?**

A: *I* → **am**. *he, she, it* → **is**. *you, we, they* → **are**.

**Q: Can I say *Is from Mexico*?**

A: No. English always needs the person: *She's from Mexico.* *He's from Honduras.*

**Q: How do I say my age?**

A: *I'm 35.* or *I'm 35 years old.* Not *I have 35 years.*

**Q: How do I make a question?**

A: Put *is* or *are* first. *She is from Peru.* → ***Is** she from Peru?*

**Q: How do I answer *Yes* or *No*?**

A: *Yes, she **is**.* *No, she **isn't**.* (Not *Yes, she's.*)

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| Is from Mexico. | **She's** from Mexico. |
| I have 35 years. | **I'm** 35. |
| Where you are from? | Where **are you** from? |
| She are from Peru. | She **is** from Peru. |
| Yes, she's. | Yes, she **is**. |
| I'm from mexico. | I'm from **M**exico. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En inglés siempre se dice la persona: *She's from Mexico* (no «Is from Mexico»). La edad usa *be*, no *have*: *I'm 35* (no «I have 35 years»). *He* = él, *she* = ella, *they* = ellos / ellas. Para preguntar, pon *is* o *are* primero: *Is she from Mexico?* Los países y las ciudades empiezan con mayúscula: *Mexico*, *Colombia*.

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** English always needs a person word: *I, you, he, she, we, they*. Age uses *be*: *I'm 35.* Short answers repeat the person and *be*: *Yes, I am. No, she isn't.*
