## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. The student pages have a Spanish line and a "My language" line for every task. Learners read those on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Listen or write?"), **then go**. Say less, show more. Use the same short words every time: *Listen. Repeat. Point. Circle. Write. Partner. Group.*

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the instruction and the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Bring a clock you can move.** A paper plate with two paper hands and a pin works well. (Any clock with hands works; a phone shows only numbers.) Teach **:00 and :30 first**, then :15 and :45.

## Lesson 6 (Week 6): What Time Is It?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask and tell the time: *What time is it? It's six o'clock / six thirty / six fifteen / six forty-five.*
- say the days of the week, and the time and day of class: *It's at 6:00. It's on Monday.*
- use ***at*** (a time), ***on*** (a day), ***in*** (*the morning / the afternoon / the evening*)
- understand store hours: *Is it open on Sunday? No, it isn't. It's closed.* • *It's open from 9:00 to 5:00.*
- say when they work: *When do you work? On Monday and Tuesday, in the morning.*
- read and write times and short day names; read an OPEN sign (Letters & Numbers block)
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Hello + Review · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. Late arrivals join any pair.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **What do you do? Where do you work? Do you work at night?** and the 3 "everyone" answers: **I'm a student. I'm a homemaker. I don't work now.**
- **Model with one learner:** "What do you do?" → "I'm a cashier." "Where do you work?" → "I work at a store."
- **Do:** pairs use the **Hello + Review** page. Switch. Then find a new partner.
- **Homework check (last 3 min):** partners show each other their Lesson 5 homework. Walk around and look at item 2 (*a / an*).
- **New learners:** pair them with a strong partner. They can do the Lesson 1–5 Progress Checks at home.
- **If behind:** one partner only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:20 · New Words · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show the class clock:** set it to 9:00. "**What time is it?** It's **nine o'clock**." Move to 9:30: "It's **nine thirty**." Write both on the board: **9:00 = nine o'clock**, **9:30 = nine thirty = half past nine**.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat twice. For *morning / afternoon / evening*, point to the sun moving (low, high, gone) with your arm.
- **Days:** say Monday–Sunday. Learners repeat. Then: "**Week** = 7 days. **Weekend** = Saturday and Sunday."
- **Pairs (4 min):** A sets a time on the class clock (or draws hands on paper), B says it. Then A points to a picture, B says the word.
- **If behind:** teach :00 and :30 only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:20–6:32 · Listening · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "Kim calls a **laundromat**." Mime washing clothes. "She hears a **message**. Then she calls a **school**." Hold up your phone.
- **Listen 1 → Task A:** learners circle the times.
- **Listen 2 → Task B:** learners check the days.
- **Listen 3 → Task C:** learners write the times and the word.
- **Pairs compare** after each task: "Show your partner." Then write the answers on the board.
- **The audio plays the message and the call once.** Press play again for the next listen.
- **If behind:** play the audio 2 times, and do Task C together.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.** Point left, then right.
- **Box 1:** move the class clock: 6:00, 6:30, 6:15, 6:45. The class answers "It's…" each time.
- **Boxes 2 and 4 (the key point):** draw 3 boxes on the board: **at** 6:00 (a clock), **on** Monday (a calendar), **in** the morning (a sun). Say: "**at** + time. **on** + day. **in** + the morning." Exception to say once: **at night**.
- **Box 3:** "When do you work?" → "On Monday and Tuesday." "In the morning." Learners who don't work: "When is English class?" → "On Monday, at 6:00."
- **Box 5:** point to a store picture: "Is the store **open** on Sunday?" → "No, it isn't. It's **closed**." "What time is it open?" → "From 9:00 to 5:00."
- **Check questions:** "Class is ___ 6:00." (*at*) • "Class is ___ Monday." (*on*) • "I work ___ the morning." (*in*)
- **Pair drill (last 3–4 min):** A asks from the left side; B answers from the right side. Then switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. It becomes homework, and Talk Time practices the same questions.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Put the Student A and Student B pages in two piles. Write on the board: **Is the ___ open on ___? What time is it open? From ___ to ___.**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:10 · Talk Time: Schedule Information Gap · 10 min
- **Grouping:** A–B pairs. Give half the class the **Student A** page and half the **Student B** page. Partners **don't show** their pages.
- **Model with one learner (you are A):** "Is the **library** open on Monday?" → "No, it isn't." "Is it open on Tuesday?" → "Yes, it is." "What time is it open?" → "From 10:00 to 6:00." Write it in the empty table on the board.
- **Part 1 (6 min):** A asks about B's 3 places and writes the days and times. Then B asks about A's places.
- **Check (1 min):** partners show their pages and compare.
- **Part 2 (3 min):** new question: "**When do you work?**" (or "When do you study? When is English class?"). Each learner says one sentence: "I work on Monday and Friday, in the morning." / "I don't work now. English class is on ___, at 6:00."
- **If behind:** skip Part 2.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:10–7:15 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** set the class clock to a time. Each learner **says** the time ("It's seven fifteen.") **or writes** one sentence: *Class is on ___, at ___.* Copying is fine.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (the first line). Point to each instruction so learners know what to do at home.
:::

:::step Letters & Numbers · 7:15–7:25 · Times, Days, Short Forms, a Store Sign · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Use the Letters & Numbers page.
- **See it (2 min):** Task A: point to each clock and the time under it. Say: *six o'clock, six fifteen, six thirty, six forty-five.* Learners repeat. Show the **:** (two dots) between the hour and the minutes.
- **Write it (3 min):** Task B: say the 4 times in the answer key, 2 times each. Learners write them with numbers (7:30).
- **Days (2 min):** Task C: days start with a **capital letter**. The short form is 3 letters + a period: *Mon.* Learners write the short forms.
- **Read it (2 min):** Task D: read the sign together. Say: "**AM** = morning. **PM** = afternoon and night." Learners circle *Yes / No* and write the time.
- **Partners:** A says a time, B writes it. Switch.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they say the times with everyone and point to the clocks. In Task C they copy *Mon.* and *Sat.* only. A helper, if you have one, sits with them.
- **If behind:** do Tasks A and B only.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 5 times (answer key), slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle.
- **Parts B and C:** learners match and write on their own. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "A little." 😐 "Not yet." Learners check one.
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 7). Part C shows *at / on / in*.
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 7 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Teacher notes

- **The big mistake this week: *They are six.*** Spanish says *Son las seis*. In English: ***It's*** *six o'clock* (never *They are*). Correct by tapping the clock: "**It's** six."
- ***at / on / in*:** Spanish uses *a las 6* and *el lunes*. In English: ***at*** *6:00*, ***on*** *Monday* (not *the Monday*), ***in*** *the morning*, but ***at night***. Learners can read the Spanish note on their Grammar page.
- **Times:** teach :00 and :30 first. :15 and :45 are said as numbers (*six fifteen, six forty-five*). *Quarter past / quarter to* is Level 2; accept it if a learner knows it.
- ***-teen / -ty* again:** *7:15* and *7:50* sound alike. Say "fif-**TEEN**" and "**FIF**-ty" with a clap, as in Lesson 3.
- **AM / PM:** say once: AM = morning, PM = afternoon and night. Signs use them; learners don't need to say them.
- **Days:** days always start with a capital letter: *Monday*. Short forms: *Mon., Tue., Wed., Thu., Fri., Sat., Sun.*
- **Work schedules:** don't ask about real hours if a learner doesn't offer them. *English class is on Monday at 6:00* is always a good answer.
- **Mixed levels:** strong learners add *from… to…* (*I work from 8:00 to 4:00*). New learners use only *It's ___.* with the clock.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they set the class clock and say times. In Talk Time, they ask the questions while a partner writes, or they point to the days.
- **Looking ahead:** Lesson 7 (money and clothes) uses numbers again for prices (*It's $12*) and *this / these*.

### Questions and answers

**Q: *It's six* or *They are six*?**

A: Always ***It's***: *It's six o'clock.*

**Q: How do I say 6:15 and 6:45?**

A: Say the numbers: *six fifteen*, *six forty-five*.

**Q: *on Monday* or *the Monday*?**

A: ***on Monday***. No *the*.

**Q: *in the night*?**

A: Say ***at night***. But: *in the morning*, *in the afternoon*, *in the evening*.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| They are six. | **It's** six. |
| Class is in 6:00. | Class is **at** 6:00. |
| I work the Monday. | I work **on** Monday. |
| I work in the night. | I work **at** night. |
| monday | **M**onday (capital letter) |
| The store is close. | The store is **closed**. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En inglés la hora usa *It's*: *It's six o'clock* (no «They are six»). *a las 6* = ***at*** *6:00*. *el lunes* = ***on*** *Monday* (sin *the*). *en la mañana* = ***in*** *the morning*, pero *en la noche* = ***at*** *night*. Los días de la semana empiezan con mayúscula: *Monday*.

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** *at* + a time (*at 6:00*), *on* + a day (*on Monday*), *in* + *the morning / the afternoon / the evening*, and *at night*. Days start with a capital letter.
