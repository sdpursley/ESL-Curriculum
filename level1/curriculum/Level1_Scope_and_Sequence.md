## 1. Rationale and Research Basis

### 1.1 Why a Level 1, and who it is for

Level 2 starts by **reviewing** *be* and *do* questions, and it expects learners to already know numbers, telling time, family and job words, *there is / there are*, and basic place words (Section 3 has the full list). Learners who arrive with little or no English cannot keep up with that. Level 1 gives them those skills in one semester, so they can start Level 2 Lesson 1 ready.

**Target learner:** an adult who is new to English (a "true beginner") or knows only a few words (a "low beginner"). About 95% speak Spanish. Most can read and write in Spanish. Some have limited schooling and find reading and writing hard in any language (see Section 4.2).

### 1.2 Level estimates (our estimate, not an official alignment)

| Framework | Level 1 (this course) | Level 2 (existing) |
|---|---|---|
| **NRS** Educational Functioning Levels for ESL (U.S. Department of Education, adult education reporting) | Starts at **Beginning ESL Literacy** / **Low Beginning ESL**; aims to finish at **Low Beginning ESL**, moving toward High Beginning | Roughly **High Beginning ESL** |
| **CEFR** (Council of Europe; the 2020 *Companion Volume* adds Pre-A1 descriptors) | **Pre-A1 → A1** | Roughly **A1+ → A2** (the Level 2 proposal's own estimate) |
| **CASAS** | Uses the same life-skill content areas (see 1.3). CASAS tests can report learners on the NRS levels above. We **do not** claim any score alignment | Same |

What these levels describe, in plain words:

- **NRS Beginning ESL Literacy:** the learner understands only isolated English words or phrases and may have little or no reading and writing in any language.
- **NRS Low Beginning ESL:** the learner understands basic greetings, simple phrases and commands, and simple personal-information questions spoken slowly and with repetition. They can read numbers, letters, and some common sight words, write basic personal information (name, address, telephone number), and complete simple forms.
- **CEFR A1:** the learner can introduce themselves and others and ask and answer simple questions about personal details (where they live, people they know, things they have), if the other person talks slowly and clearly and is ready to help. **Pre-A1** is the stage before that: single words and memorized phrases for very familiar, concrete needs.

**The Level 1 exit goal is the NRS Low Beginning description and CEFR A1, applied to everyday survival situations.** The Level 1 can-do lists (Section 2) are written to match.

> **Note on test scores:** CASAS publishes score ranges for each NRS level, and the ranges differ by test series and year. If the program needs scores for reporting, use the current official CASAS/NRS benchmark table, not this document.

### 1.3 Content areas (CASAS-style life skills)

The CASAS Competencies organize adult life-skill content into areas such as **basic communication, consumer economics (money and shopping), community resources, health, employment, and learning skills**. Level 1 lessons map onto them:

| Content area | Level 1 lessons |
|---|---|
| Basic communication (greetings, personal information, spelling, numbers, time, dates) | 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9 |
| Consumer economics (money, prices, shopping, food) | 7, 8 |
| Community resources (home, places, directions) | 10, 11 |
| Health (body, feeling sick, getting help, emergency phone call) | 12 |
| Employment (jobs, work schedules, job skills, a simple job interview) | 5, 6, 13 |
| Learning skills (classroom language, asking for help) | 1, and every week |

### 1.4 Teaching principles (and where they come from)

| Principle | What it means in Level 1 | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival language first** | Lessons follow the order of real needs: name → personal information → family → work → time → money. | NRS and CEFR beginner descriptors (personal information, immediate needs) |
| **Chunks before rules** | Learners first learn whole phrases (*What's your name? How do you spell it? Can you repeat, please?*). The grammar boxes then show how the phrase works. | Research on formulaic language (for example, Alison Wray, *Formulaic Language and the Lexicon*, 2002) |
| **Pictures with every word** | Every vocabulary word has a picture. Learners see, hear, and say it. | Dual coding (Allan Paivio): words plus pictures are remembered better than words alone |
| **Many meetings with each word** | Each word appears in New Words, the listening, the grammar box, Talk Time, the homework, and again in later lessons. | Vocabulary research (Paul Nation, *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language*): learners need many repeated encounters |
| **Listening before speaking; understandable input** | Short, slow, picture-supported audio. Learners point, circle, and check before they have to speak. | Comprehensible input (Stephen Krashen); Total Physical Response (James Asher) for commands and directions |
| **Explicit literacy for learners who need it** | Letters, sounds, numbers, sight words, and forms are taught directly in a weekly strand. | Research on adult learners with limited first-language literacy (the LESLLA network; Bigelow and Vinogradov, 2011, *Annual Review of Applied Linguistics*) |
| **Lots of pair and group talk** | Every lesson has at least three pair or group tasks. Target: learners talk for more than half of the class time. | Same principle as Level 2 |

## 2. Entry and Exit Skills

### 2.1 Entry (who should start in Level 1)

A learner belongs in Level 1 if, in a short placement chat, they **cannot yet** do most of these in English:

- answer *What's your name? Where are you from? What's your phone number?* without help
- spell their name aloud
- say a phone number or a price
- ask a simple question (*Where do you live? Do you have kids?*)

**No English is required to start.** Learners who can do all four comfortably probably belong in Level 2.

**Reading and writing:** the course assumes most learners can read and write in Spanish. A learner who cannot is still welcome. The weekly **Letters & Numbers** strand and a take-home practice packet support them (Section 4.2). See Open Question 2.

### 2.2 Exit (what learners can do at the end = what Level 2 expects)

**Talking and listening:** learners can…

- greet people, introduce themselves, and say goodbye
- give and ask for personal information: name, spelling, country, age, phone number, address, birthday
- ask for help in class: *Can you repeat, please? How do you say ___ in English? I don't understand.*
- talk about their family and their job in short sentences
- ask and tell the time, the day, and the date; understand opening hours and an appointment time
- ask the price of something and understand the answer; pay and count change
- order simple food and say what they like and don't like
- say what is in their home, and ask *Is there a ___?*
- ask for and follow simple directions to a place in town
- say that they are sick or hurt, say where it hurts (point + *here*), and give an address in an emergency phone call
- say what they can and can't do at work (*I can drive. I can't use a computer.*)
- give a short (30–60 second) talk about themselves

**Reading and writing:** learners can…

- read and write the alphabet (capital and small letters) and numbers to 100, plus prices and times
- write their name, address, phone number, and date of birth on a simple form
- read common signs (OPEN / CLOSED, EXIT, PUSH / PULL, store hours, prices)
- copy and write short sentences from the grammar boxes

**Grammar they control (in short, practiced sentences):** *be* in statements, questions, and short answers (all persons); simple present with *I / you / we / they* (*I work… Do you…? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.*); *have*; *a / an*; plurals; *my / your / his / her / our / their*; *'s* for possession; *this / that / these / those*; *there is / there are*; *can / can't*; commands (*Turn left. Call 911.*); *at / on / in* for time; *next to / across from / between*; question words *what, where, who, when, how, how old, how much, how many, what time*.

**Deliberately NOT in Level 1 (Level 2 teaches these):** *he / she* + *-s* (simple present third person), frequency words, *like* + *-ing*, the present continuous, all past tenses, comparatives and superlatives, count/noncount with *some / any*, *I'd like*, *should*, *be going to*, *have to / need to*. Level 1 learners may **hear** a few of these as fixed phrases (for example, *It hurts*), but they are not taught or tested.

## 3. Handoff to Level 2

Level 2 Lesson 1 is a **review** of *be* and *do* questions, so learners must arrive knowing both. The table lists what Level 2 assumes and where Level 1 teaches it.

| Level 2 assumes learners know… | Where Level 2 uses it | Level 1 teaches it in |
|---|---|---|
| *be* statements, questions, short answers (*Are you married? Yes, I am.*); age with *be* (*I'm 35*) | Lesson 1 (review), all lessons | Lessons 1, 2, 4 (and every lesson after) |
| Simple present with *I / you*: *Do you live / work / have…? Yes, I do.* | Lesson 1 (review), Lesson 2 (adds *he/she* + *-s*) | Lessons 4, 5, 8 |
| Countries, *from, live, work, job*; jobs with *a / an* (*I'm a cook*) | Lesson 1 | Lessons 2, 5 |
| Family words (*brother, sister, kids, married*) | Lesson 1 | Lesson 4 |
| Numbers; clock times (*It's five forty-five*); days; *at 7:00, on weekends* | Lessons 2 and 4 (time is only reviewed there) | Lessons 3, 6 |
| *like* + noun (*I like coffee*) | Lesson 1 opening, Lesson 3 (adds *-ing*) | Lesson 8 |
| Prices and money | Lessons 7, 9 | Lesson 7 |
| Dates, months, *tomorrow, next week*, appointments | Lessons 10, 11, 12 | Lesson 9 |
| *there is / there are* (Level 2 calls it "review") | Lesson 8, midterm listening | Lesson 10 |
| Places in town; *next to / across from*; simple directions | Lesson 8 (places); Lesson 7 "If time" directions | Lesson 11 |
| Body parts; *I'm sick*; *doctor* | Lesson 10 (symptoms, *My ___ hurts*, *should*) | Lesson 12 |
| *can / can't* for ability (Level 2 dropped it "probably to Level 1") | Lessons 11, 12 (*Sorry, I can't*; *Can you…?*) | Lesson 13 |
| Spelling aloud, *Could you repeat that?*, simple forms | Lesson 12 | Lessons 1, 3 |
| Same class routine, same workbook pages, same exam format | Every week | Every week (Level 1 uses the same routine with small changes; Section 4.1) |

**Planned overlap (on purpose):** a few words appear in both levels, for example *bank, pharmacy, park, restaurant* (Level 1 Lesson 11 and Level 2 Lesson 8), *from, live, work* (Lesson 1 in both), and *receipt* (Level 1 Lesson 7 and Level 2 Lesson 12). In Level 1 they are new words; in Level 2 they are a quick review with new grammar. Otherwise, Level 1 vocabulary is **chosen not to repeat** the Level 2 picture pages (for example, the Level 1 food words are different from Level 2 Lesson 9).

**Topics Level 2 left open for Level 1:** Level 2 says "describing people" may belong in Level 1 or Level 3. This plan does **not** include it (13 lessons are full). See Open Question 9.

## 4. Course Design

### 4.1 Weekly routine (same shape as Level 2, adjusted for beginners)

Teachers and learners who move up to Level 2 will find the same routine. Level 1 changes three things: **more time for New Words**, a **slower listening step**, and a standing **Letters & Numbers** block.

| Clock time | Step | About | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6:00–6:10 | 1. **Hello + Review** | 10 min | Pairs ask and answer last week's questions from a card (late arrivals join a pair). Then partners compare homework. |
| 6:10–6:20 | 2. **New Words** | 10 min | Picture page: point, say, repeat. Then a pair picture game (*A points, B says*). |
| 6:20–6:32 | 3. **Listening + "Say it"** | 12 min | Audio plays 2–3 times. Picture tasks first (point, check, circle), then a number or word to write. Repeat 2 lines. |
| 6:32–6:45 | 4. **Grammar Q&A** | 13 min | The two-sided question/answer box. Teacher reads a question, class reads the answer; then pairs drill across the box. |
| 6:45–7:00 | **BREAK** | 15 min | |
| 7:00–7:10 | 5. **Talk Time** | 10 min | The main pair or group activity of the night. |
| 7:10–7:15 | 6. **Exit Ticket + Homework** | 5 min | **Never skip.** Beginner version: say one sentence to the teacher **or** write one sentence (copying from the box is OK). |
| 7:15–7:30 | 7. **Letters & Numbers** (Weeks 1–7) / **Real-World Reading** (Weeks 9–14) | 15 min | **Strongly recommended for Level 1** (not just "if time"). Alphabet, sounds, numbers, sight words, forms, signs. See 4.2. |

> **Timing is loose on purpose.** If New Words or the listening runs long, take time from Grammar or Talk Time. If you're running behind, skip in this order: (1) the third play of the audio; (2) the pair drill at the end of Grammar (it becomes homework); (3) half of Talk Time. **Never skip** the listening tasks, the grammar box, and the exit ticket.

> **Attendance (15 → 7–8):** use the Level 2 table (pairs, groups, circles, mingles). Every Level 1 activity lists a 15-learner and a 7–8-learner version.

### 4.2 The literacy decision: no separate "Unit 0"; survival basics built into Weeks 1–3, plus a weekly strand

**Options considered:**

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A. A separate "Unit 0" (1–2 extra weeks of alphabet and numbers before Lesson 1) | Gives literacy a clear start | Breaks the 16-week structure; learners with Spanish literacy would be bored; learners who join in Week 3 miss it |
| B. **Survival basics as Lessons 1–3, plus a weekly Letters & Numbers strand (recommended)** | Keeps the same 16 weeks as Level 2; the basics are real conversation lessons; literacy practice continues all semester, so late joiners still get it | Takes 15 minutes from the "If time" block |
| C. A separate literacy class for learners who can't read in any language | Best for those learners | Needs another teacher and room (Open Question 2) |

**Recommendation: Option B**, and Option C if the program can staff it.

- **Weeks 1–3 are survival basics:** the alphabet and spelling names (Lesson 1), numbers 0–20 and ages (Lesson 2), and numbers to 100, phone numbers, addresses, and a simple form (Lesson 3). These are full conversation lessons, not drills.
- **Letters & Numbers (7:15–7:30, Weeks 1–7):** a short, repeatable routine with picture cards and mini whiteboards: letter names, capital and small letters, the sounds Spanish speakers often confuse (letter names A / E / I; G / J; H; Y; W; V / B; *sh* / *ch*; final consonants), numbers and *-teen / -ty* (13 / 30), and copying a sentence from the grammar box.
- **Real-World Reading (7:15–7:30, Weeks 9–14):** reading real-looking print with that lesson's language: a menu (Lesson 8), a calendar and appointment card (Lesson 9), an apartment ad (Lesson 10), a street map and signs (Lesson 11), a medicine label and an emergency card (Lesson 12), a job application (Lesson 13).
- **Learners who can't read in any language:** they take part fully in the speaking work (most of the class is oral). In the reading and writing steps they get a simpler worksheet (trace and copy, circle the picture). A take-home **Letters & Numbers practice packet** has the alphabet, numbers, and their own personal-information card to copy. A volunteer helper, if available, sits with them during the 7:15 block.
- **Teacher-friendly:** every Letters & Numbers block uses the same 3-step pattern (**See it → Say it → Write it**), with a script in plain English. No Spanish needed.
- **Privacy:** learners practice forms with an invented **"practice ID card"** (a fictional name, address, and phone number on the card). They can use their own information if they want. Prompts never ask about immigration status, money, or exact real addresses.

### 4.3 Grammar format: two-sided question-and-answer boxes

Same format as Level 2: the question side on the left, the answer side on the right, one word from each column. Each box has a short "Questions and answers" section in plain English, a "Common mistakes" table (✗ → ✓), and a Spanish note on the student page only. Example (Lesson 2):

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Where **are** | you / they | from? | ‖ | I'm / They're | from | Mexico. / Honduras. |
| Where **is** | he / she | from? | ‖ | He's / She's | from | Guatemala. / Colombia. |
| **Are** | you | from Peru? | ‖ | Yes, | I | **am**. |
| ^ | ^ | ^ | ‖ | No, | I'm | **not**. |
| **Is** | he / she | from Peru? | ‖ | Yes, | he / she | **is**. |
| ^ | ^ | ^ | ‖ | No, | he / she | **isn't**. |

### 4.4 Materials per lesson (same as Level 2)

| Item | Level 1 detail |
|---|---|
| **Student Workbook pages** | New Words, Listening, Grammar, Homework (4 pages), plus a Letters & Numbers / Real-World Reading page. Larger font and more white space than Level 2. Task instructions are plain English words, with no icons; each has a Spanish line and a "My language" line under it. Spanish notes are on student pages only. |
| **Pictures** | 10–12 per lesson (≈ 150 total), 600×420 PNG cards made from Twemoji graphics (CC BY 4.0), same style and naming as Level 2 (`L1_name.png`). A few words have no good emoji (for example, *refrigerator*, *stove*, *back*, *last name*). Those get a labeled drawing combining emoji shapes, or a simple form/ID-card picture with the field highlighted. Every picture gets a line in `CREDITS.md`. |
| **Listening audio** | One MP3 per lesson + midterm + final (15 files), same voice cast and tools as Level 2. **Slower and shorter:** about 0:40–1:00, 2 speakers, short lines, clear pauses. Each file plays once; teachers press play again. |
| **Slides** | One deck per lesson, same look as Level 2: the picture words, the listening tasks, the grammar box with click-to-reveal answers, and Talk Time instructions. |
| **Teacher plan** | At a glance, clock-time plan, step-by-step boxes, script, answer key, and teacher notes in plain English. Every step says exactly what to say and what to show. **Teachers need no Spanish.** |

### 4.5 Assessment and progress

| Tool | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Placement chat** | Before Week 1 or on Week 1 night | 3 minutes: the four entry questions (Section 2.1) + "Can you read this?" (one short sentence in Spanish, one in English). Decides Level 1 vs. Level 2 and flags who needs literacy support. |
| **Baseline snapshot** | Weeks 1–3 | The same 6 prompts every time: *What's your name? How do you spell it? Where are you from? What's your phone number? Do you have kids? What do you do?* About 5 learners per week during Talk Time. The final uses the same prompts to show growth. |
| **Can-do self-check** | Weeks 1, 8, 15 | Pictures + "I can…" + 😀 🙂 😐. The teacher reads each line aloud. |
| **Exit ticket** | Every lesson | One sentence, spoken or written (copying is fine). |
| **Homework** | Every lesson | 10 minutes; mostly picture-matching, copying, and "ask a family member" speaking tasks. |
| **Midterm / Final** | Weeks 8, 15 | Listening (picture-based), grammar (circle the answer), a short form to fill out, and a pair speaking interview. The same 1–3 rubric as Level 2. Section 7. |

| 13 | **Lesson 12 (Week 13):** I Don't Feel Well | *How do you feel? I'm / I feel sick.*; *Are you OK? No, I'm not.*; *Where does it hurt? Here.* (fixed phrase); *Call 911.* | Say you are sick or hurt; get help |
| 14 | **Lesson 13 (Week 14):** Yes, I Can! | *Can you drive? Yes, I can. / No, I can't.*; *I can… / I can't…* | Job skills; a simple job interview; prepare the graduation talk |
| 15 | **Week 15: Final Exam** | Review of all lessons (focus on 8–13) | Review stations + exam |
| 16 | **Week 16: Graduation** | — | 30–60 second "All About Me" talks; certificates |

**Grammar in teaching order:** 1. *What's your name? / My name is* + spelling (L1) → 2. *be*: all persons, questions, short answers, age (L2) → 3. *my / your / his / her / our / their*; numbers (L3) → 4. *this is*; *Who…?*; *have* with *I / you*; *'s* (L4) → 5. *a / an*; simple present with *I / you / we / they*; *don't* (L5) → 6. time; *at / on / in* for time (L6) → 7. *this / that / these / those*; plurals; color + noun (L7) → **midterm** → 8. *like / don't like*; *want / need* (L8) → 9. dates and ordinals; *When…?*; weather with *It's* (L9) → 10. *there is / there are* (L10) → 11. prepositions of place; commands (L11) → 12. *be* + feelings (review); commands for help (L12) → 13. *can / can't* (L13) → **final**.

**Spiral recycling:** every "Hello + Review" step reuses the questions from the week before, and every homework mixes in one older point. *be* questions come back in nearly every lesson. Learners leave Level 1 having practiced *Are you…?* and *Do you…?* many times, which is exactly what Level 2 Lesson 1 reviews.

## 6. Week by Week

*Vocabulary: ★ = most important words. Each word gets a picture card. "Talk Time" is the main pair or group activity (7:00–7:10); each lesson also has pair work in New Words, Listening, and Grammar.*

### Lesson 1 (Week 1): Hello! What's Your Name?

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Theme** | First night: greetings, names, the alphabet, classroom language. Includes orientation ("How Our Class Works") and the first can-do self-check. |
| **Can-do goals** | I can say hello and goodbye. • I can say my first and last name. • I can spell my name. • I can ask *Can you repeat, please?* |
| **Grammar Q&A focus** | *What's your name? → My name is Ana. / I'm Ana.* • *What's your last name? → It's López.* • *How do you spell it? → L-O-P-E-Z.* • *How are you? → I'm fine, thanks. And you?* (Fixed phrases; *I'm / you're* only.) |
| **Vocabulary (12)** | ★ hello • ★ goodbye • ★ name • ★ first name • ★ last name • ★ spell • letter • ★ teacher • ★ student • classmate • book • pen |
| **Classroom phrases (on a poster, not pictures)** | Listen. • Repeat. • Write. • Can you repeat, please? • I don't understand. • How do you say ___ in English? |
| **Talk Time** | **Name Mingle with Sign-In Sheet:** learners greet 5–6 classmates (everyone, with 7–8), ask *What's your name? How do you spell it?*, and write the name on a sign-in sheet. Partner checks the spelling. |
| **Listening** | First night at class: a receptionist signs in Ana and Carlos. They say and spell their names. **Tasks:** point to the person; circle the letters you hear; write one last name. |
| **Letters & Numbers** | The alphabet song/chant; capital and small letters; letter names Spanish speakers mix up (A / E / I, G / J, H, Y, W); spell your name on a mini whiteboard. |
| **Spanish note (student page)** | The English names of the letters A, E, I, G, J, H differ from Spanish; *What's* = *What is*. |
| **Teacher tip** | Write each name on the board as learners spell it; it shows instantly whether the letter names are clear. Use invented names if anyone prefers. |

### Lesson 2 (Week 2): Where Are You From?

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Theme** | Countries, cities, and age; meeting classmates. |
| **Can-do goals** | I can say where I'm from. • I can ask where someone is from. • I can say my age. • I can understand numbers 0–20. |
| **Grammar Q&A focus** | *be* with *I / you / he / she / we / they* (statements, *not*, contractions). *Where are you from? → I'm from Mexico.* • *Where is he from? → He's from Honduras.* • *Is she from Peru? → Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.* • *How old are you? → I'm 35.* (not *I have 35 years*) |
| **Vocabulary (12)** | ★ country • ★ city • ★ Mexico • ★ Guatemala • ★ Honduras • ★ El Salvador • Colombia • ★ the United States • ★ he • ★ she • ★ they • ★ How old? *(flags for the countries; adjust the list to the class — Open Question 10)* |
| **Talk Time** | **Class Map:** pairs interview each other (*Where are you from? What city?*), then introduce the partner to a group: *This is Rosa. She's from Guatemala. She's from Quetzaltenango.* Each learner puts a sticker on a world map. |
| **Listening** | Two new classmates talk at the break: where they are from, their cities, and the age of a child. **Tasks:** match person → flag; true/false with pictures; write the age. |
| **Letters & Numbers** | Numbers 0–20: say, point, write; dictation of ages; capital letters for countries and names. |
| **Spanish note** | English always needs the person: *She's from Peru* (not *Is from Peru*); age uses *be*, not *have*. |
| **Teacher tip** | Learners can choose any country or city to talk about. Never ask about immigration or how someone came here. |

### Lesson 3 (Week 3): My Phone Number and Address

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Theme** | Personal information and forms: phone numbers, addresses, email; survival literacy. |

## 7. Midterm, Final, and Graduation

Same evening structure as Level 2 (Opening → review stations with speaking interviews → listening test before the break → grammar test after the break → can-do self-check), with beginner adjustments: **pictures on every test item**, the teacher **reads every item aloud**, answers are **circle / check / write one number or word**, and a short **form** to fill out.

| | **Week 8: Midterm** | **Week 15: Final** |
|---|---|---|
| **Covers** | Lessons 1–7: greetings and spelling, *be* (all persons), countries and age, phone numbers and addresses, *my / your / his / her*, family and *Do you have…?*, jobs with *a / an* and *Do you work…?*, time and days with *at / on*, prices and *this / these* | All lessons, focus on 8–13: *Do you like…?*, ordering, dates and appointments, weather, *there is / are*, places and directions, body and getting help, *can / can't* |
| **Review stations (6:10–6:30)** | ① Spelling and Numbers Bingo, ② Listening Replay (Lessons 5–7 audio), ③ Question Cards (one per lesson), ④ Store role play (prices) | ① Grammar Auction (correct / wrong sentences), ② Listening Replay (Lessons 11–13), ③ Question Cards, ④ Talk Rehearsal (graduation talk) |
| **Listening test (6:30–6:45)** | 8 items: a phone/front-desk conversation (name, spelling, phone number, job, a time, a price). Circle the picture or number; write one number. Audio plays 2–3 times. | 8 items: a conversation that recycles food, a date and time, a place on a map, and *can / can't*. Same task types. |
| **Grammar test (7:00–7:15)** | 12 items, circle the answer (*Are / Is*; *a / an*; *his / her*; *at / on*; *this / these*; *do / don't*) | 15 items, circle the answer (tests *like / don't like*, *in / on / at*, *there is / are*, prepositions of place, commands, *can / can't*) |
| **Form task** | Fill out a 6-line form for a practice ID card (name, phone, address, city, ZIP, signature) | Fill out a short job application or appointment form (name, phone, date of birth, days available) |
| **Speaking interview (3 min per pair)** | Part 1: 2 baseline questions each (name + spelling, phone number, family, job). Part 2: pair task card, *How much is this?* shop role play | Part 1: the same baseline prompts as Weeks 1–3, to show growth. Part 2: directions on a map or a *Can you…?* mini job interview |
| **Rubric** | Level 2's 1–3 rubric (Communication / Target grammar / Pronunciation and fluency) | Same |
| **Self-check** | Can-do self-check + one goal | Can-do self-check; "Ready for Level 2" decision |

**Proposed "Ready for Level 2" standard (confirm):** attend at least 9 of the 13 lessons, complete the final, score 2 or higher in all rubric areas on the final speaking interview, and complete the form task with no more than 1–2 errors. Learners who are not ready still graduate and are encouraged to repeat Level 1 (or join Level 2 with a study buddy).

**Week 16: Graduation.** No new content. Each learner gives a **30–60 second "All About Me" talk** prepared in Lesson 13 (reading from paper is fine; 3 sentences is fine). Certificates of completion. Optional potluck: ask about allergies, and don't require anyone to bring food. Hand out a "Keep practicing" sheet with the website and QR codes for all audio.

## 8. Open Questions

1. **Placement:** how are learners placed today? Is a 3-minute placement chat (Section 4.5) OK, or does the program use a test (for example, CASAS or BEST Plus) and need NRS reporting?
2. **Learners who can't read in any language:** can the program offer a separate literacy group or a one-on-one tutor (Section 4.2, Option C), or should they stay in Level 1 with the support packet?
3. **The 7:15–7:30 block:** can Level 1 reliably run until 7:30? The plan makes Letters & Numbers / Real-World Reading a standard block there. If class usually ends at 7:15, it would shrink to a 5-minute routine inside the exit ticket.
4. **Spanish on student pages:** besides the short Spanish notes, may we add a one-line Spanish translation of each task instruction for Weeks 1–4 (for true beginners)? Teacher pages stay English-only.
5. **Practice ID cards:** OK to use invented identities (fictional names, 555-01xx phone numbers, invented addresses) for all form and personal-information practice?
6. **Practice 911 call (Lesson 12):** include it, or replace it with calling a clinic or a supervisor?
7. **Third-person *-s*:** Level 1 uses *he / she* only with *be* (*She's a cook*) and leaves *he/she works* and *Does he…?* to Level 2. Level 2 Lesson 1 already includes *Does he / she…?* in Grammar Box 2. Is that handoff OK, or should Level 1 introduce *Does he / she…?* as a preview in Lesson 5?

11. **Pictures with no good emoji** (*refrigerator, stove, back, stomach, last name, signature*): OK to use labeled drawings that combine emoji shapes, as Level 2 did for some words?
