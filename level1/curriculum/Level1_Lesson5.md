## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. The student pages have a Spanish line and a "My language" line for every task. Learners read those on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Listen or write?"), **then go**. Say less, show more. Use the same short words every time: *Listen. Repeat. Point. Circle. Write. Partner. Group.*

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the instruction and the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Everyone has an answer.** Some learners don't work now, work at home, or are students. Teach these answers on the first day of the topic: ***I'm a student. I'm a homemaker. I don't work now.*** Learners can also say a job they **want** or a job they **had**. Never ask where someone works if they don't offer it, and never ask about pay or work papers.

## Lesson 5 (Week 5): What's Your Job?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- say a job with *a / an* (*I'm a cook. I'm an office worker.*), or *I'm a student / I'm a homemaker / I don't work now*
- ask and answer *What do you do?* and *Where do you work? I work at a restaurant.*
- ask and answer *Do you work at night / on weekends? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.*
- say *I don't work…*
- **understand** (not produce) the preview *Does she work? Yes, she does.*
- choose *a* or *an*, say the *sh* sound, and read two job signs (Letters & Numbers block)
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Hello + Review · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. Late arrivals join any pair.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **Do you have kids? Do you have brothers or sisters? Who's this?**
- **Model with one learner:** "Do you have brothers or sisters?" → "Yes, I do. I have two brothers." Then show the picture-card family from Lesson 4 (or the board tree): "Who's this?" → "This is my mother."
- **Do:** pairs use the **Hello + Review** page. Learners can use their family or Ana's family. Switch. Then find a new partner.
- **Homework check (last 3 min):** partners show each other their Lesson 4 homework. Walk around and look at item 2 (*do / don't*).
- **New learners:** pair them with a strong partner. They can do the Lesson 1–4 Progress Checks at home.
- **If behind:** one partner only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:20 · New Words · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Say first:** "Today: **jobs**. What do you do? You can say: I'm a student. I'm a homemaker. I don't work now." Write these 3 answers on the board and leave them there.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat twice. **Act out** each job (cook: stir a pot; cleaner: sweep; driver: turn a wheel; cashier: scan and count money). Learners copy the action.
- **Places:** *factory, office, restaurant* are places. Say: "A cook works at a **restaurant**."
- **Pairs (4 min):** A acts out a job, B says it. Then switch. Then A points to a picture, B says it.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:20–6:32 · Listening · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "This is Rosa. This is Marco. They are **neighbors**." Point to two houses on the board, side by side. "They talk about their jobs."
- **Listen 1 → Task A:** learners match each person to a job picture (write the letter).
- **Listen 2 → Task B:** learners circle *Yes* or *No*.
- **Listen 3 → Task C:** learners write *do*, *don't*, or *does*.
- **Pairs compare** after each task: "Show your partner." Then write the answers on the board.
- **The preview line:** after Task C, play the end again or read it: "*Does your wife work, too? Yes, she **does**. She's a nurse.*" Say: "**Does** = a question about **he** or **she**. Level 2 practices it. Today: only listen."
- **The audio plays the conversation once.** Press play again for the next listen.
- **If behind:** play the audio 2 times, and do Task C together.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.** Point left, then right.
- **Box 1 (the key point):** "What do you do?" → "I'm **a** cook." Write on the board: **a** cook, **a** driver / **an** office worker. Say: before a, e, i, o, u → **an**. Point to the 3 "everyone" answers.
- **Box 2:** "Where do you work?" → "I work **at** a restaurant." Point to the place pictures.
- **Box 3:** "Do you work at night?" Nod: "Yes, I **do**." Shake: "No, I **don't**." (The same as *Do you have kids?* in Lesson 4.)
- **Box 4:** "I work at night. I **don't** work in the morning." Use the board: ✓ work / ✗ don't work.
- **Preview box (1–2 min, no practice needed):** read it once; the class repeats once. "Level 2. Only listen today."
- **Check questions:** "I'm ___ cook." (*a*) • "I'm ___ office worker." (*an*) • "Do you work on weekends?" (Yes, I do. / No, I don't.)
- **Pair drill (last 3–4 min):** A asks from the left side; B answers from the right side about a picture card (or about themselves). Then switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. It becomes homework, and Talk Time practices the same questions.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Cut or stack the job picture cards (one set per group). Write on the board: **Are you a cook? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.** and **Do you work at night? Do you work on weekends?**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:10 · Talk Time: Job Guessing Game + Find Someone Who · 10 min
- **Part 1, guessing game (4 min):** groups of 3–4. One learner takes a card (don't show it) and acts out the job. The others ask: "**Are you a cook?**" → "**No, I'm not.**" / "**Yes, I am!**" The winner takes the next card. **Model it first:** you act out "driver"; the class guesses.
- **Part 2, Find Someone Who (6 min):** everyone stands with the Talk Time page. Learners ask the questions and write a **name** when someone says *Yes, I do* (or *No, I don't* for the last line). One name per line; a different person for each line if possible.
- **Model Part 2:** "Do you work at night?" → "No, I don't." → ask someone else → "Yes, I do." → write that name.
- **Finish:** 2–3 learners report one line: "**Ana** works at night." (This is a preview of *works*; accept "Ana work at night" too.)
- **If behind:** do only the guessing game.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:10–7:15 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner **says** one sentence about work: "I'm a cook." / "I work at a factory." / "I'm a student." / "I don't work now." **or writes** *a* or *an* for two words on the board (*___ cook, ___ office worker*).
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (the first sentence). Point to each instruction so learners know what to do at home.
:::

:::step Letters & Numbers · 7:15–7:25 · A / An, the Sh Sound, Job Signs · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Use the Letters & Numbers page.
- **See it (2 min):** Task A. Point to the vowel letters **a e i o u**. Read the *a* column, then the *an* column. Learners repeat. *An* goes before a **vowel sound**: *an office, an apartment*.
- **Write it (2 min):** Task B. Learners write *a* or *an*. Check on the board.
- **Say it (2 min):** Task C. Say **sh** like "Quiet!" (finger on lips). Then: *she, shoe, cashier, English, wash*. Learners repeat each word twice. Spanish speakers often say *ch* (as in *chair*): show the difference with a long, soft *shhh*.
- **Read it (3 min):** Task D. Point to each sign and read it. Learners circle *Yes* or *No*. Ask: "Where do you see this sign?" (a store window; a door at work).
- **Partners:** A says a job, B says *a* or *an* + the job. Switch.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they say the words with everyone and point. In Task D, read the signs aloud for them and let them answer by pointing.
- **If behind:** do Tasks A and B only.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 5 words (answer key), slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle.
- **Parts B and C:** learners match and write on their own. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "A little." 😐 "Not yet." Learners check one.
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 6). Part C shows *a / an* and *do / don't*.
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 6 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Listening script: `L1_L5_listening.mp3` (about 1:10; the conversation plays once, so play the audio again if needed)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer.

> **Narrator:** Lesson five. Listening. Rosa and Marco are neighbors. They talk about their jobs. Listen.
> **Rosa:** Good morning, Marco!
> **Marco:** Hi, Rosa. How are you?
> **Rosa:** I'm fine, thanks. Marco, what do you do?
> **Marco:** I'm a cook. I work at a restaurant on Main Street.
> **Rosa:** Do you work at night?
> **Marco:** Yes, I do. I don't work in the morning. And you? What do you do?
> **Rosa:** I'm a cashier. I work at a store.
> **Marco:** Do you work on weekends?
> **Rosa:** Yes, I do. I work on Saturday and Sunday.
> **Marco:** Oh, that's a lot of work!
> **Rosa:** Yes, it is. Does your wife work, too?
> **Marco:** Yes, she does. She's a nurse.
> **Rosa:** Nice. My husband is a mechanic.
> **Marco:** Great! OK, see you later, Rosa.
> **Rosa:** Bye, Marco!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* 1 Rosa **b** (cashier), 2 Marco **a** (cook), 3 Marco's wife **d** (nurse), 4 Rosa's husband **c** (mechanic).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 **Yes**, 2 **Yes**, 3 **No** (he doesn't work in the morning), 4 **No** (at a store), 5 **Yes**.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 **do**, 2 **don't**, 3 **do**, 4 **does** (preview).
- *Grammar practice:* 1 a, 2 an, 3 at, 4 do, 5 don't.
- *Homework 2 (a / an):* 1 a, 2 an, 3 a, 4 an, 5 a.
- *Homework 3 (do / don't):* answers can be *do* or *don't*, if they match the learner's Yes / No.
- *Letters & Numbers Task B:* 1 a, 2 an, 3 a, 4 an, 5 a, 6 an.
- *Letters & Numbers Task D:* 1 **Yes**, 2 **No**, 3 **No**, 4 **Yes**.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says):* 1 **cashier**, 2 **mechanic**, 3 **nurse**, 4 **factory**, 5 **server**. Say each word 2 times.
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 c, 2 a, 3 d, 4 b.
- *Progress Check Part C:* 1 a, 2 an, 3 don't.
- *Progress Check scoring:* Part A /5 + Part B /4 + Part C /3 = /12. This is not a test grade. Part A shows the job words; Part C shows *a / an* and *don't*.

### Teacher notes

- **Respect:** *I'm a student*, *I'm a homemaker* and *I don't work now* are full, correct answers. Learners can also say a job they want ("I want to be a nurse" is too hard for now; accept "Nurse!" with a smile). Never ask about pay, hours in detail, bosses, or work papers.
- **The big mistake this week: *I'm cook.*** Spanish says *Soy cocinero* with no article. In English a job needs ***a / an***: *I'm **a** cook.* Correct by holding up one finger: "**a** cook."
- **Questions need *do*:** *Where **do** you work?* (not *Where you work?*). It is the same *do* as *Do you have kids?* in Lesson 4.
- ***at* for workplaces:** *I work **at** a restaurant / **at** a factory / **at** home.* (*in an office* is also fine; don't correct it.)
- **The *Does he / she…?* preview:** learners only **hear** and **repeat** it this week: *Does your wife work? Yes, she does. She works at a hospital.* Don't test it and don't explain the *-s* in *works*; Level 2 Lesson 1 teaches it. If a learner says *She work at a hospital*, just repeat it correctly: "Yes, she **works** at a hospital."
- **Jobs from home countries:** many learners had a different job before. Accept "In Mexico, I'm a teacher" (past tense comes later).
- **Mixed levels:** strong learners add a place and a time: *I'm a cook. I work at a restaurant. I work at night.* New learners use only *I'm a ___.* with a picture.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they play the guessing game fully and, in Find Someone Who, ask the questions while a partner writes names (or they draw a check).
- **Looking ahead:** Lesson 6 (time and days) uses *I work on Monday… at 6:00* and *When do you work?*.

### Questions and answers

**Q: *a* or *an*?**

A: Listen to the first sound. *a* cook, *a* driver. Before a, e, i, o, u: ***an*** office worker, ***an*** apartment.

**Q: I don't work now. What do I say?**

A: *I don't work now.* Or: *I'm a student.* *I'm a homemaker.* These are good answers.

**Q: Why *Does*? Why *works*?**

A: For **he** and **she**, English uses *does* and *-s* (*she works*). Level 2 teaches it. Today, just listen.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I'm cook. | I'm **a** cook. |
| I'm a office worker. | I'm **an** office worker. |
| Where you work? | Where **do** you work? |
| Do you work at night? Yes, I work. | Yes, I **do**. |
| I no work on Sunday. | I **don't** work on Sunday. |
| I work in a restaurant at night in Main Street. | I work **at** a restaurant **on** Main Street. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En inglés un trabajo lleva ***a*** o ***an***: *I'm a cook* (no «I'm cook»). Antes de vocal (a, e, i, o, u) se usa ***an***: *an office worker*. Las preguntas llevan *do*: *Where do you work?* (no «Where you work?»). Para decir «no» se usa *don't*: *I don't work on Sunday* (no «I no work»). *Does she work?* es solo una vista previa; se estudia en el Nivel 2.

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** A job needs *a* or *an*: *I'm a cook. I'm an office worker.* Questions use *do*: *Where do you work?* "No" is *don't*: *I don't work on Sunday.*
