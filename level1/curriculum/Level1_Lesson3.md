## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. The student pages have a Spanish line and a "My language" line for every task. Learners read those on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Listen or write?"), **then go**. Say less, show more. Use the same short words every time: *Listen. Repeat. Point. Circle. Write. Partner. Group.*

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the instruction and the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Privacy this week:** learners **never** need to give a real phone number or address. Every activity works with the **practice ID cards** and practice numbers (555-0100 to 555-0199). Say this at the start of class.

## Lesson 3 (Week 3): My Phone Number and Address

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Hello + Review · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. Late arrivals join any pair.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **Where are you from? What city? Where is he / she from? Is he / she from ___?**
- **Model with one learner:** "Where are you from?" → "Guatemala." "What city?" → "Antigua." Then point to another learner: "Where is **she** from?" → "She's from Mexico."
- **Do:** pairs use the **Hello + Review** page. A asks; B answers. Then they ask about a classmate (*Where is he from? Is she from Honduras?*). Switch. Then find a new partner.
- **Homework check (last 3 min):** partners show each other their Lesson 2 homework. Walk around and look at item 2 (*am / is / are*).
- **New learners:** pair them with a strong partner. They can do the Lesson 1–2 Progress Checks at home.
- **If behind:** one partner only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:20 · New Words · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Say first:** "**This is practice.** You don't need your real phone number or your real address."
- **Show:** a real paper form (no one's information on it). Point to the lines: "This is a **form**. First name. Last name. **Phone number. Address.**"
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat twice. Act out *signature* (sign your name in the air) and *email* (type on a phone).
- **zero = "oh":** write **555-0142** on the board. Say it slowly in groups: "five-five-five… **oh**-one-four-two." Point to the 0: "**Oh.** In phone numbers, 0 is often **oh**."
- **numbers 21–100:** point to the tiles and say them. Learners repeat. (Letters & Numbers practices them more.)
- **Pairs (4 min):** A points to a picture, B says the word. Then switch. Then: A points to a line on the form picture, B says the word (*city, ZIP code…*).
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:20–6:32 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "This is Ana. She's at a clinic. She's a **new patient**. The receptionist asks: What's your phone number? What's your address?" Point to the form in Task B.
- **Listen 1 → Task A** (circle the right number: phone, street, apartment, ZIP code).
- **Listen 2 → Task B** (fill out Ana's form). **Listen 3 → Task C** (check what Ana writes) and finish Task B.
- **Pairs compare** after each task: "Show your partner." Then write the answers on the board.
- **"Say it" (2 min):** no student page needed. Write 2 lines on the board (they are also in Grammar Box 3): "**What's your phone number?**" and "**Can you repeat, please?**" The class repeats each line twice. Then pairs ask and answer with a practice number.
- **The audio plays the conversation once.** Press play again for the next listen.
- **If behind:** play the audio 2 times, and do Task C together.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.** Point left, then right.
- **Box 1:** you read a question; the class reads an answer.
- **Box 2 (the key point):** point to a man: "**his** phone number." Point to a woman: "**her** phone number." Point to yourself: "**my**." To a learner: "**your**." To the class and you: "**our** class." To two learners: "**their** city." Write on the board: **he → his. she → her.**
- **Box 3:** read a wrong number on purpose: "Is your phone number 555-0124?" → "No, it isn't. It's 555-0142."
- **Box 4:** read the left side; the class reads the right side. Say the groups with a small stop: **555** … **0142**.
- **Check questions:** point to a man: "What's ___ name?" (*his*) • to a woman: (*her*) • "I'm Ana. ___ last name is Ruiz." (*My*)
- **Pair drill (last 3–4 min):** A asks from the left side about a person in the pictures (Ana, Carlos); B answers from the right side. Then switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. It becomes homework, and Talk Time practices the same questions.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Put the Student A and Student B pages in two piles. Write on the board: **What's your phone number? What's your address? Can you repeat, please? How do you spell it?**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:10 · Talk Time: Information Gap with Practice ID Cards · 10 min
- **Grouping:** A–B pairs. Give half the class the **Student A** page and half the **Student B** page. With an odd number, two Bs share one A. Partners **don't show** their pages.
- **Model with one learner (you are A):** "What's your first name?" → "Marco." "How do you spell your last name?" → "S-I-L-V-A." Write it on the board **as you hear it**. "What's your phone number?" → "555-0163." "Can you repeat, please?"
- **Part 1 (5 min):** A asks and writes B's information on the form. Then B asks and writes A's information.
- **Part 2 (2 min):** partners show their pages and **check**: is the form the same as the ID card? Fix any mistakes together.
- **Part 3 (2 min):** pairs join another pair. Each learner says 2 sentences about the partner's card: "**Her** name is Rosa Diaz. **Her** phone number is 555-0187." / "**His** address is 1850 Lake Avenue."
- **Fast pairs:** do it again with their own information **only if they want to**, or with an invented card.
- **Baseline snapshot:** while pairs work, call the last learners you haven't checked, one at a time, for 2 minutes. Use the checklist in the answer key. This is the last week of the baseline.
- **If behind:** skip Part 3.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:10–7:15 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner **says** a phone number (a practice number is fine: "My phone number is 555-01__.") **or writes** one sentence with *his* or *her* (*Her phone number is 555-0187.*). Copying is fine.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (the first two lines of the form). Point to each instruction so learners know what to do at home.
:::

:::step Letters & Numbers · 7:15–7:25 · Numbers to 100, -teen / -ty, Forms · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Use the Letters & Numbers page.
- **See it (1 min):** point to 20, 30, 40… 100 on the page and say each one.
- **Say it (3 min):** go to the ***-teen / -ty*** box. Say each pair 3 times: "thir-**TEEN**… **THIR**-ty." Clap on the strong part. *-teen* is long and strong at the end; *-ty* is short and strong at the start. Learners show **1 finger + 3** for 13 and **3 fingers + a circle (0)** for 30.
- **Quick check (1 min):** say 14 or 40, 15 or 50… Learners point on the page.
- **Write it (4 min):** Task B: say the 5 numbers in the answer key, 2 times each. Learners circle. Task C: read the 2 practice phone numbers in groups (*five-five-five… oh-one-six-three*). Learners write them. Task D: learners copy the card into the form in **CAPITAL letters**.
- **Partners:** A says a practice phone number (555-01__), B writes it. A checks. Switch.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they say the numbers with everyone and point. In Task D they copy only the first name and last name. A helper, if you have one, sits with them.
- **If behind:** do Tasks A and B only.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 5 numbers (answer key), slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle.
- **Parts B and C:** learners match and write on their own. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "A little." 😐 "Not yet." Learners check one.
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 4). Part A shows who still mixes up *-teen* and *-ty*.
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 4 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Teacher notes

- **Privacy:** never ask for or write down a real phone number, address, or email. The practice ID cards and the 555-01__ numbers are for class. If a learner wants to use real information, that's their choice, but don't collect it.
- **The big mistake this week: *his* and *her*.** Spanish uses *su* for both. In English, ***his*** is for a man and ***her*** is for a woman; the word depends on the **person**, not the thing. Correct by pointing to the person: "Carlos… **his**." Learners can read the Spanish note on their Grammar page; you don't need to explain it.
- **Phone numbers:** say one digit at a time, in groups (3 + 4), with a short stop between the groups. *0* is usually "oh." *555-0100* = "five-five-five, oh-one-hundred" is also common, but teach digit by digit for now.
- **Street numbers:** usually in pairs: *210* = "two-ten," *1850* = "eighteen-fifty," *46* = "forty-six." The number comes **before** the street name: *210 Pine Street* (not *Pine Street 210*).
- ***-teen* or *-ty*?** This is the hardest number pair for everyone. When a learner says 13 and you hear 30 (or the opposite), ask: "One-three or three-zero?" and write both on the board.
- **Spelling review:** in Talk Time, learners spell names and streets. Point to the Lesson 1 tricky letters (A / E / I, G / J) when you hear a mistake.
- **Mixed levels:** strong learners add the email and spell it (*a-n-a at mail dot com*). New learners use the box *What's your ___? It's ___.* for every line of the form.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they take part fully in the speaking. On forms, they copy their first and last name and a practice number from the card. Don't make them read aloud.
- **Looking ahead:** Lesson 4 uses *his / her* with family (*This is my sister. Her name is Ana.*) and the first *do* question: *Do you have kids?*

### Questions and answers

**Q: *his* or *her*?**

A: Look at the **person**. A man → ***his***: *Carlos… **his** phone number.* A woman → ***her***: *Ana… **her** phone number.*

**Q: How do I say a phone number?**

A: One number at a time, in groups: *555 … 0142* = "five-five-five … oh-one-four-two." *0* = "oh."

**Q: How do I say a street number?**

A: Usually two at a time: *210* = "two-ten." *1850* = "eighteen-fifty." The number comes first: *210 Pine Street*.

**Q: I don't want to say my real phone number. What do I say?**

A: Use a practice number: *555-0100* to *555-0199*.

**Q: How do I write on a form?**

A: Use CAPITAL letters: *ANA RUIZ*. Sign with your normal writing.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| Carlos… her phone number. | Carlos… **his** phone number. |
| What is you phone number? | What's **your** phone number? |
| five hundred fifty-five… | five-five-five… |
| Pine Street 210. | **210** Pine Street. |
| My phone number 555-0142. | My phone number **is** 555-0142. |
| Is 555-0142. | **It's** 555-0142. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En español *su* puede ser de él, de ella, de usted o de ellos. En inglés hay una palabra para cada persona: ***his*** (de él), ***her*** (de ella), ***your*** (tu / su de usted), ***their*** (de ellos). Mira a la **persona**, no a la cosa: *Carlos… his phone number. Ana… her phone number.* Los números de teléfono se dicen uno por uno, y el 0 se dice «oh». En la dirección, el número va primero: *210 Pine Street* (no «Pine Street 210»).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** *his* = for a man, *her* = for a woman. Say phone numbers one number at a time, in groups: *555 … 0142*. In an address, the number comes first: *210 Pine Street*.
