# Level 1 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 7 (Week 7): How Much Is It?

**Status:** Complete lesson (all 13 Level 1 lessons are published).

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `audio/L1_L7_listening.mp3`, `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures, grammar pictures, listening and store pictures, a play-money page, a receipt + `images/CREDITS.md`)

> **Topic:** money and shopping for clothes. Learners ask and understand prices (*How much is this shirt? It's $12. How much are these shoes? They're $30.*), use ***this / that / these / those***, put the color **before** the noun (*a blue shirt, black shoes*), and pay and check their change in a store role play. Letters & Numbers: reading prices (*$4.99 = "four ninety-nine"*), the short *i* in *this* and the long *ee* in *these*, and reading a receipt.

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. The student pages have a Spanish line and a "My language" line for every task. Learners read those on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Listen or write?"), **then go**. Say less, show more. Use the same short words every time: *Listen. Repeat. Point. Circle. Write. Partner. Group.*

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the instruction and the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Money is private.** Use **only play money** and the invented prices on the pages. Never ask what learners earn, spend, or pay for rent, and never ask them to show real money or real cards.

## Lesson 7 (Week 7): How Much Is It?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask the price: *How much is this shirt? How much are these shoes?*
- understand and say prices: *It's $12. They're $30. It's $4.99 ("four ninety-nine").*
- use *this / that* (one) and *these / those* (two or more)
- name clothes and colors, with the color before the noun: *a blue shirt, black shoes*
- pay and check the change: *Cash or credit card? Cash, please. Here's $20. Your change is $5.*

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Hello + Review · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. Late arrivals join any pair.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **What time is it? What time is class? What day is class? Is the store open on Sunday?**
- **Model with one learner:** "What time is it?" → "It's six o'clock." "What day is class?" → "It's on Monday."
- **Do:** pairs use the **Hello + Review** page. Switch. Then find a new partner.
- **Homework check (last 3 min):** partners show each other their Lesson 6 homework. Walk around and look at item 2 (*at / on / in*).
- **New learners:** pair them with a strong partner. They can do the Lesson 1–6 Progress Checks at home.
- **If behind:** one partner only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:20 · New Words · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** hold up a real shirt: "This is a **shirt**. It's **blue**." Hold up shoes: "**Shoes**. Two shoes." Point to colors in the room: "**Red**. **Black**. **White**."
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat twice. For **cash**, show the play money; for **credit card**, mime paying with a card. **100 cents = 1 dollar** (write it on the board).
- ***pants, shoes*:** say "**pants**" and hold up 2 fingers: one item, but the word is always plural.
- **Pairs (4 min):** A points to a picture, B says the word. Then A says a color, B points to something in the room with that color.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:20–6:32 · Listening · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "Ana is at a **clothing store**. She asks: **How much is it?**" Point to the shirts and shoes in Task A.
- **Listen 1 → Task A:** learners circle the color of the shirt and the shoes Ana buys.
- **Listen 2 → Task B:** learners write the 2 prices.
- **Listen 3 → Task C:** learners check the total, cash or card, and write the change.
- **Pairs compare** after each task: "Show your partner." Then write the answers on the board.
- **The audio plays the conversation once.** Press play again for the next listen.
- **If behind:** play the audio 2 times, and do Task C together.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:32–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 13 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.** Point left, then right.
- **Boxes 1–2:** hold up the shirt: "How much **is this** shirt?" → "**It's** $12." Hold up the shoes: "How much **are these** shoes?" → "**They're** $30." Write on the board: **one → is / it's. two or more → are / they're.**
- **Box 3 (the key point):** stand next to the shirt: "**this** shirt." Walk away and point to the far chair: "**that** shirt." Same with shoes: "**these** shoes… **those** shoes." Learners point near and far as they say the words.
- **Box 4:** write **a shirt blue** on the board and cross it out with an ✗. Write **a blue shirt** ✓. Then **black shoes** ✓ and **blacks shoes** ✗: colors never take *-s*.
- **Box 5:** model paying with play money: "That's $8." → give $10 → "Your change is $2."
- **Check questions:** hold up one shirt: "How much ___ this shirt?" (*is*) • hold up shoes: "How much ___ these shoes?" (*are*) • "shirt / red / a" (*a red shirt*)
- **Pair drill (last 3–4 min):** A points to a picture on the New Words page and asks; B invents a price. Then switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. It becomes homework, and Talk Time practices the same questions.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Put the Student A and Student B pages in two piles, and one envelope of play money per pair. Write on the board: **How much is this ___? It's $___. How much are these ___? They're $___. Cash or credit card? Here's $___. Your change is $___.**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:10 · Talk Time: Store Role Play · 10 min
- **Grouping:** A–B pairs. Give half the class the **Student A** page and half the **Student B** page, and each pair one envelope of play money. Partners **don't show** their pages.
- **Model with one learner (you are the shopper):** point to the dress on your page. "How much is this red dress?" → "It's $25." "OK. Here's $30." → "Your change is $5." Write the price and the change on the board.
- **Part 1 (4 min):** **A is the cashier** (Store 1, with prices). **B is the shopper**: B gets the play money, asks the price of the 3 things, writes the prices, buys one thing, pays, and checks the change.
- **Part 2 (4 min):** switch. **B is the cashier** (Store 2). **A is the shopper.**
- **Check (2 min):** partners show their pages. Are the prices the same?
- **Fast pairs:** buy 2 things and say the total: "That's $43."
- **If behind:** one round only (Part 1).
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:10–7:15 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** hold up a picture or a real item. Each learner **asks** "How much is this ___?" / "How much are these ___?" **or writes** one color + noun (*a red dress*). Copying is fine.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (the first line). Point to each instruction so learners know what to do at home.
- **Collect the play money** for next time.
:::

:::step Letters & Numbers · 7:15–7:25 · Prices, This / These, a Receipt · 10 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Use the Letters & Numbers page.
- **See it (2 min):** Task A: point to each price and say it. *$4.99* = "four ninety-nine"; *$12.50* = "twelve fifty"; *$20.00* = "twenty dollars". The dot is a **period**; the numbers after it are **cents**.
- **Hear it (2 min):** Task B: say the 5 prices in the answer key, 2 times each. Learners circle.
- **Say it (2 min):** Task C: *this* has a short **i** (like *six*); *these* has a long **ee** (like *three*). Smile wide for **ee**. Say the pairs; learners repeat.
- **Read it (3 min):** Task D: read the receipt together. Learners answer the 3 questions.
- **Partners:** A says a price from Task A, B points to it. Switch.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** they say the prices with everyone and point. In Task D they find and circle the **TOTAL**. A helper, if you have one, sits with them.
- **If behind:** do Tasks A and B only.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 5 prices (answer key), slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle.
- **Parts B and C:** learners match and write on their own. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "A little." 😐 "Not yet." Learners check one.
- **Collect the pages.** Week 8 is the **midterm**; Parts A and C show who needs a quick review of prices and *is / are*.
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 8 at 6:00, before the midterm.
:::

### Listening script: `L1_L7_listening.mp3` (about 1:10; the conversation plays once, so play the audio again if needed)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer. Say the prices as words: *twelve dollars, thirty dollars.*

> **Narrator:** Lesson seven. Listening. Ana is at a clothing store. She talks to the cashier. Listen.
> **Cashier:** Hi! Can I help you?
> **Ana:** Yes, please. How much is this shirt?
> **Cashier:** The blue shirt? It's $12.
> **Ana:** Do you have this shirt in red?
> **Cashier:** No, sorry. Only blue and white.
> **Ana:** OK, blue is good. And how much are these shoes?
> **Cashier:** The black shoes? They're $30.
> **Ana:** Good. The blue shirt and the black shoes, please.
> **Cashier:** OK. That's $42. Cash or credit card?
> **Ana:** Cash, please. Here's $50.
> **Cashier:** Thank you. Your change is $8.
> **Ana:** Thank you. Can I have the receipt, please?
> **Cashier:** Sure. Here's your receipt. Have a nice day!
> **Ana:** You too. Bye!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* the shirt: **b (blue)** • the shoes: **a (black)**.
- *Listening Task B:* 1 **$12**, 2 **$30**.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 ✓ **$42**, 2 ✓ **cash**, 3 **$8**, 4 ✓ **the receipt**.
- *Grammar practice:* 1 is, 2 are, 3 this, 4 these, 5 a red shirt.
- *Talk Time:* Store 1: red dress **$25**, black pants **$18**, white shoes **$35** • Store 2: green jacket **$40**, yellow shirt **$9**, blue pants **$22**.
- *Homework 1:* 1 is, 2 are, 3 are, 4 is.
- *Homework 2:* 1 a green jacket, 2 black shoes, 3 a white dress.
- *Letters & Numbers Task B (the teacher says):* 1 **$4.99** ("four ninety-nine"), 2 **$13** ("thirteen dollars"), 3 **$15.50** ("fifteen fifty"), 4 **$60** ("sixty dollars"), 5 **$2.25** ("two twenty-five"). Say each price 2 times.
- *Letters & Numbers Task D:* 1 **$40.00**, 2 **$83.00**, 3 **$17.00**.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says):* 1 **$12**, 2 **$40**, 3 **$5.99** ("five ninety-nine"), 4 **$18**, 5 **$70**. Say each price 2 times.
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 c, 2 a, 3 d, 4 b.
- *Progress Check Part C:* 1 are, 2 is, 3 a red dress.
- *Progress Check scoring:* Part A /5 + Part B /4 + Part C /3 = /12. This is not a test grade. Part A shows who can hear prices (and *-teen / -ty* again); Part C shows *is / are* and color + noun.

### Teacher notes

- **Money is private:** only play money and invented prices. If a learner talks about real prices or real money problems, listen kindly and move back to the activity.
- **The big mistake this week: the color after the noun.** Spanish says *una camisa azul*. In English the color comes **first**: *a **blue** shirt*. And colors never take *-s*: *black shoes* (not *blacks shoes*). Correct by pointing at the word order on the board. Learners can read the Spanish note on their Grammar page.
- ***is / are*:** one thing → *How much **is** this shirt? **It's** $12.* Two or more → *How much **are** these shoes? **They're** $30.* ***Pants*** and ***shoes*** always use *are*.
- ***this / that / these / those*:** near / far + one / more than one. Use your body: stand next to the thing for *this / these*, point far away for *that / those*.
- **Saying prices:** *$4.99* = "four ninety-nine" (people don't say the word *dollars* or *cents* here). *$12* = "twelve dollars". *$12.50* = "twelve fifty". *-teen / -ty* matter again: *$13* or *$30*?
- ***this* and *these*:** many learners say both the same. *this* = short **i** (*sit*); *these* = long **ee** (*see*). Don't over-correct; the grammar (*is / are*) helps listeners understand.
- **Mixed levels:** strong learners buy 2 things, say the total, and use *Do you have this in ___?* New learners use only *How much is this?* and point.
- **Learners who can't read or write yet:** in Talk Time they are good cashiers: they say the price from the tag and count the play money.
- **Looking ahead:** Week 8 is the **midterm** (Lessons 1–7). Lesson 8 (food) uses *How much is…?* again and adds *I like… / I don't like…*.

### Questions and answers

**Q: *is* or *are*?**

A: One thing → ***is*** (*How much is this shirt? It's $12.*). Two or more → ***are*** (*How much are these shoes? They're $30.*). *Pants* and *shoes* → always ***are***.

**Q: *this* or *that*?**

A: Near → ***this / these***. Far → ***that / those***.

**Q: Where does the color go?**

A: **Before** the thing: *a blue shirt*. Colors never add *-s*: *black shoes*.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| How much is these shoes? | How much **are** these shoes? |
| How much cost this? | How much **is** this? |
| a shirt blue | a **blue shirt** |
| blacks shoes | **black** shoes |
| this shoes | **these** shoes |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En inglés el color va **antes** de la cosa: *a blue shirt* (no «a shirt blue»). Los colores nunca llevan *-s*: *red shoes* (no «shoes reds»). *Pants* y *shoes* siempre son plurales: *these pants are…* Para preguntar el precio: *How much is…?* (una cosa) o *How much are…?* (dos o más), no «How much cost…?».

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** Color first: *a blue shirt*. Colors don't change: *red shoes*. One thing → *is / this / that*. Two or more → *are / these / those*.
