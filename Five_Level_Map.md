# Adult ESL Curriculum: Five-Level Progression Map (Draft for Review)

**Status:** Plan. Published so far: Level 1 Lesson 1, all of Level 2, and Level 3 Lesson 1. Levels 4 and 5 are outlines only.

**Date:** October 19, 2026

**What this is:** a one-place view of all five levels: who each level is for, what learners can do when they leave, the grammar and life-skill strands, and how learner support changes from level to level. Levels 1 and 2 are described **as they actually are** (Level 1 from `Level1_Scope_and_Sequence.md` and the built Lesson 1; Level 2 from `Level2_Curriculum_v5_public.md`). Level 3 is detailed in `level3/curriculum/Level3_Scope_and_Sequence.md`. Levels 4 and 5 are outlines only.

### Same shape at every level

Every level is one **16-week semester**: **13 lessons** (Lessons 1–7 in Weeks 1–7, Lessons 8–13 in Weeks 9–14), a **midterm in Week 8**, a **final in Week 15**, and **graduation in Week 16**. Class runs **6:00–6:45, break, 7:00–7:30**. Classes start with about 15 learners and often drop to 7–8, so every activity has a large-group and a small-group version. Every lesson has picture vocabulary (Twemoji, CC BY 4.0; no instruction icons in any level), two-sided question-and-answer grammar boxes, a listening audio file (each file plays the conversation once; the teacher presses play again; voices are synthetic), a slide deck, lots of pair and group work, an exit ticket, homework, and progress checks. Teachers are fluent-English volunteers; **no Spanish (or other language) is needed to teach any level.**

### Level estimates (our estimate, not an official alignment)

| Level | NRS ESL level (entry → exit) | CEFR (entry → exit) | In one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Beginning ESL Literacy / Low Beginning → **Low Beginning** | Pre-A1 → **A1** | Survival English: personal information, numbers, time, money, home, town, *can* |
| **2** | Low Beginning → **High Beginning** | A1 → **A1+ / A2** | Everyday routines and stories: present, past, future (*going to*), comparing, advice, needs |
| **3** | High Beginning → **Low Intermediate** | A2 → **A2+** | Connected talk about events, people, and problems: *used to*, past continuous, *will*, *if*-sentences, polite requests |
| **4** | Low Intermediate → **High Intermediate** | A2+ → **B1** | Experiences and opinions: present perfect, *would*, possibility, giving and defending an opinion |
| **5** | High Intermediate → **Advanced** | B1 → **B1+ (moving toward B2)** | Complex real-world tasks: perfect continuous, past perfect, passive, reported speech, *wish*, negotiating |

**What the frameworks say, in plain words** (paraphrased, not quoted):

- **NRS** (National Reporting System for adult education, U.S. Department of Education) has six ESL levels. *High Beginning:* understands common words and simple phrases in familiar contexts with repetition; can meet basic survival needs; some control of basic grammar. *Low Intermediate:* understands familiar phrases and limited new ones spoken slowly with repetition; asks and answers questions; takes part in some routine social conversations with some difficulty. *High Intermediate:* understands short new phrases on familiar topics; takes part in limited conversations; inconsistent control of more complex grammar. *Advanced:* communicates in a variety of daily-life and work contexts, including some unfamiliar vocabulary; can reword to clarify; can go beyond learned patterns; still has difficulty with complex structures. Newer NRS descriptors are aligned with the federal English Language Proficiency Standards for adult education; use the current official version for any reporting.
- **CEFR** (Council of Europe). *A2:* understands frequent expressions about immediate needs; handles simple, routine exchanges; describes background and immediate surroundings in simple terms. *B1:* understands the main points of clear speech on familiar matters (work, school, free time); deals with most everyday situations; connects sentences to describe experiences, events, hopes, and plans, and gives short reasons and explanations. *B2:* interacts with some fluency and spontaneity; explains a viewpoint with advantages and disadvantages. The "plus" levels (A2+, B1+) are used in the CEFR Companion Volume (2020) for the stronger half of a level.
- **CASAS-style life skills:** all five levels use the CASAS Competency content areas (basic communication, consumer economics, community resources, health, employment, government and law, independent living, and learning and thinking skills). We **do not** claim any CASAS score alignment.

## Level 1: First Steps (exists as a full scope and sequence + a built Lesson 1)

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Band** | NRS Beginning ESL Literacy / Low Beginning → Low Beginning · CEFR Pre-A1 → A1 |
| **Entry** | No English needed. Cannot yet answer *What's your name? Where are you from? What's your phone number?* or spell a name aloud. |
| **Exit can-dos** | Greet and introduce self; give and ask for personal information (spelling, phone, address, birthday); talk about family and job in short sentences; tell time, days, dates; ask prices and pay; order simple food; say what is in a home; follow simple directions; say *I'm sick / It hurts here* and give an address in an emergency call; say *I can… / I can't…*; give a 30–60 second talk. Fill out a simple form; read common signs. |
| **Core grammar strands** | *be* (all persons, questions, short answers); simple present with *I / you / we / they*; *have*; *a / an*; plurals; possessive adjectives and *'s*; *this / that / these / those*; *there is / are*; *can / can't*; commands; *at / on / in* for time; prepositions of place; question words |
| **Life-skill themes** | Personal information and forms, family, jobs, time and schedules, money and clothes, food, calendar and appointments, home, the neighborhood, the body and getting help, job skills |
| **Support** | **Spanish line** + **"My language" blank** under **every** task instruction (no instruction icons: decided October 2026, being removed from Lesson 1). Audio about 0:40–1:20, slow, 2 speakers, clear pauses; played 2–3 times. Picture-based tasks (point, circle, check). New Words gets 10 minutes. A **Letters & Numbers** block (7:15–7:30) every week. Exit ticket can be spoken or copied. |

## Level 2: Everyday Life (exists, complete)

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Band** | NRS Low Beginning → High Beginning · CEFR A1 → A1+ / A2 |
| **Entry** | Knows *be* and *do* questions with *I / you*, numbers, time, family and job words, *there is / are*, basic places (= Level 1 exit). |
| **Exit can-dos** | Describe routines (self and others); talk about likes; say what is happening now; tell about last weekend and a short life story; compare ways to travel; recommend the best place; order food and make a shopping list; describe symptoms, give advice, make an appointment; make plans and invite; handle a service phone call; give a 1-minute "then, now, and next" talk. |
| **Core grammar strands** | *be* vs. *do* (review); simple present + *-s*, frequency words; *like / love / hate* + *-ing*, *me too / me neither*; present continuous (now and plans); *was / were*; simple past regular and irregular, *Did…?*, *ago*; comparatives; superlatives; count / noncount, *some / any*, *How much / many*; *I'd like / Can I have*; *should*; *be going to*; *have to / need to*; *Can / Could you…?*; mixed tenses |
| **Life-skill themes** | Meeting people, daily routines, free time, travel and commuting, places in town, food shopping and restaurants, health and clinic calls, making plans, bills and customer service, personal goals |
| **Support** | Short **Spanish note** + **"Tip for speakers of other languages"** on each Grammar page (student pages only). No Spanish line on each instruction. Audio about 1:00–1:30, 2 speakers, natural but slow; played 2–3 times. The 7:15–7:30 block is optional ("If time"). Exit ticket: one written sentence with the day's grammar. |

## Level 3: Connecting Ideas (new; see the Level 3 scope and sequence)

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Band** | NRS High Beginning → Low Intermediate · CEFR A2 → A2+ |
| **Entry** | Level 2 exit: controls simple present, present continuous, simple past (*Did…?*), *going to*, comparatives / superlatives, *should*, *have to*, polite requests with *Can / Could you…?*; can give a 1-minute talk. |
| **Exit can-dos** | Keep a conversation going with follow-up questions and reasons (*because, so*); describe people's looks and personality; tell how life used to be; tell what happened in a small car accident or other incident (with *when / while*); offer help and make promises; talk about what will happen *if…*; return or exchange an item and change an appointment; report a lost item; talk about skills and strengths for a job; give and follow step-by-step instructions; give an opinion of an experience; report a problem at home; give a 1–2 minute talk about goals. Write a short note, message, or report of 3–5 sentences; fill out a multi-part form. |
| **Core grammar strands** | Follow-up questions + *because / so*; *What does he look like? / What's he like?*; object pronouns; *used to*; past continuous + *when / while*; *will* for offers, promises, quick decisions, predictions; first conditional; *I'd like to* + verb, *Could I / May I…?*; possessive pronouns + *Whose…?*; *good at / interested in* + *-ing*, *enjoy / don't mind* + *-ing*; sequence words + imperatives; *-ed / -ing* adjectives; *something / anything / nothing / someone…*; future review (*will, going to, hope to, plan to*) |
| **Life-skill themes** | Making connections; family and community; a small, non-graphic car accident and an accident report (workplace incident as an option); helping at work and at a school event; weather alerts and power outages; returns, exchanges, rescheduling; lost and found; job strengths and interviews; instructions at work, at home, and on medicine labels; community events and feedback; home repairs and maintenance requests; education and career goals |
| **Support** (decided) | **No instruction icons. No Spanish line on instructions** (same as Level 2); one task-words page at the front with an *Español* and a *My language* column. One short Spanish note + one "Tip for speakers of other languages" per Grammar page. **"My language" column on the New Words page** (a personal word list, filled in by the learner, optional). Audio about 1:30 (1:15–1:45), up to 3 speakers, natural speed with clear speech; the file plays the conversation once and the **teacher plays it 2 times** (gist, then details). Reading texts 80–150 words. A **Read & Write** block (7:15–7:30) most weeks. Exit ticket: two sentences, one with a reason. |

## Level 4: Experiences and Opinions (outline only)

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Band** | NRS Low Intermediate → High Intermediate · CEFR A2+ → B1 |
| **Entry** | Level 3 exit (above): tells connected stories with past continuous and *used to*, uses *will* and first conditional, gives reasons. |
| **Exit can-dos** | Talk about life experiences (*Have you ever…?*) and how long (*for / since*); describe medical history and ongoing symptoms; say what has / hasn't been done yet; ask a neighbor or coworker for a favor; talk about imaginary situations (*If I had…, I would…*); give, ask for, and politely disagree with an opinion; tell about life events in order; talk about cultural expectations (being on time, workplace dress); understand a short news report; recognize a scam; talk about possibility (*might*); ask for time off and plan a trip; leave a detailed voicemail. Write a short email (5–8 sentences) and a short opinion paragraph. |
| **Core grammar strands** | Present perfect (*ever / never, for / since, already / yet / just*); present perfect vs. simple past; second conditional (*would*); *might / may / could* for possibility; *could / why don't you / it's a good idea to* for suggestions; *too / enough*, *too much / too many*; verb + infinitive vs. verb + *-ing*; infinitive of purpose; *Would you mind…?*; defining relative clauses (*who / that*); common separable phrasal verbs; *Do you know where…?* (first look at indirect questions); time clauses (*before / after / when*) |
| **Life-skill themes** | Workplace (performance, scheduling, time off, dress codes); health history, insurance basics, dental and preventive care; housing (locks, home safety, utilities, saving energy); consumer protection and scams; local news and community meetings; travel and packing; cultural norms and etiquette |
| **Support** | No icons. **No Spanish on instruction lines**; the Spanish note becomes **optional** (a short "contrast tip" box for any language, with Spanish as one example). Personal word list continues. Audio about 1:30–2:15, 2–3 characters plus an optional narrator, natural speed; the audio says *going to* / *want to*, and each lesson explains *gonna* / *wanna* (fine in casual talk); played 2 times. Reading 150–250 words (notices, emails, short news). Exit ticket: a 3-sentence answer with a reason. |

## Level 5: Taking Part

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Band** | NRS High Intermediate → Advanced · CEFR B1 → B1+ (moving toward B2) |
| **Entry** | Level 4 exit (above): uses present perfect and *would*, gives and defends opinions, writes a short email. |
| **Exit can-dos** | Prepare for a job interview and talk about accomplishments; talk about ongoing actions (*How long have you been…?*); talk about regrets and wishes; explain conditions (*unless, as long as*); negotiate and compromise; report what someone said; ask polite indirect questions (*Do you know if…?*); confirm information (*You sent it, didn't you?*); judge whether information is reliable; talk about changes over time; file a claim or a complaint; take part in a community or school meeting; give a 2–3 minute talk with an introduction, examples, and a conclusion. Write a 1–2 paragraph email or letter (complaint, request, thank-you). |
| **Core grammar strands** | Present perfect continuous; past perfect (with *when / by the time*); *wish* + past / past perfect; third conditional (light) and *unless*; passive (present and past); reported speech (*said / told / asked*); embedded questions (*if / whether*, *wh-*); tag questions; gerunds as subjects; *so / such … that*; modals of deduction (*must / might / can't be*); common idioms and polite softeners |
| **Life-skill themes** | Career advancement and interviews; workplace conflict and compromise; worker and tenant rights and responsibilities; insurance claims; media and online information; personal finance (budget, credit); children's school system and meetings; civic participation; health systems and caregiving; personal achievement and community support |
| **Support** | No icons. **English-only** student pages, except an optional end-of-book glossary with a *My language* column. Audio about 2:00–3:00, 2–4 speakers, natural speed, some background sound (a meeting, a phone line); played 1–2 times. Reading 250–400 words (letters, articles, policies). Learners lead some Talk Time activities. Exit ticket: a short written or spoken summary. |

## How Support Changes from Level to Level

| Support | Level 1 | Level 2 (as it is) | Level 3 | Level 4 | Level 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Icons on instructions** | None (decided; being removed) | None | None | None | None |
| **Spanish line on each instruction** | Yes | No | **No** (task-words page at front has *Español*) | No | No |
| **"My language" blank** | On every instruction line | Not used | Task-words page + **New Words column** (personal word list) | Personal word list | End-of-book glossary only |
| **Spanish note on Grammar page** | Yes | Yes (*Nota para hispanohablantes*) | Yes, one short note | Optional "contrast tip" (any language) | No |
| **Tip for speakers of other languages** | Yes ("My language" line) | Yes | Yes | Folded into the contrast tip | No |
| **Audio length / speakers** | 0:40–1:20 · 2 | about 1:00–1:30 · 2 | about 1:30 (1:15–1:45) · up to 3 | 1:30–2:15 · 2–3 + optional narrator | 2:00–3:00 · 2–4 |
| **Audio speed** | Slow, clear pauses | Natural but slow | Natural, clear | Natural; full forms (*going to*, *want to*), *gonna* / *wanna* explained | Natural, some background sound |
| **Plays of the audio** (each file plays once; the teacher replays) | 2–3 | 2–3 | **2** (gist → detail) | 2 | 1–2 |
| **Reading text length** | Words, signs, forms | 1–5 sentences | 80–150 words | 150–250 words | 250–400 words |
| **Writing output** | Copy a sentence; a form | 1 sentence; 5 questions | 3–5 sentences; multi-part form | 5–8 sentence email; opinion paragraph | 1–2 paragraph letter |
| **Pacing** | New Words 10 min; Letters & Numbers block | Core 60 min + "If time" | Core 60 min + Read & Write block | Core + Read & Write; longer Talk Time | Longer Talk Time; learners lead |
| **Graduation talk** | 30–60 seconds | 1 minute | 1–2 minutes | 2 minutes | 2–3 minutes, organized |
