# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 9 (Week 10): I See Your Point

**Status:** Complete lesson (all 13 Level 4 lessons are published).

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures + `images/CREDITS.md`). Audio: `audio/L4_L9_listening.mp3`.

> **Topic:** Giving, asking for, and politely disagreeing with an opinion at a neighborhood meeting about an empty lot (a playground, a parking lot, or a community garden), and writing a short opinion paragraph. Learners use opinion phrases, *as … as / not as … as*, and paragraph words. All places, people, and plans are invented and local.

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you agree? How do you say it politely?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 9 (Week 10): I See Your Point

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- give an opinion with a reason
- ask for someone's opinion (*What do you think about…? / How do you feel about…?*)
- agree and disagree politely (*I agree. / I see your point, but…*)
- compare two ideas with *as … as / not as … as*
- write an opinion paragraph (5–8 sentences) with *First… Also… That's why…*

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *might / may / could; Do you know where…?*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "Do you know when the library opens?" The learner answers with a fact or *I'm not sure. It might…* Then you ask, "What might change in this city next year?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*might* and *Do you know…?*) and read their 3 changes (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *It might rains* (→ *It might rain*) and *Do you know where is the bank?* (→ *where the bank is*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *opinion* (what you think); *agree* (thumbs-up) and *disagree* (thumbs-down, with a smile); *I see your point* (I understand you); *community meeting*; *empty lot* (land with nothing on it); *community garden*, *parking lot*, *playground*; *survey* (questions for many people); *vote* (raise your hand); *advantage* (a plus) and *disadvantage* (a minus).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Is an empty lot full or empty?" (empty) • "If you agree, do you say *That's true* or *That's wrong*?" (*That's true*) • "Is a survey for one person or many people?" (many people).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (In my opinion, we need a playground.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Neighbors are at a community meeting. Mrs. Okafor is the leader. Ray and Lynn give their opinions about the empty lot on Cedar Street. Listen: what are they deciding?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *wanna* = want to: "Tonight we **want to** talk about the empty lot." *gonna* = going to: "Who's **going to** take care of it?" Then practice *I see your POINT, but…* (the voice goes down on *point*, then a short pause) and the stress in *It isn't as EXpensive*. The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:48).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** say, "In my opinion, summer is the best season." Ask a learner, "Do you agree?" Model two answers: a thumbs-up, "**I agree.** That's true," and a smile, "**I see your point, but** I like fall." Write: *something positive first, then* but. Then draw two prices: "A garden is $2,000. A parking lot is $20,000. A garden **isn't as expensive as** a parking lot."
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *What do you think about the empty lot? → I think we need a playground.* Box 2: agreeing and disagreeing politely. Box 3: *as … as / not as … as*. Box 4: the words in an opinion paragraph.
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*I am agree* or *I agree*?" (*I agree*) • "Which is more polite: *That's wrong* or *I see your point, but…*?" (*I see your point, but…*) • "*not as expensive as* or *not as expensive than*?" (*as … as*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A points to a picture and asks, "What do you think about a **playground** for the lot?" B gives an opinion and a reason. A agrees or disagrees politely. Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *What do you think about…? → In my opinion… • I agree. / I see your point, but… • A garden isn't as expensive as a parking lot.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Community Meeting + Opinion Lines · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** take the parent role card. Say, "In my opinion, the lot should be a playground. First, kids need a safe place to play." Ask a learner with the store owner card, "Do you agree?" Help them say, "I see your point, but customers can't find parking." Say: "Polite language is the goal, not winning."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Community Meeting (groups of 4).** Each person has a role card with a plan and 2 reasons. Each person gives an opinion; the others agree or disagree politely. The group chooses one plan and writes 3 reasons.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Opinion Lines (whole room).** Read a statement. Learners stand by the AGREE, NOT SURE, or DISAGREE sign and explain. Someone on another side answers with *I see your point, but…*
- **15 learners:** 4 groups (one of 3) for round 1; then whole-room lines.
- **7–8 learners:** 2 groups for round 1; then lines.
- **5 or fewer:** one group for round 1: you take a role card. Then lines.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A meeting flyer + two letters · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the flyer and the two letters on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write an opinion paragraph (5–8 sentences) about the empty lot: a topic sentence, two reasons (*First… Also…*), an example, and a closing (*That's why…*). Show the example. **Writing check 3:** collect this paragraph and write one strength and one fix. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *Which is better for an empty lot: a playground or a parking lot?* Example on the board: *In my opinion, a playground is better. Kids need a safe place to play because the park is far. I see the other point, but parking isn't as important as kids.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *playground / important / parking (as … as) → A playground is as important as parking.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L9_listening.mp3` (about 1:48; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play `L4_L9_listening.mp3` from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson nine. Neighbors are at a community meeting. They are talking about the empty lot on Cedar Street.
> **Mrs. Okafor:** Good evening, everyone, and welcome. Thank you for coming. Tonight we want to talk about the empty lot on Cedar Street. The city says we can choose a plan. Ray, what do you think about it?
> **Ray:** Thanks, Mrs. Okafor. In my opinion, we need a parking lot. There aren't enough parking spaces on our street. I drive around for twenty minutes every night.
> **Mrs. Okafor:** Yes, the parking problem is serious. Lynn, how do you feel about it?
> **Lynn:** I see your point, Ray, but I'm not sure I agree. I think a community garden is a better idea.
> **Ray:** A garden? Why?
> **Lynn:** First, a garden isn't as expensive as a parking lot. Also, if we had a garden, families would grow vegetables, and kids would spend more time outside.
> **Ray:** That's a good idea, but a garden is a lot of work. Who's going to take care of it?
> **Lynn:** I'd help, and I think many neighbors would help too.
> **Mrs. Okafor:** Ray, do you agree with that?
> **Ray:** Well, I agree that a garden is nice. But for me, parking is as important as a garden.
> **Mrs. Okafor:** OK. Those are two good ideas. Let's do a survey. We'll ask every neighbor on Cedar Street.
> **Lynn:** I agree. That's fair.
> **Ray:** Yes, that's a good plan. I'll help with the survey.
> **Mrs. Okafor:** Great. We'll vote at the next meeting, on May twelfth. Thank you, everyone.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (what to do with the empty lot).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 a parking lot, 2 aren't enough parking spaces, 3 a community garden, 4 isn't as expensive as, 5 take care of it, 6 do a survey.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 opinion, 2 feel, 3 point, 4 idea, 5 agree, 6 fair.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 agree, 2 as, 3 think, 4 point, 5 opinion, 6 had.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 I agree, 2 as, 3 That's a good idea, but, 4 feel, 5 expensive, 6 would.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 A playground is as important as parking. 2 A bus isn't as fast as a car. 3 What do you think about the new store? 4 I don't agree with you. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 rain, 2 is, 3 will, 4 take, 5 leaves.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 Thursday, May 12, at 7:00 p.m. • 2 A playground. • 3 Kids don't have a safe place to play. • 4 No. He sees her point, but he isn't sure he agrees. • 5 Two benches and a small garden. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "In my opinion, a playground is better. Kids need a safe place to play because the park is far. I see the other point, but parking isn't as important as kids."

### Teacher notes

- **Local and practical:** topics stay with parks, parking, store hours, bus stops, and homework. No elections or political parties.
- **Praise polite language, not who "wins."** Model disagreeing with a smile. Write good phrases you hear on the board.
- ***I agree*, not *I am agree*:** this is very common. Point to the Q&A box and keep it on the board all evening.
- **Opinion Lines:** learners who can't stand or move can hold up a sign or raise a hand from their seat.
- **Writing check 3 (Week 10):** collect the opinion paragraph. Write one strength and one fix on each. Learners keep them in their writing folder.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. Tell learners: people often say *gonna* and *wanna*, and they will hear them a lot. The short forms are fine in casual talk; in writing or at a job interview, use the full forms.

## Lesson 9 (Week 10) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: asking for and giving an opinion

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What do you think** | about the empty lot? | ‖ | **I think** | we need a playground. |
| **How do you feel** | about a parking lot? | ‖ | **In my opinion,** | it's a good idea. |
| **What's your opinion** | about the bus stop? | ‖ | **I'm not sure, but** | maybe it's too far. |

### Grammar Box 2: agreeing and disagreeing politely

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Do you agree** | with Ray? | ‖ | Yes, **I agree** with him. | **That's true.** Parking is a problem. |
| **Do you agree** | with Lynn? | ‖ | **I see your point, but** | a garden is a lot of work. |
| **Do you agree** | about the survey? | ‖ | **I'm not sure I agree,** | **because** it takes a long time. |

**Rule of thumb:** To disagree politely, say something positive first: ***I see your point, but… / That's a good idea, but…*** Say ***I agree*** (not *I am agree*) and ***I don't agree*** (not *I am not agree*).

---
### Grammar Box 3: *as … as / not as … as*

| | MEANING | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *as important as* | the same | *A playground is as important as parking.* |
| *not as expensive as* | less | *A garden isn't as expensive as a parking lot.* |
| *more … than* (review) | more | *A parking lot is more expensive than a garden.* |

### Grammar Box 4: an opinion paragraph

| PART | WORDS | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| topic sentence | *In my opinion, / I think* | *In my opinion, the lot should be a playground.* |
| reason 1 and reason 2 | *First, … Also, …* | *First, kids need a safe place to play. Also, it isn't as expensive as a parking lot.* |
| example | *For example,* | *For example, the nearest park is two miles away.* |
| closing | *That's why* | *That's why I think a playground is the best plan.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *I am agree* or *I agree*?**

A: ***I agree.*** *agree* is a verb, so there is no *am*. The negative is ***I don't agree.***

**Q: How do I disagree politely?**

A: Say something positive first: ***I see your point, but…*** or ***That's a good idea, but…***

**Q: *not as expensive as* or *not as expensive than*?**

A: Use *as … as*: ***A garden isn't as expensive as a parking lot.***

6. If we had a garden, families (**would** / **will**) grow vegetables.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I am agree. | I **agree**. |
| I am not agree. | I **don't** agree. |
| A garden isn't as expensive than parking. | A garden isn't as expensive **as** parking. |
| What do you think the plan? | What do you think **about** the plan? |
| No, that's wrong. (in a meeting) | **I see your point, but** I'm not sure. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** In some languages, a direct "No, that's wrong" is normal in a discussion. In English, people usually soften it: *I see your point, but…* (Spanish: *Entiendo, pero…*). What do people say in your language?
