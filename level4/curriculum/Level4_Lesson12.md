# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 12 (Week 13): Is It a Scam?

**Status:** Complete lesson (all 13 Level 4 lessons are published).

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures + `images/CREDITS.md`). Audio: `audio/L4_L12_listening.mp3`.

> **Topic:** Consumer protection, calm and practical: recognizing common scams by phone, text, and email (a fake prize, a fake bank text, a fake "pay your bill today" call, payment with gift cards) and what to do: stop, don't pay, check with the real company, and tell someone. Learners describe people and messages with *who / that*. All messages, names, and numbers are invented.

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Is it real or is it a scam? What should you do?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 12 (Week 13): Is It a Scam?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- describe a person or a message with *who / that* (*a caller who asks for gift cards*)
- name 4 warning signs of a scam
- say what to do with a suspicious call or text (stop, don't pay, check, tell someone)
- give a safe answer on the phone (*I don't give information on the phone. Goodbye.*)
- write a message (5–8 sentences) to warn a family member about a scam
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *to* + verb (why); *enjoy* + *-ing*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "Where would you like to go on a trip?" The learner answers with *to* + verb (*I'd like to go to Chicago to see the lake.*). Then you ask, "What do you enjoy doing there?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*to / -ing*) and read their trip plan (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *for see* (→ *to see*) and *enjoy to travel* (→ *enjoy traveling*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *scam* (a trick that takes your money); *warning sign* (something that tells you to be careful); *suspicious* (strange, maybe not true); *fake* (not real); *official* (real, from the company); *gift card*; *prize*; *link* (you click it in a text or email); *PIN* (your secret number); *hang up* (end the call); *block (a number)*; *trust*.
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Is a fake message real or not real?" (not real) • "Do you give your PIN to a caller or to nobody?" (nobody) • "When a call is suspicious, do you hang up or pay?" (hang up).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (Never give your PIN to anyone.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Mrs. Lopez gets a phone call from a man who says he's from the electric company. Later, she talks to her son, Daniel. Listen: was it a scam?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script line. *gonna* = going to: "We're **going to** turn off your power." Then practice the weak *that* (it sounds like "thut"): *a text that asks*. Stress the key words: *NEVER, GIFT cards*. Practice a calm, firm voice: *No, thank you. Goodbye.* The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 2:01).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** hold up your phone and say, "This morning I got a text. The text said I won a prize. → I got a text **that** said I won a prize." Then: "A man called. The man wanted my PIN. → A man **who** called wanted my PIN." Write: *who* = people, *that* = things (and people). Cross out *a man who he called*.
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *What's a scam? → It's a trick that takes your money.* Box 2: what to do: *Hang up on a caller who asks for gift cards.* Box 3: *never… anyone, must / should*. Box 4: the 4 steps.
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*who* or *that* for a text?" (*that*) • "*a man who he called* or *a man who called*?" (*a man who called*) • "Are most calls scams?" (no, most are real).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A points to a picture and asks, "What's a **gift card** scam?" B answers with *who / that*: "It's a call **that** asks you to pay with gift cards." Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *It's a trick that takes your money. • Don't trust a caller who asks for gift cards. • Stop. Don't pay. Check. Tell someone.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Real or Scam? + Safe Answer · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** read card 1: "Your package is late. Click this link and pay $2.99 today." Say, "I think it's probably a scam. It's a text **that** has a strange link, and it asks for money today." Then model a safe answer: a learner reads caller card 1, and you say calmly, "I don't give information on the phone. I'll call the number on my card. Goodbye."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Real or Scam? (pairs).** Pairs sort the 10 invented message cards into *probably real* and *probably a scam* and explain with *It's a text that… / It's from someone who…* Then they compare with another pair.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Safe Answer (pairs).** A is a caller with a pushy (but polite) card. B gives a safe answer. Switch.
- **15 learners:** pairs; then groups of 4 to compare; then pairs.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs; then one group to compare.
- **5 or fewer:** the whole group sorts together; then pairs.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A consumer tips flyer + a sample scam text · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the invented flyer and the sample text on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write a message (5–8 sentences) to a family member about a scam text, with *that / who* and *Don't…*. Show the example. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *What is one warning sign of a scam, and what should you do?* Example on the board: *A caller who asks for gift cards is a warning sign. You should hang up and call the real company. Don't pay, because real companies don't take gift cards.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *don't trust / a caller / ask for gift cards (who) → Don't trust a caller who asks for gift cards.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L12_listening.mp3` (about 2:01; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play `L4_L12_listening.mp3` from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson twelve. Mrs. Lopez gets a phone call. Later, she talks to her son, Daniel.
> **Caller:** Hello, is this Mrs. Lopez? This is Mike from the electric company. Your bill is late. You must pay today, or we're going to turn off your power in one hour.
> **Mrs. Lopez:** What? My bill is late? I paid it last week.
> **Caller:** No, you owe $200 on this bill today. Go to the store and buy gift cards. Then call me back and read me the numbers.
> **Mrs. Lopez:** Gift cards? That sounds very strange to me.
> **Caller:** Please hurry. This is your last chance. Don't tell anyone.
> **Mrs. Lopez:** No, thank you. I'm going to call the number that is on my real bill. Goodbye.
> **Daniel:** Hi, Mom. You look worried. What's wrong?
> **Mrs. Lopez:** The man who called me knew my name. He said he was from the electric company. He wanted me to pay with gift cards.
> **Daniel:** Gift cards? That's a warning sign. A company that asks for gift cards is a scam. What did you do?
> **Mrs. Lopez:** I said no, and I hung up.
> **Daniel:** Good! You did the right thing. Scammers are people who want your money. Did you give him any information?
> **Mrs. Lopez:** No. I didn't tell him anything.
> **Daniel:** Great. Let's check your account on the City Power website. OK, here it is. Your account is fine. You don't owe any money.
> **Mrs. Lopez:** Oh, good. I'm glad I didn't pay.
> **Daniel:** Me too. Next time, you can block the number. Be careful with texts that ask for your password, too. And never trust a caller who tells you to hurry.
> **Mrs. Lopez:** You're right. Thanks, Daniel.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (Yes, it was a scam.).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 today, 2 gift cards, 3 Don't tell anyone., 4 one hour, 5 hung up, 6 fine.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 that, 2 who, 3 that, 4 who, 5 that, 6 who.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 that, 2 who, 3 that, 4 anyone, 5 something, 6 mustn't.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 that, 2 who, 3 who called, 4 that, 5 should, 6 Hang up.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 Don't trust a caller who asks for gift cards. 2 Be careful with texts that ask for your password. 3 Never give your PIN to anyone. 4 A warning sign is something that tells you to be careful. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 to, 2 traveling, 3 to, 4 to, 5 covering.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 No. Most are real. • 2 No, they don't. • 3 Stop. Don't answer or click right away. • 4 The number on your real bill or card. • 5 It asks for your PIN, and it says you must hurry (30 minutes). • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "A caller who asks for gift cards is a warning sign. You should hang up and call the real company. Don't pay, because real companies don't take gift cards."

### Teacher notes

- **Keep it calm and practical:** "Most calls and texts are real. A few are scams. Stop, check, and ask." No scary stories.
- **Never ask learners whether they lost money** or got a scam call. Learners can share if they want to.
- **All messages are invented,** with no real numbers or links. List a local consumer-help line on the "Keep practicing" sheet (check it locally first).
- **Teacher-only:** if a learner asks about "legal help" or "immigration help" services that ask for money, point only to local legal aid. Don't give advice yourself.
- **Listening:** in the scope, a real utility agent confirms the account. To keep 3 speakers, Daniel checks the account on the company website instead. The message is the same: no money is lost.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. Tell learners: people often say *gonna* and *wanna*, and they will hear them a lot. The short forms are fine in casual talk; in writing or at a job interview, use the full forms.

### Hear it, say it

*gonna* = going to: "We're **going to** turn off your power." People often say *gonna* (going to) and *wanna* (want to), and you will hear them a lot. They are fine with friends; in writing or at a job interview, use *going to* and *want to*. The word *that* is short and weak: *a text that asks* ("thut"). Stress the key words: *NEVER, GIFT cards*. Say *No, thank you. Goodbye.* in a calm, firm voice.

## Lesson 12 (Week 13) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: describing with *who / that*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What's** | a scam? | ‖ | It's a trick | **that takes** your money. |
| **Who** | called you? | ‖ | A man | **who said** he was from the bank. |
| **What's** | a warning sign? | ‖ | It's something | **that tells** you to be careful. |

### Grammar Box 2: what to do

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What about** | a text that wants my PIN? | ‖ | **Don't answer** texts | **that ask** for your PIN. |
| **What about** | a caller who wants gift cards? | ‖ | **Hang up** on a caller | **who asks** for gift cards. |
| **Who** | can I trust? | ‖ | Call the company | at the number **that is** on your card. |

**Rule of thumb:** *who* for people; *that* for things (and also people). The *who / that* part tells you **which** person or thing. Don't add *he / it* after it: ***a man who called*** (not *a man who he called*).

---
### Grammar Box 3: *anyone, something, must, should*

| WORDS | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|
| *Never… anyone* | *Never give your PIN to anyone.* |
| *If something…* | *If something sounds too good to be true, it probably is.* |
| *mustn't / should* | *You mustn't share your password. You should tell someone you trust.* |

### Grammar Box 4: 4 steps

| STEP | WHAT TO DO |
|---|---|
| **Stop.** | Don't answer or click right away. |
| **Don't pay.** | Never pay with gift cards. |
| **Check.** | Call the number on your real bill or card. |
| **Tell someone.** | Talk to a family member or friend you trust. |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *who* or *that*?**

A: *who* for people, *that* for things. *that* is OK for people too: ***a man who called / a text that came***.

**Q: *a man who he called* or *a man who called*?**

A: ***a man who called.*** Don't add *he* or *it* after *who / that*.

**Q: Are all calls and texts scams?**

A: No. ***Most calls and texts are real. A few are scams. Stop, check, and ask.***

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| the man who he called | the man who called |
| a text who said | a text **that** said |
| Never give your PIN to nobody. | Never give your PIN to **anyone**. |
| Don't trust a caller asks for money. | Don't trust a caller **who** asks for money. |
| You must to call the bank. | You **must call** the bank. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** Spanish uses *que* for people and things: *el hombre que llamó, el mensaje que llegó.* English uses *who* (or *that*) for people and *that* for things: *the man who called, the message that came.* Many languages repeat the pronoun (*the man who he called*); English doesn't. What do you say in your language?
