# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 8 (Week 9): In the News

**Status:** Draft for review; not published.

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Are you sure, or is it maybe?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 8 (Week 9): In the News

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- understand the main points of a short local news report
- say what might or may happen, and what will happen
- ask *Do you know where / when / why…?* with statement order
- tell a classmate about a local news story
- write a text or email (5–8 sentences) telling a friend the news
:::

:::box Materials
- Student Pages for Lesson 8 (print for the number expected + 3 spares)
- Board and markers
- News cards A and B and a headline (in the workbook Talk Time pages; copy them onto index cards, optional)

- Listening: **no recording yet.** Read the listening script aloud (with a volunteer if you can), at a natural speed, two times.
- **Prep time:** 15 minutes (read the plan; practice reading the listening script; practice the news reader's pauses).

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *too / enough; must / mustn't / don't have to*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "Is 6:55 early enough for a 7:00 shift?" The learner answers with *too* or *enough* and a reason. Then you ask, "What must you wear at your job or school?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*too / enough / mustn't*) and read their 3 rules (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Also return the midterm and go over 2 common mistakes. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *It's enough big* (→ *It's big enough*) and *You don't have to smoke here* for a rule (→ *You mustn't smoke here*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *news*, *local* (in our city); *reporter* (the person who tells the news); *headline* (the big words at the top); *road closure* and *detour* (draw a street with an X and an arrow around it); *construction*; *water pipe*; *bus route*; *delay* (the bus is late); *announce* (say it to everyone); *update* (new information).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "If the road is closed, does the bus take a detour?" (yes) • "Is a headline long or short?" (short) • "If there is a delay, is the bus early or late?" (late).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (The road might be closed until Friday.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "First, you'll hear a short local news report on the radio. Then two coworkers, Marco and Joy, talk about the news. Listen: what is the news?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *gonna* = going to: "The bus is **gonna** take a detour." Then practice *might* (the last *t* is very short: *It migh' be late*) and *Do you know…?* with the voice going up at the end. Listen to the reporter's pauses. The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **No recording yet:** read the script aloud with a volunteer (about 1:59), at a natural speed, two times. Say *gonna* and *wanna* where the script has them. When the audio is ready, it plays the conversation once; press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** draw a cloud. Say, "Will it rain tomorrow? I'm not sure. It **might** rain. It **may** rain." Draw a sign *ROAD CLOSED MONDAY*: "The road **will** close on Monday. I'm sure." Then ask a learner, "**Do you know where** the library **is**?" Write it and underline *the library is*: "Statement order."
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *Will the road be closed tomorrow? → It might be. / It may be closed until Friday.* Box 2: *Do you know where / when / what time / why…?* Box 3: *will* (sure) vs. *might* (maybe). Box 4: from a question to *Do you know…?*
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*It might rains* or *It might rain*?" (*might rain*) • "*Do you know where is the bank* or *where the bank is*?" (*where the bank is*) • "The sign says *Closed Monday*. *will* or *might*?" (*will*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A points to a picture and asks, "Do you know when the **construction** ends?" B answers with a fact or *I'm not sure. It might…* Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *It might / may / could + base verb (maybe) • It will… (sure) • Do you know where the meeting is? (statement order)*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: News Swap + What Might Happen? · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** hold news card A. Ask a learner, "Do you know when the library will open on Sundays?" Help them find it and answer, "Yes, starting March 3." Then ask, "Do you know why?" Say: "Ask with *Do you know…?* Answer with a fact, or say *I'm not sure. It might…*"
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **News Swap (pairs).** A and B have different news cards. Partners ask *Do you know when / where / why…?* and write the facts. Then they change partners and retell their partner's story.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **What Might Happen? (groups).** Groups choose a headline and write 4 predictions with *might*, *may*, and *will probably*. Then they read them to the class.
- **15 learners:** pairs with one rotation for round 1; then groups of 4.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs for round 1; then 2 groups.
- **5 or fewer:** pairs for round 1 (you take a card if there is an odd number); then one group.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A local news article · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the news article on the Read & Write page, find the main idea, and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write a text or email to a friend about the news (5–8 sentences) with one *might* or *may* and one *Do you know…?*. Show the example. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *What might change in your neighborhood or at your job next year?* Example on the board: *A new store might open near my house. My job may change my schedule. I think so because there's construction on my street and my company is very busy.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *the store / may / open / in June → The store may open in June.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script (not recorded yet; about 1:59 when recorded; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** the recording is coming soon. Until then, read the script aloud two times, with a volunteer if you can, at a natural, friendly speed. Say *gonna* and *wanna* where the script has them.

> **Narrator:** Lesson eight. First, listen to a short news report on the radio. Then two coworkers talk about the news.
> **Diane Lee:** Good morning. This is Diane Lee with your local news. Starting Monday, Oak Street will be closed between First and Fifth Avenue. Workers are going to replace an old water pipe under the street.
> **Diane Lee:** The city says the construction might take two weeks. It may take longer if it rains.
> **Diane Lee:** During the road closure, the number twelve bus will take a detour on Pine Street. Riders should expect delays of about fifteen minutes. The city will post updates on its website. I'm Diane Lee, and that's the news.
> **Narrator:** Later that morning, at work, Marco and Joy talk about the news.
> **Marco:** Joy, did you hear the news? They're closing Oak Street on Monday.
> **Joy:** Oh no. I take the number twelve bus on Oak Street every day.
> **Marco:** The bus is gonna take a detour on Pine Street. It might be fifteen minutes late.
> **Joy:** Fifteen minutes? I might be late for work. Do you know when the bus leaves Pine Street?
> **Marco:** I'm not sure. You could check the city website.
> **Joy:** Good idea. Do you know how long the closure is?
> **Marco:** They said two weeks. But it might take longer if it rains.
> **Joy:** Hmm. Maybe I'll take the earlier bus. I don't want to be late.
> **Marco:** Or I could give you a ride. I drive past your street.
> **Joy:** Really? That would be great. Do you know where the detour starts?
> **Marco:** I think it starts at First Avenue. I'm gonna look at the map tonight.
> **Joy:** Thanks, Marco!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (Oak Street will close for a water pipe repair.).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 Fifth, 2 Monday, 3 water pipe, 4 two weeks, 5 Pine Street, 6 fifteen.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 might, 2 will, 3 when, 4 how, 5 might, 6 where.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 might, 2 will, 3 open, 4 is, 5 leaves, 6 be.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 take, 2 where the meeting is, 3 might not, 4 opens, 5 will, 6 may.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 The store may open in June. 2 It might not rain this weekend. 3 Do you know where the meeting is? 4 Do you know when the bus leaves? *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 enough, 2 many, 3 mustn't, 4 too, 5 much.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 A new bus route, Route 9, will start on May 6. • 2 Every 20 minutes. • 3 $1.50 (and free for the first week). • 4 Sunday service. • 5 On the city website or at the library. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "A new store might open near my house. My job may change my schedule. I think so because there's construction on my street and my company is very busy."

### Teacher notes

- **Class stories only:** all news is invented and neutral (roads, buses, a library, a store). No politics, crime, injuries, or real current events. If a learner brings up real news, say, "Let's keep to our class stories."
- **After the midterm:** this is Week 9. Return the midterm in the Opening Talk and put 2 common mistakes on the board.
- ***Do you know where…?* is a first look:** practice it with *where / when / what time / why* only. Don't teach *if / whether* (Level 5).
- ***might* + base verb:** learners often add *to* or *-s*. Point to the rule: no *to*, no *-s*.
- **News Swap:** learners keep their card hidden. If a partner can't find a fact, they say, "I'm not sure. It might…"
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening has *gonna*. Learners need to understand it; they can always say *going to*.

### A. Listen. What is the news? Circle.

**a.** Oak Street will close for a water pipe repair.     **b.** A new grocery store is opening.     **c.** The bus will be free next week.

1. Where: Oak Street, between First and (**Fifth** / **Ninth**) Avenue

3. Why: workers will replace an old (**water pipe** / **bus stop**)

### Hear it, say it

## Lesson 8 (Week 9) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *might / may / could* (maybe)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Will** the road | **be** closed tomorrow? | ‖ | It **might be**. | I'm not sure. |
| **Will** the repair | **take** two weeks? | ‖ | It **may take** longer. | It depends on the rain. |
| **When will** the road | **open**? | ‖ | I'm not sure. | It **could open** next week. |
| **Will** the bus | **be** on time? | ‖ | It **might not be**. | It **might be** 15 minutes late. |

### Grammar Box 2: *Do you know where…?* (a first look)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Do you know** | **where** the meeting **is**? | ‖ | Yes, it's | at the library. |
| **Do you know** | **when** the new bus route **starts**? | ‖ | I think | it starts in May. |
| **Do you know** | **what time** the store **opens**? | ‖ | I'm not sure. | It might open at 8:00. |
| **Do you know** | **why** the street **is** closed? | ‖ | Yes. Workers | are fixing a water pipe. |

**Rule of thumb:** ***might / may / could*** + base verb: never *to*, never *-s* (*It might **rain***). After ***Do you know where / when / why***, use statement order: *where **the meeting is*** (not *where is the meeting*).

---
### Grammar Box 3: *will* (sure) or *might* (maybe)?

| | HOW SURE? | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *will* | sure | *The road will close at 8:00 on Monday.* |
| *will probably* | almost sure | *It will probably take two weeks.* |
| *might / may / could* | maybe | *It might open early.* |
| *might not / may not* | maybe not | *The bus might not be on time.* |

### Grammar Box 4: from a question to *Do you know…?*

| QUESTION | *DO YOU KNOW…?* |
|---|---|
| *Where is the meeting?* | *Do you know where the meeting is?* |
| *When does the bus leave?* | *Do you know when the bus leaves?* (no *does*; add *-s*) |
| *Why is the street closed?* | *Do you know why the street is closed?* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *It might rains* or *It might rain*?**

A: *might* + base verb: ***It might rain.*** Never *-s*, never *to*.

**Q: Can I say *Do you know where is the meeting?***

A: Use statement order: ***Do you know where the meeting is?***

**Q: *will* or *might*?**

A: ***will*** = I'm sure. ***might*** = maybe. *The road **will** close on Monday. It **might** open early.*

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| It might rains tomorrow. | It might **rain** tomorrow. |
| The road might to close. | The road might **close**. |
| Do you know where is the bank? | Do you know where **the bank is**? |
| Do you know when does the bus leave? | Do you know when **the bus leaves**? |
| I'm sure. The road might close at 8:00. | I'm sure. The road **will** close at 8:00. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** Spanish *Puede que cierren la calle* uses a special verb form; English uses *might* + base verb: *They might close the street* (not *might to close*, not *might closes*). Spanish *¿Sabes dónde está…?* keeps the verb right after *dónde*; English moves it: *Do you know where the meeting **is**?* What does your language do?
