# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 6 (Week 6): What Would You Do?

**Status:** Draft for review; not published.

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures + `images/CREDITS.md`). Listening audio: not recorded yet (coming soon); until then, read the listening script in this plan aloud.

> **Topic:** Imagining situations and making choices: finding money, getting too much change, a free week, a magic wand, a dream job. Learners talk about imaginary situations with *If I had / found / were…, I'd…*, give reasons, and give advice with *If I were you, I'd…*. All situations are invented and everyday.

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "If it happened to you, what would you do?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 6 (Week 6): What Would You Do?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask and answer *What would you do if…?*
- talk about an imaginary situation with *If I + past, I'd + verb*
- give advice with *If I were you, I'd…* and a reason
- tell a possible situation (*If it rains, I'll…*) from an imaginary one (*If I had…, I'd…*)
- write a reply to an advice letter (5–8 sentences)
:::

:::box Materials
- Student Pages for Lesson 6 (print for the number expected + 3 spares)
- Board and markers
- Situation cards (in the workbook Talk Time page; copy them onto index cards, optional)
- A pencil or a stick as a "magic wand" (optional, for Talk Time)
- Listening: **no recording yet.** Read the listening script aloud (with a volunteer if you can), at a natural speed, two times.
- **Prep time:** 15 minutes (read the plan; practice reading the listening script; practice *I'd* and *wouldn't*).
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: favors: *Would you mind…? / Do you mind if I…?*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "Would you mind lending me a pen?" The learner answers, "No, not at all," or "Sorry, I can't, because…" Then they ask you a favor. Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*Would you mind* + *-ing*) and ask each other their 3 favors (item 3). Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *Would you mind to get…?* (→ *Would you mind getting…?*) and *Yes, I'll help* as the answer (→ *No, not at all*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *imagine* (point to your head: a picture in your mind); *choose* (hold up two things: pick one); *honest* and *tell the truth* (you don't lie); *keep it* vs. *give it back* (mime with a coin); *owner* (the person it belongs to); *envelope*; *extra money* (more than you should get); *mistake*; *magic wand* (wave a pencil); *dream job* (the job you want most).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "If a cashier gives you extra money, is it a mistake?" (yes) • "Is the owner the person who found it or the person it belongs to?" (it belongs to) • "If you tell the truth, are you honest?" (yes).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (If I found a wallet, I'd give it back to the owner.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "This is a radio show called *What Would You Do?* Mike is the host. He tells a situation, and two people call the show with their answers. Listen: what is the situation?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *wanna* = want to: "I **want to** hear your ideas." *gonna* = going to: "If you're **going to** call, call early!" Then practice *I'd* (say the *d*): *I'd give it back*, and *What would you DO?* with the stress on *do*. The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:40).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** hold up a pencil as a magic wand. Say, "I don't have a magic wand. But **if I had** a magic wand, **I'd** fix all the potholes in this city." Write it in two halves: *If I **had** a magic wand, | I**'d fix** the potholes.* Say: "It's not real. We use the past form after *if* and *would* in the other half."
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *What would you do if you found $50 at work? → If I found $50, I'd give it to my supervisor.* Box 2: possible (*If it rains, I'll…*) vs. imaginary (*If I had a car, I'd…*). Box 3: *I'd* and *wouldn't*. Box 4: *If I were you, I'd…* for advice.
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "Do I have a magic wand?" (no) "So do I say *If I have* or *If I had*?" (*If I had*) • "*If I would have time* or *If I had time*?" (*If I had time*) • "*If I were you* or *If I am you*?" (*If I were you*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A reads a situation from the pictures: "What would you do if you found an envelope with money?" B answers with *If I found…, I'd…* and a reason. Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *What would you do if you found $50? → If I found $50, I'd give it back. • If I were you, I'd… • If it rains, I'll… (possible)*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: What Would You Do? + Magic Wand Chain · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** read a card: "You find $50 on the floor at work. What would you do?" Help a learner say, "If I found $50, I'd give it to my supervisor because it isn't mine." Say: "Your answer, then *because*."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **What Would You Do? (groups of 3–4).** One person reads a situation card. Each person answers with *If I…, I'd…* and a reason. The group writes the most common answer. Then the next person reads a card.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Magic Wand Chain (circle).** Pass the "magic wand." Each person says, "If I had a magic wand, I'd…" One more round: "If I had my dream job, I'd…" Classmates can ask one follow-up question.
- **15 learners:** groups of 3–4 for round 1; then two circles for round 2.
- **7–8 learners:** two groups for round 1; then one circle.
- **5 or fewer:** one group for round 1: you read the cards. Then one circle.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: An advice column · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read a letter to an advice column and the answer on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners read a short letter about a noisy neighbor and write a reply (5–8 sentences) with *If I were you, I'd… because…*. Show the example. Invented details are fine. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *What would you do if you had a free week?* Example on the board: *If I had a free week, I'd visit my sister in Chicago. I'd also cook a big dinner for my family. I'd do that because I don't see them very often.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *if I / have / a free week, I / visit / my cousin → If I had a free week, I'd visit my cousin.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L6_listening.mp3` (about 1:40; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** the recording is coming soon. Until then, read the script aloud two times, with a volunteer if you can, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Welcome to the show. Mike is the host. Two people call the show.
> **Mike:** Good afternoon, and welcome to What Would You Do? Here's today's situation. You buy groceries, and the cashier gives you ten dollars too much change. You see it in the parking lot. What would you do?
> **Mike:** Our first caller is Grace. Hi, Grace!
> **Grace:** Hi, Mike. If that happened to me, I'd go back inside and give the money back.
> **Mike:** Really? Why would you go back?
> **Grace:** Because the cashier made a mistake. If I kept the money, she would have a problem at the end of her shift.
> **Mike:** That is a very honest answer. Thanks, Grace. Our next caller is Teresa. Teresa, what would you do?
> **Teresa:** Well, Mike, this really happened to me once. A cashier gave me twenty dollars too much.
> **Mike:** Really? What did you do?
> **Teresa:** I gave it back the next day. I was in a hurry, so I couldn't go back that day.
> **Mike:** Was the cashier happy?
> **Teresa:** Yes, she was very happy. She said thank you three times!
> **Mike:** And what would you do if it were only one dollar?
> **Teresa:** Hmm. If it were one dollar, I'd probably keep it. But I'd feel a little bad.
> **Mike:** Ha! That's an honest answer, too. Thank you, Teresa.
> **Mike:** Next week's question is this: what would you do if you found an envelope with money at work? I want to hear your ideas. And if you're going to call, call early!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (A cashier gives you too much change.).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 give the money back, 2 made a mistake, 3 $20, 4 the next day, 5 probably keep it, 6 an envelope with money at work.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 would, 2 kept, 3 were, 4 I'd, 5 I'd, 6 found.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 found, 2 take, 3 were, 4 do, 5 had, 6 I'll.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 had, 2 would, 3 were, 4 rains, 5 wouldn't, 6 What would you do.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 If I had a free week, I'd visit my cousin. 2 If I were you, I'd tell the truth. 3 What would you do if you won $500? 4 If she had a car, she'd drive to work. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 watering, 2 if, 3 at, 4 mine, 5 covering.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 At a busy restaurant. • 2 30 minutes, not 15. • 3 She'd talk to Leo. • 4 Alone, not in front of other people. • 5 If Leo still took long breaks after that. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "If I had a free week, I'd visit my sister in Chicago. I'd also cook a big dinner for my family. I'd do that because I don't see them very often."

### Teacher notes

- **Past form, present meaning:** *If I had a car* is about **now**, not the past. Say it: "I don't have a car. It's imaginary."
- ***If I were* / *If I was*:** teach *If I were*. Accept *If I was* in speaking. In writing, gently show *If I were*.
- **Situation cards:** all situations are invented and everyday. No answer is wrong. Learners choose and give a reason; never judge a learner's choice.
- **Personal topics:** learners never need to talk about real money problems. *If I won $1,000* is just a game.
- ***I'd*:** learners often drop the *d*. Model *I'd give* slowly, then smoothly.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms; they can always say the full forms.

## Lesson 6 (Week 6) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *What would you do if…?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What would** you **do** | if you **found** $50 at work? | ‖ | If I **found** $50, | I**'d give** it to my supervisor. |
| **What would** you **do** | if you **had** a free week? | ‖ | If I **had** a free week, | I**'d visit** my cousin. |
| **Would** you **keep** | the extra money? | ‖ | No, I **wouldn't**. | I**'d give** it back. |
| **What would** you **do** | if you **were** me? | ‖ | If I **were** you, | I**'d call** the owner. |

### Grammar Box 2: possible or imaginary?

<!-- subst -->
| POSSIBLE (maybe it will happen) | < | ‖ | IMAGINARY (not real now) | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| If it **rains** tomorrow, | I**'ll take** the bus. | ‖ | If I **had** a car, | I**'d drive** to work. |
| If I **get** the job, | I**'ll buy** a new phone. | ‖ | If I **won** $1,000, | I**'d pay** my bills. |
| If you **find** my keys, | **will** you **call** me? | ‖ | If I **found** a wallet, | I**'d look** for the owner. |

**Rule of thumb:** Imaginary: use the **past form** after *if* (*found, had, were*). Use ***would* / *'d* + base verb** in the other half (*I'd give, I'd visit*). Never put *would* right after *if*.

---
### Grammar Box 3: *would* short forms

| LONG | SHORT | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| I would | **I'd** | *I'd give it back.* |
| she would | **she'd** | *She'd keep it.* |
| would not | **wouldn't** | *I wouldn't keep it.* |

### Grammar Box 4: advice with *If I were you…*

| | USE | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *If I were you, I'd…* | advice | *If I were you, I'd talk to the supervisor.* |
| *were* for every person | imaginary | *If it were one dollar, I'd keep it.* |
| *If I was…* | you hear it in speaking; it's OK | in writing, use *If I were* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *If I would have time, I'd take a class*?**

A: No. Never put *would* right after *if*: ***If I had time, I'd take a class.***

**Q: *If I was you* or *If I were you*?**

A: Write ***If I were you***. In speaking, many people say *If I was you*. That's OK.

**Q: *If it rains* or *If it rained*?**

A: Possible (maybe tomorrow): *If it **rains**, I'll stay home.* Imaginary (not real now): *If I **had** a car, I'd drive.*

5. If she ____________ a car, she'd drive to work. (have)
6. If it rains tomorrow, ____________ take the bus. (I'll / I'd)

### Practice B: Circle the correct words.

1. If I (**had** / **have**) a magic wand, I'd fix all the potholes.
2. If I found $50, I (**would** / **will**) give it back.
3. If I (**were** / **would be**) you, I'd call the owner.
4. If it (**rains** / **rained**) tomorrow, I'll stay home.
5. Would you keep it? No, I (**wouldn't** / **don't**).
6. (**What would you do** / **What you would do**) if you had a free week?

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| If I would have time, I'd take a class. | If I **had** time, I'd take a class. |
| If I found $50, I will give it back. | If I found $50, I**'d** give it back. |
| What you would do? | **What would you** do? |
| If I am you, I'd call. | If I **were** you, I'd call. |
| I'd to give it back. | I'd **give** it back. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** Spanish says *Si tuviera…, haría…* with special verb forms. English is easier: use the **past** after *if* (*If I **had**…*) and *would* in the other half (*I**'d** go*). Never put *would* after *if*. What does your language do?
