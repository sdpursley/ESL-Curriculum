## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Is it still true now? Is it a length of time or a starting point?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 2 (Week 2): How Long Have You Lived Here?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask *How long have you lived / worked…?*
- answer with *for* + a length of time and *since* + a starting point
- choose the present perfect (still true) or the simple past (finished)
- give an address, an employer, a position, and a start date on an application
- write 5–8 sentences about how long you have done three things, with one *used to*
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *How long have you…?* and *Have you ever…?*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you ask, "Have you ever tried a new food?" The learner answers. Then you ask, "When did you try it? How was it?" Help them say 3 sentences: the experience, when, and how it was.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (sentences with *have / has* + a past participle) and read one answer from item 3 to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *I have seen it last year* (→ *I saw it last year*) and a missing participle (*I've never eat* → *eaten*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *current address* (now) vs. *previous address* (before); *employer* (the company) vs. *position* (your job title); *start date* and *end date* (point to two dates on the board); *reference* (a person who knows you at work; not family). Show *for* as a length (from one date to another) and *since* as a starting point (one date).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Is your employer a company or a person?" (a company, usually) • "Can your mother be your reference?" (no, usually not) • "Is *2020* a length or a starting point?" (a starting point → *since*).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (What's your current address? → It's 418 Elm Street.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Marisol is at a leasing office. She wants to rent an apartment. Mr. Chen helps her with the application. Listen: what is she applying for?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *wanna* = want to: "I **want to** fill out an application." *gonna* = going to: "My mother is **going to** live with me." Then practice the weak, short *for* (it sounds like "fer"): *for two years* • *for three years*, and link the words: *How long have you lived here?* The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **No recording yet:** read the script aloud with a volunteer (about 1:46), at a natural speed, two times. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words. When the audio is ready, it plays the conversation once; press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** draw a timeline: "2020 ———————→ now." Say: "I moved here in 2020. I still live here. I've lived here **since 2020**. That's six years. I've lived here **for six years**." Point to 2020 (since) and the whole line (for).
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *How long have you lived here? → I've lived here for two years / since 2024.* Box 2: still true or finished? *I've worked at the hotel since 2022* (still true) vs. *I worked at a bakery for two years* (finished). Box 3: sort the time words. Box 4: which one?
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*I live here since 2020.* Right or wrong?" (wrong: *I've lived here since 2020.*) • "*since three years* or *for three years*?" (*for*) • "I worked at a bakery for two years. Do I work there now?" (no).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** Use the time cards. A picks a card and says it (*March*). B makes a sentence with *for* or *since*: *I've lived here since March.* Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *How long have you lived / worked…? → I've… for (two years). / I've… since (2020).*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Timeline Interviews + At the Leasing Office · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** ask one learner, "How long have you lived in this city?" When they answer, ask, "Where did you live before? How long did you live there?" Write the answers on the board on a timeline. Say: "Still true → *have lived*. Finished → *lived*."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Timeline Interviews (pairs).** Learners mark 4 points on their own timeline on the first Talk Time page (moved to this city, started a job or class, a hobby, a phone or car). Then they interview a partner: *How long have you…? Since when?* and write 3 sentences about the partner.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **At the Leasing Office (pairs).** A is the agent and asks the questions on the role card. B is the applicant and answers (invented answers are fine). A fills in the application form. Then switch.
- **15 learners:** pairs for both rounds; rotate once between rounds.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs for both rounds; one rotation between rounds.
- **5 or fewer:** one group for round 1 (each person interviews the person on their left); pairs or one group of 3 for round 2.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A rental application + sentences about you · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the rental application on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write 5–8 sentences about how long they (or an invented person) have done three things, with *for* and *since* and one *used to*. Show the example. Invented details are fine. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *How long have you lived in this city (or worked at your job)?* Example on the board: *I've lived in this city for four years. I moved here in 2022. I moved here because my sister lives here.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *she / work / at the hotel / ___ / March → She's worked at the hotel since March.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script (not recorded yet; about 1:46 when recorded; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** the recording is coming soon. Until then, read the script aloud two times, with a volunteer if you can, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson two. Marisol is at a leasing office. She wants to rent an apartment, and Mr. Chen helps her.
> **Mr. Chen:** Good afternoon. Welcome to Park View Apartments. How can I help you?
> **Marisol:** Hi. I'm here about the two-bedroom apartment. I want to fill out an application.
> **Mr. Chen:** Great. Let's do it together. What's your current address?
> **Marisol:** It's 418 Elm Street, apartment 3.
> **Mr. Chen:** And how long have you lived there?
> **Marisol:** I've lived there for two years.
> **Mr. Chen:** OK. And your previous address?
> **Marisol:** Before that, I lived on Lake Road with my cousins. I lived there for three years.
> **Mr. Chen:** Why are you moving?
> **Marisol:** My apartment is too small. My mother is going to live with me.
> **Mr. Chen:** I see. Now, your job. Who's your employer?
> **Marisol:** Green Valley Clinic. I'm a medical assistant.
> **Mr. Chen:** How long have you worked there?
> **Marisol:** Since March 2022. Before that, I worked at a bakery for two years.
> **Mr. Chen:** And your start date at the clinic? March what?
> **Marisol:** March fifteenth.
> **Mr. Chen:** Thank you. We also need one reference. Not a family member, please.
> **Marisol:** Can I give my supervisor's name? She's known me since I started.
> **Mr. Chen:** Sure, that's fine. And will you have a roommate?
> **Marisol:** No, just my mother. When can we move in?
> **Mr. Chen:** The apartment is ready on June first. The lease is for one year.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **b** (an apartment).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 two years, 2 Lake Road, 3 Green Valley Clinic, 4 medical assistant, 5 March 15, 2022, 6 no.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 for, 2 for, 3 Since, 4 for, 5 since, 6 for.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 since, 2 for, 3 since, 4 for, 5 since, 6 for.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 have you lived, 2 worked, 3 's had, 4 did you move, 5 moved, 6 since.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 She's worked at the hotel since March. 2 We've known our neighbors for five years. 3 He's had his car since 2023. 4 I worked at a bakery for two years. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 used to, 2 used to, 3 use to, 4 didn't use to, 5 used to.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 Since August 2023. • 2 For four years (2019–2023). • 3 City Bus Company. • 4 Ana Ruiz, his supervisor. • 5 He needs a bigger apartment for his family. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "I've lived in this city for four years. I moved here in 2022. I moved here because my sister lives here."

### Teacher notes

- **Learners choose what to share.** Always ask about *this city* or *this job*, never *this country*. Never ask about income, rent, or immigration papers. Invented addresses and answers are fine in every activity.
- **The timeline:** keep "2020 → now" on the board all night. The starting point = *since*; the length = *for*. Still true now → *have lived*; finished → *lived*.
- **Real forms:** a rental application often asks for 3–5 years of addresses and a reference who is not family. Learners can write *N/A* (not applicable) when a line doesn't fit them.
- **Spelling and numbers:** in the leasing office role play, the agent asks *Can you spell that?* Encourage learners to say street numbers in pairs: *418* = "four eighteen."
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms; they can always say the full forms.

## Lesson 2 (Week 2) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *How long…?* with *for* and *since*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **How long have** you | lived here? | ‖ | I**'ve lived** here | **for** two years. |
| ^ | ^ | ‖ | ^ | **since** 2024. |
| **How long has** she | worked at the clinic? | ‖ | She**'s worked** there | **since** March. |
| **How long has** he | known his supervisor? | ‖ | He**'s known** her | **since** he started. |

### Grammar Box 2: still true or finished?

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **How long have** you | worked at the hotel? | ‖ | I**'ve worked** there | **since** 2022. (still true) |
| **How long did** you | work at the bakery? | ‖ | I **worked** there | **for** two years. (finished) |
| **When did** you | move here? | ‖ | I **moved** here | in 2021. / five years ago. |
| **Did** you | use to live with family? | ‖ | Yes. I **used to** live with my cousins. | Now I live with my mother. |

**Rule of thumb:** **still true now** → *have / has* + past participle (*I've lived here since 2020*). **finished** → simple past (*I lived there for three years*). ***for*** + a length of time; ***since*** + a starting point.

---
### Grammar Box 3: *for* or *since*?

| ***for*** + a length of time | ***since*** + a starting point |
|---|---|
| for two years • for six months | since 2020 • since March |
| for ten days • for a week | since Monday • since last summer |
| for a long time • for 20 minutes | since I was 18 • since I started |

### Grammar Box 4: Which one?

| | WHEN? | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *have / has* + past participle | it started in the past and it is **still true now** | *I've worked at the clinic since 2022.* |
| simple past | it is **finished** | *I worked at a bakery for two years.* |
| simple past + *in / ago* | **when** it started | *I moved here in 2021. / five years ago.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *I live here since 2020*?**

A: No. Use the present perfect: ***I've lived here since 2020.***

**Q: *For* or *since* with *March*?**

A: *March* is a starting point: ***since March***. *Three months* is a length: ***for three months***.

**Q: *I've worked* or *I worked* at the bakery?**

A: Do you work there now? Yes → ***I've worked there for two years.*** No → ***I worked there for two years.***

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I live here since 2020. | I**'ve lived** here since 2020. |
| I've lived here since three years. | I've lived here **for** three years. |
| I've moved here in 2021. | I **moved** here in 2021. |
| How long you have worked there? | How long **have you** worked there? |
| I am here for two years. (still true) | I**'ve been** here for two years. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** Spanish says *Vivo aquí desde 2020* (present tense). English uses *have / has* + past participle: *I've lived here since 2020* (not *I live here since 2020*). What does your language do?
