# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 13 (Week 14): My Story So Far

**Status:** Draft for review; not published.

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures + `images/CREDITS.md`). Listening audio: not recorded yet (coming soon); until then, read the listening script in this plan aloud.

> **Topic:** Personal growth and the graduation talk: learners look back at their own learning (when they started, what was hard, what they can do now) and look forward to the next step. They tell a short story with time clauses (*before / after / when*), past ability (*couldn't / was able to*), and the past continuous. Every story is the learner's choice; "my life in this class" is always enough. All names are invented.

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "What can you do now that you couldn't do before? What's next?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 13 (Week 14): My Story So Far

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- tell events in order with *before / after / when* and use a comma when the time clause comes first
- use the present after *when / after* for the future (*After I finish this class, I'll…*)
- talk about past ability with *couldn't / was able to*
- make a 5-event timeline of my learning and tell it to a group
- write an email (5–8 sentences) to my teacher about my story so far, the start of my graduation talk
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *who / that*; warning signs
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "Tell me one warning sign of a scam." The learner answers with *who / that* (*A caller who asks for gift cards.*). Then you ask, "What should you do?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*who / that*) and read their 3 warning signs (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *a man who he called* (→ *a man who called*) and *a text who said* (→ *a text that said*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *milestone* (an important step in your life); *challenge* (something hard); *succeed* (do it well, get what you wanted); *confident* (sure you can do it); *get used to* (something new becomes normal); *make friends*; *be able to* (can); *support* (help, be there for someone); *celebrate* (have a party, say "great job!"); *timeline* (events in order on a line); *speech* (a talk to many people); *audience* (the people who listen).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Is a challenge easy or hard?" (hard) • "When you are confident, are you sure or not sure?" (sure) • "Do you celebrate a milestone or a problem?" (a milestone).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (After I finish this class, I'll celebrate.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Mariana was a learner here. Now she is giving a short talk at graduation. Then her old teacher, Mr. Hill, asks her two questions. Listen: what is her main message?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script line. *gonna* = going to: "I'm **going to** take a training class." Then practice the pause at the comma: *When I started, (pause) I couldn't understand the bus driver.* Stress the key words: *NOW I can…, NEXT…* The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:54).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** draw a line on the board with 2 events: *I finished school. → I got a job.* Say, "**After I finished school,** I got a job." Point to the comma. Then: "I got a job **after I finished school.**" No comma. Then the future: "**After I finish this class, I'll** take Level 5." Cross out *When I will finish*.
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *What did you do after you finished school? → After I finished school, I worked in a store.* Box 2: *couldn't / was able to*, past continuous. Box 3: the comma. Box 4: the talk frame.
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "Time clause first: comma or no comma?" (comma) • "*When I will finish* or *When I finish*?" (*When I finish*) • "Last month, *I could call* the clinic one time, or *I was able to call*?" (*I was able to call*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A points to a picture and asks, "When did you **make friends** here?" B answers with a time clause: "**When I started** this class, I made friends." Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *When I started, I couldn't… • After I finished…, I… • Now I can… • After I finish this class, I'll…*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Story Timeline + Talk Rehearsal · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** show your own 5-event timeline on the board and tell it in 1 minute: "When I started teaching, I was nervous. After I taught my first class, I felt better…" Then model a feedback card: "One thing I liked: your timeline was clear. One tip: speak a little slower."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Story Timeline (pairs).** Each learner writes 5 events from their own learning on the timeline (their choice; "my life in this class" is enough). They tell the timeline to a partner with *when / before / after*. The partner asks one question.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Talk Rehearsal (groups of 3).** Each learner tells their story (1–2 minutes) with the talk frame. The two listeners fill in a feedback card. Switch.
- **15 learners:** pairs; then groups of 3.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs; then groups of 3 (or 2 groups of 4).
- **5 or fewer:** each learner tells the whole group; you give the feedback cards.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A learner story · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the invented story on the Read & Write page, mark the time words, and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write an email (5–8 sentences) to their teacher about their story so far, with *when / before / after*. Show the example. This is the start of their graduation talk (Homework item 3). Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *Write about one change in your life.* Example on the board: *When I started this class, I couldn't talk on the phone in English. I was afraid to call the clinic. Now I can call by myself, because I practiced every week.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *after / I / finish / work, / I / come / to class (past) → After I finished work, I came to class.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L13_listening.mp3` (about 1:54; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** the recording is coming soon. Until then, read the script aloud two times, with a volunteer if you can, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson thirteen. Mariana was a learner at this school. Now she is giving a short talk at graduation. Then her old teacher, Mr. Hill, asks her two questions.
> **Mariana:** Good evening, everyone. My name is Mariana. When I started English classes here, I couldn't understand the bus driver. I was afraid to use the phone.
> **Mariana:** The first year was hard. I had a night job at a restaurant, and I was tired. But my classmates helped me, and I made friends.
> **Mariana:** After I finished Level 2, I got a new job at a hotel. Before I started that job, I practiced my interview with my teacher. Last year, I was able to call the clinic by myself for the first time. That was a big milestone for me.
> **Mariana:** Now I can talk to guests at work, and I help my kids with their homework. I'm more confident.
> **Mariana:** What is next for me? Next, I'm going to take a training class. I want a job at the front desk. My advice to you is simple. Come to class, and do not be afraid to make mistakes. Thank you for listening.
> **Mr. Hill:** Thank you, Mariana. That was wonderful. Can I ask you two questions?
> **Mariana:** Of course.
> **Mr. Hill:** What was the biggest challenge for you?
> **Mariana:** Getting used to the phone. When I called someone, I couldn't understand them very well. Now it's easy for me.
> **Mr. Hill:** And what helped you the most?
> **Mariana:** My classmates. When I was tired, they supported me. After class ended, we often studied together. And we celebrated every small success.
> **Mr. Hill:** Thank you. Everyone, let's celebrate Mariana!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 When, 2 couldn't, 3 will, 4 able, 5 working, 6 finish.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 finish, 2 After I finished school,, 3 couldn't, 4 was able to, 5 work, 6 made.

- *Homework 1:* 1 After I finished work, I came to class. 2 Before I started this job, I couldn't drive. 3 After I finish this class, I'll take Level 5. 4 Last month, I was able to call the clinic. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 that, 2 who, 3 anyone, 4 mustn't, 5 that.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 He couldn't read the bus schedule. • 2 His neighbor told him. • 3 He came to class. • 4 He filled out a job application online. • 5 He's going to take a forklift training class. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "When I started this class, I couldn't talk on the phone in English. I was afraid to call the clinic. Now I can call by myself, because I practiced every week."

### Teacher notes

- **Stories are the learner's choice.** "My life in this class" or "my job" is always enough. Never ask about migration journeys, family separation, or other hard events. If a learner shares something hard, thank them and move on.
- **Model first:** tell your own 1-minute story with the talk frame before learners start. Reading from paper is OK, today and at graduation.
- **Writing check 5 (the graduation talk):** the Read & Write email (5–8 sentences) is the first draft. Learners finish the full talk at home (Homework item 3, 12–15 sentences, about 2 minutes). Collect it next week and give one or two tips.
- **Next weeks:** Week 15 is the final exam; Week 16 is graduation, where learners can give their talk.
- **Pronunciation:** pause at the comma after a time clause: *When I started, (pause) I couldn't…*
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to*. People often say *gonna*. Learners only need to recognize it; they can always say the full form.

## Lesson 13 (Week 14) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *before / after / when*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What did you do** | after you finished school? | ‖ | **After I finished school,** | I worked in a store. |
| **How did you feel** | when you started this job? | ‖ | **When I started this job,** | I didn't know anyone. |
| **What couldn't you do** | before this class? | ‖ | **Before I started this class,** | I couldn't fill out a form. |
| **What will you do** | after you finish this class? | ‖ | **After I finish this class,** | I'll take Level 5. |

### Grammar Box 2: *couldn't / was able to*; *was …ing*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Could you** | understand the bus driver? | ‖ | No, I **couldn't**. | Now I **can**. |
| **Were you able to** | call the clinic? | ‖ | Yes, I **was able to** | call by myself last month. |
| **What were you doing** | when you met your friend? | ‖ | **I was working** at a restaurant | **when** I met her. |

**Rule of thumb:** *before / after / when* + a full sentence (*I started*, *I finished*). The time part can come **first** (with a comma) or **second** (no comma). For the future, use the present after *when / after / before*: ***After I finish this class, I'll take Level 5.***

---
### Grammar Box 3: the comma

| TIME PART FIRST: comma | TIME PART SECOND: no comma |
|---|---|
| *When I started, I didn't know anyone.* | *I didn't know anyone when I started.* |
| *After I finish this class, I'll take Level 5.* | *I'll take Level 5 after I finish this class.* |

### Grammar Box 4: the talk frame

| PART | SAY |
|---|---|
| **Then** | *When I started…, I couldn't…* |
| **What was hard** | *It was hard because… But I've…* |
| **Now** | *Now I can… I'm more confident.* |
| **Next** | *After I finish…, I'll… because…* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *When I will finish* or *When I finish*?**

A: ***When I finish this class, I'll take Level 5.*** Use the present after *when / after / before*, like after *if*.

**Q: Do I need a comma?**

A: Time part first: comma. ***After I finished school, I worked.*** Time part second: no comma. *I worked after I finished school.*

**Q: *couldn't* or *wasn't able to*?**

A: Both are OK: ***I couldn't drive. / I wasn't able to drive.*** For one success in the past, use *was able to*: *I was able to call the clinic.*
