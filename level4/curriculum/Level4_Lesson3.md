## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Is it done? Did it happen a minute ago? Is it a question or a *not* sentence?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 3 (Week 3): Have You Finished Yet?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- say what they've already done and what they've just done
- ask *Have you… yet?* and answer *Yes, I've already… / No, not yet.*
- put *already*, *just*, and *yet* in the right place
- answer a supervisor's check-in questions in 3 sentences
- write a reply email (5–8 sentences) about what is done and not done yet
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *How long have you…?* and *for / since*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you ask, "How long have you lived in this city?" The learner answers with *for* or *since*. Then you ask, "Where did you live before? How long did you live there?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*for / since* sentences) and read one answer from item 3 to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *I live here since 2020* (→ *I've lived here since 2020*) and *since three years* (→ *for three years*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *to-do list* (things to do) vs. *checklist* (a list with boxes to tick ✓); *restock* (put more on the shelves); *inventory* (count what you have); *clock in / clock out* (start and end work; mime a time clock); *deadline* (the last day); *feedback* (what a supervisor says about your work); *check-in* (a short meeting).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Is a deadline the first day or the last day?" (the last day) • "Do you clock in at the start or the end of your shift?" (the start) • "Is *Great job!* feedback?" (yes, positive feedback).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (Have you clocked in yet? → Yes, I've already clocked in.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "It's the end of the evening shift at a grocery store. Ms. Rivera, the supervisor, talks to two workers, Jamal and Kim. Listen: is all the work finished?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *gonna* = going to: "I'm **going to** clean it before I clock out." *wanna* = want to: "I **want to** finish it this week." Then practice hearing *'s* = *has*: *It's just arrived* (= it has just arrived), the weak *have* in *Have you… yet?*, and the stress: *I've JUST finished it.* • *I've alREADY restocked them.* The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:59).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** draw a checklist on the board with 5 tasks. Tick two and say, "I've **already** restocked the shelves. I've **already** clocked in." Tick one now, in front of the class: "I've **just** cleaned the board." Point to an empty box: "I **haven't** checked the inventory **yet**."
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *Have you cleaned the break room yet? → Yes, I've already cleaned it. / No, not yet.* Box 2: add a time → simple past: *I finished them at 3:00.* Box 3: word place (*already* and *just* before the past participle; *yet* at the end). Box 4: which one?
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*I've finished it yesterday.* Right or wrong?" (wrong: *I finished it yesterday.*) • "Where does *yet* go?" (at the end) • "*It's just arrived.* *It's* = it is or it has?" (it has).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A reads a task from the board checklist and asks *Have you… yet?* B answers *Yes, I've already… / I've just… / No, not yet.* Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *Have you… yet? → Yes, I've already… / I've just… / No, not yet. I haven't… yet.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Shift Handoff + Check-in · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** hold up Card A and ask a learner to ask you, "Have you restocked aisle 3 yet?" Answer, "Yes, I've already restocked it." Then point to a task with no tick: "No, not yet. I'm going to do it before I clock out." Say: "Answer, then add one more sentence."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Shift Handoff (pairs, information gap).** A looks at Card A; B covers it. B asks *Have you… yet?* about each task and ticks *done* or *not yet* in the notes. Then switch: A asks about Card B.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Check-in (groups of 3).** The supervisor reads the 3 question cards. The worker answers with *already* and *yet* (invented answers are fine). The observer ticks each *already / yet / just* they hear. Rotate roles so everyone is the worker once.
- **15 learners:** pairs for round 1; then 5 groups of 3 for round 2.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs for round 1; then 2 groups for round 2 (the group of 4 has two workers).
- **5 or fewer:** pairs (or one group of 3) for round 1. In round 2, you play the supervisor first; then learners take turns.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: An email from a supervisor + a reply · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the email from Sandra on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write a reply email (5–8 sentences): what they've already done, what they haven't done yet, when they'll finish it, and one question. Show the example. Invented details are fine. This is **Writing Check 1**: collect it. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *What have you done this week, and what haven't you done yet?* Example on the board: *I've already paid my phone bill. I haven't done my laundry yet. I haven't done it because I worked late every night.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *you / finish / the checklist / yet? → Have you finished the checklist yet?*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L3_listening.mp3` (about 1:59; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play `L4_L3_listening.mp3` from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson three. It's the end of the evening shift at a grocery store. Ms. Rivera, the supervisor, talks to Jamal and Kim.
> **Ms. Rivera:** OK, team, it's almost nine. Let's look at the checklist. Jamal, have you restocked the shelves yet?
> **Jamal:** Yes, I've already restocked them. Those shelves are done.
> **Ms. Rivera:** Great. And the delivery? Has the truck arrived yet?
> **Jamal:** Yes, it's just arrived. Kim and I are putting it away.
> **Kim:** We have already put away the milk and the eggs.
> **Ms. Rivera:** Good. Kim, have you cleaned the cooler yet?
> **Kim:** No, not yet. I haven't cleaned it yet. The delivery came late.
> **Ms. Rivera:** That's OK. Are you going to have time tonight?
> **Kim:** I think so. I'm going to start it after the delivery. It takes about twenty minutes.
> **Ms. Rivera:** Perfect. Jamal, have you checked the bakery inventory?
> **Jamal:** I've just finished it. I gave you the list at eight.
> **Ms. Rivera:** Oh, right, I have it here. Thanks. And the break room?
> **Jamal:** Not yet. I'm going to clean it before I clock out.
> **Ms. Rivera:** OK. So the cooler and the break room aren't done yet. Everything else is done.
> **Kim:** Ms. Rivera, have you seen the new training schedule yet?
> **Ms. Rivera:** Yes, I've just seen it. The deadline for the safety training is Friday.
> **Kim:** Thanks. I want to finish it this week.
> **Ms. Rivera:** Good idea. And some quick feedback: you both did great work tonight. The shelves look really good.
> **Jamal:** Thank you. That means a lot.
> **Kim:** Thank you, Ms Rivera. I will see you tomorrow.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **b** (No, two things aren't done yet.).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 done, 2 done, 3 not yet, 4 done, 5 not yet, 6 not yet.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 already, 2 yet, 3 just, 4 yet, 5 just, 6 already.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 yet, 2 just, 3 yet, 4 yet, 5 already, 6 just.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 haven't, 2 finished, 3 them, 4 Has, 5 has just, 6 did you clock in.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 Have you finished the checklist yet? 2 She hasn't clocked out yet. 3 The truck has just arrived. 4 We've already restocked the shelves. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 them, 2 it, 3 them, 4 it, 5 them.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 The evening team. • 2 By Thursday. • 3 Friday, May 16. • 4 On the computer in the break room. • 5 The new schedule. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "I've already paid my phone bill. I haven't done my laundry yet. I haven't done it because I worked late every night."

### Teacher notes

- **Keep work talk positive and invented.** Learners never discuss real problems with real bosses or coworkers. Any job works in the role plays; invented answers are fine.
- **The board checklist:** keep it up all night. A tick = *already* (or *just*, if you tick it now); an empty box = *not yet*.
- **Word place:** teach *already* before the past participle. In speaking, *I've cleaned it already* is also common; accept it. *yet* always goes at the end.
- **'s = has:** *It's just arrived. She's already left.* Ask: "*is* or *has*?" If a past participle comes next, it is *has*.
- **Writing Check 1:** collect the reply emails. Write one strength and one fix on each (for example, the place of *yet*). Return them next week; learners keep them in a folder to see progress. No scores.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms; they can always say the full forms.

## Lesson 3 (Week 3) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *already / yet / just*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Have** you | cleaned the break room **yet**? | ‖ | Yes, I **have**. | I**'ve already** cleaned it. |
| ^ | ^ | ‖ | No, **not yet**. | I **haven't** cleaned it **yet**. |
| **Has** the truck | arrived **yet**? | ‖ | Yes, it **has**. | It**'s just** arrived. |
| **Have** they | restocked the shelves **yet**? | ‖ | Yes, they **have**. | They**'ve already** restocked them. |
| **Has** she | finished the training **yet**? | ‖ | No, she **hasn't**. | She**'s going to** finish it on Friday. |

### Grammar Box 2: done → when? (simple past)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Have** you | finished the orders? | ‖ | Yes, I **have**. | I**'ve finished** them. |
| **When did** you | finish them? | ‖ | I **finished** them | at 3:00. / an hour ago. |
| **What have** you | done this week? | ‖ | I**'ve checked** the inventory, | and I**'ve cleaned** the cooler. |

**Rule of thumb:** ***already*** = done (maybe sooner than expected). ***yet*** = in questions and *not* sentences, at the end. ***just*** = a very short time ago. Add a time (*at 3:00, yesterday*) → simple past.

---
### Grammar Box 3: word place

| WORD | WHERE? | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| ***already*** | before the past participle | *I've **already** cleaned it.* |
| ***just*** | before the past participle | *The truck has **just** arrived.* |
| ***yet*** | at the end (questions and *not*) | *Have you finished **yet**? I haven't finished **yet**.* |
| ***it / them*** | after the verb | *the cooler → I've cleaned **it**. the shelves → I've restocked **them**.* |

### Grammar Box 4: Which one?

| | WHEN? | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *have / has* + past participle | it is done (or not done); **no time** | *I've finished the orders. I haven't called her yet.* |
| simple past | a **finished time** (*at 3:00, yesterday, an hour ago*) | *I finished the orders at 3:00.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *I've finished it yesterday*?**

A: No. With a finished time, use the simple past: ***I finished it yesterday.***

**Q: Where does *yet* go?**

A: At the end: ***Have you clocked out yet? I haven't clocked out yet.***

**Q: What does *It's just arrived* mean?**

A: *It's* = ***it has***. *just* = a very short time ago. The truck arrived a minute ago.

2. I (**finished** / **'ve finished**) the inventory at 8:00.
3. The shelves? Yes, I've restocked (**them** / **it**).
4. (**Has** / **Have**) the truck arrived yet?
5. The delivery (**has just** / **just has**) arrived.
6. When (**did you clock in** / **have you clocked in**)?

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I've finished it yesterday. | I **finished** it yesterday. |
| I didn't finish yet. | I **haven't finished** yet. |
| Have you yet finished? | Have you finished **yet**? |
| I have already clean it. | I've already **cleaned** it. |
| Has arrived the truck? | **Has the truck** arrived? |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** Spanish *ya* can mean *already* (*Ya terminé* = *I've already finished*) and *yet* in questions (*¿Ya terminaste?* = *Have you finished yet?*). *Todavía no* = *not yet*. In English, *yet* goes at the end. What does your language do?
