# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 11 (Week 12): Time Off

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Why do you need time off? What do you enjoy doing on a trip?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 11 (Week 12): Time Off

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *phrasal verbs + it / them*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "What do you turn off before you go to bed?" The learner answers with *it / them* (*I turn the TV off. I turn it off at 10:00.*). Then you ask, "What do you put on when it's cold?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*it / them*) and read their 3 home tips (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *turn off it* (→ *turn it off*) and *close the light* (→ *turn off the light*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *time off* (days you don't work); *vacation days* (paid days off); *request form*; *approve* (say yes); *pack* (put things in a bag); *suitcase*; *carry-on bag* (a small bag on the plane or bus); *round-trip ticket* (there and back); *boarding pass*; *toiletries* (toothbrush, soap); *voicemail* (a phone message); *itinerary* (the plan for a trip, with times).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Does a supervisor approve or pack a request?" (approve) • "Is a round-trip ticket for one way or there and back?" (there and back) • "Is a toothbrush a toiletry or a boarding pass?" (a toiletry).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (I need to pack my toiletries.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Luis asks his supervisor, Ms. Adams, for time off. Then he leaves a voicemail for his sister, Rosa, and she calls back. Listen: why does he need time off?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *wanna* = want to: "When do you **want to** take it?" *gonna* = going to: "I'm **going to** take the bus." Then practice the phone number in groups with pauses: *555 · 214 · 8830*. The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:44).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** say, "I'm going to the store." Ask, "Why?" Answer, "**To buy** milk." Write: *Why? → To + verb.* Then say, "I **enjoy cooking**. I **want to cook** for my family." Write two lists on the board: **+ to:** *want, need, plan, decide, hope, forget, would like*; **+ -ing:** *enjoy, don't mind, finish, avoid, keep*.
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *Why are you taking time off? → To visit my sister.* Box 2: verbs + *to* and verbs + *-ing*. Box 3: the two lists. Box 4: review (*already, Would you mind…?, going to*).
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*for see* or *to see*?" (*to see*) • "*enjoy to travel* or *enjoy traveling*?" (*enjoy traveling*) • "*want to visit* or *want visiting*?" (*want to visit*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A points to a picture and asks, "Why do you need a **suitcase**?" B answers with *to* + verb: "To pack my clothes." Then B asks, "What do you enjoy doing on a trip?" Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *Why are you taking time off? → To visit my sister. • I want to go. I enjoy traveling. • I'm calling to ask about my vacation days.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Trip Planner + Voicemail Relay · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** read trip card A: "Visit family in another state for 4 days." Say, "I'm going to Dallas to visit my cousins. I want to see the new baby. I enjoy cooking with my aunt." Then model a voicemail: "Hi, it's Ms. Lee. I'm calling to tell you about my trip. I'm leaving on Friday. Call me back at 555 · 301 · 7720."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Trip Planner (pairs).** Pairs choose a trip card. They plan why (*to…*), what they want to do and enjoy doing, and what to pack. Then they fill in the time-off request.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Voicemail Relay (back to back).** A leaves a voicemail of 30–45 seconds: name, reason for calling (*to…*), dates, and an invented callback number. B takes notes and reads them back. Switch.
- **15 learners:** pairs; then back-to-back rows.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs; then one rotation.
- **5 or fewer:** pairs; you check the notes.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A time-off policy + a packing list · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the invented company policy and Luis's packing list on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write an email (5–8 sentences) to a supervisor asking for time off: the dates, the reason (*to…*), who can cover, and thanks. Show the example. **Writing check 4:** collect this email and write one strength and one fix. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *Where would you like to go on a trip, and why?* Example on the board: *I would like to go to the beach to relax. I enjoy swimming in the ocean. It's also a good place for my family, because my kids love the water.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *I / enjoy / travel / by bus → I enjoy traveling by bus.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L11_listening.mp3` (about 1:44; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play `L4_L11_listening.mp3` from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson eleven. Luis asks his supervisor for time off. Then he leaves a voicemail for his sister.
> **Luis:** Ms. Adams, do you have a minute? I'd like to ask for some time off.
> **Ms. Adams:** Sure. When do you want to take the time off?
> **Luis:** I need June fifth to June seventh. That's three days.
> **Ms. Adams:** And why are you taking time off?
> **Luis:** To visit my mother in San Antonio. She is turning seventy.
> **Ms. Adams:** How nice! Did you fill out the request form?
> **Luis:** Yes, I've already filled it out. Here it is.
> **Ms. Adams:** Thanks. Who can cover your shifts?
> **Luis:** Anna has agreed to cover Thursday and Friday. I'm still looking for someone for Saturday.
> **Ms. Adams:** Ask Ben. He doesn't mind working on weekends. Then I can approve it.
> **Luis:** Great. Thank you, Ms. Adams.
> **Luis:** Hi, Rosa, it's Luis. I'm calling to tell you about my trip. I'm going to take the bus on Thursday, June fifth. It's bus number forty-two, and it gets to San Antonio at six thirty in the evening. Can you pick me up at the bus station? Also, don't forget to bring Mom's birthday card. I want everyone to sign it. Call me back at five five five, two one four, eight eight three zero. Bye!
> **Rosa:** Hi, Luis! I got your message. Yes, I can pick you up at six thirty.
> **Luis:** Thanks, Rosa. Mom doesn't know I'm coming, right?
> **Rosa:** No, it's a surprise. She's going to be so happy. I enjoy planning surprises!
> **Luis:** Me too. See you on Thursday!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (to visit his mother).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 5 to 7, 2 birthday, 3 Thursday and Friday, 4 Ben, 5 42, 6 6:30 p.m..
- *Listening Task C:* 1 to, 2 working, 3 to, 4 to, 5 to, 6 planning.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 to, 2 traveling, 3 to, 4 to, 5 working, 6 already.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 to ask, 2 to visit, 3 packing, 4 to see, 5 taking, 6 covering.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 I enjoy traveling by bus. 2 We're planning to leave on Friday. 3 I'm calling to ask about my vacation days. 4 Don't forget to pack your charger. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 them, 2 it, 3 up, 4 sure, 5 to.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 At least two weeks ahead. • 2 From your supervisor or in the break room. • 3 Within three days. • 4 5. • 5 A birthday card. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "I would like to go to the beach to relax. I enjoy swimming in the ocean. It's also a good place for my family, because my kids love the water."

### Teacher notes

- **Trips can be invented:** don't assume learners can travel or have paid vacation days. A day trip in the city is always fine.
- **No passport, visa, or immigration topics.** Keep trips to visiting family, a lake, or a city.
- **Time-off rules vary by employer:** the policy is invented. Learners can check their own workplace rules.
- **Writing check 4 (Week 12):** collect the time-off email. Write one strength and one fix on each. Learners keep them in their writing folder.
- **Phone numbers are invented** and use 555. Practice saying them in groups with pauses (3-3-4).
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms, and they never write them.

## Lesson 11 (Week 12) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *to* + verb answers *Why?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Why are you taking** | time off? | ‖ | **To visit** | my sister in Houston. |
| **Why are you going** | to Houston? | ‖ | I'm going there **to see** | my family. |
| **Why are you** | calling? | ‖ | I'm calling **to ask** | about my vacation days. |

### Grammar Box 2: verbs + *to* and verbs + *-ing*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What do you want** | to do? | ‖ | I **want to visit** | the lake. |
| **What do you enjoy** | doing on a trip? | ‖ | I **enjoy taking** | pictures. |
| **What did you decide** | to do? | ‖ | We **decided to take** | the bus. |
| **Do you mind** | driving? | ‖ | No, I **don't mind driving.** | It's fine. |

**Rule of thumb:** There is no rule for which verbs take *to* and which take *-ing*: learn the two short lists in Box 3. *to* + verb also answers *Why?* (***To visit my sister.***)

### Questions and answers

**Q: *for see* or *to see*?**

A: Use *to*: ***I'm going to Houston to see my sister.*** (not *for see*, not *for to see*)

**Q: *enjoy to travel* or *enjoy traveling*?**

A: ***enjoy traveling.*** After *enjoy*, use *-ing*.

**Q: How do I know: *to* or *-ing*?**

A: Look at the two lists in Box 3. Learn them like vocabulary.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I went to Houston for see my sister. | I went to Houston **to see** my sister. |
| I enjoy to travel. | I enjoy **traveling**. |
| I want visit my family. | I want **to** visit my family. |
| I'm planning leave on Friday. | I'm planning **to** leave on Friday. |
| Don't forget pack your toiletries. | Don't forget **to** pack your toiletries. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** Spanish uses *para* for purpose: *Voy a Houston para ver a mi hermana.* English uses only *to*: *I'm going to Houston to see my sister* (not *for see*, not *for to see*). After *enjoy*, use *-ing*: *I enjoy traveling* (not *enjoy to travel*). How do you say *why* in your language?
