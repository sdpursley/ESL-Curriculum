## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you ask or answer first? How many sentences do you say?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 1 (Week 1): Have You Ever…?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask *Have you ever…?* and answer *Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. / No, never.*
- say *I've never… / She's lived…* with *have / has* + a past participle
- ask a follow-up in the simple past (*When did you…? How was it?*)
- tell a classmate about one experience in 3 sentences (the experience, when, how it was)
- write a short introduction email to the teacher (5–8 sentences)
:::

:::box Materials
- Student Pages for Lesson 1 (print for the number expected + 3 spares)
- Board and markers; tape for a timeline across the board
- Name tags or folded paper and markers
- Can-Do Self-Check page (in the workbook) and the baseline snapshot notes (see Teacher notes)
- Listening: **no recording yet.** Read the listening script aloud (with a volunteer if you can), at a natural speed, two times.
- **Prep time:** 15 minutes (read the plan; draw the "my life → now" timeline on the board; practice reading the listening script).
:::

:::box Key vocabulary (with pictures on the New Words page)
★ try (a new food) • ★ travel • ★ fly • ★ snow • ★ ocean • ★ mountain • ★ desert • ★ win a prize • ★ ride a horse • ★ run a race • go camping • see a famous person

*Also:* ever, never, in common, both, a goal, a tent, sushi, a 5K (a 5-kilometer race), exciting, boring.
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Welcome, orientation, and Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *Welcome! Level 4 · 6:00–7:30 · Break 6:45 · Homework every week (15–20 minutes)* and the question *What did you do last weekend?*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you ask, "What did you do last weekend?" The learner answers. Help them add a reason and a detail: "*I went to the park. I went because it was sunny. My kids played soccer.*" Then you ask a follow-up: "*How was it?*"
- **Then (3 min):** Pairs fill in the box at the bottom of the Opening Talk page: the partner's name and one thing the partner did last weekend. Two or three pairs introduce their partner to the class: "*This is Rosa. She went to a birthday party.*"
- **Welcome and orientation (2 min, first):** say your name and write it on the board. Show the workbook: "This is your book for tonight. Every week has the same parts." Point to the times on the board. Say: "We speak English in class. It's OK to make mistakes. Mistakes help us learn." Give out name tags.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *ocean* (a wave, very big water) vs. a lake; *desert* (hot, dry, sand); *win a prize* (first place, a trophy); *ride a horse* (sit on it). Mime *fly*, *run a race*, and *ride*.
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Is the ocean salty or sweet?" (salty) • "Is a desert hot and dry or cold and wet?" (hot and dry) • "Do you sleep in a tent when you go camping?" (yes).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (*I've seen the ocean. / I've never ridden a horse.*). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **Show the two new pages (1 min):** the **My Words** page (a personal word list, optional) and the **Task Words** page at the back (optional). Say: "These pages are for you. Use them if you want."
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "This is the first night of an English class. Lucía and Ahmed meet. Then the teacher, Ms. Grant, gives them a job. Listen: what have they both done?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *gonna* = going to: "Tonight we're **going to** find things we have in common." *wanna* = want to: "I **want to** go camping." Say: "People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. This listening uses *going to* and *want to*." Then practice the weak, short *have* (it sounds like "h'v"): *Have you ever seen snow?* and the contraction: *I've never ridden a horse.* The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **No recording yet:** read the script aloud with a volunteer (about 2:06), at a natural speed, two times. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words. When the audio is ready, it plays the conversation once; press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** draw a long line on the board: "my life → now." Put three X's on the line with no dates: "I've seen snow. I've run a race. I've never ridden a horse." Then write a date under one X: "I ran a race **in 2023**." Say: "No time → *have* + past participle. A time → simple past."
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *Have you ever worked in a restaurant? → Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.* Point out *Has* with *he / she*. Box 2: the follow-up in the simple past: *When did you go there? → I went in 2022. How was it? → It was exciting.* Box 3: read the 20 verbs together once; learners circle 5 they want to learn first. Box 4: which one? No time or a time.
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*I have seen snow in 2022.* Right or wrong?" (wrong: *I saw snow in 2022.*) • "*Have you ever eat sushi?* Right or wrong?" (wrong: *eaten*) • "*She's lived in three cities.* *She's* = she is or she has?" (she has).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A asks *Have you ever…?* with a word from the New Words page. B answers *Yes, I have* + a past detail (*I… in…*), or *No, never.* A asks *How was it?* Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *Have you ever…? → Yes, I have. I… (last year). / No, I've never…* and *When did you…? How was it?*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Find Someone Who Has… + Two True, One Invented · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** take the sheet and ask one learner, "Have you ever flown in a plane?" When they say yes, ask two follow-ups: "When did you fly? How was it?" Write their name on your sheet. Say: "Two follow-ups. Then write the name."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Find Someone Who Has… (mingle).** Learners stand with the Talk Time sheet and ask classmates. When someone says *Yes*, they ask two follow-ups in the past (*When? Where? How was it?*) and write the name and a short note. One name per classmate, then move on.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Two True, One Invented (groups).** Each learner writes three sentences on the second Talk Time page: two true and one invented (*I've… / I've never…*). The others in the group ask follow-up questions and then guess the invented one.
- **15 learners:** whole-room mingle for round 1; then 5 groups of 3 for round 2.
- **7–8 learners:** mingle in one circle for round 1; then 2 groups for round 2.
- **5 or fewer:** one group. In round 1, each person asks every other person 2 prompts. Round 2 in the same group.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **Baseline snapshot (during Talk Time):** listen to about 5 learners for 1 minute each (a different 5 each week until you have heard everyone). For each one, note: 3 connected sentences? a follow-up question? any present perfect? Use the snapshot notes in the Teacher notes. This is not a test; don't tell learners a score.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: Meet Our Class + an email to the teacher (+ Can-Do Self-Check) · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the three profiles on the Read & Write page. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write an email to you (5–8 sentences): who they are, two things they've done, one thing they've never done, and one goal for this class with a reason. Show the example email. Invented details are fine. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **Can-Do Self-Check (3 min, 7:22–7:25):** read each line on the Can-Do Self-Check page aloud. Learners check one face for each line and write one goal. Collect it with the exit ticket. This page comes back in Weeks 8 and 15.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *Tell me about something you have done (or never done).* Example on the board: *I've ridden a horse. I rode one in 2015 on my uncle's farm. It was exciting, but I was scared because the horse was big.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *I / never / see / the ocean → I've never seen the ocean.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script (not recorded yet; about 2:06 when recorded; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** the recording is coming soon. Until then, read the script aloud two times, with a volunteer if you can, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson one. It's the first night of an English class at a community center. Lucía and Ahmed meet before class.
> **Ahmed:** Hi, I'm Ahmed. Is this seat free?
> **Lucía:** Sure. I'm Lucía. Nice to meet you. Is this your first class here?
> **Ahmed:** Yes, it is. Have you ever taken a class at this center?
> **Lucía:** No, I haven't. But my sister has. What kind of work do you do?
> **Ahmed:** I work at a warehouse. Have you ever worked in a restaurant?
> **Lucía:** Yes, I have. I worked in a pizza restaurant in 2019. It was hard, but I liked it.
> **Ms. Grant:** Good evening! I'm Ms. Grant, your teacher. Tonight we're going to find things we have in common.
> **Ms. Grant:** Lucía and Ahmed, find two things you've both done.
> **Lucía:** OK. Ahmed, have you ever seen snow?
> **Ahmed:** Yes! I saw snow for the first time last winter. It was cold, but beautiful.
> **Lucía:** Me too. I've seen snow two times. Have you ever ridden a horse?
> **Ahmed:** No, never. I've never ridden a horse. Have you?
> **Lucía:** Yes, I have.
> **Ahmed:** Really? When did you do that?
> **Lucía:** When I was ten, on my uncle's farm. OK, snow is one thing. Have you ever run a race?
> **Ahmed:** Yes, I ran a 5k last year. I didn't win, but I finished!
> **Lucía:** I've run a race, too. My company has a race every summer.
> **Ahmed:** Great. So we have both seen snow, and we have both run a race.
> **Ms. Grant:** Good job! Now, what have you never done, but you want to try?
> **Ahmed:** I want to go camping. I've never slept in a tent.
> **Lucía:** And I want to try sushi. I've never eaten it.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **b** (seen snow and run a race).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 **False** (No, she hasn't; her sister has) • 2 **True** • 3 **True** • 4 **False** (he has never ridden a horse) • 5 **False** (he didn't win, but he finished) • 6 **True**.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 Have, 2 has, 3 Have, 4 did, 5 have, 6 has.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 eaten, 2 ridden, 3 lived, 4 flown, 5 seen, 6 won.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 Have, 2 went, 3 have, 4 did you go, 5 has never, 6 saw.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 I've never seen the ocean. 2 Has she ever ridden a horse? 3 We've flown to Miami two times. 4 He went camping last summer. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 went, 2 ate, 3 didn't work, 4 Did / watch, 5 was.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 For six years. • 2 In a hospital kitchen. • 3 Because he drives a truck. • 4 He's never flown in a plane. • 5 She ran her first race (a 5K). • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "I've ridden a horse. I rode one in 2015 on my uncle's farm. It was exciting, but I was scared because the horse was big."

### Teacher notes

- **Learners choose what to share.** Places, foods, and jobs are always safe topics. Never ask how or when learners came to the U.S. Invented answers are fine in every activity.
- **The timeline:** keep the "my life → now" line on the board all night. X's with no dates = present perfect; a date under an X = simple past.
- **Participles:** learners don't need all 20 tonight. Ask them to circle 5 in Box 3 and learn those first. The list comes back in Lessons 2 and 3.
- **Baseline snapshot:** for each learner you hear (about 5 tonight), note three things: (1) 3 connected sentences? (2) a follow-up question? (3) any present perfect? Keep the notes private. Hear everyone by Week 3. The same notes help in Weeks 8 and 15.
- **Can-Do Self-Check:** the faces are 😀 I can do it, 🙂 with a little help, 😐 not yet. Read each line aloud. Keep the pages; learners compare in Week 8.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms; they can always say the full forms.

## Lesson 1 (Week 1) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *Have you ever…?* (sometime in your life)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Have** you **ever** | worked in a restaurant? | ‖ | Yes, I **have**. | I**'ve** worked in two restaurants. |
| ^ | ^ | ‖ | No, I **haven't**. / No, **never**. | I**'ve never** worked in a restaurant. |
| **Has** she **ever** | seen snow? | ‖ | Yes, she **has**. | She**'s** seen snow two times. |
| **Has** he **ever** | lived in a big city? | ‖ | No, he **hasn't**. | He**'s never** lived in a big city. |

### Grammar Box 2: the details (simple past)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Have** you **ever** | been to Chicago? | ‖ | Yes, I **have**. | I **went** there in 2022. |
| **When did** you | go there? | ‖ | I **went** | last summer. / in 2022. |
| **How was** | it? | ‖ | It **was** | exciting. / a little boring. |
| **Did** you | like it? | ‖ | Yes, I **did**. / No, I **didn't**. | It was too cold! |

**Rule of thumb:** sometime in your life, **no time** → *have / has* + past participle (*I've seen snow*). **Add a time** (*in 2022, last year, when I was ten*) → simple past (*I saw snow last year*).

### Grammar Box 3: 20 verbs to know (verb → past → **past participle**)

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| be → was / were → **been** | go → went → **gone / been** | ride → rode → **ridden** | travel → traveled → **traveled** |
| do → did → **done** | have → had → **had** | run → ran → **run** | try → tried → **tried** |
| drive → drove → **driven** | live → lived → **lived** | see → saw → **seen** | win → won → **won** |
| eat → ate → **eaten** | make → made → **made** | swim → swam → **swum** | work → worked → **worked** |
| fly → flew → **flown** | meet → met → **met** | take → took → **taken** | write → wrote → **written** |

*Regular verbs (live, work, travel): past participle = past form (*-ed*).* • ***I've*** *= I have.* • ***She's lived*** *= she **has** lived (not she is).*

### Grammar Box 4: Which one?

| | WHEN? | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *have / has* + past participle | sometime in your life; **no time** | *I've ridden a horse. She's never flown.* |
| simple past | a **finished time** (*in 2015, last year, when I was ten*) | *I rode a horse in 2015. She flew to Texas last year.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *I have seen snow in 2022*?**

A: No. With a finished time, use the simple past: ***I saw snow in 2022.***

**Q: Why *eaten*, not *eat*?**

A: After *have / has*, use the **past participle**: ***Have you ever eaten sushi?*** Look at Box 3.

**Q: How do I answer *Have you ever…?* short?**

A: ***Yes, I have.*** or ***No, I haven't.*** or ***No, never.*** Then add a detail in the past: ***Yes, I have. I went there in 2022.***

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I have seen snow last year. | I **saw** snow last year. |
| Have you ever eat sushi? | Have you ever **eaten** sushi? |
| Yes, I have seen. | Yes, I **have**. (+ a detail: *I saw it in 2022.*) |
| She have lived in two cities. | She **has** lived in two cities. |
| I never have flown. | I've **never** flown. / I have **never** flown. |
