## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Is it still true now? Is it a strong idea or a soft idea?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *Have you… yet?* and *already / just*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you ask, "Have you done your laundry yet this week?" The learner answers with *already* or *not yet*. Then you ask, "When did you do it?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*already / yet / just* sentences) and read their to-do list (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *I've finished it yesterday* (→ *I finished it yesterday*) and *Have you yet…?* (→ *yet* at the end)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *checkup* (a visit when you are well); *health history* (a form: your past illnesses, surgery, allergies); *symptom* (what you feel: a cough, a rash); *allergy* (*I'm allergic to peanuts*); *blood pressure* (mime the arm cuff: *120 over 80*); *dizzy* (mime the room turning); *teeth cleaning* and *cavity* (a hole in a tooth); *flu shot*; *prescription* (paper from the doctor for medicine); *copay* (the money you pay at each visit).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Do you get a checkup when you are sick or when you are well?" (when you are well; it's every year) • "Is a cough a symptom?" (yes) • "Do you pay a copay at the clinic or at home?" (at the clinic).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (How long have you had the cough? → I've had it since Sunday.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Amina is at a clinic. First, Daniel, the medical assistant, asks her some questions. Then she talks to Ms. Park, the receptionist. Listen: why is Amina at the clinic?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *gonna* = going to: "The doctor's **going to** see you in a few minutes." *wanna* = want to: "I **want to** make an appointment." Then practice *Why don't you…?* as one smooth group with falling intonation (a suggestion, not a real question), and the stress: *I've had a RASH since MONday.* The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **No recording yet:** read the script aloud with a volunteer (about 2:07), at a natural speed, two times. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words. When the audio is ready, it plays the conversation once; press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** say, "I have a cough. It started on Sunday. Today is Thursday." Write: *I've **had** a cough **since** Sunday. I've had it **for** four days.* Then hold your back: "My back hurts." Point to a learner and help them say, "**Why don't you** call the clinic?" Write three suggestions on the board.
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *How long have you had this rash? → I've had it for three days.* and *Have you ever had surgery? → Yes, I had knee surgery in 2018.* Box 2: a problem → a suggestion. Box 3: how to say a symptom. Box 4: strong or soft?
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*I have a rash since Monday.* Right or wrong?" (wrong: *I've had a rash since Monday.*) • "*You could to try* or *You could try*?" (*You could try*) • "Is *Why don't you call?* a real question?" (no, it's a suggestion).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A says a problem from Box 2. B gives a suggestion with *Why don't you… / You could…* A says, "Good idea." Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *How long have you had it? → I've had it for… / since… • Why don't you…? / You could… / It's a good idea to…*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Clinic Intake + Good Idea Circle · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** sit back to back with a learner. You are the medical assistant: "What brings you in today?" The learner reads a symptom card: "I have a rash on my arm." You ask, "How long have you had it?" Then switch: the learner asks you. Say: "Answer, then add one more detail."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Clinic Intake (pairs, back to back).** A is the medical assistant and asks the 8 form questions; B chooses a symptom card and answers (invented answers). A writes short notes. Then switch, with a new card.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Good Idea Circle (circles of 5).** One learner reads a problem card. The next two learners each give a suggestion. The first learner chooses one and says why: *"Good idea. I'm going to… because…"* Then the next learner reads a card.
- **15 learners:** pairs for round 1; then 3 circles of 5 for round 2.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs for round 1; then one circle for round 2.
- **5 or fewer:** pairs (you join if there is an odd number) for round 1. In round 2, make one circle; you add problem cards and suggest first.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A clinic letter + an insurance card · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the letter from the clinic and the insurance card on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Point to *copay* and *deductible* on the card. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write an email to a friend (5–8 sentences). The friend has had a backache for a week. Ask how long, give two suggestions with reasons, and say what to bring to the clinic. Show the example. Invented details are fine. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *A friend has had a backache for a week. What do you say?* Example on the board: *How long have you had it? Why don't you call the clinic? It's a good idea because a week is a long time.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *she / have / a rash / ___ / three days → She's had a rash for three days.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script (not recorded yet; about 2:07 when recorded; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** the recording is coming soon. Until then, read the script aloud two times, with a volunteer if you can, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson four. Amina is at a clinic. First, she talks to Daniel, the medical assistant.
> **Daniel:** Hi, Amina. I'm Daniel. I'm going to ask you a few questions for your health history. What brings you in today?
> **Amina:** My lower back hurts. It isn't very bad, but it hasn't stopped.
> **Daniel:** I'm sorry to hear that. How long have you had the back pain?
> **Amina:** I've had it for about two weeks. It started when I moved some heavy boxes.
> **Daniel:** OK. Have you ever had back problems before?
> **Amina:** No, never. This is the first time.
> **Daniel:** Have you ever had surgery?
> **Amina:** Yes, I had knee surgery in 2018.
> **Daniel:** Thank you. Do you have any allergies?
> **Amina:** Yes, I'm allergic to penicillin. I get a rash.
> **Daniel:** I'll write that down. Have you tried anything for the pain?
> **Amina:** I've used a heating pad a few times. It helps a little.
> **Daniel:** Have you felt dizzy?
> **Amina:** No, I haven't.
> **Daniel:** Good. Now let me check your blood pressure. It looks normal. The doctor's going to see you in a few minutes.
> **Narrator:** Later, at checkout, Amina talks to Ms. Park, the receptionist.
> **Ms. Park:** Hi, Amina. Your copay today is twenty dollars.
> **Amina:** OK. Here's my new insurance card. I've had it since January.
> **Ms. Park:** Thank you. Also, you haven't had a teeth cleaning for a year, and you haven't had a checkup since last spring. It's a good idea to get one every six months.
> **Amina:** You're right. I want to make an appointment with the dentist.
> **Ms. Park:** Why don't you call the dental office tomorrow? Here's the number. And you could get a flu shot here next week.
> **Amina:** Good idea. Thank you!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

- *Listening Task B:* 1 back pain, 2 about two weeks, 3 no, 4 penicillin, 5 no, 6 $20.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 for, 2 ever, 3 ever, 4 since, 5 for, 6 since.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 had, 2 call, 3 felt, 4 try, 5 get, 6 had.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 've had, 2 try, 3 Why don't you, 4 for, 5 had, 6 to get.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 She's had a rash for three days. 2 Why don't you call the dentist? 3 You could try a heating pad. 4 Has he ever had surgery? *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 already, 2 yet, 3 just, 4 yet, 5 already.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 In March 2025. • 2 Every six months. • 3 Her insurance card, a photo ID, and a list of her medicines. • 4 $20. • 5 $1,000. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "How long have you had it? Why don't you call the clinic? It's a good idea because a week is a long time."

### Teacher notes

- **Keep health invented and mild.** All patients and symptoms are invented. Never ask learners about their own health, medicines, or insurance. If a learner shares something, thank them and move on.
- **Real health questions:** say, "For a real health question, ask your doctor or pharmacist. In an emergency, call 911." It is printed on the Good Idea Circle page.
- **Insurance:** stay at "read the card; know *copay* and *deductible*." A copay is what you pay at each visit; a deductible is what you pay first in a year before the plan pays more. Don't explain plans or prices.
- **for / since:** *for* + a length of time (*three days*); *since* + a start (*Monday, January*). With a finished time (*in 2018*), use the simple past.
- ***Why don't you…?*** is a suggestion, not a real question. Model falling intonation. Learners answer *Good idea* or *Thanks, I will*, not *Because…*.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms; they can always say the full forms.

## Lesson 4 (Week 4) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *How long…? / Have you ever…?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **How long have** you | **had** this rash? | ‖ | I**'ve had** it | **for** three days. |
| ^ | ^ | ‖ | ^ | **since** Monday. |
| **How long has** your back | **hurt**? | ‖ | It**'s hurt** | **for** about two weeks. |
| **Have** you **ever** | **had** surgery? | ‖ | No, **never**. / Yes, I **have**. | I **had** knee surgery in 2018. |
| **Have** you | **tried** anything for it? | ‖ | Yes, I **have**. | I**'ve used** a heating pad. |

### Grammar Box 2: a problem → a suggestion

<!-- subst -->
| PROBLEM | < | ‖ | SUGGESTION | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| My back hurts. | What can I do? | ‖ | **Why don't you** | **call** the clinic? |
| I sit all day at work. | Any ideas? | ‖ | You **could** | **take** a short walk at lunch. |
| I haven't had a checkup | for two years. | ‖ | **It's a good idea to** | **get** one every year. |
| My tooth has hurt | since Friday. | ‖ | You **should** | **see** a dentist soon. |

**Rule of thumb:** Use ***have / has* + past participle** + *for / since* for a problem that started in the past and is **still true**. After ***Why don't you, You could, It's a good idea to,*** and ***should***, use the base verb (*call, try, get*).

---
### Grammar Box 3: how to say a symptom

| SAY | EXAMPLES |
|---|---|
| *I have / I've had* + a noun | *a rash, a cough, a backache, a sore throat* → *I've had a cough since Sunday.* |
| *My* ___ *hurts / has hurt* | *My back hurts. My tooth has hurt for a week.* |
| *I feel / I've felt* + a word | *dizzy, tired* → *I've felt tired for three days.* |

### Grammar Box 4: strong or soft?

| | HOW STRONG? | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *You should…* | **strong** advice | *You should see a doctor.* |
| *It's a good idea to…* | a **general** idea (good for everyone) | *It's a good idea to get a flu shot every year.* |
| *Why don't you…? / You could…* | a **soft** idea; the person chooses | *Why don't you try a heating pad? You could stretch.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: Can I say *I have a rash since Monday*?**

A: No. It started in the past and is still true: ***I've had a rash since Monday.***

**Q: Is *Why don't you call the clinic?* a real question?**

A: No. It's a suggestion. Answer: ***Good idea. / Thanks, I will.*** Your voice goes down at the end.

**Q: *You could to try* or *You could try*?**

A: After *could*, use the base verb: ***You could try a heating pad.***
