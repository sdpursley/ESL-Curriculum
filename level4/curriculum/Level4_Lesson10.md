## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "How can you save energy and stay safe at home?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 10 (Week 11): Turn It Off

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- use phrasal verbs: *turn off, turn down, turn up, put on, put away, take out, throw away, shut off*
- put *it / them* in the right place (*turn it off*, not *turn off it*)
- give tips to save energy and stay safe at home (*Make sure you… / Don't forget to…*)
- read the main parts of a utility bill (amount due, due date, usage)
- write an email (5–8 sentences) to a roommate or family member about changes at home

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: *opinions; agree / disagree politely; as … as*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "What do you think about homework for kids?" The learner gives an opinion and a reason. Then you disagree politely: "I see your point, but…" Help them answer with 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*as … as*) and read their opinion and reasons (item 3) to each other. The partner agrees or disagrees politely. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *I am agree* (→ *I agree*) and *not as fast than* (→ *not as fast as*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *utility bill* (the bill for electricity, gas, or water); *electricity*; *thermostat* (it controls the heat); *save energy* (use less); *unplug* (take the plug out); *charger*; *outlet* (point to one in the room); *fan*; *deadbolt* (a strong lock); *peephole* (a small hole in the door to look out); *fire extinguisher*; *usage* (how much you use).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Do you plug a charger into an outlet or a peephole?" (an outlet) • "Is a deadbolt a lock or a light?" (a lock) • "Does a thermostat control the heat or the water?" (the heat).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (Make sure you unplug the charger.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Two roommates, Sofia and Hana, look at a high electric bill and make a plan. Then Sofia calls the electric company. Listen: what is the problem?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script line. *gonna* = going to: "I'm **going to** unplug my phone charger." Then practice the stress on the small word: *turn it OFF, put it aWAY*, and the linking: *turn_it_off, take_it_out*. The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **No recording yet:** read the script aloud with a volunteer (about 2:06), at a natural speed, two times. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words. When the audio is ready, it plays the conversation once; press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** point to the lights and say, "Can you **turn off** the lights?" A learner turns them off (or mimes it). You say, "Thank you. You **turned them off**." Turn them on again. Write: *turn off the lights / turn the lights off → turn them off*. Cross out *turn off them*.
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *Can you turn off the lights? → OK, I'll turn them off.* Box 2: the past: *Did you take out the trash? → Yes, I took it out.* Box 3: the 8 phrasal verbs. Box 4: *Make sure you… / Don't forget to…*
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*turn off it* or *turn it off*?" (*turn it off*) • "Is *turn the TV off* OK?" (yes) • "It's too loud. *Turn it up* or *turn it down*?" (*turn it down*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A points to a picture and says, "Can you **unplug** the **charger**?" B mimes and answers, "OK, I'll unplug it." A says, "Don't forget to lock the **deadbolt**." Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *Can you turn off the lights? → OK, I'll turn them off. • Did you take out the trash? → Yes, I took it out. • Make sure you lock the deadbolt.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Energy Detective + Home Tips · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** read the first problem on the Apartment A card: "The lights are on in an empty room." Say to a learner, "Turn them off, please." The learner mimes and says, "OK, I'll turn them off." Do one more: "The window is open, and the heat is on." (*Close it. Turn the heat down.*)
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Energy Detective (pairs).** A reads a problem from the Apartment A card and gives an instruction (*Turn them off. Close it. Put it away.*). B mimes and answers with *it / them*. Then switch to the Apartment B card.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Home Tips (groups of 3).** Groups write 5 tips to save energy or stay safe (*Turn down the heat at night. Unplug the charger.*) and present them in 1 minute.
- **15 learners:** pairs for round 1; then 5 groups of 3.
- **7–8 learners:** pairs; then 2 groups.
- **5 or fewer:** pairs; then one poster together.
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A utility bill + a "Save energy, stay safe" flyer · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the invented bill and the flyer on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write an email (5–8 sentences) to a roommate or family member: what to change at home and why. Show the example. Check for 2 phrasal verbs and one *it* or *them*. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *What can people do at home to save energy or stay safe?* Example on the board: *Turn off the lights when you leave a room. Unplug your charger and put it away. Make sure you lock the deadbolt at night, because it keeps your home safe.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *turn down / the heat (it) → Turn it down.*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script (not recorded yet; about 2:06 when recorded; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** the recording is coming soon. Until then, read the script aloud two times, with a volunteer if you can, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson ten. The two roommates look at their power bill. Then Sofia calls the electric company.
> **Sofia:** Hana, look at this. Our power bill is one forty-two dollars this month!
> **Hana:** Last month it was only $90. Why is it so high?
> **Sofia:** Well, we use too much electricity. The heat is on all day, and the lights are on in empty rooms.
> **Hana:** You're right. I always forget to turn them off. Oh, the TV is on now, and nobody is watching. I'll turn it off.
> **Sofia:** OK, let's make a plan. First, we can turn down the heat at night. Sixty-eight degrees is fine.
> **Hana:** Good idea. And I'm going to unplug my phone charger in the morning. It uses electricity all day.
> **Sofia:** Also, let's put on sweaters and not turn up the heat. Can you call the electric company? I have class.
> **Hana:** Sorry, I have work. Can you call them tonight?
> **Sofia:** Sure. I'll call them now.
> **Paul:** Thank you for calling City Power. This is Paul. How can I help you?
> **Sofia:** Hi. I'm calling about my bill. When is it due?
> **Paul:** Can I have your account number, please?
> **Sofia:** Yes. It's four, seven, two, one, nine.
> **Paul:** Thank you. Your bill is due on March fifteenth.
> **Sofia:** OK. Also, do you have any programs to help us save energy?
> **Paul:** Yes. We have a free home energy check. Someone visits your apartment and gives you tips. And you can get free light bulbs.
> **Sofia:** That sounds great. Can I sign up?
> **Paul:** Sure. Is next Tuesday morning OK?
> **Sofia:** Yes, that's perfect. Thank you.
> **Paul:** You're welcome. And don't forget to turn off the lights when you leave a room!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (The electric bill is too high.).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 $142, 2 down, 3 phone charger, 4 sweaters, 5 March 15, 6 free.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 them, 2 it, 3 down, 4 on, 5 up, 6 off.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 them, 2 up, 3 it, 4 on, 5 sure, 6 forget.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 it off, 2 throw away, 3 down, 4 put them away, 5 to lock, 6 much.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 Turn it down. 2 I took it out. 3 Put them away. 4 Make sure you lock the door. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 agree, 2 as, 3 think, 4 point, 5 opinion.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 $142.00. • 2 March 15. • 3 There is a $10 late fee. • 4 68 degrees. • 5 Look through the peephole. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "Turn off the lights when you leave a room. Unplug your charger and put it away. Make sure you lock the deadbolt at night, because it keeps your home safe."

### Teacher notes

- **Safety stays simple and calm:** practical tips only, no fire stories. Point to where the classroom fire extinguisher is.
- **Bills are invented:** never ask about learners' own bills, rent, or money.
- **Bill-help programs vary:** list local ones on the "Keep practicing" sheet. Don't promise that a program exists in your area.
- ***it / them* in the middle:** this is the main rule. Keep *turn it off* (not *turn off it*) on the board all evening.
- **Energy Detective:** the problem cards replace a picture. Learners can also point to the My Words pictures.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to*. People often say *gonna*. Learners only need to recognize it; they can always say the full form.

## Lesson 10 (Week 11) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: phrasal verbs + *it / them*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Can you turn off** | the lights? | ‖ | OK, **I'll turn them off.** | (not *turn off them*) |
| **Can you turn** | the TV **down**? | ‖ | Sure, **I'll turn it down.** | It's loud. |
| **Can you take out** | the trash? | ‖ | Yes, **I'll take it out** | now. |

### Grammar Box 2: the past

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Did you take out** | the trash? | ‖ | Yes, **I took it out** | this morning. |
| **Did you put away** | the dishes? | ‖ | No, **I didn't put them away** | yet. |
| **Did you shut off** | the water? | ‖ | Yes, **I shut it off** | before I left. |

**Rule of thumb:** With a noun, two places are OK: ***turn off the TV / turn the TV off***. With *it / them*, only the middle: ***turn it off*** (not *turn off it*).

---
### Grammar Box 3: 8 phrasal verbs for home

| VERB | MEANING | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *turn off / turn up / turn down* | stop / more / less | *Turn off the lights. Turn the heat down at night.* |
| *put on / put away* | wear / put in its place | *Put on a sweater. Put the dishes away.* |
| *take out / throw away* | take outside / put in the trash | *Take out the trash. Throw away old food.* |
| *shut off* | stop (water, gas) | *Shut off the water.* |

### Grammar Box 4: home tips

| START | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|
| *Make sure you…* | *Make sure you lock the deadbolt at night.* |
| *Don't forget to…* | *Don't forget to unplug the charger.* |
| *We use too much…* | *We use too much electricity.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *turn off it* or *turn it off*?**

A: ***turn it off.*** With *it / them*, the small word goes after: ***turn it off, put them away.***

**Q: Is *turn off the TV* OK?**

A: Yes. With a noun, both are OK: ***turn off the TV*** and ***turn the TV off***.

**Q: *turn down* or *turn off*?**

A: *turn down* = less. *turn off* = stop. ***Turn down the heat. Turn off the lights.***
