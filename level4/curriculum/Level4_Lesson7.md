# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 7 (Week 7): Too Early or On Time?

**Status:** Complete lesson (all 13 Level 4 lessons are published).

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures + `images/CREDITS.md`). Audio: `audio/L4_L7_listening.mp3`.

> **Topic:** Expectations at work and in the community: being on time, dress codes and safety rules, invitations (RSVP, what to bring), and comparing customs respectfully. Learners use *too* and *enough*, *too much / too many*, and *must / mustn't / don't have to*. The company, the rules, and the people are invented.

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Is it OK, too much, or not enough?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: imaginary situations: *What would you do if…? / If I had…, I'd…*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "What would you do if you found $50 at work?" The learner answers with *If I found…, I'd…* Then you ask, "Why?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*If I had…, I'd…*) and read their 3 dreams (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *If I would have time…* (→ *If I had time…*) and *If I found $50, I will…* (→ *I'd…*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *on time*, *early*, *late* (draw a clock: 6:50, 7:00, 7:20); *dress code* (the rules about clothes); *uniform*; *casual* (T-shirt, jeans) vs. *formal* (a shirt and tie); *closed-toe shoes* (point to a sneaker, then mime a sandal: "no"); *name badge*; *invitation* (a party card); *RSVP* (tell the host "yes" or "no"); *host* (the person who has the party).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "Is a T-shirt casual or formal?" (casual) • "Are sandals closed-toe shoes?" (no) • "If an invitation says *RSVP by Friday*, what do you do?" (tell the host by Friday).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (Jeans are too casual for an interview.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "It's Nadia's first week at a bakery. She asks her coworker, Carmen, about the rules. Then the supervisor, Mr. Brooks, comes in. Listen: what are they talking about?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *wanna* = want to: "What do you **want to** know?" *gonna* = going to: "I'm **going to** buy some today." Then practice *too / two / to* (the same sound) and the stress in *TOO late* and *not eNOUGH*. The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:52).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** draw a clock at 7:10. Say, "My shift starts at 7:00. 7:10 is **too late**." Draw 6:55: "6:55 is **early enough**." Write: *too* = more than OK (a problem); *enough* = OK. Then show a sandal or mime one: "In a kitchen, you **must** wear closed-toe shoes. You **mustn't** wear sandals. You **don't have to** wear a tie."
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *Are jeans OK for the interview? → No, they're too casual. / They aren't formal enough.* Box 2: *too much / too many / enough* + a noun. Box 3: *must / mustn't / don't have to*. Box 4: comparing customs.
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*enough early* or *early enough*?" (*early enough*) • "*too much people* or *too many people*?" (*too many people*) • "Is a tie a rule here?" (no) "So: *You mustn't wear a tie* or *You don't have to wear a tie*?" (*don't have to*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A points to a picture and asks, "Are sandals OK for the kitchen?" B answers with *too* or *enough* and a rule with *must / mustn't*. Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *too late / early enough • too much work / too many people / enough time • must / mustn't (a rule) / don't have to (not necessary)*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Customs Compare + Dress Code Check · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** read a card: "A party starts at 7:00. When do people arrive in your hometown?" Help a learner say, "In my hometown, people usually arrive at 8:00, but here many people arrive at 7:15." Say: "Describe; don't judge. No place is better."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Customs Compare (groups).** One person reads a question card. Each person answers. The group writes 2 sentences with *In my hometown…, but here…* No place is "better."
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Dress Code Check (pairs).** Pairs read the rule card and the 6 workers. For each worker, they say *too casual*, *not safe enough*, or *OK*, and the rule with *must / mustn't / don't have to*.
- **15 learners:** 5 groups of 3 for round 1; then pairs.
- **7–8 learners:** 2 groups for round 1; then pairs.
- **5 or fewer:** one group for round 1; then pairs (you join if there is an odd number).
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: An employee handbook page · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the handbook page on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write "Tips for your first week" for a new coworker (5–8 sentences) with *must*, *don't have to*, *too*, and *enough*. Show the example. **Writing check 2:** collect this writing and write one strength and one fix. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *What is one custom here that is different from your hometown (or another place you know)?* Example on the board: *Here, people usually arrive on time for a doctor's appointment. In my hometown, people often arrive a little late. I like the custom here because I don't wait a long time.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *6:55 / early / for a 7:00 shift? (enough) → Is 6:55 early enough for a 7:00 shift?*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L7_listening.mp3` (about 1:52; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play `L4_L7_listening.mp3` from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson seven. It's Nadia's first week at a bakery. She asks her coworker, Carmen, some questions.
> **Nadia:** Carmen, can I ask you a few questions? I'm still learning the rules.
> **Carmen:** Sure, Nadia. What do you want to know?
> **Nadia:** My shift starts at seven. Is six fifty-five early enough?
> **Carmen:** Yes, that's perfect. Here, on time means five minutes early. Seven ten is too late.
> **Nadia:** OK. And what about the uniform? Do I have to wear the hat?
> **Carmen:** Yes, you must wear the hat and the apron in the kitchen. But you don't have to wear them in the break room.
> **Nadia:** Got it. What about my phone?
> **Carmen:** You mustn't use your phone in the kitchen. You can use it on your break.
> **Nadia:** And are these shoes OK? They're new.
> **Carmen:** Hmm, those are sandals. They aren't safe enough for the kitchen.
> **Mr. Brooks:** Carmen's right. Good morning, Nadia. I'm Mr. Brooks, the supervisor. You must wear closed-toe shoes. The trays are too hot, and the floor is sometimes wet.
> **Nadia:** I'm sorry. I didn't know. I'm going to buy some today.
> **Mr. Brooks:** That's OK. For today, there are extra shoes in the office. Maybe they're too big, but they're safe.
> **Nadia:** Thank you. Is there anything else?
> **Mr. Brooks:** Just one more thing. If you're going to be late, you must call me before your shift.
> **Carmen:** Don't worry, Nadia. Everyone makes mistakes in the first week. There's too much to remember!
> **Nadia:** That's true. Thanks, both of you.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (the rules at work).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 five minutes early, 2 must, 3 don't have to, 4 mustn't, 5 must, 6 call Mr. Brooks.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 enough, 2 too, 3 enough, 4 too, 5 too, 6 much.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 too, 2 enough, 3 many, 4 much, 5 enough, 6 too.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 mustn't, 2 don't have to, 3 early enough, 4 many, 5 enough time, 6 aren't safe enough.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 Is 6:55 early enough for a 7:00 shift? 2 There are too many people in the store. 3 I don't have enough time for lunch. 4 You mustn't use your phone in the kitchen. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 had, 2 were, 3 would, 4 I'll, 5 give.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 5 to 10 minutes before your shift. • 2 Call your supervisor at least one hour before your shift. • 3 No. A text is not enough. • 4 No, but they must wear a name badge. • 5 In the break room. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "Here, people usually arrive on time for a doctor's appointment. In my hometown, people often arrive a little late. I like the custom here because I don't wait a long time."

### Teacher notes

- **Describe, don't judge:** customs vary inside every country, including the U.S., and inside every family. No place is "better." Model *It's different* and *I like both*.
- **Rules come from each employer:** the bakery, the kitchen, and their rules are invented. Learners can say "At my job…" if they want; they never have to.
- ***mustn't* vs. *don't have to*:** this is the biggest mix-up. Use a thumbs-down for *mustn't* (don't do it) and a shrug for *don't have to* (you choose).
- **Writing check 2 (Week 7):** collect the first-week tips. Write one strength and one fix on each. Learners keep them in their writing folder.
- **Week 8 is the midterm.** It covers Lessons 1–7. Remind learners to bring their workbook.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*. Learners only need to recognize the short forms; they can always say the full forms.

## Lesson 7 (Week 7) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *too* + adjective, adjective + *enough*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Are** jeans OK | for the interview? | ‖ | No, they're **too casual**. | They **aren't formal enough**. |
| **Is** 6:55 | **early enough**? | ‖ | Yes, it's **early enough**. | That's perfect. |
| **Is** 7:10 OK | for a 7:00 shift? | ‖ | No, it's **too late**. | Come at 6:55. |
| **Are** sandals | **safe enough**? | ‖ | No, they **aren't safe enough**. | Wear closed-toe shoes. |

### Grammar Box 2: *too much / too many / enough* + a noun

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Do you have** | **enough** time? | ‖ | No, I have **too much** work. | I don't have **enough time**. |
| **Are there** | **enough** chairs? | ‖ | No, there are **too many** people. | We need more chairs. |
| **Do we have** | **enough** food? | ‖ | Yes, we have **enough** food. | Don't buy more. |

**Rule of thumb:** *too* = more than OK (a problem). *enough* = OK. *enough* goes **after** an adjective (*early enough*) and **before** a noun (*enough time*). *too many* + things you can count; *too much* + things you can't count.

---
### Grammar Box 3: *must / mustn't / don't have to*

| | MEANING | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *must* | it's a rule; do it | *You must wear closed-toe shoes.* |
| *mustn't* | it's against the rules; don't do it | *You mustn't use your phone in the kitchen.* |
| *don't have to* | it's not necessary; you choose | *You don't have to wear a tie.* |

### Grammar Box 4: comparing customs

| TO SAY | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|
| *In my hometown…, but here…* | *In my hometown, guests usually bring food, but here people often ask the host first.* |
| *usually / often / sometimes* | *People usually arrive on time for work.* |
| describe; don't judge | *It's different. I like both.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *enough early* or *early enough*?**

A: After an adjective: ***early enough***. Before a noun: ***enough time***.

**Q: Are *mustn't* and *don't have to* the same?**

A: No! ***mustn't*** = don't do it (it's a rule). ***don't have to*** = not necessary (you can if you want).

**Q: *too much* or *too many*?**

A: ***too many*** + things you can count (*people, chairs*). ***too much*** + things you can't count (*work, time, noise*).

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| It's enough big. | It's **big enough**. |
| There are too much people. | There are too **many** people. |
| You don't have to use your phone on the floor. (It's a rule.) | You **mustn't** use your phone on the floor. |
| You must to wear a badge. | You must **wear** a badge. |
| I have too many work. | I have too **much** work. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** Spanish *demasiado* covers *too* and *too much / too many*: *demasiado caro* = *too expensive*; *demasiada gente* = *too many people*. *Suficiente* = *enough*. English puts *enough* after an adjective: *big enough* (not *enough big*). What does your language do?
