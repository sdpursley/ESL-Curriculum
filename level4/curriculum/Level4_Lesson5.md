# Level 4 Adult ESL Curriculum · Lesson 5 (Week 5): Would You Mind…?

**Status:** Complete lesson (all 13 Level 4 lessons are published).

**Date:** October 2026

**Audience:** Program coordinators and volunteer teachers

**Companion files:** `images/` (12 vocabulary pictures + `images/CREDITS.md`). Audio: `audio/L4_L5_listening.mp3`.

> **Topic:** Asking a neighbor or coworker for a favor: getting the mail and watering the plants while you're away, a spare key, covering or switching a shift, and borrowing something. Learners ask with *Would you mind…?* and *Do you mind if I…?*, and say yes or no politely. All favors, keys, and addresses are invented.

## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Some Grammar pages have one short **contrast tip**, only where a common mix-up exists. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question. Give a reason.* An optional Task Words page is at the back of the workbook for learners who want it.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Is it a problem for you? If not, what do you say?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Three sentences, not one:** in Level 4, every Talk Time answer and every exit ticket has about three connected sentences: the answer, a reason, and a detail or example.

## Lesson 5 (Week 5): Would You Mind…?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask for a favor with *Would you mind…?*
- answer *No, not at all* (= "Yes, I'll help") or say no politely, with a reason and another idea
- ask permission with *Do you mind if I…?*
- ask a coworker to switch a shift and confirm it in a short text
- write a note to a neighbor (5–8 sentences) asking for a favor while they're away
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:08 · Opening Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group with you.
- **Before class:** write on the board: suggestions: *Why don't you…? / You could… / It's a good idea to…*
- **Opening Talk (5 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers in **3 sentences** (the answer, a reason, a detail); A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** you say, "I sit all day at work. Any ideas?" The learner gives a suggestion. Then you ask, "Why is that a good idea?" Help them say 3 sentences.
- **Homework Check (3 min):** Pairs compare Homework item 1 (*for / since* and suggestions) and read their 3 good ideas (item 3) to each other. Then they fill in the box on the Opening Talk page. Walk around. Write **2** common errors on the board (often *You could to try* (→ *You could try*) and *I have a cough since Sunday* (→ *I've had a cough since Sunday*)) and correct them together. Also put up 2 corrections from last week's exit tickets.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:08–6:15 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show the hard words:** *favor* (help you ask for); *borrow* (take and give back) vs. *lend* (give and get back); *water the plants*, *feed the cat*, *get the mail* (mime each); *spare key* (an extra key: show two keys); *package*; *switch shifts* (you take my Friday, I take your Sunday) vs. *cover a shift* (you work my shift); *be away* (not at home); *owe* (*I owe you one* = I'll help you next time); *thank-you note*.
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask "If I borrow your pen, do I give it back?" (yes) • "If you cover my shift, who works?" (you do) • "Is a spare key the first key or an extra key?" (an extra key).
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (Would you mind getting my mail? → No, not at all.). Switch. **15 learners:** finish with a quick team round (3 teams; you say a clue, the first team with the word gets a point). **7–8:** 2 teams. **5 or fewer:** one team against the clock.
- **My Words:** "Write any word you want in your language on the My Words page. It's your personal list. It's optional." Learners do it at home.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:30 · Listening + "Hear it, say it" · 15 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check. With 5 or fewer, check together as a group.
- **Before you listen (2 min):** say: "Patricia is going away for a week. First, she talks to her neighbor, Mr. Wilson. Then, at work, her coworker Omar asks her for a favor. Listen: what favors do they ask for?"
- **Play 1 → Task A** (the main idea). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Hear it, say it" (3 min):** Point to the script lines. *gonna* = going to: "I'm **going to** be away next week." *wanna* = want to: "I **want to** help, but I can't." Then practice *Would you mind…?* and *Do you mind if I…?* said smoothly, with a friendly rise. The class repeats the two lines twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:54).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** give a learner your key. Say, "I'm going to be away. **Would you mind getting** my mail?" Show a thumbs-up and help the learner say, "**No, not at all.**" Write: *Would you mind + -ing?* → *No* = "No problem. I'll do it." Then ask, "**Do you mind if I open** the window?" Help them say, "No, go ahead."
- **The boxes (5 min):** Box 1: read across: *Would you mind getting my mail? → No, not at all. I'll do it. / Sorry, I can't.* Box 2: permission: *Do you mind if I…? → No, go ahead.* Box 3: saying yes and saying no politely. Box 4: how polite?
- **Rule of thumb and Questions and answers (2 min):** read the rule of thumb aloud. Read each Q and A; the class reads the right sentence after you.
- **Check questions:** "*Would you mind helping me?* You want to help. Do you say *yes* or *no*?" (*No, not at all.*) • "*Would you mind to get* or *getting*?" (*getting*) • "*Do you mind if I leaving* or *if I leave*?" (*if I leave*).
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers. Read the Common mistakes table together: "Don't say… Say…".
- **Pair check (last 3 min):** A asks a favor from the pictures with *Would you mind…?* B answers with a thumbs-up and *No, not at all*, or *Sorry, I can't, because…* Switch. **15 learners:** swap partners once. **5 or fewer:** you join one pair.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: *Would you mind + -ing? → No, not at all. (= I'll help) / Sorry, I can't, because… • Do you mind if I…? → No, go ahead.*
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:15 · Talk Time: Favor Exchange + Shift Switch Text · 15 min
- **Model it first (1 min):** hold up a favor card and ask a learner, "Would you mind getting my mail next week?" Help them check their "my week" card and answer, "No, not at all," or "Sorry, I can't, because I'll be away. Why don't you ask Ana?" Say: "Answer, give a reason, and offer an idea."
- **Round 1 (7 min):** **Favor Exchange (mingle).** Each learner has a favor card and a "my week" card. Ask three people. Each person answers *No, not at all* or *Sorry, I can't, because…* and offers another idea. Write who said yes.
- **Round 2 (6 min):** **Shift Switch Text (pairs).** Pairs write a 3-sentence text that confirms a shift switch (the day, the time, thanks). Then they read it to another pair. Remind them: "Ask your supervisor first."
- **15 learners:** a whole-room mingle for round 1; then pairs for round 2.
- **7–8 learners:** two lines face each other; move one place each round. Then pairs.
- **5 or fewer:** a circle for round 1: each person asks the person on the left. Then pairs (you join if there is an odd number).
- **Every answer needs a reason; every partner asks a follow-up.** Walk around and listen. Note 2 good sentences to praise and 1 common error for next week.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:15–7:25 · Read & Write: A building notice + a neighbor's note · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs read and answer; individual writing. With 5 or fewer, read the text aloud together.
- **Read (4 min):** Learners read the notice about packages and the note on the Read & Write page and answer the questions. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers." Pairs answer questions 1–5 together.
- **Check (1 min):** go over the answers (answer key).
- **Write (5 min):** Learners write a note to a neighbor (5–8 sentences) asking for a favor while they're away: the dates, what to do, where the key is, thanks, and an offer to help back. Show the example. Invented details are fine. Learners start in class and finish at home (Homework item 2).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:25–7:30 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket (3 min):** learners open the Exit Ticket page and answer in **3 sentences** with a reason: *Ask a classmate for a favor with *Would you mind…?** Example on the board: *Would you mind helping me with my homework? No, not at all. I'm asking because the last question is hard.* With 5 or fewer, a learner can say it to you first and then write it.
- **Collect the pages.** After class, sort them into ✓ / almost / not yet. Start next week with 2 corrections on the board (no names).
- **Homework (2 min):** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (item 1: *you / mind / feed / my cat? → Would you mind feeding my cat?*). Say: "About 15–20 minutes. Finish your Read & Write writing at home."
:::

### Listening script: `L4_L5_listening.mp3` (about 1:54; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play `L4_L5_listening.mp3` from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a natural, friendly speed. The script says *going to* and *want to*. People often say *gonna* and *wanna*; learners only need to recognize those words.

> **Narrator:** Lesson five. Patricia is going away for a week. First, she talks to her neighbor, Mr. Wilson.
> **Patricia:** Hi, Mr. Wilson. Could I ask you a favor?
> **Mr. Wilson:** Of course, Patricia. What is it?
> **Patricia:** I'm going to be away next week. I'm visiting my sister. Would you mind getting my mail?
> **Mr. Wilson:** No, not at all. I'm home all week.
> **Patricia:** Thank you! And would you mind watering my plants? Just two times, on Tuesday and Friday.
> **Mr. Wilson:** That is no problem. Where are you going to leave the key?
> **Patricia:** I'm going to give you my spare key tonight. Oh, and would you mind feeding my cat, too?
> **Mr. Wilson:** Sure, I'll do it. I love cats. Do you mind if I put your packages inside, too?
> **Patricia:** No, go ahead. That's very kind.
> **Mr. Wilson:** Have a good trip. Don't worry about anything.
> **Narrator:** The next day at work, Patricia talks with Omar.
> **Omar:** Hey, Patricia. Would you mind covering my Saturday shift? My son has a soccer game.
> **Patricia:** Oh, I'm sorry, I can't. I'll be away on Saturday. I want to help, but I can't.
> **Omar:** Oh, right. You told me.
> **Patricia:** Why don't you ask Lena? She wants more hours this month.
> **Omar:** Good idea. Do you mind if I tell her you suggested it?
> **Patricia:** No, not at all. But ask the supervisor first, OK?
> **Omar:** Sure. Thanks, Patricia. I owe you one.
> **Patricia:** You're welcome. Good luck with the game!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **a** (get the mail, water the plants, feed the cat, and cover a shift).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 yes, 2 Tuesday and Friday, 3 her spare key, 4 inside, 5 no, 6 will be away.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 getting, 2 watering, 3 feeding, 4 if, 5 covering, 6 if.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 watering, 2 open, 3 feeding, 4 switching, 5 borrow, 6 getting.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 covering, 2 No, not at all., 3 leave, 4 mine, 5 'll be, 6 to switch.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 Would you mind feeding my cat? 2 Do you mind if I leave early? 3 Would you mind covering my shift on Sunday? 4 No, not at all. *Homework 2 and 3:* answers will vary. *Homework 4:* 1 Why, 2 try, 3 to, 4 since, 5 for.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 To the new package room next to the mailroom. • 2 Within 5 days. • 3 Your text with the code. • 4 From June 10 to June 17. • 5 In an envelope under Ms. Lee's door (on Sunday). • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Exit ticket:* answers will vary. *A good answer:* "Would you mind helping me with my homework? No, not at all. I'm asking because the last question is hard."

### Teacher notes

- **No = yes:** practice the answer first, with a thumbs-up: *No, not at all!* Learners often say *Yes* and mean "I'll help." Smile and model it again.
- **Invented details:** all favor cards, keys, names, and addresses are invented. Learners never share real addresses or where they keep a key.
- **Shift switches:** every employer has its own rules. Add "Ask your supervisor first." It is printed on the Shift Switch Text page.
- ***mind* + -ing:** *Would you mind getting…?* But *Do you mind if I* + the base verb: *Do you mind if I leave…?*
- **Saying no:** model *sorry + a reason + another idea*. A polite no is a good answer.
- **Spanish and other languages:** the contrast tip on the Grammar page is optional. Learners read it on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain it.
- **Reductions:** the listening says *going to* and *want to*. Tell learners: people often say *gonna* and *wanna*, and they will hear them a lot. The short forms are fine in casual talk; in writing or at a job interview, use the full forms.

## Lesson 5 (Week 5) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *Would you mind* + *-ing*?

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Would you mind** | **getting** my mail? | ‖ | No, **not at all**. | I**'ll** do it. |
| ^ | ^ | ‖ | **Sorry**, I **can't**. | I'll be away too. |
| **Would you mind** | **covering** my shift? | ‖ | No, **not at all**. | I'm free on Saturday. |
| **Would you mind** | **lending** me a ladder? | ‖ | No problem. | You can borrow **mine**. |

### Grammar Box 2: permission: *Do you mind if I…?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Do you mind if I** | **open** the window? | ‖ | No, **go ahead**. | It's hot in here. |
| **Do you mind if I** | **leave** early today? | ‖ | **Sorry**, | we need you until 5:00. |
| **Do you mind if I** | **switch** my Friday shift? | ‖ | No, **not at all**. | Ask the supervisor first. |

**Rule of thumb:** After ***Would you mind***, use **-ing** (*getting, covering*). After ***Do you mind if I***, use the base verb (*open, leave*). The answer **No** means "No problem. I'll help." / "Yes, you can."

### Grammar Box 3: saying yes, saying no

| YOU CAN HELP (say *no*!) | YOU CAN'T HELP (be polite) |
|---|---|
| *No, not at all.* • *No problem.* • *Sure, I'll do it.* | *Sorry, I can't, because…* + another idea: *Why don't you ask Lena?* |

### Grammar Box 4: how polite?

| | WHEN? | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| *Can you…?* | a small favor, a friend | *Can you pass the salt?* |
| *Could you…?* | more polite | *Could you help me with this box?* |
| *Would you mind …ing?* | the most polite; a **bigger** favor | *Would you mind feeding my cat for a week?* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: Someone asks *Would you mind helping me?* I want to help. Do I say *yes*?**

A: No! Say ***No, not at all.*** *Mind* asks "Is it a problem?" *No* = no problem.

**Q: *Would you mind to get* or *Would you mind getting*?**

A: After *mind*, use *-ing*: ***Would you mind getting my mail?***

**Q: How do I say no politely?**

A: Say sorry, give a reason, and offer an idea: ***Sorry, I can't. I'll be away. Why don't you ask Lena?***

### Practice B: Circle the correct words.

1. Would you mind (**covering** / **to cover**) my shift?
2. A: Would you mind helping me? B: (**No, not at all.** / **Yes, I do.**) I'll help.
3. Do you mind if I (**leave** / **leaving**) early?
4. Could I borrow your pen? I forgot (**mine** / **my**).
5. Sorry, I can't. I (**'ll be** / **be**) away.
6. I'd like (**to switch** / **switching**) my Friday shift.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| Would you mind to get my mail? | Would you mind **getting** my mail? |
| Yes, I mind. I'll help you. | **No, not at all.** I'll help you. |
| Do you mind if I using your phone? | Do you mind if I **use** your phone? |
| Can I borrow your? | Can I borrow **yours**? |
| I like to switch my shift. | I**'d** like to switch my shift. |

> **Contrast tip (optional):** In many languages, a *yes* answer means "I'll help." But *Would you mind…?* asks "Is it a problem for you?", so in English **No** means "No problem." Spanish *¿Te importaría…? No, para nada* works the same way. What does your language do?
