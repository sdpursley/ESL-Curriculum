## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Each Grammar page has one short Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages. Learners read them on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question.* Learners have the Task Words page from Lesson 1.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you write or speak? How many people do you talk to?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 9 (Week 10): My Strengths

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- say what they are good at: *I'm good at fixing things. / I'm good with people.*
- say what they are interested in: *I'm interested in learning new skills.*
- say what they enjoy and don't mind: *I enjoy helping people. I don't mind working weekends.*
- give an example after a strength: *I'm reliable. I was never late at my last job.*
- answer *Tell me about yourself* in 3–4 sentences
- read two short job ads and write a 3-sentence skills summary
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **Whose… is this? It's mine. / It's hers.**
- **Opening Talk (6 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers with 2–3 sentences; A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** "Did you ever lose something important?" → (answer) → "**Really? Where did you find it?**"
- **Homework Check (4 min):** partners read their Lesson 8 Homework item 3 (*The blue cup is mine…*) to each other. Walk around. Write 2 common errors on the board (often *It's the mine* or *his* for a woman) and correct them together.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only, then the homework check.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:18 · New Words · 8 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. Show **strength**: make a strong arm. Show **reliable**: point to the clock, "always on time."
- **Show *good at*:** "I'm **good at** cooking. I'm not good at singing!" Mime both.
- **Check meaning (1 min):** "A paper with your jobs and skills is a…?" (*résumé*) • "Morning, evening, or night work time is a…?" (*shift*) • "A class at work for new workers is…?" (*training*)
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (*I'm good at solving problems.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:18–6:30 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "Lucia has a job interview at a hotel. She wants a maintenance job. Does she get a second interview? Listen."
- **Play 1 → Task A** (a second interview?). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Say it" (2 min):** say: "The end of *-ing* is in the nose: **fix-ing, help-ing, work-ing**." Don't add a **g** or **k** sound at the end. Then stress: **IN**terested, ex**PER**ience. The class repeats each word twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:15).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** write on the board: **good at + fixing**. Say: "After *at, in, about*, the verb gets **-ing**." Write **I'm good at fix.** and cross it out.
- **Boxes 1, 2, and 3 (5 min):** read a question from the left; the class answers from the right. Then ask 3 learners: "What are you good at?" Help them finish with an *-ing* word.
- **Box 4 (2 min):** read the spelling rows: **fix → fixing**, **organize → organizing** (no *e*), **shop → shopping** (double letter).
- **Check questions:** "I'm interested ___ learning." (*in*) • "I enjoy ___ (help) people." (*helping*) • "I'm good ___ cooking." (*at*) • "I don't mind ___ (work) at night." (*working*)
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers.
- **Pair drill (last 2 min):** A asks "What are you good at?" B answers and gives one example (*I'm good at cooking. I cook for my family every day.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: **What are you good at? / Are you interested in…? / Do you enjoy…? / I don't mind…** and **at / in + -ing**. Set up the two circles of chairs if you have the space.
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:12 · Talk Time: Interview Rotation · 12 min
- **Grouping:** inside–outside circles. The inner circle are **managers**; the outer circle are **applicants**. 15 learners: 7 managers + 8 applicants (one applicant waits and listens each round). 7–8 learners: 4 and 4 (or 3 and 4). 5 or fewer: one manager, the others take turns as applicants.
- **Before (2 min):** each manager chooses a job card (1–4) and the Manager Questions. Each applicant fills in the "My answers" box on their page (strengths, experience, shifts). Invented details are fine.
- **Model with one learner (1 min):** you are the manager at the hotel (card 2). "Tell me about yourself." → (answer) → "What are you good at?" → (answer) → **follow-up:** "Can you give me an example?"
- **Rotation (6 min):** each interview is about 2 minutes: the manager asks 3 questions from the card and one follow-up, then writes one strength in the notes table. Then say "**Move!**" The outer circle moves one person to the right. 3 rotations.
- **Switch (3 min):** managers and applicants change places for one or two more rotations.
- **Finish:** ask 2 managers: "Who gets a second interview? Why?" (*Ana, because she's good at organizing.*)
- **If behind:** 2 rotations, no role switch.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:12–7:17 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner writes **two sentences** on a slip: one with *good at* or *interested in* + *-ing* (*I'm good at organizing things.*), and one with an example and *because* or *so* (*I'm reliable, so I'm never late.*). Collect at the door.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (one sentence on the board). Say: "About 15 minutes. Bring it next week."
:::

:::step Read & Write · 7:17–7:25 · Two Job Ads and a Skills Summary · 8 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Read (3 min):** learners read the two job ads and answer questions 1–5. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers."
- **Pairs check (1 min):** compare answers; check together.
- **Write (4 min):** learners write a 3-sentence skills summary for themselves or an invented person: a strength with an example, experience, and an interest. Say: "You can invent the job history."
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the summary becomes homework.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 4 sentences in the answer key, slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle **a** or **b**.
- **Parts B and C:** learners do them alone. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "With a little help." 😐 "Not yet."
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 11).
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 11 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Listening script: `L3_L9_listening.mp3` (about 1:20; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed.

> **Narrator:** Lesson nine. Listening. Lucia has a job interview at a hotel. Mr. Hall is the manager. Listen.
> **Mr. Hall:** Hi, Lucia. Thank you for coming in. Please sit down. So, tell me about yourself.
> **Lucia:** Well, I have three years of experience in maintenance. I worked at a big apartment building.
> **Mr. Hall:** That's great. What are you good at?
> **Lucia:** I'm good at fixing things, and I'm good at solving problems.
> **Mr. Hall:** Are you reliable?
> **Lucia:** Yes. I was never late at my last job, and I always finish my work.
> **Mr. Hall:** Good. Do you enjoy working on a team?
> **Lucia:** Yes, I enjoy helping people. I like teamwork.
> **Mr. Hall:** Are you interested in training? We have a class for new workers.
> **Lucia:** Yes! I'm very interested in learning about the machines here.
> **Mr. Hall:** Which shift can you work?
> **Lucia:** I can work the day shift. And I don't mind working weekends.
> **Mr. Hall:** Great. I'd like you to meet our team. Can you come back on Thursday for a second interview?
> **Lucia:** Yes, of course. Thank you!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **Yes** (on Thursday).
- *Listening Task B:* 1 three years • 2 ✓ fixing things, ✓ solving problems, ✓ helping people, ✗ cooking, ✗ driving • 3 the day shift • 4 Yes, she doesn't mind working weekends. • 5 Thursday.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 fixing, 2 solving, 3 working, 4 helping, 5 learning, 6 working.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 cooking, 2 learning, 3 working, 4 organizing, 5 shopping, 6 fixing.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 at, 2 in, 3 helping, 4 working, 5 good with.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 I'm good at cooking. 2 She's interested in learning English. 3 He enjoys working with his hands. 4 I don't mind working at night. *Homework 2, 3, and 4:* answers will vary.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 Lakeview Inn and Little Stars Center • 2 Little Stars Center • 3 the night shift (11 p.m.–7 a.m.) • 4 a résumé • 5 on Saturday mornings • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says, 2 times each):* 1 "He's good at fixing cars." → **a** • 2 "She's interested in working with children." → **b** • 3 "He doesn't mind working at night." → **a** • 4 "He enjoys cooking for his family." → **b**
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 at, 2 in, 3 working, 4 helping.
- *Progress Check Part C:* answers will vary; for example 1 *I'm good at organizing. I organize the shelves at my job.* 2 *I'm interested in learning about computers.*
- *Progress Check scoring:* A /4 + B /4 + C /2 = /10. This is not a test grade. It shows who needs more help hearing the phrases (A) and with *at / in* + *-ing* (B, C).

### Teacher notes

- **Invented job histories are fine (important):** invite, don't require, real job details. Learners can talk about home, family, or volunteer work, or invent a job history. Never ask about immigration or work status, papers, or pay.
- **The one rule:** after *at, in, about* (and after *enjoy* and *don't mind*), use **-ing**: *good at fixing*, *interested in learning*, *enjoy helping*, *don't mind working*.
- ***good at* or *good with*?** *good at* + an activity (*good at cooking*). *good with* + people or things (*good with people*, *good with numbers*, *good with my hands*).
- **Give an example:** in a real interview, a strength is stronger with an example: *I'm reliable. I was never late at my last job.* Praise every example.
- **Spelling:** most verbs add *-ing*. Drop a final *e* (*organize → organizing*). Short words like *shop, run* double the last letter (*shopping, running*). Don't teach more rules than this.
- **Typical mistakes this week:** *I'm good at cook*, *interested on*, *I enjoy to work*, *I'm boring of…* (save *bored / boring* for Lesson 11), *I don't mind work*. Say the right form clearly; the learner repeats. Learners can read the Spanish note and the tip on their Grammar page themselves; you don't need to explain them.
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners add a second example and a follow-up question. Newer learners use Boxes 1–3 as a script.

### Grammar Box 1: *good at*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What are you | **good at**? | ‖ | I'm **good at** | fix**ing** things. / cook**ing**. |

| Are you | **good with** people? | ‖ | Yes, I'm **good with** | people. / numbers. / my hands. |

### Grammar Box 2: *interested in*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What are you | **interested in**? | ‖ | I'm **interested in** | learn**ing** new skills. |
| Are you | **interested in** training? | ‖ | Yes, I'm very **interested in** | work**ing** with computers. |

### Grammar Box 3: *enjoy / don't mind*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do you | **enjoy** working on a team? | ‖ | Yes, I **enjoy** | help**ing** people. |
| Can you | work weekends? | ‖ | Yes, I **don't mind** | work**ing** weekends. / work**ing** at night. |

---
### Grammar Box 4: *-ing* spelling

| MOST VERBS: + *-ing* | END IN *e*: no *e* + *-ing* | SHORT WORDS: double + *-ing* |
|---|---|---|
| fix → **fixing** | organize → **organizing** | shop → **shopping** |
| help → **helping** | solve → **solving** | run → **running** |
| work → **working** | drive → **driving** | plan → **planning** |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *good at fix* or *good at fixing*?**

A: ***good at fixing***. After *at, in, about*, use **-ing**. (not *good at fix*)

**Q: *interested in* or *interested on*?**

A: ***interested in***: *I'm **interested in** learning English.*

**Q: *I enjoy to work* or *I enjoy working*?**

A: ***I enjoy working.*** After *enjoy* and *don't mind*, use **-ing**.

**Q: *good at* or *good with*?**

A: ***good at*** + an activity: *good at cooking*. ***good with*** + people or things: *good with people*, *good with numbers*.

**Q: What does *I don't mind* mean?**

A: It's OK for me. It's not a problem: *I **don't mind** working weekends.*
