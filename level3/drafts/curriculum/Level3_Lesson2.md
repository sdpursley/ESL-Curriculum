## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Each Grammar page has one short Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages. Learners read them on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question.* Learners have the Task Words page from Lesson 1.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you write or speak? Who talks first?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 2 (Week 2): Who's Who?

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- describe how someone looks: height, hair, a beard, glasses, clothes (*She's tall. She has curly hair.*)
- describe someone's personality (*He's friendly and funny.*)
- ask and answer *What does he look like?* and *What's he like?*
- pick someone out: *Which one is your sister? → The woman with long hair.*
- use the object pronouns *me, him, her, us, them* (*I work with her. She always helps me.*)
- read a short welcome email and write 3–4 sentences about a friend or family member
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **Ask one follow-up question. Give a reason: because… / so…**
- **Opening Talk (6 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers with a reason; A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** "Why are you learning English?" → (answer) → "**Really? Where do you use English?**"
- **Homework Check (4 min):** partners compare Lesson 1 Homework item 1 (the *because* answers). Walk around. Write 2 common errors on the board and correct them together. Correct only those 2.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only, then the homework check.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:18 · New Words · 8 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. Use the class: "**Who has glasses?**" (learners with glasses raise a hand) • "**Who has curly hair?**" Act out **shy** (look down, speak very quietly: "…hi") and **patient** (wait calmly, smile, look at your watch).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** "Is a **beard** on your head or on your face?" (face) • "A **shy** person talks a lot: yes or no?" (no) • "A **hardworking** person works a little or a lot?" (a lot)
- **Two groups of words:** draw two columns on the board: **LOOKS** (tall, short, curly hair, straight hair, beard, glasses) and **PERSONALITY** (friendly, shy, funny, hardworking, patient, quiet). Learners say which column each word goes in.
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (*My brother is tall.* / *My teacher is patient.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:18–6:30 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** point to the picture on the Listening page. Say: "It's Omar's first day at a hotel. His supervisor is Linda. She tells him about two coworkers, Kevin and Nora. Who is Kevin? Who is Nora? Listen."
- **Play 1 → Task A** (write the letter of each person). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Say it" (2 min):** read the words in the "Say it" box. Clap on the strong part: **FRIEND**ly, **HARD**working, **PA**tient, **CUR**ly, **GLASS**es. The class repeats each word twice, then the line *Everyone likes him.*
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:20).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Boxes 1 and 2 (4 min):** write on the board: **look like = LOOKS** and **be like = PERSONALITY**. Ask about a famous cartoon character or a picture on the New Words page: "What does he look like?" → "He's tall. He has a beard." "What's he like?" → "He's funny."
- **Box 3 (2 min):** point to the picture on the Listening page. Ask: "**Which one is Nora?**" → "The short woman with long, straight hair." Learners ask about Kevin and Sam.
- **Box 4 (4 min):** read the two columns: *I → me, he → him, she → her, we → us, they → them.* Say: "The second word comes **after** the verb or after *with, to, for*." Read each example; the class repeats.
- **Check questions:** "Do you know Ana? Yes, I know ___." (*her*) • "Ask Kevin. Ask ___." (*him*) • "What does she look ___?" (*like*) • "She has long ___." (*hair*, not *hairs*)
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers.
- **Pair drill (last 2 min):** A names a person in the class (only names!); B describes how the person looks, and A asks *What's he / she like?* B answers only with kind words.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Lay out the crowd picture pages and the person cards (A cards in one pile, B cards in another). Write on the board: **What does he / she look like? What's he / she like? Is that him / her?**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:12 · Talk Time: Pick Them Up · 12 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, then groups of 3. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3 (two people share the A cards). 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: one group; take turns describing.
- **Round 1 (7 min):** each pair has the crowd picture (people 1–12) and two piles of cards. A takes an A card and **doesn't show it**. A says, "Can you pick up my cousin at the bus station?" B asks, "**What does she look like?**" A describes the person on the card (height, hair, glasses or a beard, the shirt color). B points and says the number. A says, "Yes, that's her!" or "No, she has glasses." Then B asks one follow-up: "**What's she like?**" A invents an answer. Switch: B takes a B card.
- **Model with one learner (1 min):** use card A1 (person 7) and the model dialogue on the Talk Time page.
- **Round 2 (4 min):** groups of 3. Each learner describes a person they admire (a family member, a friend, a teacher), **personality only**, with one reason (*My aunt is very patient, because she never gets angry.*). The others ask one follow-up question each.
- **Writing:** after round 1, learners write 2 of the people they found in the table on the Talk Time page.
- **Baseline snapshot:** while learners talk, call about 5 learners (the ones you didn't talk to in Week 1) for 2 minutes each and use the Lesson 1 baseline checklist.
- **If behind:** round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:12–7:17 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner writes **two sentences** on a slip about a friend or family member: one about how the person looks and one about what the person is like, with *because* or *so* (*My brother is funny, so everyone likes him.*). Collect at the door.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (describe one person on the board with the class). Say: "About 15 minutes. Bring it next week."
:::

:::step Read & Write · 7:17–7:25 · A Welcome Email · 8 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Read (3 min):** learners read the email on the Read & Write page and answer questions 1–5. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers."
- **Pairs check (2 min):** compare answers; check together.
- **Write (3 min):** learners write 3–4 sentences about a friend or family member: how the person looks, what the person is like, and one reason (*because*).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 4 sentences in the answer key, slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle **a** or **b**.
- **Parts B and C:** learners do them alone. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "With a little help." 😐 "Not yet."
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 3).
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 3 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Teacher notes

- **Kind words only (important):** describe **only** hair, height, glasses, beards, clothes, and personality. Never use words about weight, skin color, or age, and never as a joke. If a learner uses one, say the kind word: "We say *tall* / *short* / *curly hair*." The people in the pictures are invented.
- **Real classmates:** in the pair drill, learners describe classmates only with the New Words. If a learner seems uncomfortable, describe people in the pictures instead.
- ***look like* vs. *be like*:** the most useful check is the answer: if the answer is about the body or clothes, the question is *What does she look like?* If it's about personality, it's *What's she like?*
- **Object pronouns:** they come after the verb (*I know her*) and after small words like *with, to, for, at* (*Come with me. Look at him.*). Learners often drop them (*I know.*) or use the subject form (*I work with she*).
- ***hair*:** *hair* on the head has no *-s*: *She has long hair.* Adjectives never take *-s*: *two tall men*.
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners describe a person in the crowd with 4 details and add a comparison (*She's taller than number 3*). Newer learners use the model dialogue on the Talk Time page as a script.
- **Typical mistakes this week:** *She has long hairs*, *What does she like?* (for personality), *He is a man very tall*, *I know.* (no object), *I work with she*, *He help me.* Say the right form clearly; the learner repeats. Learners can read the Spanish note and the tip on their Grammar page themselves; you don't need to explain them.

### Questions and answers

**Q: What's the difference between *What does she look like?* and *What's she like?***

A: *look like* asks about **looks**: height, hair, glasses, clothes. *She's tall. She has glasses.* *be like* asks about **personality**: *She's friendly and patient.*

**Q: Do I say *hair* or *hairs*?**

A: ***hair*** (no *-s*) for the hair on your head: *She has long, curly **hair**.*

**Q: Where does the describing word go?**

A: **Before** the noun, and it never takes *-s*: *a **tall** man, two **tall** men, **curly** hair*. Or after *be*: *He's **tall**.*

**Q: How do I say which person?**

A: ***the* + person + *with* + thing:** *the woman **with** glasses, the man **with** a beard.* For clothes you can also say ***in***: *the man **in** the blue shirt.*

**Q: When do I use *me, him, her, us, them*?**

A: **After** a verb (*I know **her***) and **after** small words like *with, to, for, at* (*Come with **me**. Talk to **him**.*). Before the verb, use *I, he, she, we, they*.

**Q: Can I compare two people?**

A: Yes (Level 2): *She's **taller than** me. He's **funnier than** his brother.* Use *me*, not *I*, after *than* in conversation.

**Q: Are there words I shouldn't use?**

A: Yes. Talk about hair, height, glasses, beards, clothes, and personality. Words about a person's weight, skin color, or age can be rude.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| She has long hairs. | She has long **hair**. |
| What does she like? (about personality) | What's she **like**? / What does she **look like**? |
| He is a man very tall. | He's a **very tall man**. / He's **very tall**. |
| two talls men | two **tall** men |
| Do you know Ana? Yes, I know. | Yes, I know **her**. |
| I work with she. | I work with **her**. |
| He help me every day. | He **helps me** every day. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** *What's he like?* = ¿Cómo es? (su personalidad). *What does he look like?* = ¿Cómo es físicamente? En inglés se dice *hair* sin *-s*: *She has long hair* (no *hairs*). Los adjetivos van antes del sustantivo y nunca llevan *-s*: *two tall men*. Después del verbo usa *me, him, her, us, them*: *I know her* (no *I know she*).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** many verbs in English need an object word after them: *I know **her*** (not *I know*). *Can you help **me**?* (not *Can you help?* when you mean yourself).
