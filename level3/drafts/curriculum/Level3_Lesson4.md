## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Each Grammar page has one short Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages. Learners read them on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question.* Learners have the Task Words page from Lesson 1.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you write or speak? How many people do you talk to?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 4 (Week 4): It Happened So Fast

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- say what they were doing at a time in the past (*At 8:00 I was driving to work.*)
- ask and answer *Were you…? → Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.* and *Was anyone hurt?*
- tell what happened with *when* and *while* (*I was waiting at the light when a car hit my car.*)
- answer an insurance agent's questions about a small car accident
- describe what people were doing in a picture (a witness)
- read a short accident story and fill out a simple accident report
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **Did you use to…? What did you use to do?**
- **Opening Talk (6 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers with 2–3 sentences; A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** "Did you use to walk to school?" → (answer) → "**Really? How far was it?**"
- **Homework Check (4 min):** partners read their Lesson 3 Homework item 1 (3 sentences about their childhood or a place they used to live) to each other. Walk around. Write 2 common errors on the board (often *I use to live…* or *Did you used to…?*) and correct them together.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only, then the homework check.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:18 · New Words · 8 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. Show **hit** and **dent** with two toy cars or two books: move one into the other ("**hit**"), then point to a small spot ("**a dent**, a small problem").
- **Keep it light:** say "a **small** accident. No one is hurt. Only a dent." Use only the pictures; don't ask about learners' own accidents.
- **Check meaning (1 min):** "Who saw the accident?" (a **witness**) • "Who writes the **report**?" (a **police officer**) • "Red light: stop or go?" (stop) • "Where do two streets meet?" (an **intersection**)
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (*The driver stopped at the stop sign.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only. *wet floor* is for the workplace option (teacher notes).
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:18–6:30 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** point to the **accident** picture on the New Words page. Say: "This is Elena's car. She had a small accident. No one was hurt. Now she's calling her insurance company. Where did it happen? Listen."
- **Play 1 → Task A** (where? was anyone hurt?). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Say it" (2 min):** say: "In a sentence, *was* and *were* are short and quiet: *I was driving* (**wuz**). In a short answer, they're strong: *Yes, I **WAS**.*" Read the lines in the "Say it" box. The class repeats each line twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:15).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Draw a timeline on the board:** a long wavy line labeled **I was driving (8:00–8:30)** and a short arrow in the middle labeled **a car hit my car (8:15)**. Say: "The long action is *was* + *-ing*. The short action is the simple past."
- **Box 1 (3 min):** ask: "What were you doing at 8:00 this morning?" Learners answer: *I was working. I was sleeping.* Then read Box 1 across; the class answers from the right side.
- **Box 2 (2 min):** questions and short answers: *Were you driving? → Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.* *Was anyone hurt? → No, everyone was OK.*
- **Boxes 3 and 4 (4 min):** *when* + the short action; *while* + the long action. Point to the timeline each time. Box 5 is a quick *should* review (*You should take a picture.*).
- **Check questions:** "At 7:00 I ___ eating." (*was*) • "They ___ crossing the street." (*were*) • "I was cooking when the phone ___." (*rang*, a short action) • "___ I was walking home, it started to rain." (*While*)
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers.
- **Pair drill (last 2 min):** A says a time (*Yesterday at 6:00?*); B answers *I was…ing*. Switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: **What was he / she doing? He / She was…ing. Was anyone…? Yes, … was… / No, no one was…**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:12 · Talk Time: Witness · 12 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, A and B. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3 (two Bs ask questions). 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: pairs, or one group of 3.
- **Round 1 (5 min):** A opens **Picture 1 (Main Street)**. B does not look. Start the timer: A looks at the picture for **30 seconds** and then turns the page over. B opens the **Witness Form** and asks the Round 1 questions (*What was the man with the dog doing?*). A answers from memory (*He was walking.*). B writes short answers.
- **Model with one learner (1 min):** show Picture 1 to one learner for 30 seconds. Then ask: "Was anyone crossing the street?" → "Yes, a woman was crossing." Write it on the board.
- **Check (1 min):** A and B look at the picture together. B checks ✓ the right answers.
- **Round 2 (5 min):** switch. B looks at **Picture 2 (the parking lot)** for 30 seconds; A asks the Round 2 questions.
- **Fast pairs:** add one question of their own (*What was the man wearing?*).
- **If behind:** one picture only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:12–7:17 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner writes **two sentences** on a slip: one with *was / were* + *-ing* (*At 7:00 last night I was cooking dinner.*), and one with *when* or *while* and *because* or *so* (*I was walking home when it started to rain, so I ran.*). Collect at the door.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (one sentence on the board: *Yesterday at 7:00 a.m., I was…*). Say: "About 15 minutes. Bring it next week."
:::

:::step Read & Write · 7:17–7:25 · An Accident Story and Report · 8 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Read (3 min):** learners look at the 4-picture story and read Lee's story. Then they answer questions 1–5. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers."
- **Pairs check (1 min):** compare answers; check together.
- **Write (4 min):** learners fill out the **Accident Report** for Lee, with information from the story. Show where each answer is in the story (date, time, place).
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the report becomes homework.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 4 sentences in the answer key, slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle **a** or **b**.
- **Parts B and C:** learners do them alone. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "With a little help." 😐 "Not yet."
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 5).
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 5 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

> **Narrator:** Lesson four. Listening. Elena had a small car accident. Now she's calling her insurance company. Listen.
> **Mark:** Safe Road Insurance, this is Mark. How can I help you?
> **Elena:** Hi, this is Elena Ramos. I had a small car accident this morning.
> **Mark:** I'm sorry to hear that. Was anyone hurt?
> **Elena:** No, everyone was OK. But my car has a dent.
> **Mark:** Where did it happen?
> **Elena:** At the intersection of Oak Street and Second Avenue.
> **Mark:** What were you doing when it happened?
> **Elena:** I was driving to work. The light was red, so I stopped.
> **Mark:** And what was the other driver doing?
> **Elena:** He was talking on his phone. He wasn't looking, and he hit my car.
> **Mark:** Did anyone see the accident?
> **Elena:** Yes. Two people were crossing the street. One woman gave me her phone number.
> **Mark:** Good. Did the police come?
> **Elena:** Yes. A police officer came and wrote a report.
> **Mark:** OK. Please send me the report number today.
> **Elena:** I will. Thank you for your help.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* 1 **b** (at the intersection of Oak Street and Second Avenue) • 2 **No** (everyone was OK).
- *Listening Task B (order):* 1 Elena was driving to work. • 2 She stopped at the red light. • 3 The other car hit her car. • 4 A police officer wrote a report. • 5 Elena called her insurance company.
- *Listening Task C:* 1 Was, 2 were, 3 was, 4 was, 5 wasn't, 6 were.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 was eating, 2 were waiting, 3 Were … working, 4 wasn't looking, 5 were watching, 6 was … doing.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 hit, 2 was cooking, 3 rang, 4 when, 5 While, 6 should.
- *Talk Time, Picture 1 (Main Street):* 1 Yes. A woman was crossing the street. • 2 He was walking (with his dog). • 3 She was waiting for the bus. • 4 He was talking on the phone. • 5 She was eating ice cream. • 6 He was riding a bike.
- *Talk Time, Picture 2 (the parking lot):* 1 She was shopping / pushing a shopping cart. • 2 He was carrying a box. • 3 Yes. A woman was running. • 4 He / She was drinking coffee. • 5 The blue car (at the top). • 6 answers will vary.
- *Homework 1, 3 and 4:* answers will vary. *Homework 2:* 1 I was cooking when the phone rang. 2 We were watching TV when the lights went out. 3 She was walking to work when it started to rain. 4 They were eating dinner when their friend called.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 He was driving to work. • 2 at the stop sign at Pine Street and River Road • 3 He was looking at his map (he wasn't looking at the road). • 4 No. No one was hurt. • 5 a man (who was) walking his dog
- *Read & Write C (sample report):* Name: Lee Walker • Date: Tuesday, May 12 • Time: 8:10 a.m. • Place: Pine Street and River Road (at the stop sign) • What were you doing? I was waiting at the stop sign. / I was driving to work. • What happened? A truck hit my car from behind. • Was anyone hurt? No. • Damage: a dent • Witness: a man with a dog • Police report? Yes. Wording will vary.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says, 2 times each):* 1 "At eight o'clock this morning, I was driving to work." → **a** • 2 "She was waiting for the bus when it started to rain." → **b** • 3 "Was anyone hurt? No, everyone was OK." → **b** • 4 "The other driver was talking on the phone." → **a**
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 was, 2 were, 3 Were, 4 wasn't.
- *Progress Check Part C:* answers will vary; for example 1 *I was cooking dinner when my friend called.* 2 *While I was walking to work, I saw an accident.*
- *Progress Check scoring:* A /4 + B /4 + C /2 = /10. This is not a test grade. It shows who needs more help hearing the past continuous (A) and with *was / were* (B) and *when / while* (C).

### Teacher notes

- **Keep it simple and non-graphic (important):** this lesson is about dents, not injuries. Never ask about real accidents, injuries, or experiences with the police. Use the pictures and the invented stories. If a learner starts a personal story, thank them and go back to the picture. Say once, clearly: "**In a real emergency, call 911.**"
- **Workplace option:** if your class prefers, use a small workplace incident instead of the street pictures: *I was carrying a box when I slipped on the wet floor. While I was mopping, a customer walked in.* Use the **wet floor** card. The grammar is the same.
- **What the past continuous means:** an action **in progress** at a time in the past (*At 8:00 I was driving*). It is often the "background" of a story; the short action that interrupts it is the simple past (*when a car hit my car*).
- ***when* or *while*:** *while* goes with the long action (*While I was waiting…*); *when* usually goes with the short action (*…when a car hit my car*). Don't spend a long time on exceptions.
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners tell the whole picture story with *when* and *while*. Newer learners use Box 1 and Box 2 as a script in Talk Time.
- **Typical mistakes this week:** *I was drive*, *They was crossing*, *a car was hitting my car* (for the short action), *Were you work?*, *You should to call*. Say the right form clearly; the learner repeats. Learners can read the Spanish note and the tip on their Grammar page themselves; you don't need to explain them.

### Questions and answers

**Q: What does *was / were* + *-ing* mean?**

A: An action was **in progress** at a time in the past. *At 8:00 I **was driving**.* (I started before 8:00 and stopped after 8:00.)

**Q: *was* or *were*?**

A: *I / he / she / it **was***. *you / we / they **were***. Negative: *wasn't*, *weren't*.

**Q: Which action gets *-ing*?**

A: The **long** action: *I **was waiting***. The **short** action that happens in the middle is the simple past: *a car **hit** my car* (not *was hitting*).

**Q: *when* or *while*?**

A: ***while*** + the long action: *While I was waiting…* ***when*** + the short action: *…when the phone rang.*

**Q: How do I say it?**

A: In a sentence, *was* and *were* are short and quiet (**wuz**, **wer**). In short answers, they are strong: *Yes, I **was**.*

5. (**When** / **While**) I was turning left, the other driver was talking on the phone.
6. You (**should** / **should to**) call your insurance company.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I was drive to work. | I was **driving** to work. |
| They was crossing the street. | They **were** crossing the street. |
| I was waiting when a car was hitting my car. | I was waiting when a car **hit** my car. |
| Were you work at 9:00? | Were you **working** at 9:00? |
| While I cooking, the phone rang. | While I **was cooking**, the phone rang. |
| You should to call the police. | You **should call** the police. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** *I was driving* = *Estaba manejando*. Usa *was / were* + *-ing* para la acción larga. La acción corta usa el pasado simple: *when the phone rang* = *cuando sonó el teléfono* (no *was ringing*). No olvides *was / were*: *I was driving* (no *I driving*).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** long action = *was / were* + *-ing*. A short action that interrupts it = the simple past (*hit, rang, saw*).
