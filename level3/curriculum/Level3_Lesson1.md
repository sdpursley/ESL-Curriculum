## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Each Grammar page has one short Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages. Learners read them on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question.* The first student page lists them with a Spanish column and a "My language" column, so learners can look them up.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you write or speak? How many questions?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 1 (Week 1): Tell Me More

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask and answer questions about the present, the past, and the future in one conversation (review)
- ask follow-up questions: *Really? Why? What happened? How was it? What about you?*
- give a reason with *because* (*I take the bus because it's cheap*)
- say a result with *so* (*My car broke down, so I took the bus*)
- read a community center flyer and write 3 sentences about a classmate
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Welcome + "One Question, One Follow-Up" · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. Learners write a first name on a name tag.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **What did you do last weekend? → Really? How was it?**
- **Model with one learner:** ask, "What did you do last weekend?" The learner answers. Then say, "**Really? How was it?**" Point to the second question on the board and say, "This is a **follow-up question**. It asks for more."
- **Do (round 1, 4 min):** A asks one question from the board, B answers, A asks one follow-up. Switch.
- **Round 2 (4 min):** new partner. Add a second question to the board: **What are you going to do this weekend? → Why?**
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one round only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:15 · Orientation: How Our Class Works · 5 min
- **Grouping:** whole class. Open the Student Pages to **How Our Class Works**.
- **Show the times:** class 6:00, break 6:45, back 7:00, end 7:30.
- **The 3 rules (say and show):** (1) "**English in class.** A classmate can help you for a few seconds. Then back to English." (2) "**Homework every week.** About 15 minutes." (3) "**Phones are OK** for the audio and a dictionary."
- **Task Words page:** point to it. Say: "These are the words for our tasks. You can write them in your language here," and point to the *My language* column. **No one has to.**
- **Also say:** "In this class, we don't give one-word answers. We give a reason, and we ask a question back."
- **If behind:** show the times and say the 3 rules only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:22 · New Words · 7 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. Act out **small talk** (say "Nice day, isn't it?" to a learner), **introduce** (introduce one learner to another: "Rosa, this is Ana"), and **get along** (smile and shake hands with a learner).
- **Check meaning (1 min):** ask, "Is your **coworker** at home or at work?" (at work) • "Is a new job **good news** or **bad news**?" (good news) • "Does a **volunteer** get money?" (no)
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a short sentence (*My neighbor is friendly.*). Switch.
- **My language column:** learners may write each word in their language at home. Don't spend class time on it.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:22–6:34 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "This is Elena. This is David. They're neighbors. They're at the laundromat. One person has good news. One person has bad news."
- **Play 1 → Task A** (who has good news, who has bad news). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Say it" (2 min):** read 2 lines: "**Really? Why?**" and "**What about you? How was your week?**" The class repeats each line twice. Make *Really?* go up and sound interested.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:31).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 5 · 6:34–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 11 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question (or what someone says). The right side is the answer (or the follow-up).**
- **Box 1 (review, 2 min):** you read a question; the class answers from the right side. Point to the time words: *on weekends* (present), *last weekend* (past), *this weekend* (future). Don't re-teach; Level 2 taught these.
- **Box 2 (3 min):** read a sentence from the left; the class reads a follow-up from the right. Then say your own news ("I have a new cat!") and wait for follow-ups.
- **Boxes 3 and 4 (3 min):** write on the board: **because + reason** and **so + result**. Read one row from each box. Ask: "*My car broke down.* Is that the reason or the result?" (reason) "*I took the bus.*" (result).
- **Check questions:** "I stayed home ___ I was sick." (*because*) • "I was sick, ___ I stayed home." (*so*) • "Why ___ you take the bus?" (*do*)
- **Pair drill (last 3 min):** A asks a *Why…?* question; B answers with *because*; A asks one follow-up. Switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Cut up or lay out the Talk Time question cards. Write on the board: **Really? Why? What happened? How was it? What about you?**
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:00–7:12 · Talk Time: The Three-Question Rule · 12 min
- **Grouping:** inside–outside circles. 15 learners: two circles of 7–8 (the teacher or one trio fills a gap). 7–8 learners: one circle. 5 or fewer: sit at one table and rotate seats.
- **The rule:** the inner circle has a question card. **A asks the card question. B answers. A must ask two follow-up questions.** B must use *because* or *so* at least once. Then switch roles, and the outer circle moves one person to the right.
- **Model with one learner (1 min):** "What do you do on weekends?" → "I play soccer." → "**Really? Where?**" → "In the park, because it's free." → "**Who do you play with?**"
- **Do:** 3–4 rounds of about 2 minutes each. Learners write one answer on their Talk Time page after each round.
- **Baseline snapshot:** while learners talk, the teacher (or a second volunteer) calls about 5 learners, one at a time, for 2 minutes and uses the checklist after the answer key. Do the rest in Weeks 2 and 3.
- **If behind:** two rounds only.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:12–7:17 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner writes **two sentences** on a slip: one about a classmate (*Rosa takes the bus because she doesn't have a car.*) and one with *so*. Collect at the door.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together. Say: "About 15 minutes. Bring it next week."
:::

:::step Read & Write · 7:17–7:25 · A Community Center Flyer · 8 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Read (3 min):** learners read the flyer on the Read & Write page on their own and answer questions 1–5. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers."
- **Pairs check (2 min):** compare answers; check together.
- **Write (3 min):** learners write 3 sentences about a classmate from Talk Time, using the frame. One sentence needs *because*.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the writing becomes homework.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · First can-do self-check · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 4 sentences in the answer key, slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle the best follow-up.
- **Parts B and C:** learners do them alone. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "With a little help." 😐 "Not yet."
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 2).
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 2 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Listening script: `L3_L1_listening.mp3` (about 1:31; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed.

> **Narrator:** Lesson one. Listening. Elena and David are neighbors. It's Saturday morning, and they're at the laundromat. Listen.
> **Elena:** Hi, David! How are you?
> **David:** Hi, Elena. I'm OK, thanks. I'm just really tired.
> **Elena:** Really? Why?
> **David:** Because my car broke down on Tuesday.
> **Elena:** Oh no! What happened?
> **David:** It just stopped on the highway. Now it's at the mechanic, so I took the bus to work every day this week.
> **Elena:** That's bad news. When is it going to be ready?
> **David:** Maybe next Friday. What about you? How was your week?
> **Elena:** It was great! I have good news. I got a new job.
> **David:** That's wonderful! Where is it?
> **Elena:** At the hospital on Pine Street. I'm going to work in the kitchen.
> **David:** Do you like it?
> **Elena:** Yes, I love it, because the people are really friendly.
> **David:** How do you get there?
> **Elena:** I walk. It's only ten minutes from my apartment, so I don't need a car.
> **David:** Lucky you! I really need my car, because my job is far away.
> **Elena:** Do you want a ride on Monday? I start at seven.
> **David:** Thanks, but I start at six. I'm going to take the bus again.
> **Elena:** OK. Good luck with your car!
> **David:** Thanks. And congratulations on your new job!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* 1 Elena, 2 David, 3 David, 4 Elena.
- *Listening Task B:* 1 T, 2 F (he took the bus), 3 T, 4 F (at a hospital), 5 T, 6 F (he starts at six).
- *Listening Task C:* 1 Because, 2 so, 3 because, 4 so, 5 because.
- *Grammar practice (because or so):* 1 because, 2 so, 3 because, 4 so, 5 because, 6 so.
- *Read & Write:* 1 Saturdays, 10:00–11:30 a.m. (Room 4) • 2 No. It's on Tuesdays and Thursdays. • 3 children (ages 6–12) • 4 set up chairs and welcome new families • 5 Sunday.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says, 2 times each):* 1 "I'm really tired today." → **a** (*Why?*) • 2 "I went to a wedding on Saturday." → **b** (*How was it?*) • 3 "I have good news!" → **a** (*Really? What is it?*) • 4 "My phone is broken." → **b** (*Oh no! What happened?*)
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 because, 2 so, 3 so, 4 because.
- *Progress Check Part C:* any good follow-up question, for example 1 *Really? Where?* / *When do you start?* 2 *How was it?* / *Who did you go with?*
- *Progress Check scoring:* A /4 + B /4 + C /2 = /10. This is not a test grade. It shows who needs more help with follow-ups (A, C) and with *because / so* (B).

### Teacher notes

- **Privacy:** learners choose what to share. Any answer can be invented. Never ask about immigration status, money, or a real address. "I don't want to say" is always OK, and so is changing the topic.
- **Why follow-ups matter:** at Level 2, learners answered questions. At Level 3, they keep the conversation going. Praise every follow-up you hear in Talk Time, even a simple *Really?*
- ***because* vs. *so*:** *because* comes before the **reason**; *so* comes before the **result**. A quick test: "Why?" is answered by the *because* part.
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners ask three follow-ups instead of two and answer with two reasons. Learners who are new to the class use Box 2 on their page as a script.
- **New learners who skipped Level 2:** pair them with a returning learner in Step 1 and Talk Time. Check the placement questions in the scope and sequence (Section 2.1) after class.
- **Typical mistakes this week:** *Because I was tired, so I stayed home* (both words), *Why you take the bus?* (no *do*), *What you did last weekend?* (no *did* before the person), and one-word answers. Say the right form clearly; the learner repeats. Learners can read the Spanish note and the tip on their Grammar page themselves; you don't need to explain them.

### Questions and answers

**Q: What is a follow-up question?**

A: A **second question** about the same topic. It shows you are listening and keeps the conversation going. *I got a new job.* → *Really? **Where?***

**Q: When do I use *because*?**

A: Before the **reason**. *I take the bus **because** it's cheap.* The *because* part answers *Why?*

**Q: When do I use *so*?**

A: Before the **result**. *It's cheap, **so** I take the bus.* Put a comma before *so*.

**Q: Can I use *because* and *so* in the same sentence?**

A: **No.** Use one. ✓ *I was tired, **so** I stayed home.* ✓ *I stayed home **because** I was tired.*

**Q: Can I start an answer with *Because*?**

A: Yes, in a conversation, after *Why?*: *Why? → **Because** I was tired.* In writing, join it to the other sentence: *I stayed home **because** I was tired.*

**Q: How do I ask the question back?**

A: ***What about you?*** or ***And you?*** *My week was busy. **What about you?***

### Practice: Write *because* or *so*.

1. I'm learning English ____________ I want a better job.
2. It was raining, ____________ I took an umbrella.
3. She likes her neighbors ____________ they are very friendly.
4. The store was closed, ____________ we went home.
5. He drinks coffee every morning ____________ he starts work at 6:00.
6. My coworker was sick, ____________ I worked late.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| Because I was tired, so I stayed home. | I was tired, **so** I stayed home. / I stayed home **because** I was tired. |
| Why you take the bus? | Why **do** you take the bus? |
| What you did last weekend? | What **did you do** last weekend? |
| Because of it's cheap. | **Because** it's cheap. |
| I going to rest. | **I'm** going to rest. |
| (one word) "Good." | "Good, thanks. I played soccer. **What about you?**" |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En inglés, *Why?* (¿Por qué?) y *because* (porque) son dos palabras diferentes. *So* aquí significa «así que» o «por eso». No uses *because* y *so* en la misma oración: «Because I was tired, so I stayed home» está mal. Di: *I was tired, **so** I stayed home.* Y no olvides *do / did* en las preguntas: *Why **do** you…? What **did** you…?*

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** In English, a one-word answer can sound unfriendly or bored. Add a reason (*because…*) or ask a question back (*What about you?*).
