## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Each Grammar page has one short Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages. Learners read them on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question.* Learners have the Task Words page from Lesson 1.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you write or speak? How many people do you talk to?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 8 (Week 9): Lost and Found

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- ask *Whose… is this? / Whose… are these?*
- answer with *mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs* or a name + *'s* (*It's Ana's.*)
- ask and answer *Is this yours? → Yes, it's mine. / No, it isn't mine.*
- describe a lost item: size, color, and details (*a small black wallet with a zipper*)
- say what they were doing when they lost something (*I was waiting for the bus when I lost it.*)
- read a FOUND notice, fill out a lost-item report, and write a short FOUND notice

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Opening Talk + Midterm check-in · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **I'd like to… / Could I…? / May I…?**
- **Opening Talk (6 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers with 2–3 sentences; A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** "What did you do last weekend?" → (answer) → "**Really? How was it?**"
- **Midterm check-in (4 min):** give back the midterms (if they are marked). Partners say one thing that was easy and one thing that was hard (*The listening was easy. Part C was hard, because…*). Write 2 common errors from the midterm on the board and correct them together.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only, then the check-in.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:18 · New Words · 8 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. Put on imaginary **gloves** and a **scarf**; look for your **keys** in your pockets: "Where are my keys? I lost them!"
- **Show *belong to*:** hold up your own pen. "This pen **belongs to** me. It's **mine**." Point to a learner's book (don't take it): "That book belongs to you. It's **yours**."
- **Check meaning (1 min):** "Where do you keep money?" (*a wallet / a purse*) • "You listen to music with…?" (*headphones*) • "At the bus station, where do they keep lost things?" (*the lost and found*)
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (*My umbrella is black.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:18–6:30 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "David lost something on the bus. He's calling the lost and found. What did he lose? Listen."
- **Play 1 → Task A** (what did he lose?). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Say it" (2 min):** say: "*yours, hers, ours, theirs* end with a **z** sound." Read them; the class repeats. Then: "*Whose* and *who's* sound the same: **hooz**." Read the line in the "Say it" box. The class repeats each line twice.
- **The audio plays the conversation once (about 1:17).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** put three things on your desk: your pen, a cup, a book. Point to the pen: "**Whose** pen is this? It's **my** pen. It's **mine**." Give the cup to a woman: "It's her cup. It's **hers**." Give the book to a man: "It's **his**."
- **Boxes 1 and 2 (4 min):** read a question from the left; the class answers from the right. Point out **is this / are these** (one / more than one).
- **Box 3 (2 min):** read across: **my bag → mine**. In each pair, the first column needs a noun; the second stands alone.
- **Box 4 (2 min):** describing: **size → color → item → *with*…** Hold up something: "It's a **small blue** pen **with** a cap."
- **Check questions:** "This is my book. It's ___." (*mine*) • "___ keys are these?" (*Whose*) • "It's her umbrella. It's ___." (*hers*) • "a ___ wallet: black / small" (*small black*)
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers.
- **Pair drill (last 2 min):** A points to something in a picture on the New Words page: "Whose ___ is this?" B answers with a pronoun or a classmate's name + *'s*. Switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: **Whose… is this? / Is this yours? / It's mine. / It isn't mine. Maybe it's hers.** and **size → color → item → with…**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:12 · Talk Time: The Lost and Found Box · 12 min
- **Grouping:** Round 1 pairs (A = clerk, B = owner); Round 2 groups of 3–4. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 trio. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs. 5 or fewer: one clerk, the others are owners.
- **Round 1 (7 min):** each owner takes an **Owner Card** (the teacher gives one number, 1–8; use the cards in order). The owner fills in the short lost-item form on the card (*small, red, backpack…*). Then the owner describes the item to the clerk. The clerk looks at the **Lost and Found** picture and asks *Is this yours?* (pointing to a number). The owner answers: *Yes, it's mine! / No, it isn't mine. Mine is…* Then switch with a new card.
- **Model with one learner (1 min):** you are the owner with card 1: "I lost my backpack. It's big and blue." The learner points: "Is number 1 yours?" → "Yes, it's mine! Thank you!"
- **Round 2, groups (4 min):** each person keeps their last Owner Card. The group looks at the picture together and says whose each item is: *Number 3 is Maria's. It's hers. Number 9 is mine.* If no one has the card, say *It isn't ours. Maybe it's theirs* (another group's).
- **If behind:** Round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:12–7:17 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner writes **two sentences** on a slip: one with a possessive pronoun (*This umbrella isn't mine. It's hers.*), and one about losing something with *when* and *because* or *so* (*I was waiting for the bus when I lost my keys, so I called the lost and found.*). Collect at the door.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (one sentence on the board). Say: "About 15 minutes. Bring it next week."
:::

:::step Read & Write · 7:17–7:25 · A FOUND Notice and a Lost-Item Report · 8 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Read (3 min):** learners read the FOUND notice and the lost-item report and answer questions 1–5. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers."
- **Pairs check (1 min):** compare answers; check together.
- **Write (4 min):** learners choose one item from the Lost and Found picture and write a short FOUND notice (what, where, when, how to contact). Say: "Invent a first name and use a 555 phone number."
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the notice becomes homework.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 4 sentences in the answer key, slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle **a** or **b**.
- **Parts B and C:** learners do them alone. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "With a little help." 😐 "Not yet."
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 10).
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 10 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Listening script: `L3_L8_listening.mp3` (about 1:17; the conversation plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed.

> **Narrator:** Lesson eight. Listening. David lost something on the bus. He's calling the lost and found. Listen.
> **Joan:** City Bus, Lost and Found. How can I help you?
> **David:** Hi. I lost my backpack on the bus this morning.
> **Joan:** OK. Can you describe it?
> **David:** It's a big blue backpack with a black zipper. My brother has the same one, but his is green.
> **Joan:** What was inside?
> **David:** My headphones, a red scarf, and some gloves.
> **Joan:** And what were you doing when you lost it?
> **David:** I was talking on the phone. I got off at Park Street, and I left it on the seat.
> **Joan:** Let me check. We have two blue backpacks. One is small, and one is big.
> **David:** The big one is mine.
> **Joan:** There are white headphones inside. Are they yours?
> **David:** No, mine are black. Those aren't mine.
> **Joan:** Wait. Here's another big one, with black headphones and a red scarf.
> **David:** Yes! That's mine. Thank you!
> **Joan:** You're welcome. You can pick it up today before five.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

- *Read & Write B:* 1 keys (a set of keys with a blue star key chain) • 2 at Lincoln Park, near the basketball courts • 3 on Saturday, May 9 • 4 describe the key chain and say where you lost them • 5 a black umbrella with a wooden handle • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says, 2 times each):* 1 "Whose keys are these? They're mine." → **a** • 2 "Is this your umbrella? No, it's hers." → **b** • 3 "I lost a small black wallet." → **a** • 4 "These gloves aren't ours. They're theirs." → **b**
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 Whose, 2 mine, 3 Their, 4 yours.
- *Progress Check Part C:* answers will vary; for example 1 *I lost a big red backpack with a zipper.* 2 *It's mine. / It's Ana's.*
- *Progress Check scoring:* A /4 + B /4 + C /2 = /10. This is not a test grade. It shows who needs more help hearing possessives (A) and with the forms (B, C).

### Teacher notes

- **Picture cards, not real things (important):** use the pictures and Owner Cards, not learners' real wallets, purses, or phones. Remind learners never to write real ID numbers, bank card numbers, or addresses on any form, in class or in real life on a public notice.
- **Two kinds of words:** *my, your, his, her, our, their* need a noun (*my bag*). *mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs* stand alone (*It's mine*). *his* is the same in both.
- **No *the*:** *It's mine* (not *It's the mine*). No apostrophe: *yours, hers, ours, theirs* (not *your's*).
- ***Whose* and *who's*:** they sound the same. *Whose* = which person owns it (*Whose bag is this?*). *Who's* = *who is* (*Who's that?*).
- **Describing order:** size → color → item → *with*… (*a small black wallet with a zipper*). Don't teach a long rule; this one order is enough.
- **Typical mistakes this week:** *It's the mine*, *Is this your?*, *Who's bag is this?*, *It's mine bag*, *a wallet black*, *his* for a woman (*It's Maria's. It's his.*). Say the right form clearly; the learner repeats. Learners can read the Spanish note and the tip on their Grammar page themselves; you don't need to explain them.
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners add when and where they lost the item with the past continuous. Newer learners use Boxes 1 and 2 as a script.

| | SIZE | COLOR | ITEM | DETAIL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| It's a | **small** / **big** | **black** / **blue** | wallet. / backpack. | with a zipper. / with two pockets. |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *my* or *mine*?**

A: ***my*** + a noun: *It's **my** bag.* ***mine*** alone: *It's **mine**.* (not *It's mine bag*)

**Q: Can I say *It's the mine*?**

A: **No.** No *the*: *It's **mine**. It's **hers**.* And no apostrophe: *yours, hers, ours, theirs*.

**Q: *his* or *hers*?**

A: ***his*** = a man's. ***hers*** = a woman's. *It's Maria's. It's **hers**.*

**Q: *Whose* or *who's*?**

A: They sound the same. ***Whose*** = which person owns it: *Whose bag is this?* ***Who's*** = *who is*: *Who's that?*

**Q: How do I describe it?**

A: Size → color → item → *with*…: *a **small black** wallet **with** a zipper* (not *a wallet black small*).
