## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Each Grammar page has one short Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages. Learners read them on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question.* Learners have the Task Words page from Lesson 1.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you write or speak? How many people do you talk to?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 13 (Week 14): Where I'm Going

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- talk about a plan with *going to*: *I'm going to take a computer class.*
- talk about hopes with *hope to / I hope I'll*: *I hope to get my diploma.*
- say what they will do first to reach a goal: *First, I'll ask about classes.*
- choose *going to* (planned), *will* (decided now, a promise), or *hope to* (not sure)
- read a community college flyer and write a graduation talk (6–8 sentences)
- give a 1–2 minute talk about their goals: Then → Now → Next
:::

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **Is anything wrong? Something is wrong with… / No one is home.**
- **Opening Talk (6 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question; B answers with 2–3 sentences; A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** "What did you do this year that you're proud of?" → (answer) → "**How did you do it?**"
- **Homework Check (4 min):** partners read their Lesson 12 Homework item 2 to each other. Walk around. Write 2 common errors on the board (often *I didn't see nothing* or *Anything is wrong*) and correct them together.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only, then the homework check.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:18 · New Words · 8 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once.
- **Show *diploma* and *certificate*:** "A **diploma** is from a school, like high school. A **certificate** is for a job skill, like a medical assistant or a forklift driver."
- **Check meaning (1 min):** "You get a better job at the same company. It's a…?" (*promotion*) • "You put money in the bank every month. You…?" (*save money*) • "You need it to drive a car." (*a driver's license*)
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence about a goal (*I hope to get my driver's license.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:18–6:30 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "Sofia was a Level 3 student. Tonight she's visiting the class. She's talking about her goals. What is her main goal? Listen."
- **Play 1 → Task A** (her main goal). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Say it" (2 min):** say: "In a talk, pause after each idea, and stress the important word." Read the line in the box with pauses. Then: "People often say *going to* as **gonna**. You can understand it; you don't need to say it." The class repeats the line twice.
- **The audio plays the talk once (about 1:09).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question. The right side is the answer.**
- **Show it (2 min):** write three sentences on the board: **I'm going to take a class in January.** (planned) • **OK, I'll call the college tomorrow.** (decided now) • **I hope to get a better job.** (I want it, but I'm not sure).
- **Boxes 1, 2, and 3 (5 min):** read a question from the left; the class answers from the right. Point out: ***hope to*** + verb, but ***I hope I'll*** + verb.
- **Box 4 (2 min):** which one? Read the table. Then *plan to / want to / hope to* + verb.
- **Check questions:** "I ___ get my diploma." (*hope to*) • "Next year, I'm ___ take a computer class." (*going to*) • "The phone is ringing. I ___ get it!" (*'ll*) • "I hope I ___ have my own business." (*'ll*)
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers.
- **Pair drill (last 2 min):** A asks "What are you going to do next year?" B answers and adds one *I hope to…* (*I'm going to save money. I hope to buy a car.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: **Then: … years ago, I… / Now: I… / Next: I'm going to… I hope to…** and **First, I'll…**
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:12 · Talk Time: Goal Ladder + Talk Rehearsal · 12 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, then groups of 3. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 trio, then 5 groups of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs, then 2–3 groups. 5 or fewer: one group.
- **Model your own talk first (1 min):** give your 1-minute talk (Then → Now → Next, one reason). Example: "Ten years ago, I was a cook. Now I'm a volunteer teacher. Next year, I'm going to take a Spanish class, because I want to understand my neighbors. I hope to visit Mexico." Point to each part on the board as you speak.
- **Goal Ladder, pairs (4 min):** each learner fills in the ladder on the Talk Time page for one goal (step 1: *I'll…*; step 2: *I'm going to…*; top: *I hope to…*). Then explains it to a partner, who asks **two follow-up questions**.
- **Talk Rehearsal, groups of 3 (6 min):** each learner gives the talk (Then → Now → Next, with a reason), reading from the frame is fine. The 2 listeners fill in a Feedback Card: **one thing I liked** and **one tip**. About 2 minutes per speaker.
- **If behind:** the ladder only; the rehearsal becomes homework (practice at home with a family member).
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:12–7:17 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner writes **two sentences** on a slip: one with *going to* or *hope to* (*I hope to get my driver's license.*), and one with *I'll* and *because* or *so* (*First, I'll study the book, because the test is hard.*). Collect at the door.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (one sentence on the board). Say: "About 15 minutes. Next week is the final exam. In Week 16, you'll give your talk at graduation."
:::

:::step Read & Write · 7:17–7:25 · A College Flyer and the Graduation Talk · 8 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Read (3 min):** learners read the flyer and answer questions 1–5. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers."
- **Pairs check (1 min):** compare answers; check together.
- **Write (4 min):** learners start their graduation talk (6–8 sentences) with the talk frame. They finish it at home.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the talk becomes homework.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 4 sentences in the answer key, slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle **a** or **b**.
- **Parts B and C:** learners do them alone. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "With a little help." 😐 "Not yet."
- **Collect the pages** (or check them in Week 15 before the final).
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 15 at 6:00, before the final exam.
:::

### Listening script: `L3_L13_listening.mp3` (about 1:09; the talk plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed.

> **Narrator:** Lesson thirteen. Listening. Sofia was a Level 3 student. Tonight, she's talking to the class about her goals. Listen.
> **Mr. Novak:** Welcome back, Sofia! Please tell us about your goals.
> **Sofia:** Thank you. Three years ago, I came to this class. I was nervous, and my English was not very good.
> **Sofia:** Now I work at a hotel, and I can talk with my coworkers in English.
> **Mr. Novak:** That's great. What's your main goal?
> **Sofia:** My main goal is to get a medical assistant certificate. I hope to work at a clinic.
> **Mr. Novak:** How are you going to do that?
> **Sofia:** First, I'm going to take a computer class at the community college. It starts in January.
> **Sofia:** After that, I'll apply for the certificate program. And I hope to help my kids with their homework, too.
> **Mr. Novak:** Any advice for the class?
> **Sofia:** Yes. Come every week, and don't be afraid to make mistakes. You can do it!
> **Mr. Novak:** Thank you, Sofia. Good luck!
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 going to, 2 'll, 3 hope to, 4 going to, 5 'll.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 to get, 2 I'll, 3 going to, 4 to save, 5 I'll.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary.
- *Homework 1:* 1 I'm going to take a computer class. 2 I hope to get my diploma. 3 I hope I'll have my own business. 4 I'll call the college tomorrow. *Homework 2, 3, and 4:* answers will vary.
- *Read & Write B:* 1 free • 2 Computer Basics (Monday and Wednesday, 6:00–8:00 p.m.) • 3 on Saturdays, 9:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. • 4 at the Welcome Center, Room 101 • 5 Friday, January 15, at 6:30 p.m. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says, 2 times each):* 1 "I'm going to take a class in March." → **a** • 2 "I hope to open my own business." → **b** • 3 "OK, I'll call them tomorrow." → **b** • 4 "I'm meeting the counselor on Monday." → **a**
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 to get, 2 going to, 3 I'll, 4 to.
- *Progress Check Part C:* answers will vary; for example 1 *I'm going to save money for a car.* 2 *I hope to get a promotion.*
- *Progress Check scoring:* A /4 + B /4 + C /2 = /10. This is not a test grade. It shows who needs more help hearing the future forms (A) and with the forms (B, C).

### Teacher notes

- **Goals are personal (important):** every goal is a good goal. "Learn more English" and "help my kids" are great goals. Learners don't have to share personal details; an invented goal is fine. Don't ask about immigration status, money, or family problems.
- **Model your talk first:** your own 1-minute talk shows the shape (Then → Now → Next) better than any explanation. Keep it simple.
- **Three forms, three meanings:** *going to* = already planned (*I'm going to take a class in January*). *will* = decided now or a promise (*OK, I'll call them*). *hope to* = you want it, but you're not sure (*I hope to work at a clinic*).
- ***hope to*** + verb; ***I hope I'll*** + verb. Both are fine. *I hope I will to…* is wrong.
- ***gonna*:** learners will hear it everywhere. They only need to understand it; in their talk, they say *going to*.
- **Graduation (Week 16):** learners may read their talk from paper. Next week (Week 15) is the final exam; remind learners to bring their talk draft to graduation.
- **Typical mistakes this week:** *I hope get*, *I will to study*, *I'm going to studying*, *I hope I will to…*, *I plan study*. Say the right form clearly; the learner repeats. Learners can read the Spanish note and the tip on their Grammar page themselves; you don't need to explain them.
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners add a reason to every step and a time (*in January, next year*). Newer learners use the talk frame word for word.

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *going to* (a plan)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What are you | **going to** do next year? | ‖ | I'm **going to** | take a computer class. |

### Grammar Box 2: *hope to / I hope I'll* (a hope)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What do you | **hope to** do? | ‖ | I **hope to** | get my diploma. |
| ^ | ^ | ‖ | I **hope I'll** | have my own business. |
| What do you | **plan to / want to** do? | ‖ | I **plan to** / I **want to** | save money for a car. |

### Grammar Box 3: *will* (decided now, a promise)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What **will** you | do first? | ‖ | First, **I'll** | ask about classes at the community college. |
| Can you | call them? | ‖ | OK, **I'll** | call them tomorrow. |

---
### Grammar Box 4: Which one?

| | MEANING | EXAMPLE |
|---|---|---|
| ***going to*** | already planned | *I'm going to take a class in January.* |
| ***will*** (*'ll*) | decided now, or a promise | *OK, I'll call the college tomorrow.* |
| ***hope to*** / ***I hope I'll*** | I want it, but I'm not sure | *I hope to get a promotion.* |
| ***plan to / want to*** | my idea for the future | *I plan to save money.* |

### Questions and answers

**Q: *I hope get* or *I hope to get*?**

A: ***I hope to get***. *hope to* + verb.

**Q: *I hope I will to have*?**

A: **No.** ***I hope I'll have** my own business.* (no *to* after *will*)

**Q: *I'm going to study* or *I'm going to studying*?**

A: ***I'm going to study.*** *going to* + verb (no *-ing*).

**Q: What is *gonna*?**

A: Fast speech for *going to*. Understand it; in your talk, say *going to*.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I hope get my diploma. | I hope **to get** my diploma. |
| I will to study English. | I **will study** English. |
| I'm going to studying. | I'm going to **study**. |
| I plan save money. | I plan **to save** money. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** *I hope to…* = *Espero…* + infinitivo (*Espero conseguir…*). *I hope I'll…* = *Espero que…*. En inglés no se necesita una forma especial del verbo después de *I hope*.

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** *going to* = already planned. *will* = decided now or a promise. *hope to* = you want it, but you're not sure.
