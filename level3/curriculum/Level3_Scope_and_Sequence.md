## 1. Rationale and Research Basis

### 1.1 Why a Level 3, and who it is for

Level 2 graduates can talk about routines, tell a short story in the past, make plans with *going to*, compare things, give simple advice, and handle a short service call. What they usually **can't** do yet is **connect** ideas: give a reason and keep a conversation going, say what was happening when something went wrong, explain what will happen *if* something happens, or describe a person or a problem in detail. Those are the skills adults need at work, at a child's school, with a landlord, and at a store's customer service desk. Level 3 teaches them.

**Target learner:** an adult who finished Level 2 (or places at that level) and can hold a short, simple conversation on familiar topics. About 95% speak Spanish; the rest speak a range of other languages. Most read and write in their first language. Some are repeating learners who want more speaking practice.

### 1.2 Level estimates (our estimate, not an official alignment)

| Framework | Level 2 (existing) | Level 3 (this course) | Level 4 (planned) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NRS** Educational Functioning Levels for ESL (U.S. Department of Education, adult education reporting) | Roughly **High Beginning ESL** | Starts at **High Beginning ESL**; aims to finish at **Low Intermediate ESL** | Low Intermediate → **High Intermediate ESL** |
| **CEFR** (Council of Europe; "plus" levels from the 2020 *Companion Volume*) | A1+ → A2 | **A2 → A2+** | A2+ → B1 |
| **CASAS** | Same life-skill content areas (see 1.3). CASAS tests can report learners on the NRS levels. We **do not** claim any score alignment | Same | Same |

What these levels describe, in plain words (paraphrased):

- **NRS High Beginning ESL:** the learner understands common words and simple phrases on familiar topics with repetition, can meet basic survival needs, and has some control of basic grammar.
- **NRS Low Intermediate ESL:** the learner understands familiar phrases and some new ones spoken slowly with repetition, can ask and answer questions, can express survival needs, and can take part in some routine social conversations, though with some difficulty. Reading and writing cover simple, short texts on familiar topics.
- **CEFR A2:** the learner handles simple, routine exchanges about familiar matters and can describe their background, surroundings, and immediate needs in simple terms. At A2 the learner can also link ideas with simple connectors such as *and, but,* and *because*. **A2+** is the stronger half of A2: the learner takes a more active part in conversation (with some help) and handles a wider range of everyday situations.

**The Level 3 exit goal is NRS Low Intermediate and CEFR A2+, applied to work, family, and community situations.** The can-do lists in Section 2 are written to match.

> **Note on test scores:** CASAS publishes score ranges for each NRS level, and the ranges differ by test series and year. Newer NRS descriptors are aligned with the federal English Language Proficiency Standards for adult education. If the program needs scores for reporting, use the current official tables, not this document.

### 1.4 Teaching principles (and where they come from)

| Principle | What it means in Level 3 | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Connect, don't just answer** | Every Talk Time requires at least one follow-up question and one reason (*because, so*). Answers grow from one sentence to three. | CEFR A2 and A2+ descriptors for linking ideas with simple connectors (*and, but, because*) |
| **Grammar through real tasks** | Each grammar point is tied to one real job: past continuous → reporting an incident; *will* → offering help; *if* → making a plan for bad weather. | Task-based language teaching (for example, Rod Ellis, *Task-Based Language Learning and Teaching*, 2003) |
| **Pictures with every word** | Every vocabulary word has a picture card, as in Levels 1 and 2. | Dual coding (Allan Paivio) |
| **Many meetings with each word** | Each word appears in New Words, the listening, the grammar box, Talk Time, Read & Write, the homework, and later lessons. | Paul Nation, *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language* |
| **Listen for the main idea, then details** | The audio is longer and closer to natural speed, so the first listen is for gist and the second for details. | Common listening-instruction practice (pre-, while-, and post-listening stages) |
| **Notice, then use** | The Q&A box shows the form; the "Common mistakes" table and the Spanish note point out the most frequent errors; pairs use the form right away. | Focus on form within meaning-focused tasks |
| **Lots of pair and group talk** | Every lesson has at least three pair or group tasks. Target: learners talk for more than half of the class time. | Same principle as Levels 1 and 2 |

## 2. Entry and Exit Skills

### 2.1 Entry (who should start in Level 3)

A learner belongs in Level 3 if, in a short placement chat, they **can** do most of these:

- answer *What do you do every day? What did you do last weekend? What are you going to do this weekend?* in full sentences
- ask a partner 3–4 questions in different tenses (*Do you…? Did you…? Are you going to…?*)
- compare two things (*The bus is cheaper than a taxi*) and give simple advice (*You should rest*)

…but **cannot yet** comfortably do most of these:

- say what they were doing at a moment in the past (*At 8:00 I was cooking*)
- say how life used to be (*I used to live on a farm*)
- say what they will do *if* something happens (*If it rains, I'll take the bus*)

Learners who can't yet do the first list probably belong in Level 2. Learners who can do both lists probably belong in Level 4.

### 2.2 Exit (what learners can do at the end = what Level 4 expects)

**Talking and listening:** learners can…

- keep a conversation going with follow-up questions and reasons (*Why? Because… so…*)
- describe a person's appearance and personality, and pick someone out (*She's the tall woman with curly hair*)
- tell how life used to be and compare it with now
- tell what happened in a small car accident or other incident, using *was / were* + *-ing*, *when*, and *while*
- offer help, make promises, and make quick decisions with *will*
- say what will happen *if…*, and make a simple safety plan (storm, power outage)
- return or exchange an item, ask for a refund, and change or cancel an appointment politely
- report a lost item and say who things belong to
- talk about their strengths, skills, and interests in a short job interview
- give and follow step-by-step instructions
- give an opinion of an event or experience with a reason (*It was confusing, so I left early*)
- report a problem at home to a landlord or building manager
- talk about goals and plans with *will, going to, hope to, plan to*, and give a 1–2 minute talk

**Reading and writing:** learners can…

- read short texts of 80–150 words (flyers, notices, a store policy, a medicine label, short reviews, a short email) and find the main idea and key details
- fill out a multi-part form (incident report, return form, lost-item report, maintenance request)
- write a short note, text message, or email of 3–5 sentences with a reason

**Grammar they control (in practiced situations):** follow-up questions and *because / so*; *look like / be like*; object pronouns; *used to*; past continuous + *when / while*; *will* (offers, promises, decisions, predictions); first conditional; *I'd like to* + verb and *Could I / May I…?*; possessive pronouns and *Whose…?*; preposition / verb + *-ing* (*good at, interested in, enjoy*); sequence words + imperatives; *-ed / -ing* adjectives; *some- / any- / no-* words; choosing among future forms.

**Deliberately NOT in Level 3 (Level 4 teaches these):** present perfect (*Have you ever…? for / since, yet*), second conditional (*would*), *might / may* for possibility (Level 3 uses *maybe* and *probably*), *too / enough* as a grammar point (*too big* appears only as a fixed phrase), relative clauses, gerund vs. infinitive contrasts, phrasal verbs as a grammar point, and indirect questions.

## 3. Handoff from Level 2 and to Level 4

### 3.1 What Level 2 teaches and Level 3 assumes

Level 3 Lesson 1 opens with a **brief** mixed-tense review, then moves on. Everything below is **not re-taught**; it appears only in the 6:00 Opening Talk, in a homework item, or as support for a new point.

| Level 3 assumes learners know… | Level 2 teaches it in | Where Level 3 uses it |
|---|---|---|
| *be* vs. *do* questions; *Wh-* question order | Lesson 1 | Lesson 1 (review warm-up), every lesson |
| Simple present + *-s*; *doesn't*; frequency words | Lesson 2 | Lesson 1 review; Lesson 2 (*He's friendly. He always helps me.*) |
| *like / love / hate* + *-ing* | Lesson 3 | Lesson 9 builds on it (*good at / interested in* + *-ing*) |
| Present continuous (now and for plans) | Lessons 4, 11 | Lesson 4 builds past continuous on it; Lesson 13 future review |
| *was / were*; simple past regular and irregular; *Did…?*; *ago* | Lessons 5, 6 | Lessons 3, 4, 11 (stories); every Opening Talk |
| Comparatives and superlatives | Lessons 7, 8 | Lesson 2 (*She's taller than her brother*), brief review only |
| Count / noncount, *some / any*, *How much / many* | Lesson 9 | Lesson 12 builds *something / anything* on *some / any* |
| *I'd like* + noun; *Can I have…?* | Lesson 9 | Lesson 7 adds *I'd like to* + verb |
| *should / shouldn't* | Lesson 10 | Lessons 4 and 6 (advice after an accident; safety tips), review only |
| *be going to*; invitations (*Would you like to…?*) | Lesson 11 | Lesson 5 contrasts it with *will*; Lesson 13 |
| *have to / need to*; *Can / Could you…?*; phone calls | Lesson 12 | Lessons 7, 10, 12 (*You have to keep the receipt. Could you send someone?*) |
| Mixed tenses; *My goal is to…*; a 1-minute talk | Lesson 13 | Lesson 1 opening; Lesson 13 extends to *hope to / plan to / will* and a 1–2 minute talk |

**Items Level 2 passed to Level 3:** *will* for offers (Level 2 Lesson 13 shows it to recognize only) → **Level 3 Lesson 5**; object pronouns → **Level 3 Lesson 2**; describing people (Level 2 bonus page only) → **Level 3 Lesson 2**; *because* (modeled in Level 2, not taught) → **Level 3 Lesson 1**.

**Planned overlap (on purpose):** a few Level 2 words come back with new grammar: *appointment* (Level 2 Lesson 10 → Level 3 Lesson 7, now changing it), *receipt* (Level 2 Lesson 12 → Lesson 7), *fix* (Level 2 Lesson 12 → Lesson 12). They are **not** on the Level 3 picture pages. Otherwise, Level 3 vocabulary is chosen not to repeat Level 1 or Level 2 picture pages.

### 3.2 What Level 3 gives Level 4

| Level 4 will assume… | Level 3 teaches it in | Level 4 builds… |
|---|---|---|
| Past continuous + *when / while* | Lesson 4 | longer stories; *Were you…ing when…?* |
| *used to* | Lesson 3 | contrast with present perfect (*I used to…, but now I've…*) |
| *will* for offers, decisions, predictions | Lessons 5, 6, 13 | *might / may* for possibility |
| First conditional | Lesson 6 | second conditional (*If I had…, I would…*) |
| *I'd like to…*; *Could I / May I…?* | Lesson 7 | *Would you mind…?*; asking for favors |
| *something / anything / nothing* | Lesson 12 | *Do you know anyone who…?*; relative clauses |
| *good at / interested in / enjoy* + *-ing* | Lesson 9 | verb + *-ing* vs. verb + infinitive |
| Sequence words + imperatives | Lesson 10 | *Don't forget to… / Make sure you…*; time clauses with *before / after* |
| Reasons with *because / so*; opinions (*I thought it was…*) | Lessons 1, 11 | giving and defending opinions; agreeing and disagreeing politely |
| Writing 3–5 sentences | Read & Write, every week | 5–8 sentence emails; a short opinion paragraph |

## 4. Course Design

### 4.1 Weekly routine (same shape as Level 2, with a Read & Write block)

Learners coming from Level 2 will recognize every step. Level 3 changes three things: the **teacher plays the audio twice** (gist, then details; the file itself plays the conversation once), **Talk Time gets 2 more minutes**, and the last part of class becomes a standing **Read & Write** block.

| Clock time | Step | About | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6:00–6:10 | 1. **Opening Talk + Homework Check** | 10 min | Same partner for both. A question card on last week's grammar; partners must ask **one follow-up question**. Then compare homework. The teacher corrects only 2 items on the board. Late arrivals join a pair. |
| 6:10–6:18 | 2. **New Words** | 8 min | Picture page: point, say, repeat; a quick pair game. Learners may add a translation in the **My language** column (optional). |
| 6:18–6:30 | 3. **Listening + "Say it"** | 12 min | Before you listen → **Play 1:** Task A (main idea) → press play again → **Play 2:** Tasks B and C (details, grammar fill-in) → repeat 2 lines. A third play is OK if the class needs it. |
| 6:30–6:45 | 4. **Grammar Q&A + pair check** | 15 min | The two-sided question/answer box; Questions and answers; Common mistakes; then 3–5 minutes of pair practice. |
| 6:45–7:00 | **BREAK** | 15 min | |
| 7:00–7:12 | 5. **Talk Time** | 12 min | The main pair or group activity of the night. Every Talk Time needs a follow-up question and a reason. |
| 7:12–7:17 | 6. **Exit Ticket + Homework** | 5 min | **Never skip.** Two sentences with today's grammar, one with *because* or *so*. Do homework item 1 together. |
| 7:17–7:30 | 7. **Read & Write** | 13 min | **Recommended for Level 3.** A short real-world text (80–150 words) and a short writing task (a form, a note, a text message). See each lesson. |

> **Timing is loose on purpose.** If the listening or Talk Time runs long, take the time from Read & Write. If you're running behind, skip in this order: (1) the Read & Write writing task (it becomes homework); (2) the pair check at the end of Grammar; (3) half of Talk Time. **Never skip** the listening tasks, the grammar box and mistakes table, and the exit ticket.

> **Attendance (15 → 7–8):** use the Level 2 table (pairs, groups, circles, mingles). Every Level 3 activity lists a 15-learner and a 7–8-learner version, and a version for 5 or fewer.

### 4.2 Language support: tapering from Levels 1 and 2 (decided)

Level 1 puts a Spanish line and a "My language" line under **every** task instruction. Level 2 drops the per-task Spanish lines and keeps one Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages per Grammar page. By Level 3, learners know the routine and the task words (*Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Partner.*), so heavy translation now slows them down. But support for **all** first languages should stay fair.

| Support | Level 3 recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Instruction icons** | **None** (decided: no instruction icons in any level) | Instructions are short, plain English with the same task words every week |
| **Task-words page** (first workbook page) | A short list of the task words (*Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner.*) with an *Español* and a *My language* column | One page of translation replaces hundreds of lines |
| **Spanish line under each instruction** | **None** (decided; as in Level 2) | Learners know the task words; instructions stay short and in English |
| **"My language" line under each instruction** | **None**; replaced by a **My language column on the New Words page** (decided) | A personal bilingual word list is more useful at this level than translated instructions |
| **Spanish note on the Grammar page** | **Keep**, one short note per lesson (decided; student pages only) | The most common errors are still Spanish-transfer errors |
| **Tip for speakers of other languages** | **Keep**, one per lesson (decided) | Fairness for non-Spanish speakers; written so any learner can use it |
| **Teacher pages** | English only, as always | Teachers need no Spanish |

> **Teacher-friendly:** volunteers never read, translate, or explain the Spanish notes. When a learner is stuck: show it (picture, gesture, board), give one more English example, point to the task words and the grammar box, let a classmate help for a few seconds, then back to English.

### 4.3 Grammar format: two-sided question-and-answer boxes

Same format as Levels 1 and 2: the question side on the left, the answer side on the right, one word from each column. Each box has a short "Questions and answers" section in plain English, a "Common mistakes" table (✗ → ✓), a Spanish note, and a tip for speakers of other languages (student pages only). Level 3 boxes are a little longer (up to 5 rows) and often have a third column for a reason or a time clause. Example (Lesson 4):

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What **were** | you / they | doing? | ‖ | I **was** / They **were** | carrying | a box. / boxes. |
| What **was** | he / she | doing? | ‖ | He / She **was** | waiting | for the bus. / for a ride. |
| **Were** | you | driving? | ‖ | Yes, | I | **was**. |
| ^ | ^ | ^ | ‖ | No, | I | **wasn't**. |
| What | **happened** | then? | ‖ | I **was** driving | **when** | a car hit my car. / the light changed. |

### 4.4 Materials per lesson (same as Levels 1 and 2)

| Item | Level 3 detail |
|---|---|
| **Student Workbook pages** | New Words (with a *My language* column), Listening, Grammar, Talk Time sheet, Homework, and a Read & Write page. No instruction icons. Same page look as Level 2. |
| **Pictures** | 12 per lesson (≈ 156 total), 600×420 PNG cards made from Twemoji graphics (CC BY 4.0), same style and naming as Levels 1–2 (Level 3 pictures are named `L3_L<lesson>_<word>.png`, for example `L3_L4_stop_sign.png`, so they never collide with Level 1–2 files). Abstract words (*reason, reliable, reschedule, store credit, maintenance request*) get a labeled combination of two emoji or a simple form picture with the field highlighted. Every picture gets a line in `CREDITS.md`. |
| **Listening audio** | One MP3 per lesson + midterm + final (15 files), Chatterbox TTS cloned from **synthetic reference voices only** (no recordings of real people). **About 1:30 (1:15–1:45)**, up to 3 speakers (for example a narrator and two characters), natural speed with clear speech and natural pauses. Female characters have clearly female voices and male characters male voices. Each file plays the conversation **once** (it never repeats inside the file); the teacher presses play again for the second listen. |
| **Slides** | One deck per lesson, same look as Level 2: picture words, listening tasks, the grammar box with click-to-reveal answers, Talk Time instructions, and the Read & Write text. |
| **Teacher plan** | At a glance, clock-time plan, step-by-step boxes, listening script, answer key, and teacher notes in plain English. Every step says exactly what to say and show. **Teachers need no Spanish.** |

### 4.5 Assessment and progress

| Tool | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Placement chat** | Before Week 1 or on Week 1 night | 3–4 minutes: the entry questions in Section 2.1. Decides Level 2, 3, or 4. |
| **Baseline snapshot** | Weeks 1–3 | The same 6 prompts every time: *Tell me about your week. What did you do last weekend? What are you going to do next month? Describe a friend. What was your life like as a child? What will you do if it rains on Saturday?* About 5 learners per week during Talk Time. The final uses the same prompts to show growth. |
| **Can-do self-check** | Weeks 1, 8, 15 | "I can…" lines + 😀 🙂 😐, read aloud by the teacher. |
| **Exit ticket** | Every lesson | Two sentences with today's grammar, one with a reason. |
| **Homework** | Every lesson | 15 minutes: a short writing task, a speaking task with a family member or coworker, and one older grammar point. |
| **Read & Write tasks** | Most lessons | Collected every two weeks; the teacher writes one strength and one fix. |
| **Midterm / Final** | Weeks 8, 15 | Listening (the teacher plays the audio 2 times), grammar (circle + fix the mistake), a short writing task, and a pair speaking interview with the same 1–3 rubric as Level 2. Section 7. |

| 14 | **Lesson 13 (Week 14):** Where I'm Going | Future review: *will, going to,* present continuous; *hope to / plan to / want to* + verb; *I hope (that) I'll…* | Talk about goals; prepare the graduation talk |
| 15 | **Week 15: Final Exam** | Review of all lessons (focus on 8–13) | Review stations + exam |
| 16 | **Week 16: Graduation** | — | 1–2 minute "Where I'm Going" talks; certificates |

**Grammar in teaching order:** 1. follow-up questions + *because / so* (L1) → 2. *look like / be like*; object pronouns (L2) → 3. *used to* (L3) → 4. past continuous + *when / while* (L4) → 5. *will* for offers and decisions; *will* vs. *going to* (L5) → 6. first conditional; *will* for predictions (L6) → 7. *I'd like to* + verb; *Could I / May I* (L7) → **midterm** → 8. possessive pronouns; *Whose* (L8) → 9. preposition / verb + *-ing* (L9) → 10. sequence words + imperatives (L10) → 11. *-ed / -ing* adjectives (L11) → 12. *some- / any- / no-* words (L12) → 13. future forms together (L13) → **final**.

**Spiral recycling:** every Opening Talk reuses last week's questions, and every homework mixes in one older point. *because / so* (Lesson 1) and *will* (Lessons 5–6) come back in almost every lesson after they are taught. The past continuous (Lesson 4) returns in Lesson 8 (*I was waiting for the bus when I lost it*), and the first conditional (Lesson 6) returns in Lessons 7 and 12 (*If it doesn't fit, can I return it? If you don't fix it, I'll call again*).

## 6. Week by Week

*Vocabulary: ★ = most important words. Each word gets a picture card. "Talk Time" is the main pair or group activity (7:00–7:12); each lesson also has pair work in New Words, Listening, and Grammar. The teacher plays the audio twice: Task A on the first play, Tasks B and C on the second.*

### Lesson 1 (Week 1): Tell Me More

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Theme** | First night: getting to know classmates beyond the basics; how to keep a conversation going. Includes orientation, the task-words page, and the first can-do self-check. |
| **Can-do goals** | I can ask a follow-up question. • I can give a reason with *because*. • I can say a result with *so*. • I can talk about my week, my past, and my plans in one conversation. |
| **Grammar Q&A focus** | **Review (5 min):** *Do you…? Did you…? Are you going to…?* • **New:** follow-up questions: *Really? Why? What happened? How was it? What about you?* • *Why do you take the bus? → Because it's cheap.* • *I was tired, so I stayed home.* (*because* = reason; *so* = result) |
| **Vocabulary (12)** | ★ neighbor • ★ coworker • ★ small talk • ★ follow-up question • ★ reason • ★ good news • ★ bad news • ★ chat • ★ introduce • community center • volunteer • get along |
| **Talk Time** | **Three-Question Rule (inside–outside circles):** A asks one question from a card; B answers; A must ask **two follow-up questions** before the circle moves. Each round, B must use *because* or *so* once. 15 learners: two circles; 7–8: one circle; 5 or fewer: rotate seats. |
| **Listening** | Two neighbors chat in a laundromat about their week; one has good news (a new job) and one has bad news (a broken car). **Tasks:** A: good news or bad news? B: true/false; C: write *because* or *so*. |
| **Read & Write** | A community center flyer (classes, hours, a volunteer request). Write 3 sentences about a classmate, one with *because*. |
| **Pronunciation** | Rising intonation on short follow-ups (*Really? ↗ Why? ↘*); sounding interested. |
| **Spanish note** | *Why?* = *¿Por qué?* and *because* = *porque*: two different words in English. *So* here means *entonces / por eso*. |
| **Tip for other languages** | In English, a one-word answer can sound unfriendly. Add a reason or a question back. |
| **Teacher tip** | Model a "dead" conversation (one-word answers), then a good one with follow-ups. Learners hear the difference right away. |

### Lesson 2 (Week 2): Who's Who?

| Part | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Theme** | Describing people's looks and personality: a new coworker, a relative, a person you need to meet at the bus station. |

| **Spanish note** | *I hope to…* = *Espero…* + infinitive (*Espero conseguir…*). *I hope I'll…* = *Espero que…*. English doesn't need a special verb form after *I hope*. |
| **Tip for other languages** | *going to* = already planned. *will* = decided now or a promise. *hope to* = you want it but you're not sure. |
| **Teacher tip** | Model your own talk first (1 minute). Learners may read from paper at graduation. Goals are personal; "learn more English" is a great goal. |

## 7. Midterm, Final, and Graduation

Same evening structure as Level 2 (Opening → review stations with speaking interviews → listening test before the break → grammar and writing after the break → can-do self-check). Level 3 changes: the teacher plays the audio **2 times**, the grammar test adds a short **"fix the mistake"** part, and there is a short **writing task**.

| | **Week 8: Midterm** | **Week 15: Final** |
|---|---|---|
| **Covers** | Lessons 1–7: follow-up questions and *because / so*, describing people and object pronouns, *used to*, past continuous + *when / while*, *will* vs. *going to*, first conditional, *I'd like to* and *Could I / May I* | All lessons, focus on 8–13: possessive pronouns and *Whose*, *-ing* after *good at / interested in / enjoy*, sequence words and imperatives, *-ed / -ing* adjectives, *something / anything*, future forms |
| **Review stations (6:10–6:30)** | ① Grammar Auction (correct / wrong sentences), ② Listening Replay (Lessons 4–7 audio), ③ Role-Play Cards (one per lesson), ④ Pronunciation Bingo | ① Grammar Auction, ② Listening Replay (Lessons 10–13), ③ Role-Play Cards, ④ Talk Rehearsal (graduation talk) |
| **Listening test (6:30–6:45)** | 10 items: a conversation about a small car accident and a return at a store (main idea, order of events, details). Circle / true-false / write one word. The teacher plays the audio 2 times. | 10 items: a conversation that recycles a lost item, a job interview question, instructions, and a repair call. Same task types. |
| **Grammar test (7:00–7:15)** | 20 items: 15 circle the answer (*used to / use to*, *was / were* + *-ing*, *'ll / going to*, *if* + present, *like / like to*, *him / her / them*), 5 fix the mistake | 20 items: 15 circle (*mine / my*, *at cooking / at cook*, *-ed / -ing*, *something / anything*, sequence words, future forms), 5 fix the mistake |
| **Writing task (7:15–7:25)** | 4–5 sentences: a short accident report **or** a text offering help (choose one), with one reason | 4–5 sentences: a maintenance request **or** a short review of an event (choose one), with one reason |
| **Speaking interview (4 min per pair)** | Part 1: 2 baseline prompts each, with one follow-up from the interviewer. Part 2: pair task card: return an item at a service desk **or** make an *If…, we'll…* plan for a storm | Part 1: the same baseline prompts as Weeks 1–3, to show growth. Part 2: a mini job interview (*good at / interested in*) **or** a repair call |
| **Rubric** | Level 2's 1–3 rubric (Communication / Target grammar / Pronunciation and fluency). For Level 3, a **3 in Communication** means the learner gives reasons **and** asks or answers a follow-up question. | Same |
| **Self-check** | Can-do self-check + one goal | Can-do self-check; "Ready for Level 4" decision |

> **Exam-night timing with 15 learners:** 8 pairs × 4 minutes ≈ 32 minutes of interviews. Start them during the review stations and continue during the grammar and writing tests. With 7–8 learners, about 16 minutes.

**Proposed "Ready for Level 4" standard (confirm):** attend at least 9 of the 13 lessons, complete the final, score 2 or higher in all rubric areas on the final speaking interview, and write a 4–5 sentence task that a reader can understand on the first reading. Learners who are not ready still graduate and are encouraged to repeat Level 3.

**Week 16: Graduation.** No new content. Each learner gives a **1–2 minute "Where I'm Going" talk** prepared in Lesson 13 (reading from paper is fine). Certificates of completion. Optional potluck: ask about allergies, and don't require anyone to bring food. Hand out a "Keep practicing" sheet with the website, QR codes for all audio, and local community resources.
