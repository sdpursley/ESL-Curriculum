## How to Use This Plan

> **You don't need to speak Spanish or any other language.** Teach in English. Show, point, and gesture. Each Grammar page has one short Spanish note and one tip for speakers of other languages. Learners read them on their own. You never need to read, translate, or explain them.

> **Instructions:** use the same short task words every week: *Listen. Circle. Check. Write. Work with a partner. Work in a group. Ask a follow-up question.* Learners have the Task Words page from Lesson 1.

> **Instruction rule:** **Model it** (do it with one learner while everyone watches), **check it** (ask: "Do you write or speak? How many people do you talk to?"), **then go**. Say less, show more.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number, make one group of 3. *Groups:* 3–4 learners. *Inside–outside circles:* the inner circle faces out, the outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person to the right each round. *Mingle:* everyone stands and talks to many classmates. The teacher joins a pair only if one learner is left over.

> **When a learner is stuck or speaks another language to you:** (1) show it: the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more short English example; (3) point to the grammar box on their page; (4) a classmate can help for a few seconds, then back to English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

## Lesson 6 (Week 6): Be Ready

### At a glance

:::box Objectives: learners can…
- understand a short weather forecast and a weather alert
- say what will happen *if…* (*If the power goes out, I'll use a flashlight.*)
- ask and answer *What will you do if…?*
- make predictions with *probably* and *maybe* (*It will probably rain. It probably won't snow.*)

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Opening Talk + Homework Check · 10 min
- **Grouping:** pairs, the same partner for both parts. 15 learners: 7 pairs + 1 group of 3. 7–8 learners: 3–4 pairs.
- **Before class:** write on the board: **I'll… / I'm going to… / Don't worry, I won't…**
- **Opening Talk (6 min):** learners open the **Opening Talk** page. A asks a card question or reads a problem; B answers with 2–3 sentences; A asks one follow-up. Switch after each card.
- **Model with one learner:** "What are you going to do this weekend?" → (answer) → "**Really? Who with?**"
- **Homework Check (4 min):** partners read their Lesson 5 Homework item 1 (offers with *I'll*) to each other. Walk around. Write 2 common errors on the board (often *I help you* or *I will to…*) and correct them together.
- **Late arrivals:** join any pair as a group of 3.
- **If behind:** one card only, then the homework check.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:18 · New Words · 8 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the New Words page.
- **Do:** point to each picture and say the word. Learners repeat once. Act out **heat wave** (fan your face), **snowstorm** (shiver), and **power outage** (click a light switch: "Oh no! No lights!").
- **Group the words (1 min):** draw three columns on the board: **WEATHER** (forecast, storm, snowstorm, flood, heat wave), **PROBLEMS** (weather alert, power outage), and **BE READY** (flashlight, battery, emergency kit, bottled water, shelter). Learners say where each word goes.
- **Check meaning (1 min):** "No lights, no fridge, no TV. It's a…?" (*power outage*) • "Where do you put a flashlight, water, and batteries?" (*an emergency kit*) • "A safe public building for a storm?" (*a shelter*)
- **Pairs (2 min):** A says a word; B points to the picture and makes a sentence (*I have a flashlight in my kitchen.*). Switch.
- **If behind:** teach the ★ words only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:18–6:30 · Listening + "Say it" · 12 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** say: "First, you'll hear a **weather alert** on the radio. Then two roommates, **Nadia** and **Ben**, talk about it. What is the weather problem? Listen."
- **Play 1 → Task A** (what is the weather problem?). Pairs compare. Check together.
- **Read Tasks B and C together (1 min)** so learners know what to listen for.
- **Play 2 → Tasks B and C.** Pairs compare. Then show the answers (answer key).
- **"Say it" (2 min):** say: "*It will* is usually ***it'll***. It sounds like **id-ul**." Read *it'll, we'll, I'll* and the line in the "Say it" box, stressing the key words: *If the **power** goes **out**, we'll use a **flashlight**.* The class repeats each line twice.
- **The audio plays once (about 1:16).** Press play again for play 2. A third play is fine if the class needs it.
- **If behind:** do Task C together on the board, reading the lines yourself.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:30–6:45 · Grammar Q&A · 15 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs. Show the Grammar page. The **left side is the question or the *if* part. The right side is the answer.**
- **Draw on the board:** **IF + present → will**. Under it write: *If it **rains**, I**'ll** take the bus.* Circle *rains* and say "present, no *will*." Circle *'ll* and say "the result: *will*."
- **Box 1 (3 min):** read a question from the left; the class answers from the right. Then ask learners: "What will you do if it snows tomorrow?"
- **Box 2 (2 min):** the two halves can change places: *I'll stay home **if** it snows.* No comma when *if* is in the middle.
- **Boxes 3 and 4 (3 min):** draw a line from **0%** to **100%** on the board. Put *won't* at 0%, *probably won't* at 25%, *maybe* at 50%, *probably* at 75%, *will* at 100%. Read Boxes 3 and 4 across. Box 5 is a quick *should* review.
- **Check questions:** "If it ___ tomorrow, I'll stay home." (*snows*) • "If the power goes out, we ___ use flashlights." (*'ll*) • "It will ___ rain." (*probably*) • "It ___ won't snow." (*probably*)
- **Practice A and B (3 min):** learners write, then pairs check. Go over the answers.
- **Pair drill (last 2 min):** A says "**What will you do if…**" + a problem (*the bus doesn't come? / it's very hot tomorrow?*); B answers with *If…, I'll…*. Switch.
- **If behind:** skip the pair drill. Talk Time practices it.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Write on the board: **If it snows, I'll… → If I…, I'll…** and **probably / maybe / probably not**.
:::

:::step Step 5 · 7:00–7:12 · Talk Time: What If? Chain + Ready Plan · 12 min
- **Grouping:** groups of 3–4. 15 learners: 4 groups. 7–8 learners: 2 groups. 5 or fewer: one group.
- **Round 1, the chain (5 min):** the first person says a sentence from a start card (*If it snows tomorrow, I'll stay home.*). The next person starts with the end of that sentence (*If I stay home, I'll cook soup.*), and so on around the group. Keep going until the chain stops, then start a new card.
- **Model with two learners (1 min):** "If it snows tomorrow, I'll stay home." → Learner 1: "If I stay home, I'll watch TV." → Learner 2: "If I watch TV, I'll…" Laugh at funny chains; that's fine.
- **Round 2, Ready Plan (6 min):** each group gets one Scenario Card (a storm, a heat wave, or a power outage). Together they write **4 sentences** with *If…, we'll…* and a list of **5 things** for the emergency kit. Then one person presents the plan in 1 minute.
- **If behind:** Round 1 only.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:12–7:17 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** each learner writes **two sentences** on a slip: one with *If…, I'll…*, and one prediction with *probably* and *because* or *so* (*It will probably rain tomorrow, so I'll take my umbrella.*). Collect at the door.
- **Homework:** open the Homework page. Do item 1 together (one sentence on the board). Say: "About 15 minutes. Bring it next week."
:::

:::step Read & Write · 7:17–7:25 · A Weather Alert and a Ready Plan · 8 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Read (3 min):** learners read the weather alert text and the kit checklist, and answer questions 1–5. Say: "Read fast first. Then find the answers."
- **Pairs check (1 min):** compare answers; check together.
- **Write (4 min):** learners fill in the "My Ready Plan" card. Say: "**First names only.** Don't write phone numbers or addresses." An invented plan is fine.
- **If behind:** questions 1–5 only; the plan card becomes homework.
:::

:::step Progress Check · 7:25–7:30 · 5 min
- **Grouping:** individuals. Open the Progress Check page.
- **Part A:** read the 4 sentences in the answer key, slowly, 2 times each. Learners circle **a** or **b**.
- **Parts B and C:** learners do them alone. Walk around and help with the instructions only.
- **Part D:** read each "I can…" line aloud and point to the 3 faces: 😀 "I can do it." 🙂 "With a little help." 😐 "Not yet."
- **Collect the pages** (or check them at the start of Week 7).
- **If behind:** do the Progress Check in Week 7 at 6:00, while learners arrive.
:::

### Listening script: `L3_L6_listening.mp3` (about 1:16; it plays once, so press play again for the second listen)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer, at a normal, friendly speed. The weather alert is read by the narrator.

> **Narrator:** Lesson six. Listening. First, listen to a weather alert on the radio. Then listen to two roommates, Nadia and Ben.
> **Narrator:** This is a weather alert for Green County. A big storm will arrive tonight at about nine o'clock. There will probably be strong wind and heavy rain. The power will probably go out in some areas.
> **Nadia:** Ben, did you hear that? A storm is coming tonight.
> **Ben:** Yes. If the power goes out, we won't have any lights.
> **Nadia:** Do we have a flashlight?
> **Ben:** We have one, but it doesn't have batteries. I'll buy some after work.
> **Nadia:** Good. And if the power goes out, the fridge won't work.
> **Ben:** Then we'll eat the food in the fridge first! I'll buy bottled water, too.
> **Nadia:** Great. Should we buy candles?
> **Ben:** No, candles aren't safe. We'll use the flashlight. And I'll charge our phones before nine.
> **Nadia:** OK. If the storm is really bad, will we go to work tomorrow?
> **Ben:** Probably not. It'll probably rain all night. I'll check the forecast in the morning.
> **Narrator:** That's the end of the listening.

### Answer key

- *Listening Task A:* **b** (a storm: strong wind and heavy rain).
- *Listening Task B (check ✓):* ✓ 1 batteries, ✓ 2 bottled water, ✗ 3 candles, ✓ 4 charged phones, ✗ 5 a snow shovel, ✗ 6 a new fridge. (3 checks.)
- *Listening Task C:* 1 goes, won't • 2 'll • 3 won't • 4 'll • 5 is • 6 'll.
- *Grammar Practice A:* 1 rains, 'll take • 2 goes, 'll use • 3 'll stay, snows • 4 don't have, 'll bring • 5 is, 'll go • 6 will, closes.
- *Grammar Practice B:* 1 rains, 2 probably rain, 3 probably won't, 4 it snows, 5 will use, 6 should.
- *Talk Time:* answers will vary. Sample Ready Plan (power outage): *If the power goes out, we'll use flashlights. If the fridge doesn't work, we'll eat the cold food first. If our phones die, we'll use a radio with batteries. If it's very cold, we'll wear coats.* Kit: flashlight, batteries, bottled water, food in cans, a first aid kit.
- *Homework 1–4:* answers will vary. *Homework 2* example: *It will probably be sunny tomorrow. It probably won't snow.*
- *Read & Write B:* 1 a heat wave • 2 from Tuesday to Friday • 3 about 100 degrees • 4 from 12:00 to 5:00 p.m. • 5 at Oak Park Library, from 9:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. • *Writing (C):* answers will vary.
- *Progress Check Part A (the teacher says, 2 times each):* 1 "If it snows tomorrow, I'll stay home." → **a** • 2 "It will probably rain tonight." → **a** • 3 "If the power goes out, we'll use flashlights." → **b** • 4 "It probably won't be hot tomorrow." → **b**
- *Progress Check Part B:* 1 rains, 2 'll call, 3 probably won't, 4 it snows.
- *Progress Check Part C:* answers will vary; for example 1 *If the power goes out, I'll use a flashlight.* 2 *It will probably rain this weekend.*
- *Progress Check scoring:* A /4 + B /4 + C /2 = /10. This is not a test grade. It shows who needs more help hearing *if* sentences and predictions (A) and with the forms (B, C).

### Teacher notes

- **Choose your weather:** use the weather that fits your area (snow, heat, storms, floods). Skip a word that never happens where you live, or teach it quickly for the news.
- **Safety (important):** keep candles out of practice plans (fire risk); use flashlights. Never use a grill or a generator inside. Use local alert systems only as examples. Don't do real sign-ups in class. Say once: "**In a real emergency, call 911.**"
- **Feelings:** some learners have lived through a hurricane, a flood, or an earthquake. Keep the stories in the lesson invented. Never ask about real disasters. "I don't want to say" is always OK.
- **After *if*, use the present:** *If it **rains*** (not *If it will rain*). *will* goes in the other half. In English *if* needs a subject: *If **it** rains* (not *If rains*).
- ***probably* goes after *will* and before *won't*:** *It will **probably** rain. It **probably** won't snow.* *Maybe* goes at the start: ***Maybe** it will snow.*
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners add *because* to each Ready Plan sentence. Newer learners use Box 1 as a script.
- **Typical mistakes this week:** *If it will rain*, *If rains*, *It will rain probably*, *It won't probably*, *You should to buy*. Say the right form clearly; the learner repeats. Learners can read the Spanish note and the tip on their Grammar page themselves; you don't need to explain them.

## Lesson 6 (Week 6) · Grammar

**Read across. A reads from the left side. B answers from the right side.** Choose one part from each column.

### Grammar Box 1: *What will you do if…?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER (*If* + present, *will*) | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What **will** you do | **if** the power **goes** out? | ‖ | **If** the power **goes** out, | I**'ll** use a flashlight. / I**'ll** call my landlord. |
| What **will** you do | **if** it **snows** tomorrow? | ‖ | **If** it **snows**, | I**'ll** stay home. / I**'ll** take the bus. |
| What **will** you do | **if** it**'s** very hot? | ‖ | **If** it**'s** very hot, | I**'ll** drink a lot of water. / I**'ll** go to the library. |

### Grammar Box 2: The *if* part can go first or second

<!-- subst -->
| *IF* FIRST (comma) | ‖ | *IF* SECOND (no comma) |
|---|---|---|
| **If** it **rains**, I**'ll** take the bus. | ‖ | I**'ll** take the bus **if** it **rains**. |
| **If** school **closes**, my kids **will** stay home. | ‖ | My kids **will** stay home **if** school **closes**. |

### Grammar Box 3: *Will…?* Short answers

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER |
|---|---|---|---|
| **If** it snows tomorrow, | **will** school be closed? | ‖ | Yes, it will. / Probably. / Maybe. / Probably not. / No, it won't. |
| **If** the storm is bad, | **will** the buses run? | ‖ | Probably not. / Maybe. / I don't know. |

| How sure? | Prediction |
|---|---|
| 100% sure | It **will** rain. |
| 75% sure | It will **probably** rain. |
| 50% sure | **Maybe** it will rain. |
| 25% sure | It **probably won't** rain. |

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### Questions and answers

**Q: Which verb comes after *if*?**

A: The **present**: *If it **rains**…* (not *If it will rain*). Put ***will*** in the other half: *…I**'ll** stay home.*

**Q: Can I say *If rains*?**

A: **No.** English needs a subject: *If **it** rains. If **it's** hot.*

**Q: Where does *probably* go?**

A: After *will*: *It will **probably** rain.* Before *won't*: *It **probably won't** snow.* *Maybe* goes first: ***Maybe** it will snow.*

**Q: How do I say it?**

A: *it'll* sounds like **id-ul**. Stress the important words: *If the **power** goes **out**, we'll use a **flashlight**.*

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| If it will rain, I'll stay home. | If it **rains**, I'll stay home. |
| If rains, I'll take the bus. | If **it** rains, I'll take the bus. |
| If the power goes out, I use a flashlight. (tonight) | If the power goes out, I**'ll** use a flashlight. |
| It will rain probably. | It will **probably** rain. |
| It won't probably snow. | It **probably won't** snow. |
| You should to buy batteries. | You **should buy** batteries. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** es como *si* + presente en español: *If it rains, I'll stay* = *Si llueve, me quedo / me quedaré.* No uses *will* después de *if*: *If it rains* (no *If it will rain*). En inglés se necesita *it*: *If it rains* (no *If rains*).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** never put *will* right after *if*. Put *will* in the other half of the sentence.

### Round 1: Work in a group of 3–4. Person 1 reads a start card. Person 2 starts with the end of that sentence. Keep going around the group.

> **1:** If it snows tomorrow, I'll stay home.
> **2:** If I stay home, I'll cook soup.
> **3:** If I cook soup, I'll invite my neighbor.
> **4:** If I invite my neighbor, we'll watch a movie!

| Start cards | |
|---|---|
| **1.** If it snows tomorrow, I'll stay home. | **2.** If the power goes out tonight, I'll use a flashlight. |
| **3.** If it's very hot this weekend, I'll go to the beach. | **4.** If the bus doesn't come, I'll walk to work. |
| **5.** If it rains on Saturday, I'll clean my house. | **6.** If I win some money, I'll buy a new car. |

*Useful:* **If I…, I'll… • Then… • That's funny! • Your turn.**
