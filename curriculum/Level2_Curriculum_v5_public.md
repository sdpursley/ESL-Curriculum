## 2. The Class Routine (Same Every Week)

### 2.1 Clock-time template

*The **core** runs **6:00–6:45** and **7:00–7:15** (60 minutes). It has six steps, each with room to breathe. **7:15–7:30 is optional** ("If time"). Week 1 uses a modified version (see Lesson 1).*

| Clock time | Step | Minutes | What happens | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6:00–6:10 | 1. **Opening Talk + Homework Check** | 10 | Same partner for both. *First ~5 min:* a question about last week's lesson (late arrivals join a pair). *Next ~5 min:* compare homework. The teacher corrects **only 2** items on the board. | Core |
| 6:10–6:18 | 2. **New Words** | 8 | Picture page: point, say, repeat; quick pair game | Core |
| 6:18–6:30 | 3. **Listening + "Say it"** | 12 | Before you listen → Task A → Task B → Task C (grammar fill-in) → 1–2 min repeating 2 lines from the audio | Core |
| 6:30–6:45 | 4. **Grammar Q&A + quick pair check** | 15 | Learners read the Q's; class answers; examples, common mistakes, check questions; then 3–5 min of pair practice | Core |
| 6:45–7:00 | **BREAK** | 15 | | |
| 7:00–7:10 | 5. **Talk Time** | 10 | The main speaking activity of the night | Core |
| 7:10–7:15 | 6. **Exit Ticket + Homework** | 5 | One sentence on a slip; explain the homework and do item 1 together | **Never skip** |
| 7:15–7:30 | **If time** (pick in order) | 0–15 | ① Pronunciation activity (5) ② Pair practice game, long version (5–10) ③ Extension / second Talk Time round (10) | Optional |

> **If you're running behind, skip in this order:** (1) everything in "If time"; (2) the third play of the audio, so do Task C together with the teacher reading the lines; (3) the pair check at the end of Grammar, which becomes part of the homework; (4) teach only the ★ words (the others stay on the page for home study); (5) the second half of Talk Time, so do one round only. **Never skip:** the Listening tasks, the Grammar Q&A mistakes table, and the Exit Ticket + Homework.

> **If you're ahead of time:** go to the "If time" menu at 7:15, or let Talk Time run 5 minutes longer and switch partners once more.

> **Late-arrival rules:** Step 1 is designed for people walking in late. A late learner joins any pair (making a group of 3) and starts talking right away. Keep 3 extra copies of every handout by the door.

### 2.2 Adapting to attendance (15 → 7–8 learners)

| Situation | 15 learners | 7–8 learners | 5 or fewer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pairs | 7 pairs + 1 group of 3 | 3–4 pairs | Pairs; the teacher joins only if one person is left |
| Small groups | 3–4 groups of 4–5 | 2 groups of 3–4 | One group |
| Circles (inside–outside) | Two circles of 7–8, or one big circle | One circle | Use "rotate seats" instead |
| Mingles | Each learner talks to 5–6 people | Each learner talks to everyone | Turn the mingle into a table interview |
| Printing | Print for the **number expected**, plus 3 spares | | |

> **Why attendance drops, and what helps:** adults miss class because of work and family. The homework check and opening question give returning learners a quick way back in. Each lesson's handout stands alone, so a learner who missed a week can still follow.

### 2.3 Assessment and progress (semester version)

| Tool | When | What | Who / how long |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Baseline snapshot** | Weeks 1–3 | Same 6 speaking prompts every time (name, from, live, work, routine, free time) | Teacher or a second volunteer, 2 minutes per learner, about 5 learners per week during Talk Time |
| **Can-do self-check** | Week 1, Week 8, Week 15 | "I can…" list with 😀 🙂 😐 | Learner, 5 minutes |
| **Exit ticket** | Every lesson | One sentence or question with today's grammar | Learner; teacher reads it after class |
| **Homework** | Every lesson | Short written or speaking task | Pairs check in the 6:00–6:10 opening block |
| **Midterm** | Week 8 | Listening (audio), grammar (15 items), speaking (pair task + rubric) on Lessons 1–7 | See Section 4.1 and Part E |
| **Final** | Week 15 | Same format, all lessons, focus on 8–13; speaking uses the baseline prompts to show growth | See Section 4.1 and Part E |
| **Speaking rubric** | Midterm, final, and some Talk Times | Communication / target grammar / pronunciation and fluency, each scored 1–3 | Teacher |

**Speaking rubric:**

| Score | Communication | Target grammar | Pronunciation and fluency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Completes the task; asks and answers follow-up questions | Uses the target form correctly most of the time | Easy to understand; few long pauses |
| 2 | Completes most of the task with some help | Uses the form, with errors that don't block meaning | Mostly understandable; some pauses |
| 1 | Needs a lot of help; very short answers | Rarely uses the form, or errors block meaning | Hard to understand without effort |

*Proposed graduation standard (confirm): attend at least 9 of the 13 lessons (plus the exams) and complete the final. "Ready for Level 3" = 2 or higher in all rubric areas on the final, plus 🙂 or 😀 on at least 80% of the can-do list. Learners who are not ready still graduate from the course and are encouraged to repeat Level 2.*

## 3. Scope and Sequence: 13 Lessons

### 3.1 Lesson list

| Lesson (Week) | Title | Grammar | Real-life function | Listening (audio) | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson 1 (Week 1) | Nice to Meet You | *be* vs. *do/does* questions; short answers | Meet a new classmate or neighbor | Two neighbors meet at a community center | Rising ↗ / falling ↘ question intonation |
| Lesson 2 (Week 2) | A Day in My Life | Simple present; *he/she* + *-s*; *doesn't*; frequency words | Describe routines; compare weeks | Coworkers talk about a long day | *-s* endings /s/ /z/ /ɪz/ |
| Lesson 3 (Week 3) | Free Time | *love/like/don't mind/hate* + noun or *-ing*; *me too / me neither* | Break-room small talk | Coworkers talk about weekends | /ŋ/ in *-ing*; stress for feeling |
| Lesson 4 (Week 4) | Right Now | Present continuous (*am/is/are* + *-ing*); now vs. every day | Phone call: "What are you doing?"; telling time (review) | A phone call while waiting for the bus | Contractions *I'm, she's, they're* |
| Lesson 5 (Week 5) | Last Weekend | *was/were*; simple past regular verbs | Talk about the weekend; ask *How was…?* | Friends talk about their weekend | *-ed* endings /t/ /d/ /ɪd/ |
| Lesson 6 (Week 6) | My Story | Common irregular past verbs; *Did you…?*; negatives; *ago* | Tell a short life story (moving, first job, first days here) | A learner tells how she started a new life in a new city | *Did you* → "didja" (recognize) |
| Lesson 7 (Week 7) | Getting Around | Comparatives (*-er / more / better*); *How long does it take?*; *by bus / on foot* | Compare commutes and ways to travel | Two people compare bus vs. car | *-er* /ər/; word stress |
| **Week 8: Midterm** | **Midterm** | Review of Lessons 1–7 | Review stations, speaking interview, listening + grammar tests | Midterm listening clip | |
| Lesson 8 (Week 9) | The Best Place in Town | Superlatives (*the -est / the most / the best*); *there is / there are* (review) | Recommend a store, park, or clinic | Neighbors recommend places | *th* /ð/ in *the, there* |
| Lesson 9 (Week 10) | Food and Shopping | Count/noncount; *some/any*; *How much/How many*; *I'd like / Can I have…?* | Order food; make a shopping list | Ordering at a restaurant | /ɪ/ vs. /i/ (*chip/cheap*) |
| Lesson 10 (Week 11) | Health | *I have a…*; *My ___ hurts*; *should / shouldn't* | Describe symptoms; make an appointment by phone | Calling a clinic | /ʃ/ vs. /tʃ/ (*shoe/chew*); silent *l* in *should* |
| Lesson 11 (Week 12) | Making Plans | *be going to*; present continuous for arrangements; invitations | Invite, accept, decline politely | Friends make weekend plans | *going to* → "gonna" (recognize) |
| Lesson 12 (Week 13) | Getting Things Done | *have to / need to*; *Can/Could you…?* | Call about a bill; mail a package; leave a voicemail | A customer service call | *have to* → "hafta"; polite intonation |
| Lesson 13 (Week 14) | Then, Now, and Next | Past + present + future in one story | Prepare and practice a 1-minute graduation talk | A former learner's short talk | Sentence stress and linking |
| **Week 15: Final Exam** | **Final Exam** | Review of all lessons | Review stations, speaking interview, listening + grammar tests | Final listening clip | |
| **Week 16: Graduation** | **Graduation** | — | 1-minute talks, certificates | — | |

### 3.2 What we dropped or moved (to fit 13 lessons)

| Topic | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Describing people (*What's he like? / What does he look like?*) | Optional bonus worksheet; may belong in Level 1 or 3 | Lower priority than survival functions; confirm with Level 1 content |
| Directions and prepositions of movement | Optional "If time" activity in Lesson 7 | Lesson 7 already contains comparatives and travel time |
| *a few / a little* | Light mention in Lesson 9 only | Keeps Lesson 9 manageable |
| *can / can't* for ability | Dropped (probably Level 1) | Confirm with Level 1 |
| *will* for offers; object pronouns | Moved to Level 3 | Not essential for this level's goals |
| Feelings about moving | Folded into Lesson 6 (*I felt nervous / happy*) | Fits the life-story topic |
| Holidays and celebrations | Optional in Lesson 6 (learners choose what to share) | Optional |
| Telling time as a full lesson | Reviewed inside Lesson 4 | Most learners know numbers; practice the language in context instead |

### 3.3 Grammar in teaching order

1. *be* vs. *do* questions (Lesson 1) → 2. simple present + *-s* (Lesson 2) → 3. frequency adverbs (Lesson 2) → 4. *like* + *-ing*, *me too/neither* (Lesson 3) → 5. present continuous (Lesson 4) → 6. simple present vs. continuous (Lesson 4) → 7. *was/were* (Lesson 5) → 8. regular past (Lesson 5) → 9. irregular past + *did* (Lesson 6) → 10. comparatives (Lesson 7) → **midterm** → 11. superlatives (Lesson 8) → 12. count/noncount, *some/any*, *how much/many* (Lesson 9) → 13. *I'd like / Can I have* (Lesson 9) → 14. *should* (Lesson 10) → 15. *be going to* (Lesson 11) → 16. present continuous for plans (Lesson 11) → 17. *have to / need to*, *Can/Could you* (Lesson 12) → 18. tense review (Lesson 13) → **final**.

**Spiral recycling:** every Opening Talk reuses last week's grammar. The homework always mixes in one older point. The midterm and final review stations revisit everything.

### 4.1 Midterm (Week 8) and Final (Week 15)

| Clock time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 6:00–6:10 | **Opening Talk + Homework Check** | Same routine as every week |

- Certificates of completion. Optional potluck: ask about allergies, and don't require anyone to bring food.
- Hand out a "Keep practicing" sheet with the website and QR codes for all audio.

## 5. Teacher Guide: How to Use the Lesson Plans

> **How to read these plans:** Each lesson has (A) a **Teacher Plan** with clock times, steps, the listening script, and answer keys, and (B) a **Student Page** to print. The student page has the listening tasks, grammar box, Spanish note (*Nota para hispanohablantes*, written for learners to read on their own; teachers don't need to read or explain it), tips for all learners, and the homework. Audio files are in `audio/` (L1_listening.mp3 … L13_listening.mp3, plus the exam files `Midterm_listening.mp3` and `Final_listening.mp3`). Each file plays the conversation **once**; play the audio again if needed. **No speaker or device?** Read the script aloud with a volunteer learner.

> **Grouping toolkit:** *Pairs:* turn to a neighbor; with an odd number make one group of 3. *New partners:* matching cards, or line up by birthday month and "fold" the line. *Inside–outside circles:* inner circle faces out, outer circle faces in; the outer circle moves one person clockwise each round. *Instruction rule:* **Model it** (show with one learner), **check it** (ask "Do you write or speak?"), **then go** (start the timer).

> **You don't need to speak Spanish.** Teach in English. The Spanish notes on the student pages are for learners to read on their own. They are not something you need to read, translate, or explain.

> **When a learner is stuck or asks something in Spanish:** (1) show it: use the picture, a gesture, or the board; (2) give one more simple English example; (3) point to the Grammar Box or the Spanish note on their page; (4) ask a classmate to help for a few seconds, then continue in English. You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other.

> **Where are the student pages?** In **Part C: Student Workbook**. Each lesson there has four pages: New Words, Listening, Grammar, Homework. The exam papers are in **Part E: Assessment Packets**.

### Step by step

:::step Step 1 · 6:00–6:10 · Welcome + Name Chain · 10 min
- **Grouping:** two circles (15 learners) / one circle (7–8 learners).
- **Model:** "I'm Ana. I like coffee."
- **Do:** each next person repeats the previous person, then adds their own: "She's Ana. She likes coffee. I'm Carlos. I like soccer."
- **Late arrivals:** join the circle.
- **If behind:** go around once only.
:::

:::step Step 2 · 6:10–6:15 · Orientation: "How Our Class Works" · 5 min
- **Grouping:** whole class.
- **Cover:** class times and the break; homework every week (short!), checked at the start of class.
- **Language rule:** "**English in class. If you're stuck, a classmate can help you for a few seconds. Then back to English.**" You don't need to understand what classmates say to each other. Just smile and say "Now in English, please."
- **Also say:** phones are OK for audio and dictionary. "You can tell the truth or make it up."
- **If behind:** hand out the sheet and read the 3 key rules only.
:::

:::step Step 3 · 6:15–6:23 · New Words · 8 min
- **Grouping:** pairs.
- **Do:** open the Student Workbook to the New Words page. Point to each picture, say the word; learners repeat twice.
- **Pairs:** A points to a picture, B says the word. Then switch.
- **Extra example:** show the ID card for a fictional person.
- **If behind:** teach only the ★ words.
:::

:::step Step 4 · 6:23–6:33 · Listening + "Say it" · 10 min
- **Grouping:** individuals, then pairs check.
- **Before you listen (1 min):** "Rosa and Sam meet. What questions do they ask?" Write 2–3 guesses.
- **Listen 1 → Task A** (true/false). **Listen 2 → Task B** (circle). **Listen 3 → Task C** (write *are / do*).
- **Pairs compare** after each task.
- **"Say it" (1–2 min):** pause after "Where are you from?" and "Where do you live?" The class repeats each line twice.
- **Bridge to grammar:** "When do we use *are*? When do we use *do*?"
- **If behind:** skip the third play; do Task C together (teacher reads the 4 lines).
:::

:::step Step 5 · 6:33–6:45 · Grammar: be or do? · 12 min
- **Grouping:** whole class, then pairs.
- **Show:** the Grammar Box (question side / answer side).
- **Say:** "English questions need a **helper word at the front**. With *am/is/are*, move it to the front. With no *be*, **borrow *do* or *does***."
- **Q&A:** a learner reads a **Q**; the class guesses; read the **A**; write one example on the board.
- **Check questions:** "___ you like rice?" (*do*) • "___ you tired?" (*are*) • "Where ___ she work?" (*does*)
- **Pair check (last 3–4 min):** A builds a question from the box; B answers from the answer side.
- **If behind:** skip the pair check.
:::

:::step Break · 6:45–7:00 · 15 min
- Set up the "Find Someone Who" sheets during the break.
:::

:::step Step 6 · 7:00–7:10 · Talk Time: Find Someone Who · 10 min
- **Grouping:** mingle (talk to 6+ people / everyone).
- **Do:** learners turn each prompt into a correct question (*Do you live near here? / Are you a morning person?*).
- **When someone says yes:** write their name and ask **one follow-up question**. One name per row.
- **Baseline snapshot:** the teacher (or a second volunteer) uses the checklist: Can they ask a *do* question? Answer in a full sentence? Give 1 extra detail?
- **Later:** formal 2-minute baseline interviews in Weeks 2–3 (about 5 learners per week).
- **If behind:** learners talk to 4 people.
:::

:::step Step 7 · 7:10–7:15 · Exit Ticket + Homework · 5 min · NEVER SKIP
- **Exit ticket:** write one *be* question and one *do* question.
- **Homework:** explain it from the workbook and do item 1 together.
:::

:::step If time · 7:15–7:30 · pick in this order
- **① Pronunciation (5 min):** Yes/No questions go **up ↗**; Wh- questions go **down ↘**. Hum the melody, then say the words. Pairs: A says a question, B draws the arrow in the air.
- **② Question Scramble (5 min):** each pair builds 4 questions from an envelope of word cards.
- **③ Interview and Introduce (10 min):** partners interview each other, then introduce the partner to a group: "This is Marta. She's from Guatemala." (Previews Lesson 2's *-s*. Don't teach it yet.)
:::

### Listening script: `L1_listening.mp3` (about 1:10; the conversation plays once, so play the audio again if needed)

> **Audio:** play from a phone + small speaker. No speaker? Read the script with a volunteer learner.

> **Narrator:** Lesson one. Listening. Rosa and Sam meet at the community center. Listen.
> **Rosa:** Hi! Are you new here?
> **Sam:** Yes, I am. I'm Sam. Nice to meet you.
> **Rosa:** Nice to meet you too. I'm Rosa. Where are you from, Sam?
> **Sam:** I'm from Honduras. And you?
> **Rosa:** I'm from Colombia. Where do you live?
> **Sam:** I live on Pine Street, near the park.
> **Rosa:** Oh, me too! Do you work near here?
> **Sam:** Yes, I do. I work at a hotel. I'm a cook. What do you do?
> **Rosa:** I work at a bakery. I start very early, at five in the morning!
> **Sam:** Wow. Do you have kids?
> **Rosa:** Yes, I do. I have two daughters. Are you married?
> **Sam:** No, I'm not. But my brother lives here with me.
> **Rosa:** That's nice. Well, see you in class!
> **Sam:** See you!

### Answer key

- *Task A:* 1 T, 2 F (Honduras), 3 T, 4 F (a hotel), 5 T.
- *Task B:* 1 a cook, 2 5:00, 3 brother.
- *Task C:* 1 are, 2 do, 3 Do, 4 Are.

### Teacher notes

- **Privacy:** never ask about immigration status, money, or exact addresses. *Married/single* is optional; *Do you have family here?* is an alternative.
- **Mixed levels:** stronger learners add a follow-up question to every answer; newer learners use the question cards as a script.
- **Typical mistakes from Spanish speakers:** *I have 35 years* (Spanish uses "have" for age) and *Where you live?* (Spanish questions need no helper word). These are the two most common errors this week. Write the correct form on the board, and **point learners to the Spanish note** on their Grammar page. They can read it themselves; you don't need to explain it.

### Speaking interview (3 minutes per pair)

:::box Procedure
- **Part 1 (1 minute, individual):** ask each learner **2 questions** from the Speaking Interview Card. A different 2 for each partner.

### Questions and answers

**Q: How do I make a question in English?**

A: Put a **helper word at the front**: *am / is / are* or *do / does*.

**Q: When do I use *am / is / are*?**

A: With **where you're from, age, job title, feelings, married/single, and places**. Look at Box 1.

**Q: When do I use *do / does*?**

A: With **action verbs** like *live, work, have, like, speak*. Look at Box 2.

**Q: What order do the words go in?**

A: **Question word → helper → person → the rest.** Look at Box 3.

**Q: How do I give a short answer?**

A: Use the **same helper** as the question. *Are you…?* → *Yes, I **am**.* *Do you…?* → *Yes, I **do**.*

**Q: Can I use *do* and *are* together?**

A: **No, never.** ✗ *Do you are married?* ✓ *Are you married?*

**Q: How do I say my age?**

A: With *be*: *I'**m** 35.* (not *I have 35 years*)

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| Where you are from? | Where **are you** from? |
| Where you live? | Where **do** you live? |
| Do you are married? | **Are** you married? |
| I have 35 years. | I'**m** 35. |
| Is from Peru. | **She's** from Peru. |
| Yes, I live. | Yes, I **do**. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En español decimos «¿Dónde vives?» sin palabra extra. En inglés la pregunta necesita **do/does** o **am/is/are** al principio: *Where **do** you live?* La edad usa **be**: *I'm 35* (no «I have 35 years»). Y siempre usa la persona: *She's from Peru* (no «Is from Peru»).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** If your language makes questions with only your voice, or puts the question word at the end, remember: English needs a helper word (*do* / *are*) at the **beginning**.

### Grammar Box 2: *What time…?* and *How often…?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | < | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What time | **do** | you | get up? / start work? | ‖ | I | get up / start work | at 6:00. / at 7:30. |
| What time | **does** | he / she | get up? / start work? | ‖ | He / She | get**s** up / start**s** work | at 6:00. / at 7:30. |

### Sentences: positive and negative

| Person | ✔ Positive | ✘ Negative |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | I **work**. | I **don't work**. |
| **He / She / It** | She **works**. | She **doesn't work**. |

### Questions and answers

**Q: When do I use the simple present?**

A: For **routines and facts**: things that happen again and again. *I work at a hospital. I get up at 6.*

**Q: When do I add *-s* to the verb?**

A: **Only with *he, she, it*** (one other person or thing). *She work**s**. Marco take**s** the bus.*

**Q: How do I spell the *-s*?**

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most verbs: **+s** | work → works, play → plays |
| -sh, -ch, -s, -x, -o: **+es** | watch → watches, go → goes, do → does |
| consonant + y: **-ies** | study → studies |
| Special | have → **has** |

**Q: After *doesn't* or *does*, does the verb keep the *-s*?**

A: **No.** The **"one -s" rule**: the -s moves to *does*. *She work**s**.* → *She doe**s**n't work.* → *Doe**s** she work?*

**Q: How do I say how often?**

| always 100% | usually | often | sometimes | rarely | never 0% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I **always** take the bus. | | | She **sometimes** cooks. | | He **never** drives. |

**Q: Where do *always, usually, never…* go?**

A: **Before** the main verb: *I **usually** take the bus.* But **after** *be*: *He **is always** late.* Longer phrases go at the **end**: *I cook **every day**.*

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| She work at a store. | She work**s** at a store. |
| He doesn't works. | He doesn't **work**. |
| Does she works? | Does she **work**? |
| I go always to the park. | I **always go** to the park. |
| I am work at a restaurant. | I **work** at a restaurant. |
| He have two kids. | He **has** two kids. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En español el verbo cambia con cada persona (*trabajo, trabajas, trabaja*). En inglés **solo cambia con he/she/it**: agrega **-s** (*she works*). ¡No olvides la -s! Y no uses *am* con otro verbo: *I work* (no «I am work»).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** If your language has no verb endings, make a habit: **he / she / it = -s**. Circle every -s in your homework.

**Q: How do I spell the *-ing*?**

|---|---|
| Most verbs: **+ing** | cook → cooking, play → playing |
| Silent **e**: drop it | dance → dancing, make → making |
| Short verb (1 vowel + 1 consonant): double the last letter | swim → swimming, shop → shopping |

**Q: *Me too* or *Me neither*?**

A: Look at your partner's sentence. **Positive** (*I like…*) → *Me too.* **Negative** (*I don't like…*) → *Me neither.* Look at Box 3.

**Q: Where do *a lot* and *very much* go?**

A: At the **end**: *I like soccer **a lot**.*

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I like cook. | I like cook**ing**. |
| I am like pizza. | I like pizza. |
| I don't like coffee. / Me too. | … / **Me neither.** |
| She like dancing. | She like**s** dancing. |
| I like very much soccer. | I like soccer **a lot**. |
| I don't like nothing. | I don't like **anything**. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** «Me gusta la música» → en inglés **la persona va primero**: ***I** like music*. Para actividades usa **-ing**: *I like cooking* (no «I like cook»). Usa **una sola** negación: *I don't like anything* (no «I don't like nothing»). *Me too* = «yo también»; *Me neither* = «yo tampoco».

### Questions and answers

**Q: When do I use *was* and *were*?**

A: They are the past of *be*. *am / is* → **was**. *are* → **were**. *I'm tired today.* → *I **was** tired yesterday.*

**Q: How do I make other verbs past?**

A: Most verbs add **-ed**: *work → work**ed**, clean → clean**ed**, watch → watch**ed***.

**Q: Do I add *-s* for *he* or *she* in the past?**

A: **No.** The past is the **same for everyone**: *I worked. She worked. They worked.*

**Q: How do I spell the *-ed*?**

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most verbs: **+ed** | clean → cleaned, watch → watched |
| Ends in **e**: **+d** | dance → danced, live → lived |
| consonant + y: **-ied** | study → studied, try → tried |
| Short verb (1 vowel + 1 consonant): double | stop → stopped, plan → planned |

**Q: How do I say the *-ed*?**

A: Three ways. /t/ *worked*, /d/ *played*, /ɪd/ *visited*. You hear an extra syllable **only after t or d**.

**Q: What does *What did you do?* mean?**

A: It asks about **activities in the past**. Answer with an *-ed* verb: *I cleaned the house.* (Lesson 6 explains *did*.)

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I was clean the house. | I clean**ed** the house. |
| She were tired. | She **was** tired. |
| We was busy. | We **were** busy. |
| Yesterday I work. | Yesterday I work**ed**. |
| I studyed. | I stud**ied**. |
| The movie was bored. | The movie was **boring**. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** En español «estuve / estaba / fui» → en inglés **was** (I, he, she, it) o **were** (you, we, they). Para otros verbos, agrega **-ed**: *I worked, she worked* (igual para todos, ¡sin -s!). No uses *was* con otro verbo: *I cleaned* (no «I was clean»). Y cuidado: la película es **boring** (aburrida); la persona está **bored** (aburrida).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** If your language doesn't change the verb for the past, use **two signals**: a time word **and** *-ed*: *Yesterday I worked.*

| long: expensive, comfortable | **more** + word | **more expensive** than |
| irregular | | good → **better**, bad → **worse**, far → **farther** |

### Questions and answers

**Q: How do I compare two things?**

A: Short words: add **-er**. Long words: use **more**. Then say **than**: *The bus is cheap**er than** the car.*

**Q: Can I say *more faster*?**

A: **No.** Use *-er* **or** *more*, never both: *faster*, *more expensive*.

**Q: What are the special words?**

A: *good → **better***, *bad → **worse***, *far → **farther***.

**Q: What does *How long does it take?* ask?**

A: It asks **how much time** a trip needs. Answer: *It **takes** twenty minutes.*

**Q: *By* or *on*?**

A: ***by** bus, **by** car, **by** train, **by** bike*, but ***on** foot* (= walk).

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| The bus is more cheap. | The bus is **cheaper**. |
| The car is more faster. | The car is **faster**. |
| It's bigger that my house. | It's bigger **than** my house. |
| The bike is more good. | The bike is **better**. |
| How much time it takes? | How **long does it take**? |
| I go by foot. | I go **on foot**. / I **walk**. |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** «más rápido que» → **faster than** (palabra corta: **-er**; no «more fast»). «más cómodo que» → **more comfortable than** (palabra larga). «que» = **than** (no «that»). «¿Cuánto tiempo se tarda?» → **How long does it take?** «a pie» = **on foot**.

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** Comparing **two** things? Look for **-er** or **more** and then **than**.

### Questions and answers

**Q: *How much* or *How many*?**

A: **How many** + things you can count (*eggs, apples*). **How much** + things you can't count (*rice, milk*) and **money**.

**Q: *Some* or *any*?**

**Q: What does *I'd like* mean?**

**Q: Can I say *breads* or *rices*?**

**Q: How do I say prices?**

A: *$3.50* = "three fifty" or "three dollars and fifty cents." *$24.50* = "twenty-four fifty."

### Grammar Box 4: Fixed plans (present continuous)

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What | are you doing | on Saturday? / tomorrow? / next week? | ‖ | I'm | working / helping my brother / visiting family | on Saturday. |

### Questions and answers

**Q: How do I make *going to*?**

A: ***am / is / are* + going to + base verb**: *I'**m going to visit** my mother.* *She'**s going to rest**.*

**Q: *going to* or *-ing* for the future?**

A: Both are OK for plans. *-ing* is common for **fixed plans with a time**: *I'm working on Saturday.*

**Q: What's the difference between *Would you like to* and *Do you want to*?**

A: Same meaning. *Would you like to* is **more polite**. *Do you want to* is **friendly**.

**Q: How do I say no politely?**

A: *Sorry, I can't.* + **a reason**: *I'm working. / I'm visiting my mother.* Maybe add *Thanks for asking!*

**Q: What's *gonna*?**

A: Fast speech for *going to*. Understand it; in writing, use *going to*.

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I going to work. | I'**m** going to work. |
| I go to visit my mother tomorrow. | I'm **going to** visit my mother tomorrow. |
| She is going to visits. | She is going to **visit**. |
| Would you like come? | Would you like **to** come? |
| No, I can't. (too short) | **Sorry**, I can't. **I'm working.** |
| Are you free in Saturday? | Are you free **on** Saturday? |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** «Voy a visitar» → **I'm going to visit** (no olvides *am / is / are*). «¿Quieres venir?» → **Do you want to come?** o, más cortés, **Would you like to come?** (con *to*). Para decir que no: **Sorry, I can't** + una razón. «el sábado» → **on Saturday**.

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** Plans need **three parts**: *am / is / are* + *going to* + verb.

### Grammar Box 3: *Can I…?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Can I** | pay / leave a message / talk to a manager | by phone? / now? / please? | ‖ | Yes, you can. / Sure. | One moment, please. |
| ^ | ^ | ^ | ‖ | Sorry, you can't. | You have to come in person. |

### have to / need to / don't have to

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| necessary | I **have to** pay the bill. She **has to** work. |
| necessary | We **need to** fill out a form. He **needs to** call. |
| **not** necessary (you can choose) | You **don't have to** pay today. She **doesn't have to** come. |

### Questions and answers

**Q: What's the difference between *have to* and *need to*?**

A: Almost the same: something is **necessary**. *I have to pay the bill. I need to pay the bill.*

**Q: What does *don't have to* mean?**

A: **Not necessary.** You can choose. *You don't have to pay today* = you can pay later. It does **not** mean "you can't."

**Q: *have to* or *has to*?**

A: *he / she / it* → ***has to***: *She **has to** work.* Questions: ***Does** she **have** to work?*

**Q: *Can you* or *Could you*?**

A: Both are requests. *Could you* is **more polite**: good for phone calls, offices, and strangers.

**Q: What do I say when I don't understand?**

A: *Could you repeat that, please? Could you speak more slowly? Can you spell that?*

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I have to paying. | I have to **pay**. |
| I must to pay. | I **have to** pay. / I must pay. |
| She have to work. | She **has** to work. |
| Could you to help me? | Could you **help** me? |
| Does she has to go? | Does she **have** to go? |
| You don't have to smoke here. (= not necessary) | You **can't** smoke here. (= not allowed) |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** «Tengo que pagar» → **I have to pay** / **I need to pay** (con *to* + verbo base). «No tienes que pagar hoy» → **You don't have to pay today** = no es necesario (¡no es una prohibición! Prohibido = **can't**). «¿Podría…?» → **Could you…?** (más cortés que *Can you…?*).

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** On the phone, always keep these phrases ready: *Could you repeat that? Can you spell that? Could you speak more slowly?*

<!-- subst -->
| QUESTION | < | ‖ | ANSWER | < | < |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What's your | goal? / dream? | ‖ | My goal / My dream | is to | get a better job. / improve my English. / help my kids. |

### Tense helper

| Time words | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ago, last year, yesterday | **Past** | I **moved** here two years ago. I **was** nervous. |
| every day, usually, always | **Present** | I **work** at a bakery. I **take** the bus. |
| now, right now, today | **Present continuous** | I'**m studying** English **now**. |
| tomorrow, next year, this weekend | **going to** (plans) | I'**m going to take** a computer class. |
| (offers, promises) | *will* (recognize) | I'**ll** help you. |

### Questions and answers

**Q: How do I choose the tense?**

A: **Look at the time word.** *ago* → past. *every day* → present. *now* → *-ing*. *next year* → *going to*.

**Q: *was* or *did*?**

A: ***was / were*** with adjectives and places: *I **was** nervous.* ***did*** with other verbs in questions and negatives: ***Did** you **study**?*

**Q: How do I talk about a goal?**

A: *My goal is **to** + verb*: *My goal is **to get** an office job.*

**Q: Can I mix tenses in one talk?**

A: **Yes!** A good talk goes **then → now → next**: *I was… Now I… Next year I'm going to…*

**Q: How do I end a talk?**

A: *Thank you to my teachers and classmates. Thank you for listening.*

### Common mistakes

| ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|
| I am agree. | I **agree**. |
| One year ago I am nervous. | One year ago I **was** nervous. |
| Next year I take a class. | Next year I'**m going to** take a class. |
| In the next year… | **Next year**… |
| My goal is get a job. | My goal is **to** get a job. |
| I have 30 years. | I'**m** 30 (years old). |

> **Nota para hispanohablantes:** «Estoy de acuerdo» → **I agree** (sin *am*). «El próximo año» → **next year** (sin *in the*). «Hace un año estaba nerviosa» → **One year ago I was nervous**. «Mi meta es conseguir…» → **My goal is to get…** (con *to*). «Tengo 30 años» → **I'm 30**.

> **Tip for speakers of other languages:** Before you speak, find the **time word**. It tells you which tense to use.

## 6. Listening Audio: Free AI Voices

### 6.1 What we needed
The voices had to be **completely free (no paid tier required)**, with a license that clearly allows classroom use, nonprofit or commercial use, and **redistribution** (posting MP3s on a website and sharing them with learners).

### 6.2 Comparison

| Option | Cost | License / terms for our use | Quality and ease | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chatterbox TTS** by Resemble AI (open model, runs on a computer) | Free | **MIT License** for both the code (github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox) and the model weights (huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox). Every file it makes carries an inaudible **Perth watermark** that marks the audio as AI-generated. We clone every voice from a short synthetic reference clip made with Kokoro-82M; Chatterbox's built-in default voice is not used. | Very natural, expressive voices; runs on an ordinary computer without a GPU (about 5–8 minutes per lesson, including automatic checks). | ✅ **Recommended** (used for the final audio) |
| **Kokoro-82M** (open model, runs on a computer) | Free | **Apache 2.0** weights. The model card says it "can be deployed anywhere," it is used in commercial products, and it was trained only on permissive or non-copyrighted audio. The runner we used (kokoro-onnx) is MIT-licensed. | Natural voices; about 20 US English voices; runs on an ordinary laptop or server without a GPU. Each 1.5-minute clip took a few seconds. | ✅ Good free backup (used for the first drafts; listeners found it a little rushed and flat) |
| **Piper TTS** (open, runs on a computer) | Free | The code is open source, but each voice has its own license, and the voices are trained on recordings of real people. | Good and very fast | ❌ Not used (this curriculum uses only synthetic voices, not voices based on recordings of real people) |
| **Microsoft Edge "Read Aloud" / edge-tts** | Free | edge-tts is an **unofficial** client for Edge's Read Aloud service. Microsoft has **not** published permission to redistribute the audio, and Microsoft Q&A answers point to the paid Azure Speech service for publishing. | Excellent voices | ❌ Not recommended (rights unclear) |
| **Google Cloud Text-to-Speech** | Has a monthly free allowance (e.g., 4M characters for Standard/WaveNet, 1M for Neural2/Chirp 3 HD), **but billing must be enabled** with automatic charges past the free tier | Commercial output is allowed under Google Cloud terms | Excellent | ❌ Fails the "no paid tier needed" rule (needs a credit card / billing account) |
| **TTSMaker** (website) | Free up to 20,000 characters per week | The site says audio can be used "for any lawful purpose, including commercial use," with no attribution required, but it "reserv[es] the right to make adjustments" to the free policy | Easy (no install); quality varies by voice | ⚠️ Acceptable backup. Save a screenshot of the terms on the day you make the audio |
